## A. WHAT WAS REPAIRED

### 1. Absolutist Claims Removed/Qualified

**Before:**
- "NOT formally approved as an independent conciliar decree at Ephesus 431" (absolute negation)
- "only restored at Constantinople II (553)" (implies complete absence 431-553)
- "122-year gap in conciliar reception" (implies no reception whatsoever)

**After:**

- "spectrum from textual presence to explicit conciliar endorsement" (not binary absence/presence)
- "not read as an independent document" at Chalcedon 451 (precise procedural fact)
- "not required as a condition of reconciliation" in Formula of Reunion 433 (diplomatic framing)

### 2. Five-Category Distinction Framework Added

All references to the Twelve Anathemas now distinguish:

**(A) Textual presence** — The Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas was physically present in the Ephesus 431 Acts  
**(B) Reading of the document** — Likely read or referenced during the council (evidence: inclusion in Acts)  
**(C) Theological endorsement** — Substantive theological content affirmed in spirit by Cyrillian-leaning council  
**(D) Formal conciliar approval** — Status historically disputed: no evidence of vote comparable to Nicene Creed or Second Letter  
**(E) Later explicit conciliar reception** — Constantinople II (553) provided explicit, formal conciliar endorsement

### 3. Ephesus 431 Status Clarified

**Before:** Binary claim that Twelve Anathemas were "not formally approved"  
**After:** Nuanced position:
- Textual presence: confirmed (in Acts)
- Reading: likely (inferred from Acts structure)
- Theological endorsement: implicit (council favored Cyrillian theology)
- Formal conciliar approval: historically disputed (no vote evidence, unlike Second Letter)
- This distinction became critical at Chalcedon 451

### 4. Formula of Reunion 433 Treatment Refined

**Before:** "deliberately set aside" (implies rejection)  
**After:** "not required as a condition of reconciliation" (diplomatic framing)

Three interpretations now presented:
- **Interpretation A:** Twelve Anathemas had no formal approval at Ephesus 431, so nothing to "set aside"
- **Interpretation B:** Diplomatic pragmatism — not required for reconciliation with Antiochene bishops
- **Interpretation C:** Implicit theological endorsement without formal conciliar status

### 5. Chalcedon 451 Treatment Corrected

**Before:** "DELIBERATELY OMITTED" (implies intentional rejection)  
**After:** "not read as an independent document" (precise procedural fact)

Key distinctions:
- The Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas was not read as a standalone document
- But Cyrilline theological substance was present and affirmed (through Second Letter, Formula of Reunion)
- Distinction between "document not read" vs. "theology rejected" is critical
- Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings were readmitted without retraction

### 6. Constantinople II 553 Contextualized

**Before:** "only restored at Constantinople II (553)" + "122-year gap" (implies complete absence)  
**After:** "most explicit conciliar reception" + explicit warning against "122-year gap" formulation

Key clarifications:
- Explicit conciliar reaffirmation in the context of the Three Chapters controversy
- This does NOT mean the Twelve Anathemas "disappeared" between 431 and 553
- The reception shows a spectrum: textual presence → implicit theological endorsement → explicit conciliar reception
- The 431-553 period includes Formula of Reunion 433, Chalcedon 451, and ongoing theological influence

- Constantinople II 553 is explicit conciliar reception, not "restoration"
- The 431-553 period is not a "122-year gap" but a spectrum of reception
- The reception shows: textual presence → implicit endorsement → explicit reception
