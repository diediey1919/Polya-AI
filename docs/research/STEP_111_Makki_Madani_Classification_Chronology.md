## 1. DEFINITIONS OF MAKKI/MADANI

### 1.1 Three Classical Methodologies

According to **al-Zarkashi** (d. 794 AH/1392 CE) in *Al-Burhan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an* (vol. 1, p. 187) and **al-Suyuti** (d. 911 AH/1505 CE) in *Al-Itqan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an* (vol. 1, pp. 45-47), there are **three main definitions**:

**Definition 1 (Geographical):**
- Makki = revealed in Mecca or surrounding areas
- Madani = revealed in Medina or surrounding areas
- **Proponents:** al-Mawardi (d. 450 AH)
- **Problem:** Cannot classify verses revealed during travel (e.g., at Hudaybiyyah, during Hijrah)

**Definition 2 (Chronological - Most Common):**
- Makki = revealed before Hijrah (622 CE), even if revealed outside Mecca
- Madani = revealed after Hijrah, even if revealed in Mecca or outside Medina
- **Proponents:** Majority of classical scholars; "most popular and preferred view"
- **Source:** Attributed to Ibn 'Abbas tradition

**Definition 3 (Thematic/Address):**
- Makki = addresses people of Mecca
- Madani = addresses people of Medina
- **Proponents:** Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 241 AH/855 CE)
- **Problem:** Many verses address "O mankind" or believers generally, not specific groups

**Source:** Learn Quran Post (https://learn-quran.co/post/makki-and-madani-verses-in-quran); New Age Islam (https://newageislam.com/islamic-q-a/sources,-meanings-significance-quranic-verses/d/117538)

### 1.2 Stylistic Criteria (Internal Evidence)

Classical scholars identified textual markers:

**Makki Indicators:**
- Use of "O mankind" (يأيها الناس)
- Use of "kalla" (كلا - "never") - found only in second half of Qur'an
- Presence of sajda verses (except Q.13, Q.22)
- Huruf muqatta'at (alif lam mim, etc.) - except Q.2, Q.3, Q.13
- Short verses, rhymed prose (saj')
- Oaths and eschatological imagery

**Madani Indicators:**
- Use of "O you who believe" (يأيها الذين آمنوا)
- "O People of the Book"
- Mention of munafiqun (hypocrites) - except Q.29:11
- Legal detail, warfare, community organization
- Longer verses, discursive style

**Source:** Wikipedia (Meccan surah); About Islam (https://aboutislam.net/shariah/quran/introduction-to-the-quran/two-phases-of-quran-revelation-what-differences/2)

## 2. CLASSICAL CHRONOLOGICAL LISTS

### 2.1 Ibn 'Abbas Tradition

The most widely cited chronological list is attributed to **'Abd Allah ibn 'Abbas** (d. 687 CE), cousin of the Prophet. His list begins:
1. Q.96 (al-'Alaq - Iqra')
2. Q.68 (al-Qalam - Nun)
3. Q.73 (al-Muzzammil)
4. Q.74 (al-Muddaththir)
5. Q.111 (al-Masad)
6. Q.81 (al-Takwir)
...continuing through all 114 surahs

**Source:** Tanzil.net revelation order (https://tanzil.net/docs/revelation_order); Islamiqate (https://www.islamiqate.com/3222/what-the-traditional-order-earliest-quranic-surahs-chapters)

### 2.2 Jabir ibn Zayd List

**Jabir ibn Zayd** (d. 93 AH/711 CE) provided an alternative early list. Differences from Ibn 'Abbas are minor but exist in ordering of early surahs.

**Source:** Islamiqate notes "slight differences" between narrations; "later narration traditionally more widely accepted"

### 2.3 Ibn al-Nadim's Fihrist

**Abu'l-Faraj Muhammad ibn Ishaq al-Nadim** (d. 995 CE) in *Al-Fihrist* records a list "based upon Nu'man ibn Bashir":

**Order:** 96, 68, 73, 74, 111, 81, 94, 103, 89, 93, 92, 100, 108, 102, 107, 109, 105, 112, 113, 114, 53, 80, 97, 91, 85, 95, 106, 101, 75, 104, 77, 50, 90, 55, 72, 36, 7, 25, 35, 19, 20, 56, 26, 27, 28, 17, 11, 12, 10, 15, 37, 31, 23, 34, 21, 37, 40, 41, 47, 43, 44, 45, 46, 51, 88, 18, 6, 16, 71, 14, 32, 52, 67, 69, 70, 78, 79, 82, 84, 30, 29, 83, 54, 86

**Citation:** Ibn al-Nadim, *Al-Fihrist* 1:49-52; cited in About Islam article

### 2.4 al-Zarkashi (al-Burhan)

**Badr al-Din al-Zarkashi** (d. 794 AH/1392 CE) in *Al-Burhan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an* discusses the three definitions but does not provide a complete alternative chronological list. He emphasizes the Ibn 'Abbas tradition as authoritative.

**Source:** Al-Zarkashi, *Al-Burhan* vol. 1, p. 187

### 2.5 al-Suyuti (al-Itqan)

**Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti** (d. 911 AH/1505 CE) in *Al-Itqan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an* (vol. 1, pp. 45-47) compiles multiple lists and reconciles differences. He notes:

- **Agreed Madani surahs:** 20
- **Agreed Makki surahs:** 82
- **Disputed surahs:** 12

He follows Ibn 'Abbas as the primary authority but acknowledges variant traditions.

**Source:** Al-Suyuti, *Al-Itqan* vol. 1, pp. 45-47; Islamiqate

### 2.6 al-Sijistani

**Abu Ya'qub al-Sijistani** (d. after 971 CE) was an Ismaili da'i and philosopher. However, he is not primarily known for Qur'anic chronology. The name may be confused with other early transmitters. No complete chronological list attributed to al-Sijistani was found in available sources.

**Source:** Grokipedia (Abu Ya'qub al-Sijistani); note that al-Sijistani is known for Ismaili theology, not Qur'anic sciences

### 2.7 Tanzil.net Order (Modern Compilation)

The Tanzil project provides a complete chronological list based on classical sources (primarily Ibn 'Abbas tradition):

**First 10:** 96, 68, 73, 74, 1, 111, 81, 87, 92, 89
**Last 10:** 110, 112, 113, 114, 5, 9, 62, 48, 60, 66

**Notable:** Q.1 (al-Fatiha) is placed 5th in revelation order, not 1st as in the mushaf.

**Source:** Tanzil.net (https://tanzil.net/docs/revelation_order)

### 2.8 Comparison of Lists

**Do the lists agree?**

- **Broad agreement** on: Q.96 as first; Q.68, 73, 74 as early; general Makki/Madani division
- **Minor disagreements** on: exact ordering within first 10-20 surahs; classification of disputed surahs (Q.13, Q.22, Q.55, Q.76)
- **Major differences:** Ibn al-Nadim's list (based on Nu'man ibn Bashir) differs significantly in middle and late Meccan surahs from Ibn 'Abbas tradition

**Source:** Comparative analysis of Tanzil, Ibn al-Nadim, and Islamiqate data

## 3. FIRST REVELATION

### 3.1 Competing Traditions

**Position A: Q.96:1-5 (al-'Alaq / Iqra') - FIRST ABSOLUTE**

- **Hadith source:** Sahih al-Bukhari (no. 3), Sahih Muslim (no. 160), via **'Aisha** (d. 678 CE)
- **Narrative:** Angel Gabriel appeared to Muhammad in Cave of Hira; commanded "Iqra'" (Recite/Read); Muhammad replied "I cannot read"; Gabriel embraced him tightly and repeated command three times; then revealed Q.96:1-5
- **Scholarly consensus:** This was the **first revelation absolutely** (awwal ma unzila)

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 3; Sahih Muslim 160; Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* on Q.96:1-5

**Position B: Q.74:1-7 (al-Muddaththir) - FIRST AFTER FATRAH**

- **Hadith source:** Sahih al-Bukhari (no. 4954), Sahih Muslim (no. 247), via **Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah** (d. 78 AH/697 CE)
- **Narrative:** Jabir reported that after the initial revelation (Q.96:1-5), there was a period of interruption (fatrah) during which revelation ceased. Then Gabriel appeared again and revealed Q.74:1-7
- **Interpretation:** This was the **first revelation after the fatrah** (awwal ma unzila ba'da al-fatrah)

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4954; Sahih Muslim 247; Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* on Q.74:1-7

**Position C: Q.73:1-10 (al-Muzzammil) - EARLY BUT NOT FIRST**

- Some early lists place Q.73 third or fourth in revelation order (after Q.96, Q.68)
- Ibn Kathir notes Q.73 was revealed "after the fatrah" but does not claim it was first after fatrah
- **Status:** Early Meccan, but not widely claimed as "first"

**Source:** Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* on Q.73:1

### 3.2 The Fatrah (Period of Interruption)

**Definition:** Fatrah = period when revelation ceased between initial vision (Q.96:1-5) and resumption of revelation

**Duration:** Disputed
- **Six months:** Most common estimate (Ibn Kathir)
- **One to two years:** Alternative view
- **Three years:** Some exegetes (e.g., Sheikh Ma'rifat, as cited in Iqra Online)

**Theological significance:**
- Fatrah explains why Q.74:1-7 or Q.73:1-10 could be considered "first" by some narrators
- Distinction between "first revelation absolutely" vs. "first revelation after interruption"

**Source:** Iqra Online (https://iqraonline.net/the-suspension-of-revelation); Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* on Q.73 introduction

### 3.3 Scholarly Positions

**Al-Zarkashi, al-Suyuti, Ibn Kathir:** All affirm Q.96:1-5 as first absolutely, with Q.74:1-7 as first after fatrah

**Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani** (d. 852 AH/1449 CE) in *Fath al-Bari* reconciles the hadiths: 'Aisha's report refers to the absolute beginning; Jabir's report refers to the resumption after fatrah

**Source:** Ibn Hajar, *Fath al-Bari* commentary on Bukhari 3 and 4954

## 4. FINAL/LAST REVELATION

### 4.1 Competing Traditions (No Single Consensus)

**Important note:** None of the traditions about the "last verse" are marfu' (directly from the Prophet); all are companion reports (mawquf)

**Source:** Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan, Irsyad Fatwa Series 292 (https://muftiwp.gov.my/en/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum-cat/3140-irsyad-al-fatwa-series-292-the-last-verse-of-the-quran-that-was-revealed)

**Position 1: Q.2:281 (Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah)**

- **Source:** Multiple narrations from Ibn 'Abbas; al-Alusi, *Tafsir Ruh al-Ma'ani* 3/54
- **Claim:** Prophet lived 9 nights after this revelation
- **Supporters:** Ibn 'Abbas, Sa'id ibn Jubayr; preferred by Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani (*Fath al-Bari* 8/317)

**Position 2: Q.2:278 (O you who believe, fear Allah and give up what remains of interest)**

- **Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari (4544), from Ibn 'Abbas
- **Claim:** "The last verse revealed to the Prophet was the verse dealing with usury (riba)"
- **Reconciliation:** al-Suyuti notes Q.2:278, 281, and 282 were revealed together; Q.2:281 was last among them

**Position 3: Q.5:3 (This day I have perfected for you your religion)**

- **Source:** Revealed at 'Arafah during Hajjat al-Wida' (Farewell Pilgrimage), 10 AH
- **Claim:** Last verse regarding rulings of permissibility and prohibition
- **Problem:** Prophet lived 80 nights after this (per Sheikh Muhammad Ra'fat Sa'id, *Tarikh Nuzul al-Qur'an* 1/52)
- **Reconciliation:** al-Alusi explains this was last verse on legal rulings, not last verse absolutely

**Position 4: Q.4:176 (They request from you a ruling concerning kalalah)**

- **Source:** Sahih Muslim (1618), from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib
- **Claim:** "The last verse revealed is that pertaining to kalalah (inheritance)"
- **Problem:** Ibn Hajar rejects this, stating Prophet lived 50 days after this verse (*Fath al-Bari* 8/317)

**Position 5: Q.110 (Surah al-Nasr)**

- **Source:** Narration from Ibn 'Abbas
- **Claim:** Last complete surah revealed; revealed at Mina during Last Hajj
- **Interpretation:** Hinted at Prophet's impending death

**Position 6: Q.2:282 (Verse of debt)**

- **Source:** Some companions
- **Reconciliation:** al-Suyuti groups this with Q.2:278 and 2:281 as revealed together

**Position 7: Q.9:128-129 (Last verses of al-Tawbah)**

- **Source:** Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b
- **Problem:** al-Zarqani rejects this, stating Ubayy meant "last verses of surah al-Tawbah," not last verses absolutely

**Position 8: Q.18:110 (Say, I am only a man like you)**

- **Source:** Mu'awiyah ibn Abi Sufyan, cited by Ibn Kathir
- **Interpretation:** Ibn Kathir suggests this means "last verse not abrogated"

### 4.2 Scholarly Consensus

**Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani:** Prefers Q.2:281 as most accurate

**Al-Baqillani** (d. 403 AH/1013 CE): States "knowing the last verse is not an obligation in religion" and notes companions' opinions were based on limited knowledge (*Al-Intisar lil-Qur'an* 1/245-246)

**Modern preference:** Q.2:281, as it is "not rejected or denied by other verses in other scholars' narrations"

**Source:** Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan, Irsyad Fatwa Series 292

## 5. MECCAN PERIOD PHASES

### 5.1 Duration and Subdivisions

**Total Meccan period:** Approximately 13 years (610-622 CE)

**Modern subdivision (Nöldeke system):**
- **Early Meccan:** ~48 surahs
- **Middle Meccan:** ~21 surahs
- **Late Meccan:** ~21 surahs

**Source:** Nöldeke, *Geschichte des Qorāns*; Wikipedia (Geschichte des Qorāns)

### 5.2 Early Meccan Characteristics (c. 610-615 CE)

**Themes:**
- Tawhid (divine unity) - God as creator, sustainer
- Eschatology - Day of Judgment, resurrection, reward/punishment
- Moral corruption and social injustice
- Oaths by natural phenomena (sun, moon, stars, time)
- Destruction of past civilizations as divine punishment

**Style:**
- Very short verses (often <5 verses per surah: Q.97, 103, 105, 108, 111)
- Intense rhyme and rhythm (saj')
- Vivid, dramatic imagery
- Urgent, warning tone
- Use of oath formulas
- Tripartite structure in some surahs

**Examples:** Q.96, 74, 111, 108, 104, 107, 102, 105, 92, 90, 94, 93, 97, 86, 91, 80, 68, 87, 95, 103, 85, 73, 101, 99, 82, 81, 53, 84, 100, 79, 77, 78, 88, 89, 75, 83, 69, 51, 52, 56, 70, 55, 112, 109, 113, 114, 1

**Source:** Nöldeke Group 1; Wikipedia (Meccan surah); About Islam

### 5.3 Middle Meccan Characteristics (c. 615-619 CE)

**Themes:**
- Prophetic narratives (Noah, Abraham, Moses, Joseph)
- Polemic against Meccan polytheism
- Resurrection and afterlife
- Signs of God in nature
- Moral exhortation

**Style:**
- Longer verses than Early Meccan
- Narrative sections
- Still uses rhyme and rhythm but less intense
- More discursive argumentation

**Examples:** Q.54, 37, 71, 76, 44, 50, 20, 26, 15, 19, 38, 36, 43, 72, 67, 23, 21, 25, 17, 27, 18

**Source:** Nöldeke Group 2; Wikipedia (Geschichte des Qorāns)

### 5.4 Late Meccan Characteristics (c. 619-622 CE)

**Themes:**
- Extended prophetic stories (Moses vs. Pharaoh as paradigm)
- Polemic against Meccan opposition
- Signs and proofs of prophethood
- Preparation for migration
- Consolation to Prophet

**Style:**
- Even longer verses
- More prose-like, less rhymed
- Detailed narratives
- Argumentative and defensive tone

**Examples:** Q.32, 41, 45, 16, 30, 11, 14, 12, 40, 28, 29, 31, 42, 35, 7, 6, 34, 39, 10, 2, 13

**Source:** Nöldeke Group 3; Wikipedia (Geschichte des Qorāns)

## 6. MEDINAN PERIOD PHASES

### 6.1 Duration and Context

**Total Medinan period:** Approximately 10 years (622-632 CE)

**Number of Medinan surahs:** 20-31 (depending on classification system)
- **Consensus:** 20 surahs
- **Extended counts:** up to 28-29

**Source:** Wikipedia (Medinan surah); Islamiqate

### 6.2 Medinan Themes and Style

**Major themes:**
- Legal legislation (shari'ah): inheritance, marriage, divorce, contracts, punishment
- Social organization and community building
- Warfare (jihad), military expeditions, spoils of war
- Relations with People of the Book (Jews, Christians)
- Hypocrites (munafiqun) and internal opposition
- Ritual obligations (prayer, fasting, zakat, hajj)
- Political authority and governance

**Style:**
- Long, discursive verses
- Legal detail and precision
- Direct addresses ("O you who believe")
- Calmer, instructive tone
- Less rhetorical flourish, more practical guidance

**Source:** Wikipedia (Medinan surah); Grokipedia (Medinan surah); About Islam

## 7. HYBRID/MIXED SURAHS

### 7.1 Surahs with Both Makki and Madani Verses

Classical scholars recognized that many surahs contain verses from both periods:

**Al-Baqarah (Q.2):** Medinan overall, but v.281 revealed at Mina during Last Hajj

**Al-i-Imran (Q.3):** Medinan overall

**Al-Nisa' (Q.4):** Medinan overall

**Al-Ma'idah (Q.5):** Medinan overall, but v.3 revealed at 'Arafah during Last Hajj

**Al-Anfal (Q.8):** Medinan overall, but vv.30-36 from Mecca

**Al-Tawbah (Q.9):** Medinan overall, but last two verses from Mecca

**Al-Hajj (Q.22):** Medinan overall, but vv.52-55 revealed between Mecca and Medina (during travel)

**Al-Ra'd (Q.13):** Disputed - majority consider Makki with some Madani verses (e.g., v.43)

**Al-Rahman (Q.55):** Disputed - some classify as Makki, others as Madani

**Al-Insan (Q.76):** Disputed - some classify as Makki, others as Madani

**Source:** Tanzil.net; Islamiqate; Wikipedia (Medinan surah)

### 7.2 Classical Positions on Disputed Surahs

**Al-Ra'd (Q.13):**
- **Makki view:** Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Abu Talhah, Qatadah, Sa'id ibn Jubayr
- **Madani view:** Based on weak and fabricated narrations (per Islamiqate analysis)
- **Conclusion:** More likely Makki based on structure and sound narrations

**Al-Mutaffifin (Q.83):**
- **View 1:** Last surah revealed in Mecca (Ibn 'Atiyyah)
- **View 2:** First surah revealed in Medina (Ibn 'Ashur)
- **View 3:** Revealed on route to Medina
- **Conclusion:** Likely Medinan based on content (story of cheating trader)

**Al-Rahman (Q.55) and Al-Insan (Q.76):**
- Both disputed in classical sources
- Tanzil classifies both as Medinan (positions 97 and 98)
- Some traditional lists place them in Late Meccan

**Source:** Islamiqate (https://islamiqate.com/3506/is-there-a-list-separating-the-meccan-and-medinan-suras); Tanzil.net

### 7.3 Significance of Mixed Surahs

**Theological implications:**
- Demonstrates Qur'an was revealed piecemeal over 23 years
- Shows editorial process in compiling surahs (verses from different periods combined)
- Supports view that surah arrangement was ijtihadi (companion reasoning), not tawqifi (divinely ordained)

**Source:** Analysis based on classical sources

## 8. SURAH ORDER VS. REVELATION ORDER

### 8.1 The Mushaf Order (Uthmanic Codex)

**Current order:** 114 surahs from Al-Fatihah (Q.1) to Al-Nas (Q.114)

**Organization principle:** Primarily by length (longest to shortest), with exceptions:
- Al-Fatihah (7 verses) opens the mushaf
- Al-Baqarah (286 verses) is longest
- Short surahs clustered at end (Juz' 'Amma, Q.78-114)

**Source:** Wikipedia (List of chapters in the Quran); Grokipedia

### 8.2 Tawqifi vs. Ijtihadi Debate

**Position A: Tawqifi (Divinely Ordained)**

**Claim:** Surah order was fixed by the Prophet himself through divine instruction; Abu Bakr and Uthman simply preserved this order

**Evidence:**
- Arrangement of Ha-Mim surahs, Ta-Sin series
- Distinction of Musabbihat surahs
- Alif-Lam-Mim surahs not sequential but deliberately separated
- Lack of chronological arrangement suggests divine wisdom

**Proponents:** Minority scholarly position; Imam Zarkashi in *Al-Burhan* advocates this

**Source:** Seekers Guidance (https://seekersguidance.org/answers/quran/who-set-the-order-of-the-suras-found-in-the-quran-today); Nur al-Din Itr, *'Ulum al-Qur'an*

**Position B: Ijtihadi (Companion Reasoning) - MAJORITY VIEW**

**Claim:** Surah order was determined by companions during compilation, particularly under Uthman (644-656 CE)

**Evidence:**
- Different companions had different codex orders:
  - **Ibn Mas'ud codex:** Al-Fatihah, Al-Baqarah, Al-Nisa', Al-i-Imran (not Uthmanic order, not revelation order)
  - **Ubayy ibn Ka'b codex:** Different arrangement
  - **'Ali ibn Abi Talib codex:** Arranged by revelation order (started with Q.96, then Q.74, then Q.68)
- Disagreement between Ibn 'Abbas and Uthman regarding placement of Al-Anfal and Al-Tawbah (no Bismillah between them)
- Order of verses within surahs is unanimously tawqifi; order of surahs is not

**Proponents:** Majority of scholars; Al-Qadi 'Iyad, al-Nawawi, contemporary scholars

**Source:** Seekers Guidance; IslamOnline (https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/tankees-in-reciting-the-quran); Bukhari 4996 commentary

## 9. KEY FINDINGS AND IMPLICATIONS

### 9.1 Areas of Scholarly Consensus

1. **First revelation absolutely:** Q.96:1-5 (via 'Aisha hadith in Bukhari/Muslim)
2. **First revelation after fatrah:** Q.74:1-7 (via Jabir hadith in Bukhari/Muslim)
3. **Three definitions of Makki/Madani:** Geographical, chronological (preferred), thematic
4. **Verse order within surahs:** Tawqifi (divinely ordained) - unanimous
5. **Surah order:** Ijtihadi (companion reasoning) - majority view
6. **Medinan themes:** Law, warfare, community, hypocrites, People of the Book
7. **Makki themes:** Tawhid, eschatology, prophetic narratives, polemic

### 9.2 Areas of Scholarly Disagreement

1. **Last verse revealed:** No consensus; multiple companion reports (Q.2:281, 2:278, 5:3, 4:176, Q.110)
2. **Exact chronological order:** Ibn 'Abbas vs. Ibn al-Nadim vs. Nöldeke differ significantly
3. **Classification of disputed surahs:** Q.13, Q.22, Q.55, Q.76, Q.83
4. **Duration of fatrah:** 6 months vs. 1-2 years vs. 3 years
5. **Number of Medinan surahs:** 20 (consensus) vs. 28-29 (extended counts)

### 9.3 Forensic Implications

**For textual criticism:**
- Mixed surahs demonstrate editorial compilation process
- Disputed chronologies suggest human judgment in classification
- Companion codices (Ibn Mas'ud, Ubayy, 'Ali) had different arrangements

**For historical reconstruction:**
- Fatrah period explains gaps in revelation narrative
- Meccan/Medinan division reflects historical context (persecution vs. state-building)
- Chronological lists based on companion testimony, not Prophet's direct instruction

**For theological claims:**
- "Last verse" traditions are mawquf (companion reports), not marfu' (prophetic)
- Surah order is ijtihadi, not tawqifi (majority view)
- No single authoritative chronological list exists in classical sources
