## 3. HISTORICAL CONTEXT
Chain (non-linear, controversy-driven):
NT Christology (Logos incarnate, Kyrios) → Irenaeus (~180, recapitulation, Logos incarnate vs Gnostics) → Nicaea 325 (homoousios vs Arius) → Cappadocians (~360-380, ousia/hypostasis grammar) → Constantinople 381 (reaffirm Nicaea + expand pneumatology) → Antiochene/Alexandrine tension (Word-man vs Word-flesh emphases) → Ephesus 431 (Cyril vs Nestorius, Theotokos) → Eutyches/Flavian crisis 448-449, Ephesus II (449) → Leo's Tome (449) → Chalcedon 451 under Marcian → Later reception: Chalcedonian vs non-Chalcedonian (Oriental Orthodox) split.

## 4. CHALCEDONIAN DEFINITION ANALYSIS
Core formula (Greek key terms preserved):
- hena kai ton auton Huion (one and the same Son)
- teleion en theoteti / teleion en anthropoteti (perfect in Godhead / perfect in manhood)
- theon alethos kai anthropon alethos (truly God and truly man)
- homoousion to Patri kata ten theoteta / homoousion hemin kata ten anthropoteta (consubstantial with Father per Godhead / with us per manhood)
- ek duo physeon / en duo physesin (from/in two natures — textual variant; Chalcedon reads EN duo physesin)
- asynchytos, atreptos, adiairetos, achoristos (without confusion, without change, without division, without separation)
- eis mian hypostasin / mian prosopon syntrechouse (concurring into one hypostasis / one prosopon)
- ouk eis duo prosopa merizomenon e diairoumenon (not parted or divided into two persons)

Function: rejects Arian subordination (true God), Docetic/Eutychian absorption (true man, two natures preserved), Nestorian division (one hypostasis, not two Sons).

## 5. GREEK TERMINOLOGY
- hypostasis: concrete subsistent / person-subject. At Chalcedon = the one who exists (Christ).
- physis: nature / essence-as-instantiated (divine nature, human nature with rational soul and body).
- prosopon: person-as-presented; used synonymously with hypostasis here (one prosopon = one hypostasis).
- ousia: shared essence (used in homoousion clauses, carried from Nicaea).
Safety: do NOT treat physis=ousia=hypostasis=prosopon as interchangeable. At Chalcedon: ONE hypostasis/prosopon IN/T rejoins TWO physeis; ousia language anchors each physis to its consubstantial group (Father / humanity).

## 6. HYPOSTASIS/PHYSIS + ONE PERSON/TWO NATURES
- "One person, two natures" is a reasonable summary (SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION): captures one hypostasis/prosopon + two intact physeis. Limitation: English "person" carries modern psychological connotations absent from hypostasis; summary omits the four adverbs and the homoousion double-clause which do the anti-heretical work. Never infer one physis=one hypostasis, nor two physeis=two Christs — Chalcedon explicitly denies both.

## 7. TRINITY VS CHRISTOLOGY SEPARATION
- TRINITY: mia ousia, treis hypostaseis (one divine essence, three persons).
- CHRISTOLOGY: mia hypostasis, duo physeis (one hypostasis of the Son incarnate, two natures).
Collapse = category error. Trinity answers "how is God three-in-one"; Chalcedon answers "how is the incarnate Son God and man." Do NOT use Chalcedon to prove Trinity nor Trinity to prove Chalcedon.

## 8. CYRIL ANALYSIS
- Cyril of Alexandria (d.444): mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkomene (one incarnate nature of God the Word), Theotokos, hypostatic union, communicatio idiomatum. Chalcedon endorses Cyril's letters as orthodox but rephrases in two-physeis idiom acceptable to Antiochenes + Leo. Do NOT claim Cyril's formula and Chalcedon's formula are verbally identical; continuity of intent (one Christ) with terminological development.

## 9. NESTORIUS ANALYSIS
- Primary Nestorian corpus NOT INGESTED. From hostile/secondhand + Bazaar fragments: Nestorius resisted Theotokos (proposed Christotokos), stressed prosopic conjunction of two natures, accused by Cyril of dividing Christ into two Sons. Do NOT define him flatly as "believed in two Christs" — that is Cyril's polemical reading; modern reconstruction sees a two-prosopa/two-physis conjunction model. Status: DISPUTED / MEDIATED.

## 10. EPHESUS 431
- Background: Nestorius (Constantinople 428) vs Cyril; Theotokos as shibboleth for unity of subject. Council (431, Ephesus, Cyril presiding, John of Antioch counter-synod) condemned Nestorius, affirmed Theotokos and Cyrilline unity. Political/ecclesiastical context (Alexandria vs Constantinople vs Antioch, imperial convocation by Theodosius II) inseparable from theology. Fact vs later gloss must stay separated.

## 11. CHALCEDON 451 + LEO'S TOME
- Imperial context: Marcian/Pulcheria convoke; ~520 bishops. Proceedings: condemn Dioscorus/Eutyches and Ephesus II (449), receive Leo's Tome (two-nature idiom: each nature acts in communion with the other) as consonant with Cyril, draft Definition balancing Cyrilline one-subject with Antiochene-Leonine two-nature integrity. Rejects Eutychian confusion/absorption ("from two natures" collapsed into "one nature after union"). Note: "merely Jesus is God and man" is an UNSAFE simplification — omits hypostasis/physis grammar and four adverbs.

## 12. ORIENTAL ORTHODOX / MIAPHYSITE DISTINCTION
- Do NOT label Oriental Orthodox (Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian/Eritrean) as "monophysite" without qualification (UNSAFE). Distinguish: Eutychian Monophysitism (one nature absorbs humanity, condemned) vs Miaphysite (mia physis composite — Cyrilline idiom: one incarnate nature from two, humanity intact). Oriental Orthodox receive Cyril + Ephesus, reject Chalcedon as Nestorianizing. Theological correctness NOT adjudicated here; historical positions recorded only.

## 13. SCRIPTURE VS CREED + JOHN 1:1 BOUNDARY
- NT text ≠ Nicaea ≠ Cappadocians ≠ Chalcedon. Chalcedon is conciliar interpretation, not manuscript/autograph/canon/critical edition. Existing John 1:1 grammar audit UNCHANGED. Chain: John 1:1 (Logos was God + became flesh John 1:14) → Johannine/patristic reception → Nicene homoousios → post-Nicene disputes → Chalcedonian two-nature grammar. John 1:1 does NOT explicitly contain Chalcedonian formula; Chalcedon develops (not invents ex nihilo, not verbally repeats) Johannine Christology.

## 14. CLAIM CLASSIFICATIONS A-K
- A. Chalcedon invented deity of Christ: UNSAFE (pre-Nicene high Christology + Nicaea precede it).
- B. Chalcedon invented humanity of Christ: UNSAFE (humanity affirmed in NT + Irenaeus onward).
- C. Chalcedon simply repeated exact NT language: UNSAFE (conciliar philosophical idiom goes beyond bare NT wording).
- D. Chalcedon formulated precise person/nature distinction: SAFE.
- E. "One person, two natures" reasonable summary: SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION (see §6).
- F. Chalcedon teaches two separate Christs: UNSAFE (explicitly anathematized division).
- G. Chalcedonian and Oriental Orthodox should automatically be treated identical: NOT ESTABLISHED (requires separate comparative work; terminological + historical split real).
- H. Calling Oriental Orthodox simply "monophysite" sufficiently precise: UNSAFE (conflates Eutychian with Miaphysite).
- I. Chalcedon represents terminological/conceptual development: SAFE.
- J. Development automatically proves fabrication: UNSAFE (genetic fallacy).
- K. Antiquity automatically proves truth: UNSAFE (appeal to antiquity).

## 15. HISTORICAL-DEVELOPMENT MODEL
EARLY CHRISTOLOGY → NICAEA (Son/Father) → CONSTANTINOPLE 381 (Spirit + reaffirm Son) → CYRIL/NESTORIUS (unity vs distinction) → EPHESUS 431 (one subject, Theotokos) → CHALCEDON 451 (one hypostasis in two natures) → LATER TRADITIONS (Chalcedonian Byzantine/Latin; Miaphysite Oriental; Church of East dyophysite). Preserve diversity; no single linear evolution.

## 16. STEELMAN
- A (clarify/stabilize): Chalcedon preserves apostolic conviction (true God/true man, one Christ) with necessary precision against division/confusion.
- B (significant development): Chalcedon imports non-biblical metaphysics (physis/hypostasis) to settle what NT leaves unformalized; formula underdetermines later puzzles (will, energy, knowledge of Christ).
- Truth not decided by historiography alone.
