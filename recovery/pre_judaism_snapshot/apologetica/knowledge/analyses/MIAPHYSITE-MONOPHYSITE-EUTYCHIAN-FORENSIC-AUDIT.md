## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This audit performs rigorous forensic analysis of Christological terminology and identities that emerged from the 5th-century Christological controversies. The goal is to prevent conflation of historically distinct positions, figures, and later denominational identities.

**Core Findings:**

1. **Semantic Shift:** Greek terms (*physis*, *hypostasis*, *prosopon*) underwent significant semantic evolution. Cyril used *physis* ≈ *hypostasis* (concrete reality); Chalcedon used *physis* = abstract nature.

2. **Critical Distinctions:**
   - Cyril ≠ Eutyches (Cyril never taught absorption)
   - Dioscorus ≠ Eutyches (Dioscorus opposed Eutychian confusion)
   - Severus ≠ Eutyches (Severus explicitly rejected Eutyches)
   - Oriental Orthodox ≠ Eutychians (OO explicitly reject Eutychianism)
   - Miaphysite ≠ Monophysite (different theological commitments)

3. **Terminological Precision:**
   - "Mia physis" in Cyril's usage ≠ "monophysis" in Eutyches's usage
   - "Monophysite" is a polemical label, not a self-designation
   - "Miaphysite" is the preferred scholarly and OO self-designation

4. **Identity Formation:** Later denominational identities (Oriental Orthodox, Chalcedonian, Eastern Orthodox, Roman Catholic) should NOT be projected backward into 451.

## 2. TERMINOLOGY FIRST: DEFINITIONS AND DISTINCTIONS

### 2.1 Key Greek Terms

#### **φύσις (physis)**

- **Basic meaning:** Nature, reality, concrete existence, essential character
- **Etymology:** From φύω (phyō, "to grow, to be")
- **Semantic range (5th century):**

| Usage | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Pre-Chalcedonian (Cyril) | Concrete individual reality ≈ hypostasis | "One incarnate nature of God the Word" |
| Chalcedonian (451) | Set of properties/characteristics defining a kind | "In two natures" |
| Philosophical (general) | Essential nature, what something is | "Human nature" = rational animal |

**Key distinction:** Cyril used *physis* to mean "concrete individual reality" (≈ *hypostasis*). Chalcedon used *physis* to mean "abstract set of properties" (nature as such). This semantic shift is critical.

**Translation risk:** Translating *physis* uniformly as "nature" obscures the semantic shift and creates false equivalence.

---

#### **μία φύσις (mia physis)**

- **Full formula:** μία φύσις τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη (*mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē*)
- **Literal translation:** "One incarnate nature of God the Word"
- **Cyril's meaning:** One concrete incarnate subject/reality of the Word
- **Eutyches's interpretation:** One nature after union (absorption of humanity into divinity)
- **Miaphysite interpretation:** One united nature from two without confusion
- **Chalcedonian concern:** Sounds like monophysitism if *sesarkōmenē* ("incarnate") is dropped

**Critical observation:** The participle *sesarkōmenē* ("incarnate," "enfleshed") is essential. Cyril's formula explicitly affirms the incarnation. Eutyches's formula ("two natures before, one after") lacks this safeguard.

---

#### **ὑπόστασις (hypostasis)**

- **Basic meaning:** Concrete individual existence, subsistence, underlying reality
- **Etymology:** From ὑφίστημι (hyphistēmi, "to stand under")
- **Semantic range:**

| Usage | Meaning | Context |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Cappadocian (Trinitarian) | Concrete individual person | "Three hypostases" |
| Chalcedonian (Christological) | Concrete individual person | "One hypostasis" |
| Cyril (Christological) | Sometimes conflated with physis | "One hypostasis" ≈ "one physis" |

**Key distinction:** *Hypostasis* = who/what exists (concrete individual). *Physis* = how/what kind (nature). In Chalcedonian usage, Christ is one *hypostasis* in two *physeis*.

---

#### **πρόσωπον (prosopon)**

- **Basic meaning:** Face, countenance, person, role, mask
- **Etymology:** From πρός (pros, "toward") + ὤψ (ōps, "face")
- **Semantic range:**

| Usage | Meaning | Context |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Nestorian | Common prosopon uniting two hypostases | "Prosopon of union" |
| Chalcedonian | One prosopon and one hypostasis | "One person" |
| Theatrical | Role, character, mask | Actor's persona |

**Distinction from hypostasis:** *Prosopon* = external manifestation, role, or appearance. *Hypostasis* = concrete existence, underlying reality. Nestorius used *prosopon* to describe the union without conflating the two *hypostases*.

---

#### **οὐσία (ousia)**

- **Basic meaning:** Being, essence, substance, reality
- **Etymology:** From εἰμί (eimi, "to be")
- **Semantic range:**

| Usage | Meaning | Context |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Trinitarian (Cappadocian) | Essence, substance | "One ousia, three hypostases" |
| Christological | Rare; *homoousios* more common | "Consubstantial" |

**Homoousios (ὁμοούσιος):** "Same essence/substance." Used at Nicaea (325) to affirm the Son's consubstantiality with the Father. Applied in Christology to affirm Christ's consubstantiality with humanity (Chalcedon).

---

### 2.2 Key Compound Terms

#### **Monophysis (μονοφύσις)**

- **Etymology:** μόνος (monos, "alone, only") + φύσις (physis, "nature")
- **Literal meaning:** "One nature only"
- **Theological meaning:** Christ has only one nature (divine), with humanity absorbed or extinguished
- **Association:** Eutyches, Eutychianism
- **Self-designation:** NO — "monophysite" is a polemical label

#### **Miaphysis (μιαφύσις)**

- **Etymology:** μία (mia, "one") + φύσις (physis, "nature")
- **Literal meaning:** "One nature" (but from two)
- **Theological meaning:** One united nature from two (divine and human) without confusion
- **Association:** Severus of Antioch, Oriental Orthodox
- **Self-designation:** YES — Oriental Orthodox prefer "Miaphysite"

**Critical distinction:**
- **Monophysis:** One nature ONLY (humanity absorbed)
- **Miaphysis:** One nature FROM TWO (humanity preserved in union)

#### **Dyophysis (δυοφύσις)**

- **Etymology:** δύο (dyo, "two") + φύσις (physis, "nature")
- **Literal meaning:** "Two natures"
- **Theological meaning:** Christ exists in two natures (divine and human)
- **Association:** Chalcedon, Leo's Tome
- **Self-designation:** YES — Chalcedonians affirm "in two natures"

## 3. CYRIL'S MIA PHYSIS: DEDICATED AUDIT

### 3.1 The Formula

**Greek:** μία φύσις τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη

**Transliteration:** *mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē*

**Standard translation:** "One incarnate nature of God the Word"

**Cyril's context:** Cyril used this formula in his writings and attributed it to Athanasius (via pseudo-Athanasian texts in Alexandrian archives). The formula was central to Cyril's anti-Nestorian polemic.

---

### 3.2 Cyril's Meaning

**Cyril's interpretation:**

1. **"Mia physis" = one concrete reality:** Cyril used *physis* to mean concrete individual reality, approximately equivalent to *hypostasis*. For Cyril, "one nature" meant "one concrete subject," not "one abstract nature."

2. **"Sesarkōmenē" = incarnate:** The participle is essential. It explicitly affirms that the one concrete subject is the incarnate Word — God the Word made flesh. Without this, the formula could be interpreted as abstract monophysitism.

3. **"Tou Theou Logou" = of God the Word:** The subject is the divine Logos. The formula is Christological, not Trinitarian.

**Cyril's key commitments (from STEP 78):**

- One subject (the Word)
- Hypostatic union (*henōsis kat' hypostasin*)
- Complete humanity (rational soul + body)
- No confusion or change of natures
- Distinction preserved conceptually (not in reality after union)

**Source:** Cyril, *Second Letter to Nestorius* (430); *Formula of Reunion* (433)

---

### 3.3 What Cyril Does NOT Mean

**Cyril does NOT teach:**

1. **Absorption of humanity:** Cyril explicitly affirms Christ's complete humanity, including a rational soul and body. He rejects any notion that the divine nature absorbed or extinguished the human nature.

2. **Apollinarianism:** Cyril explicitly affirms that Christ had a human rational soul (*psyche logike*). He rejects the Apollinarian view that the Logos replaced the human mind.

3. **Confusion of natures:** Cyril insists that the union occurs "without confusion or change" (*asynchytōs, atreptōs*). The divine and human natures are united but not mixed.

4. **Division after union:** Cyril rejects any notion that Christ can be divided into two subjects or two sons after the union. The one concrete subject is the Word.

**Source:** Cyril, *Second Letter to Nestorius*; *Formula of Reunion*; *Letters to Succensus*

---

### 3.4 The Apollinarian Origin

**Critical fact:** Cyril's formula originated in pseudo-Athanasian texts that were actually Apollinarian forgeries. Cyril inherited the formula from Alexandrian archives and attributed it to Athanasius in good faith.

**Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. 310–390):**

- Taught that the Logos replaced the human rational soul in Christ
- Used the formula "one incarnate nature of God the Word"
- Condemned at Constantinople I (381)

**Cyril's response to Apollinarianism:**

- Cyril explicitly rejected Apollinarian mind-replacement
- Cyril affirmed Christ's complete humanity (rational soul + body)
- Cyril's use of the formula was anti-Apollinarian in content, despite Apollinarian origin

**Assessment:** Cyril's Christology is anti-Apollinarian in substance, even though his formula came from Apollinarian sources. The formula's meaning in Cyril's usage is determined by Cyril's explicit commitments, not by Apollinaris's original intent.

## 4. CYRIL AND APOLLINARIANISM: EXPLICIT INVESTIGATION

### 4.1 The Accusation

**Question:** Does Cyril's *mia physis* terminology deny Christ's complete humanity or rational soul?

**Accusation:** Cyril's formula sounds like Apollinarianism ("one nature" = Logos replaced human mind).

---

### 4.2 Evidence Against the Accusation

**Evidence:**

1. **Cyril explicitly affirms rational soul:**
   - Second Letter to Nestorius (430): "flesh animated by a rational soul" (*sarx empsychōmenē psychē logikē*)
   - Formula of Reunion (433): "rational soul and body"

2. **Cyril explicitly affirms complete humanity:**
   - Formula of Reunion (433): "perfect God and perfect man"
   - Double consubstantiality: "consubstantial with the Father in divinity, consubstantial with us in humanity"

3. **Cyril explicitly rejects Apollinarian mind-replacement:**
   - Cyril never taught that the Logos replaced the human mind
   - Cyril explicitly affirmed that Christ assumed a complete human nature, soul and body

4. **Cyril's anti-Apollinarian intent:**
   - Cyril used the formula to defend against Nestorian division, not to deny humanity
   - Cyril's explicit commitments contradict Apollinarian interpretation

**Source:** Cyril, *Second Letter to Nestorius*; *Formula of Reunion*; *Letters to Succensus*

---

### 4.3 Conclusion

**Finding:** Cyril's Christology is explicitly anti-Apollinarian in content, even though his archive mistakenly supplied him with a phrase originated by Apollinaris.

**Reasoning:** Cyril's explicit commitments (rational soul, complete humanity, no confusion) contradict Apollinarian interpretation. The formula's meaning in Cyril's usage is determined by Cyril's explicit commitments, not by the formula's Apollinarian origin.

**Assessment:** The accusation that Cyril's *mia physis* is Apollinarian is **UNSAFE** without qualification. Cyril's Christology is anti-Apollinarian in substance.

## 5. MIA PHYSIS VS MONOPHYSIS: LINGUISTIC AND THEOLOGICAL TEST

### 5.1 The Question

**Question:** Is "mia physis" (Cyril's formula) equivalent to "monophysis" (Eutyches's formula)?

**Test:** Are the two formulas historically and linguistically interchangeable?

---

### 5.2 Linguistic Analysis

**Mia physis (μία φύσις):**

- **Etymology:** μία (mia, "one") + φύσις (physis, "nature")
- **Literal meaning:** "One nature"
- **Cyril's usage:** One concrete incarnate subject (≈ hypostasis)
- **Key feature:** Includes *sesarkōmenē* ("incarnate")

**Monophysis (μονοφύσις):**

- **Etymology:** μόνος (monos, "alone, only") + φύσις (physis, "nature")
- **Literal meaning:** "One nature only"
- **Eutyches's usage:** One nature after union (humanity absorbed)
- **Key feature:** "Two natures before, one after"

**Linguistic distinction:**

- *Mia* = "one" (numeral, neutral)
- *Monos* = "alone, only" (exclusive, restrictive)

**Assessment:** The formulas are NOT linguistically equivalent. *Mia physis* is neutral; *monophysis* is exclusive.

---

### 5.3 Theological Analysis

**Cyril's "mia physis":**

- One concrete incarnate subject
- Complete humanity preserved (rational soul + body)
- No confusion or absorption
- Distinction preserved conceptually

**Eutyches's "monophysis":**

- One nature after union
- Humanity absorbed into divinity ("like a drop of honey in the sea")
- Christ's body not consubstantial with ours
- Distinction extinguished

**Theological distinction:**

- Cyril: One subject FROM TWO (without confusion)
- Eutyches: One nature ONLY (humanity absorbed)

**Assessment:** The formulas are NOT theologically equivalent. Cyril's formula affirms complete humanity; Eutyches's formula denies it.

---

### 5.4 Conclusion

**Finding:** "Mia physis" ≠ "monophysis" historically, linguistically, or theologically.

**Reasoning:**
1. Linguistic: *mia* (one) ≠ *monos* (alone, only)
2. Theological: Cyril affirms complete humanity; Eutyches denies it
3. Historical: Cyril never taught absorption; Eutyches did

**Assessment:** The claim that "mia physis" = "monophysis" is **UNSAFE**. The formulas represent different theological commitments.

## 6. EUTYCHES: FORENSIC PROFILE

### 6.1 Historical Identity

**Name:** Eutyches (Εὐτυχής)

**Dates:** c. 378–454

**Position:** Archimandrite (abbot) of a monastery in Constantinople

**Historical context:** Eutyches was a monk who became involved in the Christological controversy after Cyril's death (444). He was tried at the Home Synod (448), reinstated at Ephesus II (449), and condemned at Chalcedon (451).

**Source:** Acts of the Home Synod (448); Acts of Ephesus II (449); Acts of Chalcedon (451)

---

### 6.2 Eutyches's Christology

**Core formula:** "Two natures before union, one nature after union"

**Key commitments:**

1. **One nature after incarnation:** After the union, Christ has only one nature (divine).
2. **Absorption of humanity:** Christ's humanity was absorbed into divinity ("like a drop of honey in the sea").
3. **Christ's body not consubstantial:** Christ's body is not consubstantial with ours (it is heavenly, not earthly).
4. **No distinction after union:** The distinction between divine and human is extinguished after the union.

**Source:** Acts of the Home Synod (448); Leo's Tome (449)

---

### 6.3 Eutyches's Relationship to Cyril

**Question:** Did Eutyches accurately represent Cyril's Christology?

**Answer:** NO.

**Evidence:**

1. **Eutyches and sesarkōmenē:** According to hostile conciliar records, Eutyches appears to have rejected or omitted Cyril's qualifier *sesarkōmenē* (incarnate). The characterization that Eutyches "dropped" or rejected Cyril's qualifier *sesarkōmenē* should be treated cautiously. The surviving evidence concerning Eutyches is mediated through his confession, opponents, synodal proceedings, and later Christological controversy. The wording should therefore not be used as a standalone proof of Eutyches's complete theological position without source-specific analysis.

2. **Eutyches taught absorption:** Cyril explicitly rejected absorption and affirmed complete humanity.

3. **Eutyches denied consubstantiality:** Cyril affirmed Christ's consubstantiality with humanity (Formula of Reunion, 433).

4. **Cyril never taught "two before, one after":** Cyril's formula is "one incarnate nature," not "two before, one after."

**Assessment:** Eutyches distorted Cyril's Christology by dropping the incarnation language and teaching absorption. Eutyches is NOT a faithful interpreter of Cyril.

---

### 6.4 Condemnation of Eutyches

**Home Synod (448):**

- Condemned by Flavian of Constantinople
- Eutyches refused to affirm "two natures after union"
- Deposed and exiled

**Ephesus II (449):**

- Reinstated by Dioscorus of Alexandria
- Dioscorus's reinstatement was procedural error (Dioscorus did not endorse Eutyches's theology)

**Chalcedon (451):**

- Condemned as heretical
- Eutyches's formula anathematized
- "Two natures after union" affirmed

**Assessment:** Eutyches was correctly condemned. His Christology (absorption, denial of consubstantiality) is incompatible with Cyril's Christology and with Chalcedon.

---

## 7. DIOSCORUS: AUDIT SEPARATE FROM EUTYCHES

### 7.1 Historical Identity

**Name:** Dioscorus (Διόσκορος)

**Dates:** c. 390–454

**Position:** Patriarch of Alexandria (444–451)

**Historical context:** Dioscorus succeeded Cyril as patriarch and inherited Cyril's Christological commitments. He presided over Ephesus II (449), was deposed at Chalcedon (451), and died in exile (454).

**Source:** Acts of Ephesus II (449); Acts of Chalcedon (451)

---

### 7.2 Dioscorus's Christology

**Core commitment:** Rigorous Cyrillian Christology

**Key positions:**

1. **One incarnate nature of the Word:** Dioscorus affirmed Cyril's formula.
2. **Opposed Eutychian confusion:** Dioscorus rejected Eutyches's absorption theology.
3. **Defended Cyril's terminology:** Dioscorus insisted on "one nature" language.
4. **Rejected "two natures after union":** Dioscorus believed this language was Nestorian.

**Critical distinction:** Dioscorus opposed Eutychian absorption while affirming Cyril's "one nature" formula.

**Source:** Acts of Ephesus II (449); Acts of Chalcedon (451)

---

### 7.3 Dioscorus at Ephesus II (449)

**What Dioscorus did:**

1. **Presided over the council:** Dioscorus was appointed president by Emperor Theodosius II.
2. **Reinstated Eutyches:** Dioscorus restored Eutyches to his monastery and communion.
3. **Condemned Flavian:** Dioscorus deposed Flavian of Constantinople.
4. **Condemned others:** Dioscorus deposed Theodoret, Ibas, and others.

**What Dioscorus did NOT do:**

1. **Endorse Eutyches's theology:** Dioscorus reinstated Eutyches on procedural grounds (Flavian's synod was flawed), not theological grounds.
2. **Teach absorption:** Dioscorus explicitly rejected Eutychian absorption.
3. **Deny Christ's humanity:** Dioscorus affirmed Cyril's complete humanity.

**Assessment:** Dioscorus's reinstatement of Eutyches on procedural grounds. Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship. Dioscorus's Christology was rigorous Cyrillian, not Eutychian.

---

### 7.4 Dioscorus's Condemnation at Chalcedon

**Grounds for condemnation:**

1. **Procedural:** Dioscorus refused to appear when summoned (three times).
2. **Political:** Dioscorus was seen as the leader of the anti-Chalcedonian faction.
3. **Jurisdictional:** Dioscorus had deposed Flavian and others without proper authority.

**NOT primarily theological:** Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship. His deposition should not be reduced either to a purely procedural matter or to a simple identification of Dioscorus with Eutychianism.

**Assessment:** Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship. Dioscorus's Christology (rigorous Cyrillian) was not condemned at Chalcedon.

---

### 7.5 Conclusion

**Finding:** Dioscorus ≠ Eutyches.

**Reasoning:**
1. Dioscorus opposed Eutychian absorption.
2. Dioscorus affirmed Cyril's complete humanity.
3. Dioscorus reinstated Eutyches on procedural grounds. Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship.
4. Dioscorus's condemnation should not be reduced either to a purely procedural matter or to a simple identification of Dioscorus with Eutychianism.

**Assessment:** The claim that "Dioscorus was simply an Eutychian" is **UNSAFE**. Dioscorus was a rigorous Cyrillian who opposed Eutychian absorption.

## 8. SECOND COUNCIL OF EPHESUS (449): LATROCINIUM

### 8.1 Historical Context

**Date:** August 8, 449

**Location:** Ephesus

**Convened by:** Emperor Theodosius II

**President:** Dioscorus of Alexandria

**Purpose:** To adjudicate the appeal of Eutyches against his condemnation at the Home Synod (448).

**Source:** Acts of Ephesus II (449); Leo's Tome (449)

---

### 8.2 Key Actions

**What the council did:**

1. **Reinstated Eutyches:** Eutyches was restored to his monastery and communion.
2. **Condemned Flavian:** Flavian of Constantinople was deposed.
3. **Condemned Eusebius:** Eusebius of Dorylaeum was deposed.
4. **Condemned Theodoret:** Theodoret of Cyrrhus was deposed.
5. **Condemned Ibas:** Ibas of Edessa was deposed.
6. **Condemned Domnus:** Domnus of Antioch was deposed.

**What the council did NOT do:**

1. **Endorse Eutyches's theology:** The council reinstated Eutyches on procedural grounds, not theological grounds.
2. **Affirm absorption:** The council did not affirm Eutychian absorption.

---

### 8.3 Violence and Coercion

**Allegations:**

- Flavian was beaten by monks and imperial soldiers.
- Flavian died shortly after (August 11, 449) from injuries or harsh treatment.
- Papal legates were prevented from reading Leo's Tome.
- Bishops were coerced into signing condemnations.

**Assessment:** The council was procedurally flawed and involved violence. Dioscorus's leadership was heavy-handed and coercive.

**Source:** Acts of Ephesus II (449); Leo's Tome (449)

---

### 8.4 The Polemical Label "Latrocinium" (Robber Council)

**Origin of the label:**

- Coined by Pope Leo I in his correspondence (449)
- Latin: *latrocinium* = "robbery, banditry"
- Greek: *lēstrikē synodos* = "robber synod"
- This is Leo's polemical characterization, not a neutral historical designation

**Assessment:** "Latrocinium" is a polemical label reflecting Leo's perspective, not a neutral description. The council was procedurally flawed and involved violence, but the label is not historically neutral.

**Recommendation:** Use "the Second Council of Ephesus (449)" or "Ephesus II (449)" as the neutral designation. The term "Latrocinium" may be noted as Leo's polemical characterization but should not be treated as a neutral historical term.

---

### 8.5 Chalcedon's Response (451)

**What Chalcedon did:**

1. **Reversed all decisions:** Chalcedon annulled the acts of Ephesus II.
2. **Rehabilitated the condemned:** Flavian, Theodoret, Ibas, and others were restored.
3. **Condemned Dioscorus:** Dioscorus was deposed for procedural violations.
4. **Affirmed "two natures":** Chalcedon affirmed "in two natures" against Eutychianism.

**Assessment:** Chalcedon's reversal was correct. Ephesus II was procedurally flawed and theologically problematic (reinstatement of Eutyches).

---

### 8.6 Theological Assessment

**Finding:** Ephesus II (449) was procedurally flawed and theologically problematic.

**Reasoning:**
1. Procedural: Dioscorus's leadership was coercive and heavy-handed.
2. Theological: Reinstatement of Eutyches was an error (though not necessarily endorsement of Eutychian theology).
3. Violence: The council involved violence and coercion.

**Critical distinction:** Dioscorus reinstated Eutyches on procedural grounds. Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship.

## 9. FLAVIAN OF CONSTANTINOPLE

### 9.1 Historical Identity

**Name:** Flavian (Φλαβιανός)

**Dates:** c. 400–449

**Position:** Patriarch of Constantinople (446–449)

**Historical context:** Flavian presided over the Home Synod (448) that condemned Eutyches. He was deposed at Ephesus II (449) and died shortly after.

**Source:** Acts of the Home Synod (448); Acts of Ephesus II (449)

---

### 9.2 Flavian at the Home Synod (448)

**What Flavian did:**

1. **Presided over the synod:** Flavian convened the synod to adjudicate charges against Eutyches.
2. **Examined Eutyches:** Flavian questioned Eutyches about his Christology.
3. **Condemned Eutyches:** Flavian deposed Eutyches for refusing to affirm "two natures after union."

**Assessment:** Flavian's synod was procedurally sound. Eutyches was correctly condemned for refusing to affirm orthodox Christology.

---

### 9.3 Flavian at Ephesus II (449)

**What happened:**

1. **Deposed:** Dioscorus deposed Flavian.
2. **Beaten:** Flavian was beaten by monks and soldiers.
3. **Exiled:** Flavian was sent into exile.
4. **Died:** Flavian died shortly after (August 11, 449).

**Assessment:** Flavian's treatment was unjust. He was condemned on procedural grounds, not theological grounds.

---

### 9.4 Rehabilitation at Chalcedon (451)

**What Chalcedon did:**

1. **Rehabilitated Flavian:** Chalcedon restored Flavian's memory and reputation.
2. **Condemned Dioscorus:** Chalcedon deposed Dioscorus for his treatment of Flavian.

**Assessment:** Flavian was correctly rehabilitated. His condemnation at Ephesus II was unjust.

---

### 9.5 Conclusion

**Finding:** Flavian was a faithful defender of orthodox Christology who was unjustly condemned at Ephesus II.

**Reasoning:**
1. Flavian correctly condemned Eutyches at the Home Synod.

## 10. SEVERUS OF ANTIOCH: INDEPENDENT AUDIT

### 10.1 Historical Identity

**Dates:** c. 465–538

**Position:** Patriarch of Antioch (512–518)

**Answer:** NO.

**Severus's reasoning:**

2. **Cyril's formula is sufficient:** Severus believed Cyril's "one incarnate nature" formula is the orthodox standard.

**Assessment:** Severus's rejection of Chalcedon was based on his interpretation of Cyril. Severus believed he was defending Cyril's Christology against Nestorian innovation.

### 10.5 Conclusion

**Finding:** Severus is a sophisticated Miaphysite theologian, NOT an Eutychian.

**Reasoning:**
1. Severus explicitly condemned Eutyches.
2. Severus affirmed complete humanity.
3. Severus rejected absorption.
4. Severus's Christology is rigorous Cyrillian Miaphysitism.

**Assessment:** The claim that "Severus was simply an Eutychian" is **UNSAFE**. Severus was a sophisticated Miaphysite who explicitly rejected Eutychianism.

## 11. MIAPHYSITE CHRISTOLOGY: NEUTRAL MODEL

### 11.1 Definition

**Miaphysite Christology:** The Christological position that Christ has one united nature from two (divine and human) without confusion, mixture, or absorption.

**Key commitments:**

1. **One incarnate nature:** Christ has one nature (*mia physis*) that is incarnate (*sesarkōmenē*).
2. **From two:** The one nature is from two (divine and human).
3. **Complete humanity:** Christ's humanity is complete (rational soul + body).
4. **No confusion:** The union occurs without confusion or mixture (*asynchytōs*).
5. **No division:** Christ is one concrete subject, not two.
6. **Distinction preserved conceptually:** The divine and human are distinct in thought (*theōria*) but united in reality.

---

### 11.2 Miaphysite vs Monophysite

**Critical distinction:**

| Feature | Miaphysite | Monophysite (Eutychian) |
|---------|------------|-------------------------|
| Number of natures | One from two | One only |
| Humanity | Complete | Absorbed |
| Consubstantiality | Affirmed | Denied |
| Confusion | Rejected | Affirmed |
| Self-designation | Yes (Miaphysite) | No (polemical label) |
| Association | Severus, Oriental Orthodox | Eutyches |

**Assessment:** Miaphysite ≠ Monophysite. The two positions have different theological commitments.

---

### 11.3 Miaphysite Theological Rationale

**Why Miaphysites affirm "one nature":**

1. **Cyril's authority:** Miaphysites believe Cyril's formula is the orthodox standard.
2. **Unity of Christ:** Miaphysites emphasize the unity of Christ (one subject).
3. **Anti-Nestorian:** Miaphysites reject any language that implies division.
4. **Incarnation:** Miaphysites affirm the incarnation (one incarnate nature).

**Why Miaphysites reject "two natures":**

1. **Implies division:** Miaphysites believe "two natures after union" implies Christ is divided.
2. **Betrayal of Cyril:** Miaphysites believe "two natures" language betrays Cyril.
3. **Nestorian tendency:** Miaphysites believe "two natures" language is Nestorian.

2. **Complete humanity:** Chalcedon affirms "consubstantial with us in humanity."
3. **Complete divinity:** Chalcedon affirms "consubstantial with the Father in divinity."
4. **No confusion:** Chalcedon affirms "without confusion, without change."
5. **No division:** Chalcedon affirms "without division, without separation."
6. **Cyril's authority:** Chalcedon canonizes Cyril's letters.

**Assessment:** Chalcedonian Christology is a legitimate clarification that protects both unity and distinction.

---

### 22.2 Steelman B: Miaphysite Continuity

**Position:** Cyrillian language emphasizes the concrete unity of the incarnate Word and can resist interpretations that appear to divide Christ into two subjects.

**Rationale:**

1. **One subject:** Cyril's formula emphasizes one concrete subject.
2. **Incarnation:** Cyril's formula includes *sesarkōmenē* (incarnate).
3. **Complete humanity:** Cyril affirms complete humanity.
4. **No confusion:** Cyril affirms "without confusion or change."
5. **Anti-Nestorian:** Cyril's formula resists division.

**Assessment:** Miaphysite Christology is a legitimate interpretation of Cyril that emphasizes unity.

---

### 22.3 Assessment

**Both steelmans are strong.** Some modern and ecumenical assessments identify substantial common Christological commitments together with significant terminological differences. However, important historical, theological, ecclesiological, and interpretive disagreements remain, so the relationship cannot be reduced to terminology alone. Both positions affirm core commitments (one person, complete humanity, complete divinity). The disagreement is about how to express these commitments in language and how to interpret the Christological tradition.

**Recommendation:** Recognize both positions as legitimate interpretations of the Christological tradition. Avoid polemical labels and caricatures.

## 23. HISTORICAL VS THEOLOGICAL TRUTH

### 23.1 Critical Distinctions

**Maintain:**

1. **Historical identity ≠ theological truth:** The fact that a position became dominant does not prove it was correct.
2. **Later institutional identity ≠ original meaning:** Later denominational identities developed over centuries and should not be projected backward.
3. **Polemical terminology ≠ neutral description:** Labels like "Monophysite" and "Robber Council" are polemical, not neutral.
4. **Conciliar condemnation ≠ automatic proof of doctrinal falsehood:** Councils can be wrong (e.g., Ephesus II).
5. **Theological continuity ≠ automatic proof of truth:** Continuity does not prove correctness.

---

### 23.2 Methodological Principles

**Apply:**

1. **Distinguish primary sources from polemical summaries:** Read Cyril, Nestorius, and Chalcedon directly, not just hostile characterizations.
2. **Distinguish terminology from substance:** Different terminologies can express the same substance.
3. **Distinguish historical context from theological content:** Context explains but does not determine content.
4. **Distinguish reception history from theological truth:** Reception history is important but does not prove truth.

## 24. LIMITATIONS

### 24.1 Fragmentary Sources

**Problem:** Many primary sources are lost or fragmentary.

- **Nestorius:** Most of Nestorius's Greek writings were destroyed; we rely on the Syriac *Bazaar of Heracleides*.
- **Eutyches:** Eutyches's own writings are lost; we rely on hostile summaries.
- **Dioscorus:** Dioscorus's writings are fragmentary; we rely on conciliar acts.

**Impact:** We must be cautious about characterizing figures based on fragmentary or hostile sources.

---

### 24.2 Polemical Transmission

**Problem:** Many sources are transmitted through polemical contexts.

- **Nestorius:** Transmitted through Cyril's hostile summaries.
- **Eutyches:** Transmitted through Flavian's and Leo's hostile summaries.
- **Dioscorus:** Transmitted through Chalcedonian hostile summaries.

**Impact:** We must distinguish primary sources from polemical summaries.

---

### 24.3 Translation Difficulties

**Problem:** Greek terms underwent semantic evolution and are difficult to translate.

- **Physis:** Cyril used it ≈ hypostasis; Chalcedon used it = abstract nature.
- **Hypostasis:** Different meanings in Trinitarian vs Christological contexts.
- **Prosopon:** Different meanings in Nestorian vs Chalcedonian contexts.

**Impact:** We must be cautious about translating terms uniformly.

---

### 24.4 Semantic Shifts

**Problem:** Terms changed meaning over time.

- **Physis:** Shifted from "concrete reality" to "abstract nature."
- **Hypostasis:** Shifted from "concrete existence" to "person."

**Impact:** We must be cautious about applying later meanings to earlier texts.

---

### 24.5 Later Doctrinal Reinterpretation

**Problem:** Later traditions reinterpreted earlier texts in light of later developments.

- **Chalcedonians:** Interpreted Cyril through Chalcedon.
- **Miaphysites:** Interpreted Chalcedon through Cyril.

**Impact:** We must distinguish original meaning from later reinterpretation.

---

### 24.6 Incomplete Evidence for Eutyches

**Problem:** Eutyches's own writings are lost.

**Impact:** We must be cautious about characterizing Eutyches based on hostile summaries.

---

### 24.7 Incomplete Evidence for Dioscorus

**Problem:** Dioscorus's writings are fragmentary.

**Impact:** We must be cautious about characterizing Dioscorus based on hostile summaries.

---

### 24.8 Regional Diversity

**Problem:** The Christological controversy was not uniform across regions.

- **Alexandria:** Strongly Cyrillian
- **Antioch:** Mixed (some Chalcedonian, some Miaphysite)
- **Constantinople:** Mixed (shifted with imperial policy)
- **Rome:** Consistently Chalcedonian
- **Armenia:** Absent from Chalcedon; later rejected it

**Impact:** We must be cautious about generalizing across regions.

---

### 24.9 Modern Historiographical Terminology

**Problem:** Modern scholars use different terminology than 5th-century actors.

- **Miaphysite:** Modern scholarly term; not used in 5th century.
- **Monophysite:** Polemical label; rejected by Oriental Orthodox.
- **Chalcedonian:** Historical label; later developed denominational meaning.

**Impact:** We must be cautious about applying modern terminology anachronistically.

## 27. FINAL REPORT

### 27.1 Terminology Findings

**Key findings:**

1. **Semantic shift:** Greek terms (*physis*, *hypostasis*, *prosopon*) underwent significant semantic evolution.
2. **Cyril vs Chalcedon:** Cyril used *physis* ≈ *hypostasis*; Chalcedon used *physis* = abstract nature.
3. **Mia physis vs monophysis:** Cyril's formula ≠ Eutyches's formula.
4. **Miaphysite vs Monophysite:** Miaphysite is the preferred term; Monophysite is polemical.

---

### 27.2 Cyril

**Findings:**

1. Cyril's "mia physis" affirms complete humanity (includes *sesarkōmenē*).
2. Cyril's Christology is anti-Apollinarian in content.
3. Cyril never taught absorption.
4. Cyril ≠ Eutyches.

---

### 27.3 Eutyches

**Findings:**

1. Eutyches taught absorption (one nature after union).
2. Eutyches denied Christ's consubstantiality with humanity.
3. Eutyches distorted Cyril's Christology.
4. Eutyches was correctly condemned.

---

### 27.4 Dioscorus

**Findings:**

1. Dioscorus was a rigorous Cyrillian.
2. Dioscorus opposed Eutychian absorption.
3. Dioscorus reinstated Eutyches on procedural grounds. Dioscorus was deposed at Chalcedon (451) amid disputes involving procedure, jurisdiction, his conduct at the Second Council of Ephesus, and Christological controversy. The precise weighting of procedural, ecclesiastical, and theological factors is interpreted differently in later Chalcedonian, non-Chalcedonian, and modern historical scholarship.
4. Dioscorus ≠ Eutyches.

---

### 27.5 Flavian

**Findings:**

1. Flavian correctly condemned Eutyches at the Home Synod.
2. Flavian was unjustly deposed at Ephesus II.
3. Flavian was rehabilitated at Chalcedon.

---

### 27.6 Ephesus 449

**Findings:**

1. Ephesus II was procedurally flawed and involved violence.
2. "Latrocinium" is a polemical label, not a neutral description.
3. Dioscorus's procedural error ≠ endorsement of Eutychian theology.
4. Chalcedon correctly reversed Ephesus II.

---

### 27.7 Severus

**Findings:**

1. Severus was a sophisticated Miaphysite theologian.
2. Severus explicitly condemned Eutyches.
3. Severus affirmed complete humanity.
4. Severus ≠ Eutyches.

---

### 27.8 Miaphysite Christology

**Findings:**

1. Miaphysitism affirms one nature from two (without confusion).
2. Miaphysitism affirms complete humanity.
3. Miaphysitism rejects Eutychianism.
4. Miaphysitism ≠ Monophysitism.

---

### 27.9 Monophysite Terminology

**Findings:**

1. "Monophysite" is a polemical label.
2. "Monophysite" implies Eutychianism.
3. Oriental Orthodox reject the label.
4. Use "Miaphysite" or "Oriental Orthodox" instead.

---

### 27.10 Eutychian Boundary

**Findings:**

1. Eutychianism teaches absorption; Miaphysitism rejects it.
2. Eutychianism denies complete humanity; Miaphysitism affirms it.
3. Eutychianism is condemned at Chalcedon; Miaphysitism is not.
4. Eutychianism ≠ Miaphysitism.

---

### 27.11 Chalcedonian Comparison

**Findings:**

1. Some modern and ecumenical assessments identify substantial common Christological commitments between Chalcedonianism and Miaphysitism.
2. Some modern and ecumenical assessments identify substantial common Christological commitments together with significant terminological differences. However, important historical, theological, ecclesiological, and interpretive disagreements remain, so the relationship cannot be reduced to terminology alone.
3. The two positions can be reconciled through careful terminological clarification, though this does not erase genuine substantive disagreements.
4. Chalcedonianism ≠ Nestorianism; Miaphysitism ≠ Eutychianism.

1. Oriental Orthodox reject Eutyches.
2. Oriental Orthodox affirm complete humanity.
3. Oriental Orthodox theology is Miaphysite, not Eutychian.
4. "Monophysite" is a polemical label; use "Miaphysite" or "Oriental Orthodox."
