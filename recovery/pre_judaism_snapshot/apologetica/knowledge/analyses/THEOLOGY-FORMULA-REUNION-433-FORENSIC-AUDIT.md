# THEOLOGY-FORMULA-REUNION-433-FORENSIC-AUDIT

## Executive Summary

The Formula of Reunion (433 CE) represents a negotiated theological settlement between Cyril of Alexandria and John of Antioch following the fractured Council of Ephesus (431). It is NOT proof that Cyril abandoned his Christology, nor proof that Antioch defeated Alexandria, nor proof that the Twelve Anathemas were rejected. It is a diplomatic reconciliation document that established minimal theological boundaries while leaving significant questions unresolved. The Formula was later read and approved at Chalcedon (451) alongside Cyril's Second Letter, but the Twelve Anathemas were not explicitly affirmed at Chalcedon.

---

## I. PRIMARY TEXT & TRANSMISSION

### 1A. Document Identity

**Primary Source:** Cyril's Letter 39 to John of Antioch (*Laetentur caeli*, "Let the heavens rejoice")

**Date:** April 433 CE

**Transmission:** 
- Greek: Preserved in ACO I.1.4, pp. 15-20
- Latin: Standard Western transmission
- Modern editions: Migne PG 77, cols. 173-182; Wickham (Oxford, 1983)

**Authorship Complexity:**
- The Formula TEXT was drafted by Antiochenes (likely Theodoret of Cyrrhus as primary drafter)
- Sent to Cyril by John of Antioch
- Cyril ACCEPTED and incorporated it into his letter
- Cyril's letter (Epistula 39) is the primary transmission vehicle

**Critical Distinction:** The Formula is embedded within Cyril's letter. Cyril's acceptance is the historical event. The Formula itself is a joint confession, not purely Cyril's composition.

### 1B. Key Textual Passages

**Opening Confession:**
> "We confess, therefore, our Lord Jesus Christ, the Only Begotten Son of God, perfect God and perfect man, consisting in a rational soul and flesh; begotten before the ages of the Father according to his divinity, and in the last days, the same for us and for our salvation, of the Virgin Mary according to his humanity; of the same substance as the Father according to his divinity, and of the same substance as us according to his humanity."

**Union of Two Natures:**
> "For there was a union of two natures (ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων / henōsis dyo physeōn)."

**Theotokos Affirmation:**
> "In accordance with this understanding of the unconfused union, we confess the holy Virgin to be the Mother of God (Θεοτόκον / Theotokon), because God the Word was incarnate and became man, and from the very conception he united the temple taken from her with himself."

**Distinction Clause:**
> "...recognize that theologians employ some [statements] indifferently in view of the unity of person (ὡς ἐφ' ἑνὸς προσώπου / hōs eph' henos prosōpou) but distinguish others in view of the duality of natures (ὡς ἐπὶ δύο φύσεων / hōs epi dyo physeōn)..."

### 1C. Greek Terminology (Verified)

- **ἕνωσις (henōsis):** union
- **δύο φύσεις (dyo physeis):** two natures
- **Θεοτόκος (Theotokos):** God-bearer / Mother of God
- **ὁμοούσιος (homoousios):** consubstantial / of the same substance
- **πρόσωπον (prosōpon):** person
- **τέλειος Θεός, τέλειος ἄνθρωπος (teleios Theos, teleios anthrōpos):** perfect God, perfect man

## II. HISTORICAL CONTEXT (428-433)

### 2A. Pre-Ephesus Tensions (428-431)

**Nestorius Controversy:**
- 428: Nestorius becomes Archbishop of Constantinople
- 428-430: Theotokos controversy erupts
- 430: Cyril writes Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas to Nestorius
- 430: Pope Celestine authorizes Cyril to demand Nestorius's recantation within 10 days

**Imperial Intervention:**
- Emperor Theodosius II convokes Council at Ephesus (431)
- Both Cyril and Nestorius summoned
- John of Antioch and Eastern bishops delayed in travel

### 2B. Council of Ephesus (431) - Fractured Proceedings

**Cyril's Council (June 22, 431):**
- Cyril opens council BEFORE John of Antioch arrives
- Nestorius condemned and deposed (did not attend)
- Cyril's Second Letter approved as Nicene orthodoxy
- Twelve Anathemas included in Acts but NOT formally canonized as conciliar decree
- **Critical:** The Cyrillian synod did NOT canonize the Twelve Anathemas as binding dogma

**John's Counter-Council (Late June 431):**
- John of Antioch and Eastern bishops arrive
- Convene separate council
- Depose Cyril and Memnon (Bishop of Ephesus)
- Do NOT rehabilitate Nestorius
- **Critical:** The anti-Cyrillian synod did NOT restore Nestorius

**Imperial Response:**
- Theodosius II initially orders both Nestorius and Cyril deposed
- Eventually confirms Nestorius's deposition
- Cyril and Memnon restored

### 2C. Post-Ephesus Schism (431-433)

**Continued Division:**
- Alexandria and Antioch remain out of communion
- Nestorius in exile (first at monastery near Antioch, later Great Oasis, Egypt)
- Cyril writes defenses of Twelve Anathemas against Antiochene critics
- Theodoret of Cyrrhus writes extensively against Cyril's Anathemas

**Negotiations:**
- Paul of Emesa mediates between Cyril and John
- Acacius of Beroea involved in diplomatic efforts
- Imperial pressure for reconciliation
- Both sides recognize need for ecclesiastical unity

### 2D. Reconciliation (433)

**The Formula:**
- John of Antioch sends draft confession to Cyril
- Formula drafted by Antiochenes (Theodoret likely primary drafter)
- Cyril accepts enthusiastically in Letter 39 (*Laetentur*)
- April 433: Reunion achieved

**Two Critical Omissions Enable Reunion:**
1. Cyrillian synod had NOT canonized Twelve Anathemas
2. Anti-Cyrillian synod had NOT rehabilitated Nestorius

These omissions provided diplomatic space for reconciliation.

## III. THEOLOGICAL CONTENT ANALYSIS

### 3A. What the Formula Affirms

**Christological Unity:**
- One Christ, one Son, one Lord
- Perfect God and perfect man
- Rational soul and flesh (full humanity)

**Double Consubstantiality:**
- Consubstantial (homoousios) with Father in divinity
- Consubstantial (homoousios) with us in humanity

**Union:**
- Union of two natures (ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων)
- Unconfused union

**Theotokos:**
- Virgin Mary confessed as Theotokos
- God the Word incarnate and became man
- From conception, united temple taken from her with himself

**Distinction Principle:**
- Some statements apply to unity of person
- Some statements apply to duality of natures
- Both levels of predication legitimate

### 3B. What the Formula Does NOT Say

**No Explicit "Two Natures After Union":**
- Formula says "union OF two natures" (not "IN two natures")
- Does not use later Chalcedonian formula "in two natures" (ἐν δύο φύσεσιν)
- Ambiguous on whether two natures persist after union

**No Mention of Twelve Anathemas:**
- Does not affirm Twelve Anathemas
- Does not reject Twelve Anathemas
- Does not mention them at all
- Silent on their status

**No Detailed Union Mechanism:**
- Does not specify hypostatic union vs. prosopic union
- Does not use Cyril's "one incarnate nature" formula
- Does not use Nestorius's "prosopic union" terminology explicitly

**No Soteriological Elaboration:**
- Minimal discussion of salvation mechanics
- Focuses on Christological confession, not soteriology

### 3C. Theological Ambiguities

**Nature Terminology:**
- Uses "two natures" but unclear if this means:
  - Two natures BEFORE union only (Miaphysite reading)
  - Two natures persisting AFTER union (Chalcedonian reading)
  - Two natures as conceptual distinction only

**Person Terminology:**
- Uses "one person" (πρόσωπον) but unclear if this means:
  - Cyril's hypostatic union (one hypostasis)
  - Nestorius's prosopic union (one prosopon of union)
  - Something else

**Theotokos:**
- Affirms Theotokos but with qualification ("in accordance with this understanding of unconfused union")
- Leaves room for different interpretations of what Theotokos entails

## IV. CYRIL'S ACCEPTANCE: WHAT IT MEANS

### 4A. What Cyril Accepted

**Explicit Acceptance:**
- Perfect God and perfect man
- Union of two natures
- Theotokos
- Double consubstantiality
- Distinction principle (person vs. nature predication)

**In Letter to Eulogius (his agent in Constantinople):**
Cyril defends his acceptance:
> "The doctrinal statement which the Easterns have produced is under attack in certain quarters and it is being asked why the bishop of Alexandria tolerated, even applauded it, seeing that they use the words 'two natures'. The Nestorians are saying that he shares their view..."

Cyril's defense: "Two natures" language is acceptable IF understood correctly - as referring to the union FROM two natures, not IN two natures after union.

### 4B. What Cyril Did NOT Accept

**No Renunciation of Twelve Anathemas:**
- Cyril never explicitly renounced or repudiated the Twelve Anathemas
- Never said they were wrong
- Never said they were heretical
- Simply did not make them a condition of reunion

**No Adoption of "Two Natures After Union":**
- Cyril accepted "union OF two natures"
- Did NOT accept Chalcedonian "IN two natures" formula
- Maintained his "one incarnate nature" language in later writings

**No Abandonment of Hypostatic Union:**
- Continued to insist on hypostatic union (not mere prosopic union)
- Letter to Acacius (433): emphasizes single subjectivity of the Logos
- Letters to Succensus (438): defends his Christology against critics

### 4C. Cyril's Strategic Calculation

**Why Cyril Accepted:**
1. **Ecclesiastical Unity:** Sincere desire to heal schism
2. **Nestorius Already Condemned:** Primary goal achieved
3. **Minimal Concession:** Formula could be read in Cyrillian way
4. **Diplomatic Reality:** Imperial pressure, exhaustion from controversy
5. **Theological Flexibility:** Cyril believed "two natures" language could be interpreted orthodoxy

**Cyril's Interpretation:**
- "Two natures" = the two natures FROM WHICH the union occurred
- After union = one incarnate nature of God the Word
- The Formula's "union of two natures" = union FROM two natures
- Therefore, Formula compatible with his Christology

### 4D. What Cyril Did NOT Concede

**Against Miaphysite Critics:**
- Did NOT abandon "one incarnate nature" formula
- Did NOT accept that Christ exists "in two natures" after union
- Did NOT renounce Twelve Anathemas as erroneous
- Did NOT accept Nestorian prosopic union

**Later Writings (433-444):**
- Letter to Acacius (433): defends his position
- Letters to Succensus (438): extensive defense of his Christology
- Against Theodoret (438-444): attacks Theodoret's interpretation
- Continued to use "one incarnate nature" language

## V. JOHN OF ANTIOCH: WHAT HE ACCEPTED

### 5A. John's Theological Position

**Antiochene Concerns:**
- Preserve distinction between divine and human natures
- Protect divine impassibility (God cannot suffer)
- Avoid Apollinarian confusion of natures
- Maintain full humanity of Christ
- Reject Cyril's "one nature" language as potentially monophysite

**John's Acceptance:**
- Accepted deposition of Nestorius (by 433)
- Accepted Theotokos (with qualification)
- Accepted "union of two natures"
- Accepted double consubstantiality

**John's Strategic Calculation:**
1. Nestorius already deposed - no point continuing fight
2. Formula protects two-nature language
3. Formula does NOT require acceptance of Twelve Anathemas
4. Imperial pressure for reconciliation
5. Ecclesiastical unity valuable

### 5B. What John Did NOT Accept

**No Adoption of Cyril's Full Christology:**
- Did NOT accept "one incarnate nature" formula
- Did NOT accept hypostatic union in Cyril's strong sense
- Did NOT accept communicatio idiomatum in Cyril's sense
- Did NOT accept that divine Logos is sole subject of all Christ's actions

**Antiochene Interpretation:**
- "Two natures" = two natures persist after union
- "Union" = prosopic union (Nestorius's term) or loose conjunction
- Theotokos = Mary bore the human Jesus who was united to divine Logos
- Distinction principle = strict separation of divine and human predicates

### 5C. Theodoret's Role

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus:**
- Likely primary drafter of Formula
- Wrote extensively against Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Accepted Formula as reconciliation basis
- Continued to oppose Cyril's "one nature" language
- Later rehabilitated at Chalcedon (451) after condemning Nestorius

**Theodoret's Interpretation:**
- Formula vindicates Antiochene two-nature Christology
- Cyril's acceptance = Cyril's concession to orthodoxy
- Twelve Anathemas effectively rejected by omission
- Formula supersedes Cyril's earlier extreme statements

## VI. THE TWELVE ANATHEMAS: STATUS IN 433

### 6A. What the Formula Says About Twelve Anathemas

**Nothing.**

The Formula:
- Does not mention Twelve Anathemas
- Does not affirm them
- Does not reject them
- Does not require their acceptance
- Does not require their renunciation

**Five-Category Analysis (per STEP 80R Framework):**

Following the precision framework established in STEP 80R, the status of the Twelve Anathemas at 433 must be distinguished across five dimensions:

**(A) Textual Presence:** The Twelve Anathemas remained in Cyril's Third Letter and were part of the documentary record from Ephesus 431. They were textually present in the ecclesiastical landscape of 433 — they had not disappeared from circulation or been destroyed.

**(B) Reading/Recitation:** The Formula does not explicitly read or recite the Twelve Anathemas. Unlike Cyril's Second Letter (which was formally read at both Ephesus 431 and Chalcedon 451), the Twelve Anathemas were not given a liturgical or conciliar reading in the reconciliation process of 433.

**(C) Theological Endorsement:** The Formula does not theologically endorse the Twelve Anathemas. It does not quote them, cite them as authority, or require assent to their specific formulations. The theological substance of the Formula is compatible with a Cyrillian reading but does not explicitly affirm the Anathemas as doctrinal standards.

**(D) Formal Conciliar Approval:** The Twelve Anathemas received no formal conciliar approval in 433. The Formula was a bilateral agreement between Cyril and John, not a council decree. The Anathemas were not canonized, promulgated, or given conciliar authority through the reconciliation process.

**(E) Later Conciliar Reception:** The Twelve Anathemas would eventually receive formal conciliar reception at Constantinople II (553), but in 433 they remained in a state of limbo — neither formally approved nor formally rejected, but diplomatically set aside as a condition for reunion.

**Critical Observation:** The Formula's silence on the Twelve Anathemas is itself a theological and diplomatic act. By not requiring their acceptance or their renunciation, the reconciliation agreement created space for both Cyrillian and Antiochene interpretations to coexist, deferring the question of the Anathemas' authority to future resolution.

### 6B. What "Setting Aside" Means

**Historical Reality:**
- Twelve Anathemas NOT made condition of reunion
- John of Antioch NOT required to accept them
- Cyril NOT required to renounce them
- Simply not part of reconciliation agreement

**Possible Interpretations:**

**Interpretation 1 (Cyril's View):**
- Twelve Anathemas remain valid
- Simply not relevant to this specific reconciliation
- Can be understood in orthodox way
- Not needed as condition because John accepts orthodox Christology

**Interpretation 2 (Antiochene View):**
- Twelve Anathemas effectively rejected by omission
- Cyril's acceptance of Formula = implicit rejection of Anathemas
- Formula supersedes Anathemas
- Anathemas recognized as overly aggressive/polemical

**Interpretation 3 (Diplomatic View):**
- Both sides agreed to disagree on Anathemas
- Focus on what unites (Formula) not what divides (Anathemas)
- Anathemas left ambiguous for future resolution
- Strategic silence

### 6C. Cyril's Later Behavior

**Evidence Cyril Did NOT Renounce Anathemas:**
- Never explicitly said they were wrong
- Never retracted them
- In letters after 433, does not emphasize them but does not repudiate
- In controversy with Theodoret (438-444), does not cite Anathemas prominently but does not disavow

**Evidence Cyril De-Emphasized Anathemas:**
- After 433, Twelve Anathemas fade into background
- Not central to his later theological arguments
- Focus shifts to defending his own Christology, not pressing Anathemas
- In letters to Succensus, defends his position but does not cite Anathemas as primary authority

**Assessment:**
Cyril likely viewed Anathemas as:
- Valid against Nestorius's specific errors
- Not required as universal dogmatic standard
- Polemical documents for specific controversy
- Compatible with Formula when properly understood

### 6D. Status at Chalcedon (451)

**What Chalcedon Did:**
- Read and approved Cyril's Second Letter
- Read and approved Formula of Reunion
- Did NOT read or approve Cyril's Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas
- Did NOT explicitly reject Twelve Anathemas

**Chalcedon's Silence:**
- Does not mention Twelve Anathemas
- Does not affirm them
- Does not condemn them
- Focuses on Second Letter and Formula

**Possible Interpretations:**

**Interpretation 1 (Chalcedonian):**
- Twelve Anathemas not authoritative
- Superseded by Formula and Definition
- Cyril's mature position = Formula, not Anathemas
- Anathemas recognized as overly aggressive

**Interpretation 2 (Anti-Chalcedonian / Later Miaphysite):**
- Chalcedon rejected Cyril's full Christology
- Omission of Anathemas = betrayal of Cyril
- Chalcedon adopted Nestorian language
- Anathemas remain authoritative

**Interpretation 3 (Historical):**
- Chalcedon focused on immediate controversy (Eutyches)
- Did not need to address Anathemas
- Anathemas not central to Eutychian controversy
- Silence does not equal rejection

### 6E. Constantinople II (553) and Twelve Anathemas

**What Constantinople II Did:**
- Explicitly affirmed Cyril's Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas
- Declared Anathemas orthodox and authoritative
- Condemned those who rejected Anathemas
- Elevated Anathemas to dogmatic status

**Historical Context:**
- Emperor Justinian's attempt to reconcile Miaphysites
- Three Chapters controversy
- Neo-Chalcedonian movement to synthesize Cyril and Chalcedon
- Political/diplomatic context

**Assessment:**
- Twelve Anathemas NOT rejected in 433
- NOT explicitly affirmed in 451
- Explicitly affirmed in 553
- Status remained disputed for 120 years
- Formula of Reunion did NOT resolve Anathemas question

## VII. THE FORMULA AND CHALCEDON (451)

### 7A. Formula at Chalcedon

**What Happened:**
- Formula of Reunion read in Session II or III
- Approved alongside Cyril's Second Letter
- Used as basis for Chalcedonian Definition
- Influenced language of Definition

**Chalcedonian Definition Language:**
- "One and the same Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten"
- "Made known in two natures" (ἐν δύο φύσεσιν)
- "Without confusion, without change, without division, without separation"
- "The distinction of natures being in no way annulled by the union"

### 7B. Continuity and Discontinuity

**Continuity with Formula:**
- Perfect God and perfect man
- Double consubstantiality
- One Christ, one Son, one Lord
- Theotokos
- Union language

**Discontinuity with Formula:**
- Chalcedon: "IN two natures" (ἐν δύο φύσεσιν)
- Formula: "union OF two natures" (ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων)
- Chalcedon more explicit about two natures persisting after union
- Chalcedon more explicit about four negative qualifiers (without confusion, etc.)

### 7C. Chalcedon's Use of Formula

**Chalcedon's Interpretation:**
- Formula supports two-nature Christology
- Cyril's acceptance = Cyril's mature, orthodox position
- Formula compatible with Leo's Tome
- Formula bridges Cyril and Leo

**Problems with Chalcedon's Interpretation:**
- Formula is more ambiguous than Chalcedon suggests
- "Union OF two natures" ≠ "IN two natures"
- Formula does not explicitly say two natures persist after union
- Cyril's later writings (433-444) do not support Chalcedonian reading

### 7D. Miaphysite Critique of Chalcedon

**Miaphysite Argument:**
- Chalcedon betrayed Cyril
- Cyril accepted Formula but not "two natures after union"
- Chalcedon adopted Nestorian language
- Formula's "union OF two natures" = Miaphysite "from two natures"
- Chalcedon's "IN two natures" = Nestorian

**Assessment:**
- Miaphysite critique has textual basis
- Formula IS more ambiguous than Chalcedon suggests
- Cyril's later writings support Miaphysite reading more than Chalcedonian
- Chalcedon's interpretation requires reading Formula through Leonine lens
- Formula alone does not prove Chalcedonian Christology

## VIII. CLAIM TESTS: SPECIFIC PROPOSITIONS

### 8A. "The Formula of Reunion represents Cyril's abandonment of his Twelve Anathemas."

**Status: UNSUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Cyril never explicitly renounced Anathemas
- Formula does not mention Anathemas
- Cyril's later writings do not disavow Anathemas
- Cyril defended his acceptance of Formula as compatible with his Christology

**Assessment:** This is an interpretation, not established historical fact. Antiochene interpretation, not demonstrable from evidence.

### 8B. "The Formula proves Cyril accepted 'two natures' Christology."

**Status: UNSUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Cyril accepted "union OF two natures"
- Cyril did NOT accept "IN two natures" (Chalcedonian formula)
- Cyril's later writings maintain "one incarnate nature" language
- Cyril interpreted Formula as compatible with his Miaphysite Christology

**Assessment:** Cyril accepted "two natures" language but interpreted it differently than Chalcedonians. Formula does not prove Cyril accepted Chalcedonian dyophysitism.

### 8C. "The Formula proves the Antiochene party defeated Cyril."

**Status: UNSUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Cyril achieved primary goal (Nestorius condemned)
- Cyril accepted Formula enthusiastically
- Formula can be read in Cyrillian way
- Cyril's later writings show no sense of defeat
- Both sides made concessions

**Assessment:** This is polemical interpretation, not historical fact. Reconciliation involved mutual concessions, not victory.

### 8D. "The Formula proves Cyril defeated the Antiochene party."

**Status: UNSUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- John of Antioch achieved significant concessions
- Formula uses "two natures" language (Antiochene concern)
- Twelve Anathemas not made condition of reunion (Antiochene concern)
- Cyril accepted Formula drafted by Antiochenes

**Assessment:** This is also polemical interpretation. Both sides gained something.

### 8E. "The Formula established the theological basis for Chalcedon."

**Status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Formula read and approved at Chalcedon
- Formula influenced Chalcedonian Definition
- Chalcedon cited Formula as orthodox

**Limitations:**
- Chalcedon went beyond Formula (added "IN two natures")
- Formula more ambiguous than Chalcedon suggests
- Formula alone insufficient for Chalcedonian Definition
- Chalcedon also relied on Leo's Tome, Cyril's Second Letter

**Assessment:** Formula was ONE basis for Chalcedon, not THE basis. Chalcedon developed Formula further.

### 8F. "The Formula resolved the Nestorian controversy."

**Status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Reconciled Cyril and John of Antioch
- Nestorius remained in exile
- Antiochene party accepted Nestorius's deposition

**Limitations:**
- Formula only temporary truce (broke down by 438)
- Did not resolve underlying theological disagreements
- Controversy resurfaced in Eutychian crisis (448-449)
- Chalcedon (451) needed further clarification

**Assessment:** Formula achieved temporary reconciliation but did not permanently resolve theological issues.

### 8G. "The Formula uses Chalcedonian 'two natures' language."

**Status: FALSE**

**Evidence:**
- Formula: "union OF two natures" (ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων)
- Chalcedon: "IN two natures" (ἐν δύο φύσεσιν)
- Different Greek prepositions
- Different theological implications

**Assessment:** Formula and Chalcedon use different terminology. Chalcedonian reading of Formula requires interpretation, not plain reading.

### 8H. "The Twelve Anathemas were rejected by the Formula."

**Status: UNSUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Formula does not mention Anathemas
- Does not affirm them
- Does not reject them
- Silent on their status

**Assessment:** Formula neither affirmed nor rejected Anathemas. Simply did not address them. Status remained ambiguous.

### 8I. "Cyril's acceptance of the Formula was a strategic concession, not theological change."

**Status: SUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Cyril defended acceptance as compatible with his Christology
- Cyril's later writings show no theological shift
- Cyril continued using "one incarnate nature" language
- Cyril interpreted Formula in Miaphysite way

**Assessment:** Cyril viewed Formula as diplomatic concession for ecclesiastical unity, not theological capitulation.

### 8J. "The Formula represents genuine theological agreement between Cyril and John."

**Status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

**Evidence:**
- Both accepted Formula
- Both celebrated reconciliation
- Both interpreted Formula as orthodox

**Limitations:**
- Both interpreted Formula differently
- Underlying disagreements remained
- Truce broke down within 5 years
- Cyril and Theodoret continued controversy

**Assessment:** Formula represented minimal agreement on basic Christological confession, not full theological consensus.

## IX. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### 9A. Steelman: Cyril's Acceptance Was Theologically Consistent

**Argument:**
Cyril's acceptance of the Formula was theologically consistent with his earlier position. The Formula uses "union OF two natures" which Cyril interpreted as "union FROM two natures" - compatible with his "one incarnate nature" formula. Cyril achieved his primary goal (Nestorius condemned) and made a strategic concession for ecclesiastical unity without abandoning his core Christology. His later writings (433-444) show no theological shift and continued use of "one incarnate nature" language. Cyril viewed the Formula as a diplomatic document that could be interpreted orthodoxy, not a theological capitulation.

**Strengths:**
- Supported by Cyril's own defenses
- Supported by Cyril's later writings
- Formula IS ambiguous and can be read in Miaphysite way
- Explains why Cyril accepted enthusiastically
- Explains why Cyril did not renounce Anathemas

**Weaknesses:**
- Antiochene party interpreted Formula differently
- Chalcedon (451) interpreted Formula in Chalcedonian way
- Formula's "two natures" language more naturally read as dyophysite
- Cyril's interpretation requires charitable reading

### 9B. Steelman: The Formula Represents Antiochene Victory

**Argument:**
The Formula represents a victory for Antiochene Christology. Cyril accepted "two natures" language, which he had previously opposed. The Twelve Anathemas were effectively rejected by omission. The Formula was drafted by Theodoret (Antiochene) and accepted by Cyril. Cyril's acceptance shows he recognized his earlier position was too extreme. The Formula established the basis for Chalcedon's dyophysite Definition. Cyril's later de-emphasis of Anathemas shows he recognized they were problematic.

**Strengths:**
- Formula uses "two natures" language (Antiochene concern)
- Twelve Anathemas not mentioned (Antiochene goal)
- Formula drafted by Antiochenes
- Chalcedon cited Formula as basis for dyophysite Definition
- Explains why Antiochene party accepted

**Weaknesses:**
- Cyril's own interpretation contradicts this
- Cyril's later writings show no theological shift
- Formula's "union OF two natures" ≠ Chalcedon's "IN two natures"
- Requires assuming Cyril was dishonest or self-deceived
- Ignores Cyril's primary goal achieved (Nestorius condemned)

### 9C. Steelman: The Formula Was Genuine Theological Agreement

**Argument:**
The Formula represents genuine theological agreement on basic Christological confession. Both Cyril and John accepted:
- Perfect God and perfect man
- Union of two natures
- Theotokos
- Double consubstantiality
- One Christ, one Son, one Lord

Both interpreted this as orthodox. Both celebrated reconciliation. The Formula established minimal boundaries that both could accept. Different interpretations of details do not negate genuine agreement on fundamentals. The Formula succeeded in achieving ecclesiastical unity and healing schism.

**Strengths:**
- Both sides accepted Formula
- Both celebrated reconciliation
- Formula does establish minimal orthodox boundaries
- Explains why both sides accepted
- Achieved actual reconciliation (temporary)

**Weaknesses:**
- Different interpretations undermine "genuine agreement"
- Truce broke down within 5 years
- Underlying disagreements remained
- Both sides continued controversy
- Formula too ambiguous to constitute real theological consensus

## X. THE FORMULA AND THEOTOKOS

### 10A. The Formula's Theotokos Affirmation

**Text:**
> "In accordance with this understanding of the unconfused union, we confess the holy Virgin to be the Mother of God (Theotokon), because God the Word was incarnate and became man, and from the very conception he united the temple taken from her with himself."

**Key Elements:**
- Theotokos affirmed
- Qualified by "in accordance with this understanding of unconfused union"
- Basis: God the Word incarnate and became man
- From conception, united temple (human nature) taken from Mary

### 10B. Theotokos Interpretations

**Cyril's Interpretation:**
- Theotokos because divine Logos is sole subject
- Mary bore God the Word incarnate
- Theotokos follows from hypostatic union
- No qualification needed (qualification is just clarification)

**Antiochene Interpretation:**
- Theotokos because of union (not identity)
- Mary bore human Jesus united to divine Logos
- Theotokos qualified by "unconfused union"
- Qualification protects against confusion of natures

**Assessment:**
Both interpretations possible from text. Theotokos affirmed but with different theological bases.

### 10C. Theotokos and Later Mariology

**Formula Does NOT Establish:**
- Immaculate Conception
- Assumption
- Perpetual Virginity
- Mediatrix/Co-Redemptrix
- Any post-patristic Marian doctrines

**Formula DOES Establish:**
- Theotokos as Christological boundary
- Mary's unique role in incarnation
- Theotokos follows from Christology, not independent Mariology

**Assessment:**
Formula's Theotokos is Christological, not Mariological. Later Marian doctrines cannot be derived from Formula alone.

## XI. CHRISTOLOGICAL TERMINOLOGY ANALYSIS

### 11A. Key Terms in Formula

**ἕνωσις (henōsis) - Union:**
- Used once: "union of two natures"
- Does not specify type of union
- Could mean hypostatic union (Cyril) or prosopic union (Nestorius)
- Ambiguous

**φύσις (physis) - Nature:**
- Used twice: "two natures" and "duality of natures"
- Does not specify if natures persist after union
- Could mean "from two natures" (Miaphysite) or "in two natures" (Chalcedonian)
- Ambiguous

**πρόσωπον (prosōpon) - Person:**
- Used once: "unity of person"
- Does not specify if this is hypostasis (Cyril) or prosopon of union (Nestorius)
- Ambiguous

**ὁμοούσιος (homoousios) - Consubstantial:**
- Used twice: consubstantial with Father (divinity) and consubstantial with us (humanity)
- Clear Nicene language
- Not ambiguous
- Affirms full divinity and full humanity

**Θεοτόκος (Theotokos) - God-bearer:**
- Used once
- Affirmed with qualification
- Not ambiguous in affirmation
- Ambiguous in theological basis

### 11B. Comparison with Cyril's Terminology

**Cyril's Standard Terms:**
- ἕνωσις καθ' ὑπόστασιν (henōsis kath' hypostasin) - hypostatic union
- μία φύσις τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη (mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē) - one incarnate nature of God the Word
- ὑπόστασις (hypostasis) - concrete existence / person

**Formula's Terms:**
- ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων (henōsis dyo physeōn) - union of two natures
- Does not use "hypostatic union"
- Does not use "one incarnate nature"
- Uses "person" (prosōpon) not "hypostasis"

**Assessment:**
Formula uses different terminology than Cyril's standard vocabulary. More neutral/diplomatic language.

### 11C. Comparison with Nestorius's Terminology

**Nestorius's Standard Terms:**
- συνάφεια (synapheia) - conjunction
- πρόσωπον ἑνώσεως (prosōpon henōseōs) - prosopon of union
- δύο φύσεις (dyo physeis) - two natures

**Formula's Terms:**
- ἕνωσις (henōsis) - union (not synapheia)
- πρόσωπον (prosōpon) - person (but not "prosopon of union")
- δύο φύσεις (dyo physeis) - two natures (same as Nestorius)

**Assessment:**
Formula avoids Nestorius's controversial "synapheia" and "prosopon of union" terminology. Uses "union" (henōsis) which is more acceptable to Cyril.

### 11D. Comparison with Chalcedon's Terminology

**Chalcedon's Terms:**
- ἐν δύο φύσεσιν (en dyo physesin) - IN two natures
- ὑπόστασις (hypostasis) - person / concrete existence
- ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως - without confusion, without change, without division, without separation

**Formula's Terms:**
- ἕνωσις δύο φύσεων (henōsis dyo physeōn) - union OF two natures
- πρόσωπον (prosōpon) - person
- Does not use four negative qualifiers

**Assessment:**
Chalcedon uses more precise, technical language. Formula is more ambiguous. Chalcedon's "IN two natures" is stronger dyophysite statement than Formula's "union OF two natures."

## XII. POLITICAL AND DIPLOMATIC CONTEXT

### 12A. Imperial Pressure

**Theodosius II's Role:**
- Convoked Council of Ephesus (431)
- Initially ordered both Nestorius and Cyril deposed
- Eventually confirmed Nestorius's deposition
- Pressured both sides toward reconciliation (431-433)
- Imperial interest in ecclesiastical unity

**Imperial Motivation:**
- Political stability requires ecclesiastical unity
- Schism weakens imperial authority
- Doctrinal controversy disrupts social order
- Emperor as guardian of orthodoxy and unity

### 12B. Ecclesiastical Politics

**Cyril's Political Calculations:**
- Achieved primary goal (Nestorius condemned)
- Alexandria's prestige enhanced
- Further controversy risks imperial disfavor
- Reconciliation consolidates gains
- Strategic concession for long-term stability

**John of Antioch's Political Calculations:**
- Nestorius already deposed - no point continuing fight
- Antioch's prestige requires reconciliation
- Imperial pressure cannot be resisted indefinitely
- Formula protects Antiochene concerns
- Strategic acceptance for long-term influence

**Mediators' Roles:**
- Paul of Emesa: trusted by both sides
- Acacius of Beroea: respected elder bishop
- Imperial officials: pressure and facilitation

### 12C. Regional Dynamics

**Alexandria:**
- Traditional rivalry with Constantinople
- Cyril's strong position after Ephesus
- Egyptian monastic support for Cyril
- Theological tradition (Athanasian legacy)

**Antioch:**
- Traditional theological school (Diodore, Theodore)
- Suspicion of Alexandrian "one nature" language
- Desire to preserve two-nature Christology
- Regional pride and independence

**Constantinople:**
- Nestorius deposed and exiled
- Proculus installed as bishop
- Imperial seat, political center
- Desire for stability and unity

**Rome:**
- Celestine supported Cyril against Nestorius
- Papal authority enhanced by Ephesus
- Generally supportive of reconciliation
- Less directly involved in post-Ephesus negotiations

## XIII. THE FORMULA'S LIMITATIONS

### 13A. What the Formula Did NOT Resolve

**Theological Ambiguities:**
- Nature of union (hypostatic vs. prosopic)
- Persistence of two natures after union
- Status of Twelve Anathemas
- Communicatio idiomatum (communication of properties)
- Sole subjectivity of Logos

**Ecclesiastical Issues:**
- Underlying theological disagreements
- Regional rivalries
- Interpretation of Ephesus (431)
- Authority of different documents

**Long-term Stability:**
- Truce broke down by 438
- Cyril vs. Theodoret controversy resumed
- Eutychian crisis (448-449)
- Chalcedon (451) needed further clarification

### 13B. Why the Formula Failed

**Fundamental Ambiguity:**
- Formula too vague to constitute real agreement
- Both sides interpreted it differently
- No mechanism to resolve interpretive disputes
- Underlying disagreements remained

**Lack of Enforcement:**
- No conciliar authority behind Formula
- No imperial enforcement mechanism
- Depended on good faith of both parties
- Good faith eroded quickly

**Theological Insufficiency:**
- Formula addressed surface issues, not deep disagreements
- Did not resolve hypostatic union vs. prosopic union
- Did not clarify "two natures" meaning
- Did not address Twelve Anathemas

**Personal Factors:**
- Cyril and Theodoret continued personal rivalry
- Mutual suspicion remained
- Regional loyalties stronger than theological agreement
- No personal reconciliation, only institutional

## XIV. HISTORICAL ASSESSMENT

### 14A. What the Formula WAS

**Accurate Descriptions:**
- Diplomatic reconciliation document
- Minimal theological confession
- Strategic concession by both sides
- Temporary truce (433-438)
- Basis for later Chalcedonian Definition (one of several bases)
- Example of theological negotiation
- Document of ecclesiastical politics

**Inaccurate Descriptions:**
- Proof of Cyril's theological capitulation
- Proof of Antiochene victory
- Rejection of Twelve Anathemas
- Establishment of Chalcedonian dyophysitism
- Permanent theological resolution
- Definitive Christological statement

### 14B. What the Formula WAS NOT

**Common Mischaracterizations:**

**"Cyril abandoned his Christology":**
- FALSE. Cyril's later writings show no theological shift.

**"The Twelve Anathemas were rejected":**
- UNSUPPORTED. Formula does not mention them. Status remained ambiguous.

**"The Formula established Chalcedonian orthodoxy":**
- EXAGGERATED. Formula was one basis for Chalcedon, but Chalcedon went beyond Formula.

**"The Formula resolved the Nestorian controversy":**
- FALSE. Only temporary truce. Controversy resurfaced.

**"The Formula proves two natures Christology":**
- MISLEADING. Formula uses "union OF two natures" not "IN two natures." Ambiguous.

### 14C. Formula's Historical Significance

**Genuine Achievements:**
- Achieved temporary ecclesiastical unity
- Established minimal orthodox boundaries
- Demonstrated possibility of reconciliation
- Provided basis for later theological development
- Showed diplomatic skill of both parties

**Genuine Limitations:**
- Only temporary (broke down by 438)
- Did not resolve underlying disagreements
- Too ambiguous to constitute real theological consensus
- Required further clarification (Chalcedon 451)
- Ultimately failed to prevent Eutychian crisis

**Historical Legacy:**
- Read and approved at Chalcedon (451)
- Cited as orthodox by both Chalcedonians and Miaphysites
- Example of theological negotiation and compromise
- Demonstrates complexity of Christological development
- Shows difference between diplomatic reconciliation and theological resolution

- Cite Formula as authority

- Varying interpretations

- Chalcedon dependent on Formula

## XVI. EPISTEMIC BOUNDARIES

### 16A. What Can Be Historically Established

**Established Facts:**
- Formula drafted by Antiochenes, accepted by Cyril (433)
- Formula uses "union of two natures" language
- Formula affirms Theotokos, double consubstantiality
- Twelve Anathemas not mentioned in Formula
- Cyril's later writings show no theological shift
- Formula read and approved at Chalcedon (451)
- Formula broke down as reconciliation by 438

**Established Interpretations:**
- Cyril interpreted Formula in Miaphysite way
- Antiochenes interpreted Formula in dyophysite way
- Chalcedon interpreted Formula in Chalcedonian way
- Formula is genuinely ambiguous on key points

### 16B. What Cannot Be Historically Established

**Theological Judgments:**
- Whether Cyril's interpretation is correct
- Whether Antiochene interpretation is correct
- Whether Chalcedonian interpretation is correct
- Whether Formula is orthodox or heretical
- Whether Twelve Anathemas should have been affirmed or rejected

**Counterfactuals:**
- What would have happened if Cyril rejected Formula
- What would have happened if Formula explicitly addressed Anathemas
- Whether Formula could have been more specific
- Whether reconciliation could have been permanent

**Ultimate Questions:**
- What is the correct Christology
- Whether Chalcedon or Miaphysitism is true
- Whether Cyril or Nestorius was right
- Whether Formula succeeded or failed

### 16C. What Requires Theological Judgment

**Interpretive Questions:**
- How to interpret "union of two natures"
- Whether "two natures" persist after union
- Whether Formula is compatible with Cyril's "one incarnate nature"
- Whether Formula supports Chalcedonian or Miaphysite Christology
- Whether Twelve Anathemas are orthodox

**Normative Questions:**
- Whether Formula should be authoritative
- Whether Chalcedon correctly interpreted Formula
- Whether Constantinople II (553) correctly affirmed Anathemas
- Whether Formula represents authentic theological development

## XVII. CONCLUSION: HISTORICAL STATUS

### 17A. The Formula in Historical Context

**Accurate Historical Summary:**

The Formula of Reunion (433) was a diplomatic reconciliation document that achieved temporary ecclesiastical unity between Cyril of Alexandria and John of Antioch following the fractured Council of Ephesus (431). The Formula:

- Was drafted by Antiochenes (likely Theodoret) and accepted by Cyril
- Confessed perfect God and perfect man, union of two natures, Theotokos, double consubstantiality
- Did NOT mention or resolve status of Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Did NOT use Chalcedonian "in two natures" language
- Did NOT resolve underlying theological disagreements
- Was interpreted differently by Cyril (Miaphysite reading) and Antiochenes (dyophysite reading)
- Broke down as reconciliation by 438
- Was later read and approved at Chalcedon (451) alongside Cyril's Second Letter
- Was NOT sufficient to prevent Eutychian crisis (448-449) or Chalcedonian controversy

### 17B. What the Formula Is NOT Evidence For

**The Formula does NOT prove:**
- Cyril abandoned his Christology or Twelve Anathemas
- The Antiochene party defeated Cyril
- The Twelve Anathemas were rejected
- Chalcedonian dyophysitism is correct
- Miaphysite Christology is correct
- The Nestorian controversy was permanently resolved
- Formula is identical to Chalcedonian Definition

### 17C. What the Formula IS Evidence For

**The Formula does prove:**
- Cyril and John of Antioch achieved temporary reconciliation
- Both sides made strategic concessions
- Formula established minimal orthodox boundaries
- "Two natures" language was acceptable to Cyril (with interpretation)
- Theotokos and double consubstantiality were agreed upon
- Formula was considered orthodox by Chalcedon (451)
- Formula was genuinely ambiguous on key theological points
- Diplomatic reconciliation ≠ theological resolution
- Christological development involved negotiation and compromise

### 17D. Final Assessment

**The Formula of Reunion (433) was:**
- A diplomatic achievement (temporary reconciliation)
- A theological compromise (minimal confession)
- A strategic document (both sides gained something)
- An ambiguous text (subject to multiple interpretations)
- A historical artifact (evidence of 5th-century Christological negotiation)
- A basis for later development (one of several bases for Chalcedon)

**The Formula of Reunion (433) was NOT:**
- Proof of Cyril's theological capitulation
- Proof of Antiochene victory
- Rejection of the Twelve Anathemas
- Establishment of Chalcedonian orthodoxy
- Permanent theological resolution
- Definitive Christological statement
