## C. SIRAH VS MAGHAZI & RELATED GENRES

- **Sirah**: Biography of the Prophet (life, genealogy, prophetic mission).
- **Maghazi**: Military expeditions and campaigns of the Prophet.
- **Akhbar**: Historical reports and anecdotes.
- **Tabaqat**: Biographical dictionaries categorized by generations.
- **Tarikh**: Universal annalistic history.
- **Hadith**: Prophetic sayings and actions transmitted via isnads.
- **Tafsir**: Qur'anic exegesis.

---

## D. EARLY SIRAH TRADITION & MAJOR HISTORIANS

1. **'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr (d. c. 94/712)**: Earliest known systematic collector of historical reports, transmitting via letters to Caliph Abd al-Malik.
2. **Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri (d. 124/742)**: Central pivot of early Umayyad historical transmission; gathered reports from Urwa, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, and others.
3. **Musa ibn 'Uqba (d. 141/758)**: Author of an early *Maghazi* work preserved partially in later quotations.
4. **Ma'mar ibn Rashid (d. 153/770)**: Author of *Kitab al-Maghazi* (part of *Jami'*), transmitting Zuhri and Qatada traditions.
5. **Muhammad ibn Ishaq (d. 150/767)**: Author of the monumental *Kitab al-Siyar wa-l-Maghazi*, preserved through recensions.
6. **Muhammad ibn 'Umar al-Waqidi (d. 207/823)**: Author of *Kitab al-Maghazi*, renowned for chronological and topographical detail.
7. **Ibn Sa'd (d. 230/845)**: Author of *Kitab al-Tabaqat al-Kabir*, utilizing Waqidi and Ibn Ishaq.
8. **Al-Tabari (d. 310/923)**: Author of *Tarikh al-Rusul wa-l-Muluk*, preserving competing early transmission lines.

---

## E. 'URWA CORPUS & IBN ISHAQ / IBN HISHAM AUDITS

- **'Urwa Corpus**: Evaluated through his epistles to Abd al-Malik. Represents the earliest strata of historical reports, though later transmitters (e.g., Zuhri) shaped the packaging.
- **Ibn Ishaq's Recension**: Ibn Ishaq's original complete autograph does not survive. It is reconstructed through fragments in Tabari, Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and primarily Ibn Hisham's recension.
- **Ibn Hisham's Editorial Role**: Abu Muhammad Abd al-Malik ibn Hisham (d. 218/833) edited Ibn Ishaq's text, explicitly omitting offensive poetry, controversial genealogical polemics, and unverified reports (*ma yashnuhu*), while adding philological and genealogical glosses. Thus, **Ibn Hisham ≠ Ibn Ishaq autograph**.

## G. CASE STUDIES (15 MANDATORY AUDITS)

1. **First Revelation (Cave Hira)**: Source: Ibn Ishaq/Hisham, Bukhari. Transmission: Zuhri ← Urwa. Historical core: Prophetic visionary experience; narrative framing reflects later hagiography.
2. **Early Meccan Preaching**: Source: Tabari, Ibn Sa'd. Transmission: Medinan and Iraqi chains. Historical core: Gradual emergence of monotheistic group facing Quraysh opposition.
3. **Abyssinian Migration**: Source: Ibn Ishaq. Transmission: Umm Salama reports via early isnads. Historical core: Early refugee movement to Christian Axum reflecting persecution pressure.
4. **Pledges of Aqaba**: Source: Ibn Ishaq, Waqidi. Transmission: Ansar isnads. Historical core: Secret negotiations establishing Medinan political base.
5. **Hijrah**: Source: Comprehensive isnad network. Historical core: Coordinated migration to Yathrib c. 622 CE.
6. **Battle of Badr**: Source: Qur'an (Q. 3) + Maghazi. Historical core: Decisive early military victory over Quraysh caravan escort.
7. **Battle of Uhud**: Source: Ibn Ishaq, Waqidi. Historical core: Tactical defeat for Muslims; strategic survival.
8. **Khandaq and Banu Qurayza**: Source: Ibn Ishaq. Historical core: Siege of Medina; harsh political punishment of Banu Qurayza for treasonous collaboration during siege.
9. **Treaty of Hudaybiyyah**: Source: Surah 48 + Sirah. Historical core: Diplomatic truce granting political recognition to early Muslim community.
10. **Khaybar**: Source: Maghazi literature. Historical core: Subjugation of northern Jewish oasis for agricultural and strategic security.
11. **Conquest of Mecca**: Source: Ibn Ishaq, Waqidi. Historical core: Bloodless or low-casualty entry into Mecca c. 630 CE.
12. **Christian Encounters (Najran)**: Source: Ibn Ishaq. Historical core: Diplomatic delegations and treaty arrangements with southern Christian communities.
13. **Constitution of Medina**: Source: Ibn Ishaq / Abu Ubayd. Historical core: Foundational pact regulating tribal and confederate relations in Yathrib.
14. **Final Illness and Death**: Source: Aisha reports in Bukhari/Sirah. Historical core: Death of Muhammad in Medina c. 632 CE.
15. **Saqifa and Succession**: Source: Tabari, Ibn Ishaq. Historical core: Rapid political succession council securing Abu Bakr's leadership amidst tribal tensions.
