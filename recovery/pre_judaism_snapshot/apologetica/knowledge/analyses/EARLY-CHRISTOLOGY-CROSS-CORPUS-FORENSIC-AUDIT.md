## 0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This audit examines the Christological trajectory from the historical Jesus through the earliest Jesus movement, Pauline letters, Synoptic Gospels, Johannine Gospel, patristic reception, and Nicene-Chalcedonian formulation. The central question:

> "Did high Christology originate with Paul, develop within early Christian communities, or have roots in the Jesus movement before written Gospels?"

**FINDING:** High Christology emerges as a multi-layered phenomenon. Evidence supports that Paul *inherited* pre-existing Christological traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5, Phil 2:6–11) while also *developing* original formulations. The Synoptics present a different but not contradictory Christological profile. John represents the most explicit high Christology but is not the *origin* of high Christology. Nicene formulation is a *later terminological and conceptual development* of earlier Christological trajectories, not an invention from nothing nor a simple repetition of first-century language.

## 2. CRITICAL DISTINCTIONS — EIGHT LAYERS

These eight layers MUST be kept separate. No layer may substitute for another without explicit documentation.

### Layer A: Historical Jesus
- What the historical figure Jesus of Nazareth actually said, did, and claimed.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Extremely limited. Reconstructed from later sources (Paul, Gospels, Josephus, Tacitus). No direct testimony survives.
- **Can establish:** Jesus existed, was crucified under Pilate, had followers.
- **Cannot establish:** Exact self-understanding, precise Christological claims, inner theology.

### Layer B: Earliest Jesus Movement
- The Jerusalem community immediately after Jesus's death (c. 30–35 CE).
- **Epistemic boundary:** Reconstructed from Acts (later source), Paul's Galatians (c. 48–55), and inferred traditions.
- **Can establish:** Community existed, practiced devotion, had leadership (James, Peter, John).
- **Cannot establish:** Full Christological formulation, written confessions.

### Layer C: Pauline Tradition
- Paul's letters (undisputed: 1 Thess, Gal, 1–2 Cor, Rom, Phil, Phlm; disputed: Eph, Col, 2 Thess, Pastorals).
- **Epistemic boundary:** Paul writes c. 48–62 CE. Letters are occasional, not systematic theology.
- **Can establish:** Paul's Christology at the time of writing, traditions he claims to have received.
- **Cannot establish:** Paul's entire theology, Jesus's own self-understanding, later developments.

### Layer D: Synoptic Tradition (Mark, Matthew, Luke)
- Three Gospels sharing material, conventionally dated c. 65–90 CE.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Literary products of communities, not transcripts of events.
- **Can establish:** Christological language and narrative at time of composition.
- **Cannot establish:** Historical Jesus's exact words (ipsissima verba), pre-Gospel oral traditions with certainty.

### Layer E: Johannine Tradition
- Gospel of John (c. 90–110 CE), possibly Johannine epistles.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Most theologically developed Gospel narrative; relationship to Synoptics debated.
- **Can establish:** Johannine Christological language at time of composition.
- **Cannot establish:** Historical Jesus's exact words, independence from Synoptic traditions.

### Layer F: Patristic Interpretation
- Irenaeus (c. 180), Tertullian, Origen, etc.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Interpreting earlier texts through developing theological frameworks.
- **Can establish:** 2nd-century reception and interpretation.
- **Cannot establish:** First-century intent, autograph content.

### Layer G: Nicene Formulation
- Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Conciliar, technical, responding to Arian controversy.
- **Can establish:** 4th-century doctrinal consensus.
- **Cannot establish:** That this was the original meaning of 1st-century texts.

### Layer H: Chalcedonian Formulation
- Chalcedon 451 and subsequent reception.
- **Epistemic boundary:** Technical Christological terminology (physis, hypostasis, prosopon).
- **Can establish:** 5th-century doctrinal formulation.
- **Cannot establish:** That 1st-century authors used these categories.

**FUNDAMENTAL RULE:** Never use Layer G to prove Layer C. Never use Layer E to prove Layer A. Never use Layer F to prove Layer B. Each layer has its own epistemic boundary.

## 3. DEFINITION: "HIGH CHRISTOLOGY"

### 3.1 Operational Definition

For this audit, "HIGH CHRISTOLOGY" is operationally defined by the presence of one or more of the following indicators:

| # | Indicator | Description |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Pre-existence | Christ existed before creation / before birth |
| 2 | Divine agency | Christ functions as God's unique agent in creation/salvation |
| 3 | Cosmic creation | Christ participated in creation of the cosmos |
| 4 | Divine titles | Titles applied that in Jewish context belong to God alone (Kyrios applied to YHWH texts) |
| 5 | Divine functions | Functions reserved for God: judgment, forgiveness, creation, sustaining universe |
| 6 | Worship/devotion | Christ receives worship, prayer, hymnic praise |
| 7 | Invocation | Christ invoked in prayer alongside God |
| 8 | Exaltation | Christ exalted to divine throne / right hand of God |
| 9 | Participation in divine identity | Included within the unique identity of YHWH (Bauckham framework) |
| 10 | YHWH-text application | OT YHWH passages applied directly to Jesus |
| 11 | Forgiveness authority | Authority to forgive sins (divine prerogative in Jewish context) |
| 12 | Eschatological authority | Authority over final judgment and eschatological events |
| 13 | Son of God (unique) | Sonship beyond mere messianic / adoptive sense |
| 14 | Lordship (Kyrios) | Kyrios applied in contexts echoing divine sovereignty |

### 3.2 Critical Boundary

**HIGH CHRISTOLOGY ≠ NICENE TRINITY**

High Christology indicates Christ is placed in a unique relationship with God, possibly within the divine identity. It does NOT automatically mean:
- Nicene homoousios (same substance as Father)
- Cappadocian treis hypostaseis (three persons)
- Chalcedonian duo physeis (two natures)

These are *later technical formulations* that may develop from high Christology trajectories but are not identical to them.

**HIGH CHRISTOLOGY ≠ AUTOMATIC DIVINE ONTOLOGY**

The presence of high Christological language does not automatically resolve whether the author intended metaphysical identity with God or functional/exalted agency. This requires passage-by-passage analysis.

## 4. PAULINE CHRISTOLOGY (INTEGRATING STEP 90)

### 4.1 Corpus Control

**Undisputed Pauline letters (7):** Romans, 1 Corinthians, 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon.

**P46 witness (SRC-MS-005):** Contains Romans, Hebrews, 1–2 Corinthians, Ephesians, Galatians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 Thessalonians. Manuscript date c. 175–225 CE. This is manuscript evidence for *transmission* of Pauline texts, not Paul's autographs.

**Disputed/deutero-Pauline:** Ephesians, Colossians, 2 Thessalonians, 1–2 Timothy, Titus. These are NOT used as primary evidence for Paul's own Christology unless explicitly flagged.

### 4.2 Key Passages

#### 4.2.1 — 1 Corinthians 8:6

**Text:** ἀλλ' ἡμῖν εἷς θεὸς ὁ πατήρ, ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἡμεῖς εἰς αὐτόν, καὶ εἷς κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστός, δι' οὗ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἡμεῖς δι' αὐτοῦ.

**Translation:** "yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and we for him, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and we through him."

**Christological implication:**
- Shema (Deut 6:4) reframed: "one God" = Father, "one Lord" = Jesus Christ.
- Prepositional distinction: Father = source (ἐξ οὗ), Christ = mediator (δι' οὗ).
- Christ placed in *cosmic creation* role (δι' οὗ τὰ πάντα).
- Yet distinguished from the Father.

**Jewish context:** The Shema is the foundational monotheistic confession. Paul's reframing includes Christ within the divine creative agency while maintaining the "one God" = Father distinction.

**Confidence:** HIGH (undisputed letter, clear text).
**Limitations:** Does not use homoousios language; does not resolve ontological relationship; "one Lord" could be functional or ontological.

#### 4.2.2 — Philippians 2:6–11

**Text:** ὃς ἐν μορφῇ θεοῦ ὑπάρχων οὐχ ἁρπαγμὸν ἡγήσατο τὸ εἶναι ἴσα θεῷ...

**Christological implication:**
- Pre-existence: Christ existed "in the form of God" (ἐν μορφῇ θεοῦ).
- Pre-incarnate status: "equal with God" (ἴσα θεῷ) — whether ἁρπαγμὸν means "robbery" or "something to be exploited" is debated.
- Kenosis (self-emptying): voluntary humiliation.
- Exaltation: God "highly exalted him" and gave him "the name above every name."
- Universal worship: "every knee should bow... every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord (Κύριος)."
- Isaiah 45:23 (YHWH text) applied to Jesus.

**Classification:** Pre-Pauline hymn or tradition (PROBABLE). Paul incorporates it.
**Confidence:** HIGH for text; DISPUTED for exact interpretation of μορφῇ θεοῦ and ἁρπαγμὸν.
**Limitations:** "Form of God" does not = homoousios. Exaltation language could be adoptionist or confirmatory. The hymn's pre-Pauline origin is probable but not certain.

#### 4.2.3 — 1 Corinthians 15:3–5

**Text:** παρέδωκα γὰρ ὑμῖν ἐν πρώτοις, ὃ καὶ παρέλαβον, ὅτι Χριστὸς ἀπέθανεν ὑπὲρ τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ἡμῶν κατὰ τὰς γραφάς, καὶ ὅτι ἐτάφη, καὶ ὅτι ἐγήγερται τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ κατὰ τὰς γραφάς, καὶ ὅτι ὤφθη Κηφᾷ εἶτα τοῖς δώδεκα.

**Christological implication:**
- Paul explicitly states he *received* (παρέλαβον) and *delivered* (παρέδωκα) this tradition.
- Technical tradition-transmission language (paradidōmi/paralambanō).
- Core claims: Christ died for sins, was buried, was raised, appeared to witnesses.
- "According to the scriptures" — tied to Jewish scripture.

**Classification:** PRE-PAULINE TRADITION (STRONG EVIDENCE). Paul did not invent this formula.
**Confidence:** HIGH that this is received tradition; DISPUTED on exact date and origin (Jerusalem? Damascus? Antioch?).
**Limitations:** "Received" does not specify from whom or when. Could be from Damascus road experience, Jerusalem apostles, or Antioch community.

#### 4.2.4 — Romans 10:9–13

**Text:** ἐὰν ὁμολογήσῃς ἐν τῷ στόματί σου Κύριον Ἰησοῦν... πᾶς γὰρ ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου σωθήσεται.

**Christological implication:**
- Confession "Jesus is Lord" (Κύριον Ἰησοῦν).
- Joel 2:32 (LXX) quotation: "everyone who calls on the name of the LORD (YHWH) will be saved" — applied to Jesus.
- YHWH-text application to Jesus.

**Confidence:** HIGH for text; HIGH that YHWH text is applied to Jesus.
**Limitations:** Does not resolve whether "Lord" here means YHWH-identity or exalted-messianic Lord.

#### 4.2.5 — Galatians 1–2

**Christological implication:**
- Paul's independence claim: gospel received "through a revelation of Jesus Christ" (Gal 1:12).
- Jerusalem contacts: Cephas (15 days), James (Gal 1:18–19), later "pillars" (Gal 2:9).
- Paul's gospel agrees with Jerusalem apostles on core content.

**Confidence:** HIGH that Paul claims divine revelation; HIGH that Paul had Jerusalem contacts.
**Limitations:** Paul's claim of independence does not mean he *invented* Christology. He acknowledges received traditions (1 Cor 15:3).

#### 4.2.6 — 2 Corinthians 4:4

**Text:** ὁ θεὸς τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου... πρὸς τὸ μὴ αὐγάσαι τὸν φωτισμὸν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου τῆς δόξης τοῦ Χριστοῦ, ὅς ἐστιν εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ.

**Christological implication:**
- Christ is "the image of God" (εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ).
- Wisdom Christology resonance (Wisdom as image of God in Jewish tradition).

**Confidence:** HIGH for text.
**Limitations:** "Image of God" has multiple possible meanings (representation, reflection, identity).

#### 4.2.7 — Romans 9:5

**Text:** ἐξ ὧν ὁ Χριστὸς τὸ κατὰ σάρκα, ὁ ὢν ἐπὶ πάντων θεὸς εὐλογητὸς εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας, ἀμήν.

**Christological implication:**
- Syntactically disputed: does ὁ ὢν ἐπὶ πάντων θεὸς refer to Christ or to God the Father?
- If referring to Christ: explicit ascription of θεός to Jesus.
- If doxology to God: separate from Christ clause.

**Classification:** DISPUTED (punctuation/syntax). Both readings are grammatically possible.
**Confidence:** TEXT is certain; INTERPRETATION is DISPUTED.

### 4.3 Paul: Inherited or Invented?

Based on STEP 90 integration and passage analysis:

**A. Paul INVENTED high Christology?** — UNSAFE.
Evidence: 1 Cor 15:3–5 uses explicit received-tradition language. Phil 2:6–11 has formal features of a pre-Pauline hymn. Paul acknowledges Jerusalem apostles and shared gospel.

**B. Paul INHERITED high Christology?** — SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION.
Evidence: Received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5), possible hymns (Phil 2:6–11), shared confessions ("Jesus is Lord"). Qualification: "high Christology" at this stage ≠ Nicene formulation.

**C. Paul DEVELOPED existing traditions?** — SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION.
Evidence: Paul's cosmic Christology (1 Cor 8:6 reframing of Shema), YHWH-text application (Rom 10:13), Wisdom Christology (2 Cor 4:4) go beyond simple received formulas.

**D. Paul COMBINED inherited and original formulations?** — SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION.
This is the most defensible position: Paul inherited core traditions (death/resurrection, Lordship) and developed them with original theological applications (cosmic mediator, Shema reframing, YHWH-text application).

## 5. MARK CHRISTOLOGY

### 5.2 Mark Christological Profile

**Composition date:** c. 65–75 CE (PROBABLE, mainstream consensus).
**Priority:** Markan priority assumed (mainstream position).

#### Key Passages:

**Mark 1:1** — Ἀρχὴ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ [υἱοῦ θεοῦ].
- "Beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ [Son of God]."
- "Son of God" in opening verse (textually disputed — bracketed in critical editions).
- If original: programmatic Christological claim.

**Mark 1:11** — Baptism voice: σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός, ἐν σοὶ εὐδόκησα.
- Psalm 2:7 + Isaiah 42:1 resonance.
- Divine sonship + servant identity.

**Mark 2:5–12** — Forgiveness of sins.
- Jesus forgives sins (ἐξουσίαν... ἀφιέναι ἁμαρτίας).
- Scribes object: "Who can forgive sins but God alone?" (τίς δύναται ἁμαρτίας ἀφιέναι εἰ μὴ εἷς ὁ θεός;)
- Jesus claims divine prerogative.

**Mark 8:29** — Peter's confession: σὺ εἶ ὁ Χριστός.
- Messiahship as central identity claim.

**Mark 10:45** — Ransom saying: ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου... δοῦναι τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ λύτρον ἀντὶ πολλῶν.
- Son of Man as suffering servant.
- Substitutionary death.

**Mark 12:35–37** — David's Lord.
- Jesus cites Psalm 110:1: "The Lord said to my Lord..."
- Messiah is more than David's son — he is David's Lord.

**Mark 14:61–62** — Trial confession.
- "Are you the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?"
- Jesus: ἐγώ εἰμι... ὄψεσθε τὸν υἱὸν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐκ δεξιῶν καθήμενον τῆς δυνάμεως καὶ ἐρχόμενον μετὰ τῶν νεφελῶν τοῦ οὐρανοῦ.
- Daniel 7:13 + Psalm 110:1 combined.
- Son of Man seated at right hand of Power, coming with clouds.

**Mark 15:37–39** — Centurion's confession.
- "Truly this man was the Son of God" (υἱὸς θεοῦ).
- Climactic Christological recognition at the cross.

### 5.3 Mark Christological Assessment

| Indicator | Status | Evidence |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Messiah | PRESENT | Mark 8:29, 14:61 |
| Son of God | PRESENT | Mark 1:1(?), 1:11, 15:39 |
| Son of Man | PRESENT (dominant self-designation) | Mark 2:10, 2:28, 8:31, 10:45, 14:62 |
| Lord (Kyrios) | LIMITED | Mark 12:36–37 (Psalm 110) |
| Forgiveness authority | PRESENT | Mark 2:5–12 |
| Divine agency | IMPLICIT | Exorcism authority, nature miracles |
| Pre-existence | NOT ESTABLISHED | No explicit pre-existence language in Mark |
| Cosmic creation | NOT ESTABLISHED | No creation language |
| Worship | NOT ESTABLISHED | No explicit worship of Jesus in Mark |
| YHWH-text application | LIMITED | Mark 1:2–3 (Isa 40:3 + Mal 3:1 applied to Jesus's way-preparer) |
| Resurrection | PRESENT | Mark 16 (original ending debated) |
| Exaltation | PRESENT | Mark 14:62 (Dan 7:13 + Ps 110:1) |

**CLASSIFICATION:** Mark contains MODERATE Christology. Jesus is presented as Messiah, Son of God, Son of Man with divine authority (forgiveness, eschatological judgment). However, Mark does NOT contain explicit pre-existence, cosmic creation, or Logos language. Mark's Christology is HIGH in authority and identity terms but LOWER in explicit divine ontology compared to John or Paul.

**CRITICAL:** This is NOT binary (high/low). Mark's Christology has its own profile: narrative authority, messianic secrecy, suffering Son of Man, climactic cross-confession.

## 6. MATTHEW CHRISTOLOGY

### 6.1 Store Limitation

Same as Mark: no dedicated Synoptic manuscript sources in store. LIMITER BY STORED EVIDENCE applies.

### 6.2 Matthew Christological Profile

**Composition date:** c. 80–90 CE (PROBABLE).
**Relationship to Mark:** Uses Mark as source (Markan priority assumed).

#### Key Passages:

**Matthew 1:23** — Emmanuel: ἰδοὺ ἡ παρθένος ἐν γαστρὶ ἕξει καὶ τέξεται υἱόν, καὶ καλέσουσιν τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Ἐμμανουήλ, ὅ ἐστιν μεθερμηνευόμενον Μεθ' ἡμῶν ὁ θεός.
- Isaiah 7:14 quotation.
- "God with us" — Jesus embodies divine presence.
- Programmatic: bookends with Matt 28:20 ("I am with you always").

**Matthew 3:17** — Baptism voice (parallels Mark 1:11).

**Matthew 11:27** — Mutual knowledge: πάντα μοι παρεδόθη ὑπὸ τοῦ πατρός μου, καὶ οὐδεὶς ἐπιγινώσκει τὸν υἱὸν εἰ μὴ ὁ πατήρ, οὐδὲ τὸν πατέρα τις ἐπιγινώσκει εἰ μὴ ὁ υἱὸς καὶ ᾧ ἐὰν βούληται ὁ υἱὸς ἀποκαλύψαι.
- Unique Father-Son mutual knowledge.
- Johannine resonance (cf. John 10:15, 17:25).
- Exclusive revelation through Son.

**Matthew 14:33** — Disciples worship: οἱ δὲ ἐν τῷ πλοίῳ προσεκύνησαν αὐτῷ λέγοντες· ἀληθῶς θεοῦ υἱὸς εἶ.
- Proskynesis (worship/bowing) directed to Jesus.
- "Truly you are the Son of God."
- Post-walking-on-water confession.

**Matthew 16:16** — Peter's confession: σὺ εἶ ὁ Χριστὸς ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ζῶντος.
- Enhanced from Mark 8:29 ("You are the Christ" → "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God").

**Matthew 28:18–20** — Great Commission.
- "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me."
- Baptism "in the name [singular: εἰς τὸ ὄνομα] of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."
- "I am with you always, to the end of the age."

### 6.3 Matthew 28:19 — Trinity Control

**CRITICAL:** Matthew 28:19 is NOT automatic proof of the Nicene Trinity.

- Triadic *pattern* (Father, Son, Holy Spirit named together): PRESENT.
- Triadic *formula* (singular "name"): PRESENT.
- Technical Nicene terminology (homoousios, ousia, hypostasis): ABSENT.
- Metaphysical formulation of three persons in one substance: ABSENT.

Matthew 28:19 establishes a triadic *devotional/liturgical pattern* but does not establish Nicene *metaphysical categories*. This is a triadic NAME, not a Trinitarian DOCTRINE.

### 6.4 Matthew Christological Assessment

| Indicator | Status | Evidence |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Messiah | PRESENT | Matt 16:16 |
| Son of God | PRESENT | Matt 3:17, 14:33, 16:16, 27:54 |
| Emmanuel (God with us) | PRESENT | Matt 1:23, 28:20 |
| Son of Man | PRESENT | Multiple |
| Worship (proskynesis) | PRESENT | Matt 14:33, 28:9, 28:17 |
| Divine authority | PRESENT | Matt 28:18 |
| Forgiveness | PRESENT | Matt 9:2–8 |
| Father-Son unique relationship | PRESENT | Matt 11:27 |
| Triadic formula | PRESENT | Matt 28:19 |
| Pre-existence | NOT ESTABLISHED | No explicit pre-existence language |
| Cosmic creation | NOT ESTABLISHED | No explicit creation language |
| YHWH-text application | LIMITED | Emmanuel (Isa 7:14) as divine presence |

**CLASSIFICATION:** Matthew has HIGHER Christology than Mark. Emmanuel frame (1:23 + 28:20) creates a divine-presence inclusio. Worship of Jesus (proskynesis) is explicit. Matt 11:27 has Johannine-quality Father-Son exclusivity. But pre-existence and cosmic creation remain NOT ESTABLISHED in Matthew's explicit language.

### 7.2 Luke Christological Profile

**Composition date:** c. 80–90 CE (PROBABLE).

#### Key Passages — Gospel of Luke:

**Luke 1:31–35** — Annunciation.
- "He will be called Son of the Most High" (υἱὸς ὑψίστου).
- "The Holy Spirit will come upon you... therefore the child will be called holy, Son of God" (υἱὸς θεοῦ).
- Davidic throne (1:32).
- Virgin conception by Spirit.

**Luke 2:11** — Birth announcement: ἐτέχθη ὑμῖν σήμερον σωτήρ, ὅς ἐστιν Χριστὸς κύριος, ἐν πόλει Δαυίδ.
- "Savior, who is Christ the Lord."
- Triple title in birth narrative.

**Luke 4:18–19** — Nazareth programmatic sermon.
- Isaiah 61:1–2 applied to Jesus.
- "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me..."
- Messianic anointing by Spirit.

**Luke 5:20–24** — Forgiveness of sins (parallels Mark 2).
- "Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?"

**Luke 7:13, 19** — Jesus as "the Lord" (ὁ κύριος) in narrator's voice.
- Luke applies Kyrios to Jesus in narrative frame (not just speech).

**Luke 9:35** — Transfiguration: οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἐκλελεγμένος, αὐτοῦ ἀκούετε.
- "My Son, the Chosen One; listen to him."

**Luke 24:34** — Resurrection confession: ἠγέρθη ὁ κύριος ὄντως.
- "The Lord has truly risen."
- Kyrios applied to risen Jesus.

#### Key Passages — Acts:

**Acts 2:36** — Peter's Pentecost speech: κύριον αὐτὸν καὶ χριστὸν ἐποίησεν ὁ θεός, τοῦτον τὸν Ἰησοῦν ὃν ὑμεῖς ἐσταυρώσατε.
- "God has made him both Lord and Christ."
- Exaltation Christology: God *made* him Lord (adoptionist? or confirmatory?).

**Acts 2:21** — Joel quotation: πᾶς ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου σωθήσεται.
- Joel 2:32 applied: calling on the Lord (Jesus) for salvation.

**Acts 3:15** — "Author of life" (τὸν ἀρχηγὸν τῆς ζωῆς).

**Acts 7:56** — Stephen's vision: τὸν υἱὸν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐκ δεξιῶν ἑστῶτα τοῦ θεοῦ.
- Son of Man standing at right hand of God.

**Acts 10:36** — "He is Lord of all" (οὗτός ἐστιν πάντων κύριος).

**Acts 13:33** — Psalm 2:7 applied to resurrection: υἱός μου εἶ σύ, ἐγὼ σήμερον γεγέννηκά σε.

### 7.3 Luke vs Acts Distinction

**CRITICAL:** Acts speeches are Lukan literary compositions representing early apostolic preaching. They are NOT verbatim transcripts.

- Luke (Gospel): narrative Christology, Jesus as Lord in narrator's voice.
- Acts: speeches present exaltation Christology (God made him Lord), emphasizing resurrection and heavenly session.

Acts 2:36 ("God made him Lord") is sometimes cited as "adoptionist" but this is DISPUTED. It may mean:
(a) God appointed/declared him Lord (functional).
(b) God confirmed/manifested what was already true (confirmatory).
(c) Exaltation to cosmic Lordship post-resurrection (developmental).

### 8.2 Johannine Christological Profile

**Composition date:** c. 90–110 CE (PROBABLE).

#### Key Passages:

**John 1:1** — Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος.
- Pre-existence: "In the beginning was the Word."
- Divine relationship: "the Word was with God."
- Divine predicate: "the Word was God" (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος — anarthrous theos, predicate nominative).
- Full analysis in existing audits.

**John 1:14** — καὶ ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο καὶ ἐσκήνωσεν ἐν ἡμῖν.
- Incarnation: "the Word became flesh."
- Unique Johannine claim: pre-existent divine Word enters material existence.

**John 1:18** — θεὸν οὐδεὶς ἑώρακεν πώποτε· μονογενὴς θεός, ὁ ὢν εἰς τὸν κόλπον τοῦ πατρός, ἐκεῖνος ἐξηγήσατο.
- "The only-begotten God" (μονογενὴς θεός — textually disputed: some read μονογενὴς υἱός).
- Unique Son in the Father's bosom reveals the Father.

**John 5:18** — ἴσον ἑαυτὸν ποιῶν τῷ θεῷ.
- Jewish opponents understand Jesus as "making himself equal with God."

**John 8:58** — πρὶν Ἀβραὰμ γενέσθαι ἐγὼ εἰμί.
- "Before Abraham was, I AM" (ἐγὼ εἰμί — divine name resonance, Ex 3:14).
- Absolute pre-existence claim.
- Johannine "I AM" sayings.

**John 10:30** — ἐγὼ καὶ ὁ πατὴρ ἕν ἐσμεν.
- "I and the Father are one."
- Unity claim. "One" (ἕν) is neuter — unity of essence/action, not person.

**John 17:5** — καὶ νῦν δόξασόν με σύ, πάτερ, παρὰ σεαυτῷ τῇ δόξῃ ᾗ εἶχον πρὸ τοῦ τὸν κόσμον εἶναι παρὰ σοί.
- "Glorify me with the glory I had with you before the world existed."
- Explicit pre-incarnate glory and pre-existence.

**John 20:28** — ὁ κύριός μου καὶ ὁ θεός μου.
- Thomas's confession: "My Lord and my God."
- Explicit theos applied to Jesus.
- Climactic confession of the Gospel.

### 10.2 Critical Assessment

**"Paul's Christology is fundamentally different from the Jesus of the Gospels."**

**Classification:** UNSAFE as stated.

**Finding:** Paul and the Synoptics share core Christological affirmations (Messiah, death, resurrection, Lordship) but differ in emphasis. Paul emphasizes cosmic/divine Christology; Synoptics emphasize kingdom, parables, and narrative authority. This is difference in *genre and emphasis*, not fundamental *contradiction*.

**Reason:**
1. Paul writes occasional letters, not Gospel narratives. Different genres produce different emphases.
2. Paul acknowledges received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5) that align with Gospel core.
3. Paul's "gospel" (1 Cor 15:1–11) overlaps with Synoptic narrative: death, burial, resurrection, appearances.
4. The Synoptics were composed AFTER Paul's letters, so "difference" may reflect literary choice, not theological contradiction.

### 10.3 Paul's Silence on Kingdom

Paul's relative silence on "Kingdom of God" (Jesus's central message in Synoptics) is sometimes cited as evidence that Paul "invented a different religion." This is UNSAFE.

**Reason:**
1. Paul does reference Kingdom (Rom 14:17, 1 Cor 4:20, 6:9–10, 15:50, Gal 5:21, 1 Thess 2:12).
2. Paul's letters are occasional (addressing specific community issues), not comprehensive theology.
3. "Kingdom" language may be presupposed rather than explicitly developed.

## 11. PAUL vs JOHN

### 11.1 Critical Test

**"Paul and John independently invented the same divine Christology."**

**Classification:** NOT ESTABLISHED.

**Analysis:**

1. **Chronology:** Paul wrote c. 48–62 CE. John was composed c. 90–110 CE. Paul predates John by 30–60 years.
2. **Literary dependence:** NOT ESTABLISHED. John may or may not know Paul's letters. No explicit Pauline citation in John.
3. **Shared tradition:** PROBABLE. Both operate within early Christian communities with shared traditions about Jesus.
4. **Jewish conceptual background:** Both draw on Jewish Wisdom, Word (Logos/Dabar), and divine agency traditions.
5. **Independent development:** POSSIBLE but NOT PROVEN. Both could independently develop high Christology from shared Jewish monotheistic resources + Jesus traditions.

## 12. EARLIEST HIGH CHRISTOLOGY — CHRONOLOGICAL EVIDENCE LADDER

### LEVEL 1: Historical Jesus Evidence (c. 27–33 CE)
- **Sources:** Tacitus (Annals 15.44), Josephus (Ant. XX.9.1, XVIII.3.3), Paul (Gal 1, 1 Cor 15), Gospels.
- **Can establish:** Jesus existed, was crucified under Pilate, had followers who continued after his death.
- **Cannot establish:** Jesus's exact self-understanding, whether he claimed divine status, precise Christological formulations.
- **Status:** LIMITED.

### LEVEL 2: Earliest Jesus Movement (c. 30–35 CE)
- **Sources:** Paul (Gal 1–2), Acts (later composition).
- **Can establish:** Community existed in Jerusalem, had leadership (James, Peter, John), practiced baptism, had shared meals.
- **Cannot establish:** Full Christological formulation, written confessions, exact devotional practices.
- **Status:** LIMITED.

### LEVEL 3: Received Pauline Traditions (pre-50 CE)
- **Sources:** Paul's own testimony (1 Cor 15:3–5, Phil 2:6–11).
- **Can establish:** Paul received traditions about Christ's death/resurrection and possibly pre-formed hymns BEFORE writing his letters.
- **Cannot establish:** Exact date of these traditions, their precise origin, whether they constitute "high Christology."
- **Status:** PROBABLE.

### LEVEL 4: Pauline Letters (c. 48–62 CE)
- **Sources:** Undisputed Pauline letters (P46 witness c. 175–225 CE).
- **Can establish:** Paul's Christology at time of writing: pre-existence (Phil 2:6), cosmic mediation (1 Cor 8:6), YHWH-text application (Rom 10:13), received traditions.
- **Cannot establish:** Whether this represents earliest community belief or Paul's development.
- **Status:** STRONG EVIDENCE.

### LEVEL 5: Synoptic Gospels (c. 65–90 CE)
- **Sources:** No Synoptic manuscripts in store; scholarly consensus.
- **Can establish:** Synoptic Christological profiles at time of composition.
- **Cannot establish:** Whether Synoptic Christology predates or postdates Paul's.
- **Status:** MODERATE (store-limited).

### LEVEL 6: Johannine Gospel (c. 90–110 CE)
- **Sources:** P66 (c. 100–250), P75 (c. 175–225), Sinaiticus, Vaticanus.
- **Can establish:** Johannine high Christology at time of composition: pre-existence, incarnation, divine predicates.
- **Cannot establish:** Whether John represents late development or early tradition.
- **Status:** STRONG EVIDENCE.

### LEVEL 7: Second-Century Patristic Evidence (c. 180 CE)
- **Sources:** Irenaeus (SRC-PAT-001).
- **Can establish:** By c. 180, four-Gospel canon recognized, high Christology normative, Logos theology established.
- **Cannot establish:** First-century practice, autograph content.
- **Status:** ESTABLISHED.

### LEVEL 8: Nicene Theology (325–451 CE)
- **Sources:** SRC-THEO-001 (Nicaea 325), SRC-THEO-003 (Constantinople 381), SRC-THEO-005 (Chalcedon 451).
- **Can establish:** Technical Trinitarian and Christological formulation (homoousios, hypostasis, physis).
- **Cannot establish:** That this was the original meaning of 1st-century texts.
- **Status:** ESTABLISHED (as later formulation).

## 13. PRE-PAULINE MATERIAL

### 13.1 Identified Pre-Pauline Traditions

#### A. 1 Corinthians 15:3–5
- **Status:** STRONG EVIDENCE for pre-Pauline origin.
- **Evidence:** Paul uses explicit received-tradition language (παρέδωκα/παρέλαβον). Formulaic structure. Parallelismus membrorum.
- **Content:** Christ died for sins, was buried, was raised, appeared to witnesses.
- **Date:** NOT ESTABLISHED precisely. Could be Damascus (c. 33–35), Jerusalem (c. 36–38), or Antioch (c. 40s).
- **Classification:** PRE-PAULINE — PROBABLE.

#### B. Philippians 2:6–11
- **Status:** PROBABLE pre-Pauline hymn.
- **Evidence:** Formal features (rhythmic structure, parallelism, hapax legomena, non-Pauline vocabulary). Most scholars identify as pre-Pauline hymn or tradition, though some argue Pauline composition.
- **Content:** Pre-existence ("form of God"), incarnation (kenosis), death, exaltation, universal worship.
- **Date:** NOT ESTABLISHED. If pre-Pauline, must predate Philippians (c. 54–62 CE).
- **Classification:** PRE-PAULINE — PROBABLE but DISPUTED.

#### C. Other Possible Pre-Pauline Material
- **Romans 1:3–4** — Possible early creed: "descended from David according to the flesh, designated Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead."
  - **Status:** DISPUTED. Some scholars identify as pre-Pauline formula; others as Pauline composition.
- **1 Corinthians 16:22** — Maranatha (Μαρὰν ἀθά): Aramaic invocation "Our Lord, come!"
  - **Status:** STRONG EVIDENCE for pre-Pauline Aramaic Christian liturgical formula.
  - **Significance:** Aramaic = earliest Jesus movement language. Invocation of Jesus as "Lord" in worship context.

### 13.2 Critical Boundaries

**"Pre-Pauline" does NOT mean:**
- Proven exact date.
- Proven Jerusalem origin.
- Proven historical Jesus origin.

**"Pre-Pauline" MEANS:**
- Paul himself states he received the tradition from earlier sources.
- Formal/textual features suggest non-Pauline origin.
- The tradition predates Paul's letter (which itself dates c. 48–62 CE).

## 15. JEWISH MONOTHEISM

### 15.1 Second Temple Jewish Monotheism

**Core features:**
- One God (YHWH) alone is creator, sovereign, judge.
- No other being shares divine identity.
- Strict prohibition of worship of any being other than YHWH.
- Divine agency exists: God sends agents (angels, prophets, Wisdom, Word) but these are NOT God.
- Heavenly figures exist (exalted patriarchs, angelic beings) but are NOT worshipped.

### 15.2 Paul Within Jewish Monotheism

**1 Corinthians 8:4–6:**
- Paul affirms "there is no God but one" (v. 4) — strict monotheism.
- Paul redefines: "one God, the Father... one Lord, Jesus Christ" (v. 6).
- This is NOT abandonment of monotheism. It is monotheism REDEFINED to include Jesus within the divine identity.
- Bauckham's framework: Jesus is included within the unique divine identity (creator, sovereign) while remaining distinguished from the Father.

**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION — Paul operates within Jewish monotheistic conceptual frameworks but redefines the identity of the one God to include Jesus. This is not polytheism; it is christological monotheism.

### 15.3 Synoptics Within Jewish Monotheism

The Synoptics present Jesus as God's unique agent — Messiah, Son of God, Son of Man — without explicit statements that Jesus IS YHWH. This is consistent with Jewish divine agency patterns.

### 15.4 John Within Jewish Monotheism

John's Gospel pushes Christology furthest within a Jewish framework: the Logos is theos (1:1), but the Father is "the only true God" (John 17:3). This creates a tension that later Trinitarian theology attempts to resolve.

### 15.5 Critical Boundaries

**REJECTED:** "Jewish monotheism means Jesus cannot be divine." — This assumes a fixed definition of Jewish monotheism that did not allow for divine agency, heavenly mediation, or identity redefinition. The evidence shows that early Christians DID include Jesus within the divine identity while claiming to remain monotheists.

**REJECTED:** "Jesus is divine, therefore Christianity abandoned Judaism." — This assumes that inclusion of Jesus within divine identity necessarily constitutes abandonment of Jewish monotheism. The evidence is more complex: early Christians understood themselves as faithful to Jewish monotheism while redefining the identity of the one God.

## 16. RESURRECTION

### 16.1 Layer Separation

**Layer 1 — Historical claim:** Jesus was believed to have risen from the dead.
- **Evidence:** Paul (1 Cor 15:3–8), all four Gospels, Acts.
- **Status:** ESTABLISHED that early followers *believed* and *proclaimed* resurrection.

**Layer 2 — Historical inference:** Early followers sincerely proclaimed resurrection (not fabrication).
- **Evidence:** Paul's willingness to suffer (2 Cor 11:23–28), early martyrdom tradition, transformation of movement.
- **Status:** PROBABLE that proclamation was sincere.

**Layer 3 — Theological claim:** Jesus actually rose bodily from the dead.
- **Evidence:** Faith claim, not empirically verifiable by historical method.
- **Status:** THEOLOGICAL CLAIM — cannot be established or refuted by historical method alone.

### 16.2 Resurrection and Christology

The resurrection claim is the *catalyst* for high Christology:
- Without resurrection, Jesus is a dead teacher.
- With resurrection, Jesus is exalted Lord (Acts 2:36, Rom 1:4, Phil 2:9).
- Resurrection enables: exaltation, Lordship, divine session, universal authority.

**Critical rule:** Historical methodology can establish that early followers *believed* resurrection occurred. It cannot establish that resurrection *actually occurred* as a metaphysical event. This boundary must not be silently crossed.

## 17. DIVINE IDENTITY

### 17.1 "Jesus Is Included in the Divine Identity"

This phrase (Bauckham's framework) requires careful breakdown:

**Textual observation:** Early Christian texts apply YHWH-texts to Jesus (Rom 10:13 = Joel 2:32; Phil 2:10–11 = Isa 45:23), place Jesus in cosmic creation (1 Cor 8:6), and include Jesus in worship (Phil 2:10–11).

**Jewish-context argument:** In Second Temple Judaism, YHWH-texts, cosmic creation, and universal worship belong exclusively to God. Applying these to Jesus constitutes inclusion within the divine identity.

**Theological inference:** Therefore, early Christians included Jesus within the unique divine identity of the God of Israel.

**Classification:** The *textual observation* is STRONG EVIDENCE. The *Jewish-context argument* is STRONG INTERPRETATION. The *theological inference* is INTERPRETATION — defensible but not raw fact.

### 17.2 Degrees of Inclusion

| Text | Degree of Divine Identity Inclusion |
|------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 Cor 8:6 | STRONG — cosmic mediation, Shema reframing |
| Phil 2:6–11 | STRONG — pre-existence, YHWH-text application |
| John 1:1 | STRONGEST — explicit theos predicate |
| John 20:28 | STRONGEST — explicit theos confession |
| Mark 2:5–12 | MODERATE — divine prerogative (forgiveness) |
| Matt 28:19 | MODERATE — triadic naming |
| Acts 2:36 | MODERATE — exaltation to Lordship |

## 18. TRINITY CONTROL

### 18.1 Early Christology vs Nicene Trinity Matrix

| Feature | Early Evidence (Paul/Gospels) | Nicaea 325 | Constantinople 381 | Chalcedon 451 |
|---------|-------------------------------|-----------|--------------------|---------------|
| Father as God | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Son as Lord | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Spirit | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| One God | PRESENT (Shema) | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Pre-existence of Son | PRESENT (Paul, John) | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Divine status of Son | PRESENT (various degrees) | PRESENT (homoousios) | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Creation through Son | PRESENT (1 Cor 8:6, John 1:3) | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Homoousios (same substance) | NOT PRESENT | PRESENT (key term) | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Ousia (substance/essence) | NOT PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Hypostasis (person) | NOT PRESENT (technical sense) | DISPUTED | PRESENT (Cappadocian) | PRESENT |
| Three persons/hypostases | NOT PRESENT (technical) | NOT PRESENT | PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Two natures (physis) | NOT PRESENT (technical) | NOT PRESENT | NOT PRESENT | PRESENT |
| Technical Trinitarian formulation | NOT PRESENT | EARLY | PRESENT | PRESENT |

### 18.2 Critical Conclusion

**Early high Christology: PRESENT.**
Early texts place Jesus within divine identity, apply YHWH-texts to him, and include him in worship and cosmic functions.

**Nicene technical terminology: LATER FORMULATION.**
Homoousios, ousia, hypostasis (in technical Trinitarian sense), physis (in Chalcedonian sense) are NOT present in 1st-century texts.

**DO NOT CONFUSE THESE.**
- Early high Christology ≠ Nicene Trinity.
- Nicene Trinity develops FROM early high Christology trajectories.
- But the technical language, metaphysical categories, and conciliar formulations are products of 3rd–5th century theological development.

## 19. DEVELOPMENT MODEL

### 19.1 Four Categories

| Category | Definition | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| CONTINUITY | Same concept, same language | "Jesus is Messiah" — Paul to Nicaea |
| TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT | Same concept, new language | "Son of God" → homoousios |
| CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT | Related concept, expanded scope | "Lord" → universal sovereignty → Nicene divine ontology |
| SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT | New concept not clearly present earlier | Technical Trinitarian formulation (3 hypostases, 1 ousia) |

### 19.2 Application

| Layer | Classification |
|-------|----------------|
| Paul → Mark | CONTINUITY (core: Messiah, death, resurrection) with different emphasis |
| Mark → Matthew | CONTINUITY + TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT (Emmanuel, enhanced Son of God) |
| Matthew → Luke-Acts | CONTINUITY + CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT (exaltation emphasis, Spirit) |
| Paul → John | CONTINUITY + CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT (Logos, explicit pre-existence, theos) |
| John → Irenaeus | CONTINUITY + CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT (Logos theology, four-Gospel canon) |
| Irenaeus → Nicaea | TERMINOLOGICAL + CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT (homoousios, ontological categories) |
| Nicaea → Constantinople | TERMINOLOGICAL + CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT (Spirit's divinity, Cappadocian framework) |
| Constantinople → Chalcedon | TERMINOLOGICAL + CONCEPTUAL + SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT (two natures, hypostatic union) |

### 19.3 Critical Boundaries

**NOT every development is corruption.** Terminological and conceptual development can be faithful explication of earlier trajectories (e.g., homoousios as explication of what "Son of God" and "form of God" imply).

**NOT every continuity proves doctrinal identity.** Shared language ("Son of God") does not mean identical theology across centuries. The semantic range of terms shifts.

**SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT** (new concepts not clearly present earlier) must be acknowledged honestly. The technical Trinitarian formulation (three hypostases in one ousia) IS a substantive development beyond what 1st-century texts explicitly state.

## 20. CLAIM TESTS (20 Claims: A–T)

### Claim A: "Paul invented Christianity."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Paul did not invent Christianity. He joined an existing movement, acknowledged Jerusalem apostles, received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5), and operated within established community structures.

**Evidence:** Gal 1:17–2:9 (Jerusalem contacts), 1 Cor 15:3–5 (received tradition), 1 Cor 11:23 (received Lord's Supper tradition).

**Reason:** Paul explicitly states he received traditions from predecessors. His letters presuppose existing Christian communities, baptism, and worship practices.

**Confidence:** HIGH.
**Limitations:** Paul's Damascus experience was transformative, but transformation ≠ invention.

---

### Claim B: "Paul invented high Christology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Paul inherited high Christological traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5, Phil 2:6–11) while also developing original formulations (1 Cor 8:6 Shema reframing).

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 15:3 ("I delivered what I also received"), Phil 2:6–11 (probable pre-Pauline hymn), 1 Cor 16:22 (Aramaic Maranatha).

**Reason:** Paul's own received-tradition language and the formal features of key Christological passages indicate inheritance, not pure invention.

**Confidence:** HIGH.
**Limitations:** Paul's unique contributions (cosmic mediator, Shema reframing) are real developments of inherited traditions.

---

### Claim C: "High Christology existed before Paul."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Pre-Pauline traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5, Phil 2:6–11, Maranatha) attest to Christological formulations that predate Paul's letters. Whether these constitute "high Christology" depends on definition.

**Evidence:** Paul's explicit "received" language, Aramaic Maranatha, formal features of Phil 2:6–11.

**Reason:** The traditions Paul received must predate his letters (c. 48–62 CE). Maranatha in Aramaic points to earliest Aramaic-speaking community.

**Confidence:** HIGH that traditions predate Paul; MODERATE that they constitute "high Christology" in the fullest sense.
**Limitations:** "Before Paul" does not mean "from the historical Jesus." The exact dating and origin of pre-Pauline traditions remain DISPUTED.

---

### Claim D: "Paul abandoned Jewish monotheism."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Paul explicitly affirms Jewish monotheism (1 Cor 8:4: "there is no God but one"; Rom 3:30) while redefining the identity of the one God to include Jesus.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:4–6 (Shema affirmed and reframed), Gal 3:20 ("God is one"), Rom 3:30.

**Reason:** Paul's Christology operates WITHIN Jewish monotheism, not against it. He includes Jesus within the divine identity while maintaining one-God confession.

**Confidence:** HIGH.
**Limitations:** Whether Paul's reframing is consistent with all forms of Second Temple monotheism is DISPUTED.

---

### Claim E: "1 Cor 8:6 places Jesus within Jewish monotheism."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** 1 Cor 8:6 reframes the Shema to include "one God, the Father" and "one Lord, Jesus Christ" within a single monotheistic confession.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:4–6 text; Deuteronomy 6:4 resonance; prepositional structure (ἐξ οὗ / δι' οὗ).

**Reason:** Paul maintains monotheistic structure ("one God... one Lord") while including Jesus in cosmic creation and salvation.

**Confidence:** HIGH.
**Limitations:** Whether this constitutes "inclusion in divine identity" or "exalted divine agency" is DISPUTED among scholars.

---

### Claim F: "Philippians 2 demonstrates pre-existence."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Phil 2:6 ("being in the form of God") implies pre-incarnate existence. However, "form of God" (μορφῇ θεοῦ) is interpreted variously.

**Evidence:** Phil 2:6–7 text; contrast between pre-incarnate and incarnate states.

**Reason:** The movement from "form of God" to "form of a servant" implies a prior state. Most scholars read this as pre-existence.

**Confidence:** HIGH for pre-existence implication; DISPUTED for exact ontological content.
**Limitations:** μορφῇ θεοῦ ≠ homoousios. The text does not use Nicene categories.

---

### Claim G: "Mark contains high Christology."
**Classification:** DISPUTED

**Finding:** Mark contains MODERATE Christology: Messiah, Son of God, Son of Man, forgiveness authority, eschatological authority. But lacks explicit pre-existence, cosmic creation, and divine ontology.

**Evidence:** Mark 1:1, 1:11, 2:5–12, 8:29, 14:62, 15:39.

**Reason:** Mark's Christology is high in authority and identity but moderate in explicit divine ontology. Whether this qualifies as "high Christology" depends on definition.

**Confidence:** MODERATE.
**Limitations:** Mark's Christological profile is distinct from John's. Binary "high/low" classification oversimplifies.

---

### Claim H: "Matthew contains early divine Christology."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Matthew has higher Christology than Mark: Emmanuel (God with us), worship of Jesus, Matt 11:27 (Johannine-quality Father-Son exclusivity), triadic formula (28:19).

**Evidence:** Matt 1:23, 14:33, 11:27, 28:18–20.

**Reason:** Matthew's Emmanuel frame and explicit worship indicate divine Christology beyond Mark.

**Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH.
**Limitations:** Pre-existence and cosmic creation remain NOT ESTABLISHED in Matthew.

---

### Claim I: "Luke-Acts contains high Christology."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Luke-Acts presents moderate-high Christology: Jesus as Lord, Messiah, Son of God, exalted to God's right hand, YHWH-text application (Joel 2:32). But lacks explicit pre-existence.

**Evidence:** Luke 1:35, 2:11, Acts 2:21, 2:36, 7:56, 10:36.

**Reason:** Luke-Acts emphasizes exaltation Christology and divine agency. Pre-existence is not explicit.

**Confidence:** MODERATE.
**Limitations:** Luke's Christological profile emphasizes exaltation over incarnation.

---

### Claim J: "John represents a later high Christology."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** John has the highest explicit Christology of any Gospel and is conventionally dated latest (c. 90–110 CE). However, "later" does not mean "invented" — John may represent explicit literary expression of earlier traditions.

**Evidence:** John 1:1, 1:14, 8:58, 17:5, 20:28; composition date estimates.

**Reason:** John's explicitness is chronologically later than Paul's letters, but the Christological concepts (pre-existence, divine identity) appear already in Paul.

**Confidence:** HIGH for chronological laterness; DISPUTED for whether John represents development or explicitation.

**Limitations:** Conventional dating is scholarly estimate; some scholars date parts of Johannine tradition earlier. "Later" is chronological, not evaluative.

---

### Claim K: "John copied Paul's Christology."
**Classification:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Finding:** No evidence of direct literary dependence of John on Paul's letters. Shared Christological concepts may derive from common tradition, not copying.

**Evidence:** No explicit Pauline citation in John; shared themes (pre-existence, divine agency) but different language and literary form.

**Reason:** Both Paul and John may draw on shared early Christian traditions and Jewish Wisdom/Word concepts.

**Confidence:** HIGH that direct copying is NOT ESTABLISHED.

**Limitations:** Absence of evidence of dependence is not proof of independence; both may share lost common sources.

---

### Claim L: "Paul copied John's Christology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Paul's letters (c. 48–62 CE) predate John's Gospel (c. 90–110 CE) by 30–60 years. Paul could not have copied John.

**Evidence:** Composition date consensus.

**Reason:** Chronological impossibility.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Chronological impossibility rests on scholarly date consensus for both corpora, which is PROBABLE rather than ESTABLISHED for the Gospels.

---

### Claim M: "Synoptic Christology and Pauline Christology are contradictory."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Synoptic and Pauline Christologies differ in emphasis and genre but share core affirmations (Messiah, death for sins, resurrection, Lordship).

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 15:3–5 aligns with Gospel narrative core; shared Kyrios confession; shared resurrection proclamation.

**Reason:** Differences are best explained by genre (letters vs narrative) and emphasis (cosmic vs kingdom), not contradiction.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Genre and emphasis differences can obscure genuine tensions (e.g., Paul's silence on Kingdom narrative, Gospels' silence on justification); contradiction claims and harmony claims both risk overstatement.

---

### Claim N: "Early Jesus devotion predates Nicaea."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** Devotion to Jesus as Lord (invocation, baptism, Maranatha, hymns) is attested in Paul's letters (c. 50 CE), predating Nicaea (325 CE) by approximately 275 years.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 1:2, 16:22, Phil 2:10–11, Rom 10:9–13.

**Reason:** Direct textual evidence from undisputed Pauline letters.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Paul's letters attest devotion c. 50 CE; they do not directly establish the exact year devotion began, nor prove continuity of that practice without gap.

---

### Claim O: "Early Christology proves Nicene Trinity."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Early Christology establishes trajectories that Nicene theology later formalized, but early texts do not contain Nicene technical terminology (homoousios, ousia, hypostasis in Trinitarian sense).

**Evidence:** Comparison of 1st-century texts with Nicene Creed (SRC-THEO-001).

**Reason:** Early high Christology is the *foundation* from which Nicene theology developed, but foundation ≠ finished building.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Trajectory identification is interpretive; Nicaea's sources include non-stored philosophical and theological influences (Origen, Alexander, Arius fragments) beyond this store.

---

### Claim P: "Nicene Trinity was invented from nothing."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Nicene formulation develops from identifiable trajectories in early Christian texts: high Christology, triadic patterns, YHWH-text application, Father-Son-Spirit naming.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:6 (Father/Lord distinction), Matt 28:19 (triadic formula), 2 Cor 13:14 (triadic benediction), Phil 2:6–11 (divine Christology).

**Reason:** Nicaea responds to Arian controversy using earlier Christological resources. It is development, not invention from nothing.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** "Identifiable trajectories" are retrospective reconstructions; the earliest-level commitment to a triune ontology specifically (vs. binitarian devotion) remains DISPUTED and is not directly evidenced in this store.

---

### Claim Q: "Jesus's divinity is a fourth-century invention."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Divine Christological language appears in 1st-century texts (Paul: 1 Cor 8:6, Phil 2:6–11; John: 1:1, 20:28). The 4th century formalized this language with technical terminology but did not invent the concept.

**Evidence:** 1st-century texts with divine Christological language; 4th-century conciliar documents.

**Reason:** Continuity exists between 1st-century high Christology and 4th-century Nicene formulation, though significant terminological and conceptual development occurred.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** "Continuity" does not mean identity; the 4th century added metaphysical categories and conciliar authority not present in 1st-century texts.

---

### Claim R: "Early Christology developed while remaining within Jewish monotheistic conceptual frameworks."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Paul explicitly affirms monotheism (1 Cor 8:4) while including Jesus within divine identity (1 Cor 8:6). This operates within Jewish monotheistic frameworks while redefining divine identity.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:4–6, Rom 3:30, Gal 3:20.

**Reason:** Early Christians understood their Christology as consistent with Jewish monotheism, even as they redefined the identity of the one God.

**Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH.
**Limitations:** Whether this redefinition is truly "within" Jewish monotheism or constitutes a break is DISPUTED among scholars.

---

### Claim S: "High Christology and Nicene Christology are identical."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** High Christology (1st-century) and Nicene Christology (4th-century) share trajectories but differ in terminology, metaphysical precision, and conciliar authority.

**Evidence:** Comparison of Phil 2:6–11 with Nicene Creed (SRC-THEO-001).

**Reason:** Nicene Christology adds technical categories (homoousios, ousia) not present in 1st-century texts.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** The comparison spans different genres and centuries; terminological differences do not by themselves prove theological difference, and similarities do not prove identity.

---

### Claim T: "High Christology existed before formal Nicene terminology."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** High Christological language (pre-existence, divine identity, YHWH-text application, worship) appears in 1st-century texts, predating Nicene terminology by 250+ years.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:6, Phil 2:6–11, John 1:1, John 20:28 — all predate Nicaea 325.

**Reason:** Direct chronological comparison of textual evidence.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** "Before Nicene terminology" establishes chronological priority, not genetic derivation; terminological priority does not by itself prove that 4th-century categories are the only valid expression of those beliefs.

## 21. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### Side A: "High Christology was present very early and Nicaea formalized an existing trajectory."

**Strongest case:**

1. Paul's letters (c. 48–62 CE) already contain high Christology: pre-existence (Phil 2:6), cosmic creation mediation (1 Cor 8:6), YHWH-text application (Rom 10:13), divine identity inclusion.
2. Paul explicitly states he received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5), pushing Christological formulations to within 5–15 years of Jesus's death.
3. Aramaic Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22) attests to Jesus invocation in the earliest Aramaic-speaking community.
4. Phil 2:6–11, if pre-Pauline, contains explicit pre-existence and divine worship before Paul's letters.
5. All four Gospels, despite different profiles, affirm Jesus as Messiah, Son of God, risen Lord.
6. John's explicit theos language (1:1, 20:28) does not appear from nowhere but develops trajectories already present in Paul.
7. Irenaeus (c. 180) confirms four-Gospel canon and high Christology as normative.
8. Nicaea (325) responds to Arian controversy by formalizing what was already believed: Christ is divine, not created.
9. The trajectory from "Jesus is Lord" (pre-Pauline) → "one Lord" (Paul, 1 Cor 8:6) → "the Word was God" (John 1:1) → homoousios (Nicaea 325) is a continuous development, not an invention.
10. Hurtado's thesis: devotion to Jesus as divine is explosively early, within the first years after the crucifixion.

**Side A is STRONGEST on:** Early attestation of high Christological language, received-tradition evidence, devotional practices, trajectory continuity.

---

### Side B: "Later Christian theology substantially developed beyond the earliest Jesus traditions."

**Strongest case:**

1. The historical Jesus, as reconstructed from earliest sources, preached the Kingdom of God, not his own divine ontology.
2. Paul's high Christology, while early, is post-resurrection interpretation, not necessarily Jesus's own self-understanding.
3. Mark (earliest Gospel) has the most restrained Christology — no pre-existence, no Logos, no explicit theos.
4. John (latest Gospel) has the highest Christology — suggesting development over time.
5. Paul's letters (earliest NT documents) already represent theological interpretation, not raw historical reportage.
6. The gap between Jesus's Aramaic preaching and Paul's Greek theological formulations involves significant cultural, linguistic, and conceptual translation.
7. Nicene homoousios is a 4th-century Greek philosophical category unknown to 1st-century Jewish-Christian communities.
8. The development from "God's agent" to "God himself" involves substantive theological change, not merely terminological clarification.
9. Acts 2:36 ("God made him Lord") suggests exaltation Christology, which differs from eternal divine identity.
10. The Synoptic Jesus prays to God, is obedient to God, and distinguishes himself from God ("Why do you call me good? No one is good but God alone" — Mark 10:18).
11. Multiple "Christianities" existed in the first centuries; Nicene Christianity won the political-theological contest but was not the only option.

**Side B is STRONGEST on:** Historical Jesus vs later theology gap, Markan restraint, Johannine laterness, Nicene philosophical categories, development trajectory.

---

### What Neither Side Can Establish

1. **Jesus's exact self-understanding:** Both sides reconstruct from later sources. Neither can prove what Jesus "really thought" about his own identity.
2. **Exact date of high Christology's emergence:** Both sides agree it's early (within 20 years), but neither can pinpoint the exact moment.
3. **Whether development is faithful or corrupt:** This is a theological judgment, not a purely historical one.
4. **Whether Nicene formulation is the only valid reading of early Christology:** Multiple readings of the same early texts are possible.

## 23. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE

### 23.1 Types of Independence

| Type | Definition | Application |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Physical independence | Different physical objects | P46, P66, P75, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus are physically distinct |
| Literary independence | No literary dependence | Paul and Mark: NOT ESTABLISHED; Paul and John: NOT ESTABLISHED |
| Theological independence | Independent theological development | NOT ESTABLISHED for any pair |
| Historical independence | Independent historical testimony | Limited; all NT sources share community context |
| Genealogical independence | No shared textual ancestor | P66/P75/Sin/Vat: close Alexandrian affinity; genealogical independence NOT ESTABLISHED |

### 23.2 Critical Rules

**DO NOT count Paul + John + Irenaeus + Nicaea as "four independent witnesses"** to the same Christological claim. They are:
- Chronologically sequential.
- Theologically related (standing in the same tradition).
- Not independent attestations of the same event.

**DO NOT count P66 + P75 + Sinaiticus + Vaticanus as "four independent manuscript witnesses"** without qualification. They are:
- Physically distinct objects.
- Textually related (Alexandrian tradition).
- Genealogical independence requires detailed textual criticism, not assumed.

### 23.3 Actual Independent Attestation

For "high Christology existed by the late 1st century":
- **Paul (1 Cor, Phil):** Independent letter collection.
- **Mark:** Independent Gospel narrative.
- **John:** Independent Gospel narrative (relationship to Synoptics DISPUTED).
- **Matthew:** Dependent on Mark (literary dependence).
- **Luke:** Dependent on Mark (literary dependence).

**Effective independent witnesses to high Christology by c. 100 CE:** Paul, Mark, John (3 streams, with qualifications). Matthew and Luke add corroborating but literarily dependent evidence.

## 24. HISTORICAL METHOD — WHAT CAN IT ESTABLISH?

### 24.1 Historical Method CAN Establish

- Existence of texts (manuscripts, citations).
- Approximate dating of composition (paleography, internal evidence, external testimony).
- Textual transmission (manuscript relationships, variants).
- Early belief (what communities believed and proclaimed).
- Development (changes in Christological language over time).
- Historical relationships (literary dependence, community connections).
- Comparative analysis (how different texts present Jesus).

### 24.2 Historical Method CANNOT Automatically Establish

- Divine inspiration of texts.
- Miracle occurrence (resurrection as metaphysical event).
- Metaphysical ontology (whether Jesus IS ontologically divine).
- Truth of revelation claims.
- Which theological interpretation is "correct."
- Whether development is "faithful" or "corrupt."

### 24.3 Boundary Application

**This audit uses historical method to establish:**
- WHAT texts say.
- WHEN texts were approximately composed.
- HOW Christological language developed.
- WHAT communities believed.

**This audit does NOT use historical method to establish:**
- Whether Jesus IS God (theological claim).
- Whether Nicaea is "true" (confessional claim).
- Whether early Christians were "right" (normative claim).

## 25. APOLOGETIC ERROR AUDIT

The following common apologetic and polemical claims are explicitly REJECTED as unsafe:

### REJECTED CLAIMS:

1. **"Paul invented Christianity."** → UNSAFE. Paul joined existing movement, received traditions.

2. **"Constantine invented Christianity."** → UNSAFE. Christianity existed 300 years before Constantine. Nicaea formalized existing Christological beliefs.

3. **"Nicaea invented Jesus's divinity."** → UNSAFE. Divine Christological language appears in 1st-century texts (Paul, John). Nicaea formalized with new terminology.

4. **"John invented divine Christology."** → UNSAFE. Divine Christology appears in Paul (earlier than John). John is the most explicit, not the originator.

5. **"Jesus never claimed divine status because Synoptics use different language."** → UNSAFE. Argument from silence. Mark/Matthew/Luke present Jesus with divine authority (forgiveness, eschatological judgment, Son of God). Different language ≠ absence of claim.

6. **"Paul proves the Trinity."** → UNSAFE. Paul's texts show high Christology and triadic patterns but not technical Trinitarian formulation.

7. **"John 1:1 proves the Trinity by itself."** → UNSAFE. John 1:1 establishes Logos-theos relationship but does not contain Trinitarian technical formulation.

8. **"High Christology automatically proves Christianity true."** → UNSAFE. Historical attestation of belief ≠ proof of theological truth.

9. **"Later doctrine automatically proves earlier doctrine false."** → UNSAFE. Development is not automatically corruption. Terminological development can be faithful explication.

10. **"Doctrinal development automatically means fabrication."** → UNSAFE. All living traditions develop. The question is whether development is continuous with earlier trajectories, not whether development occurred.

## 26. FINAL SYNTHESIS

### 26.1 Twelve Questions Answered

**1. What is the earliest evidence for high Christology?**
Paul's letters (c. 48–62 CE) contain high Christology: pre-existence (Phil 2:6), cosmic mediation (1 Cor 8:6), YHWH-text application (Rom 10:13). The received traditions Paul cites (1 Cor 15:3–5, Phil 2:6–11) push this earlier, possibly to within 5–15 years of Jesus's death.

**2. Can it securely be placed before Paul?**
PROBABLE but not certain. Paul's explicit "received" language (1 Cor 15:3) and Aramaic Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22) indicate pre-Pauline Christological formulations. Whether these constitute "high Christology" depends on definition.

**3. What evidence indicates Paul inherited traditions?**
1 Cor 15:3–5 (explicit received language), Phil 2:6–11 (probable pre-Pauline hymn), 1 Cor 11:23 (received Lord's Supper), 1 Cor 16:22 (Aramaic Maranatha).

**4. What did Paul uniquely develop?**
Shema reframing (1 Cor 8:6), cosmic mediator Christology, extensive YHWH-text application, Wisdom Christology (2 Cor 4:4), and theological application of received traditions to Gentile communities.

**5. What Christological elements appear in Mark?**
Messiah, Son of God, Son of Man (dominant), forgiveness authority, eschatological authority, suffering servant. NOT present: explicit pre-existence, cosmic creation, Logos.

**6. What appears in Matthew?**
All of Mark's elements PLUS: Emmanuel (divine presence), explicit worship (proskynesis), Johannine-quality Father-Son exclusivity (11:27), triadic formula (28:19).

**7. What appears in Luke-Acts?**
Messiah, Lord, Son of God, exaltation to God's right hand, YHWH-text application (Joel 2:32), salvation through Jesus alone (Acts 4:12). NOT present: explicit pre-existence.

**8. What appears in John?**
Logos, pre-existence, cosmic creation, incarnation, divine predicates (theos), "I AM" sayings, Father-Son mutual indwelling, explicit worship. Highest Christological explicitness.

**9. How much continuity exists?**
SIGNIFICANT continuity in core affirmations: Messiah, death for sins, resurrection, Lordship, Son of God. All NT sources share these.

**10. How much development exists?**
SIGNIFICANT development in explicitness: from Markan restraint to Johannine explicitness to Nicene technical formulation. Terminological and conceptual development is clear.

**11. What remains disputed?**
- Exact date of high Christology's emergence.
- Whether Phil 2:6–11 is pre-Pauline.
- Whether Mark's Christology is "high" or "moderate."
- Whether development is faithful or corrupt.
- Whether early high Christology proves Nicene Trinity.

**12. What cannot be projected backward from Nicaea?**
Homoousios, ousia, hypostasis (Trinitarian sense), technical Trinitarian formulation, three-person metaphysics. These are 4th-century categories not present in 1st-century texts.

## 28. FINAL CONCLUSION

### TEXTUAL FACT
1st-century Christian texts (Paul's letters, four Gospels) present Jesus with titles, functions, and devotional status that in Jewish monotheistic context belong to God alone: pre-existence, cosmic creation, YHWH-text application, worship, divine predicates.

↓

### HISTORICAL INFERENCE
These Christological formulations did not originate with a single author. Paul received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5). The Aramaic Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22) points to earliest Aramaic-speaking community. Multiple independent streams (Pauline, Markan, Johannine) attest high Christology.

↓

### CHRISTOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
Early Christians included Jesus within the unique divine identity of the God of Israel while maintaining monotheistic confession. This is neither simple adoptionism (Jesus became divine) nor full Nicene Trinitarianism (three hypostases, one ousia). It is an early, distinctive form of christological monotheism.

↓

### DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT
Over three centuries, this early Christology was formalized through:
- Patristic interpretation (Irenaeus, Origen, Athanasius).
- Conciliar formulation (Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381, Chalcedon 451).
- Technical terminology (homoousios, ousia, hypostasis, physis).
The development is CONTINUOUS with early trajectories but NOT IDENTICAL to early formulations.

---

### CONTINUITY:
Core Christological affirmations (Messiah, Lord, Son of God, resurrection, exaltation) persist from earliest traditions through Nicaea and Chalcedon. Pre-existence, divine agency, and worship of Jesus appear in 1st-century texts and persist through all subsequent layers.

### DEVELOPMENT:
Significant terminological and conceptual development occurs: from "Jesus is Lord" (pre-Pauline) → homoousios (325) → treis hypostaseis (381) → duo physeis (451). These technical categories are NOT present in 1st-century texts.

### DISPUTED:
- Exact date of high Christology's emergence.
- Whether Phil 2:6–11 is pre-Pauline.
- Whether "high Christology" in Paul equals "divine ontology."
- Whether development is faithful explication or substantive change.
- Whether early Christology "proves" Nicene Trinity.
- Whether Jesus's own self-understanding included divine identity.

### NOT ESTABLISHED:
- Jesus's exact self-understanding (historical Jesus layer).
- Full Christological formulation of earliest Jerusalem community.
- Literary dependence between Paul and John.
- Whether Synoptic "low" Christology is truly "low" or differently focused.
- Whether non-Nicene Christianities represent earlier or later traditions.

### LATER FORMALIZATION:
Nicene homoousios (325), Cappadocian treis hypostaseis (381), and Chalcedonian duo physeis (451) are later formalizations of trajectories present in 1st-century texts. They are CONTINUOUS with early Christology but NOT IDENTICAL to it. Projecting Nicene categories backward onto Paul or John is anachronistic. Denying continuity between early Christology and Nicene formulation is equally unsafe.
