## 1. PRIMARY AUDIT PRINCIPLE

For every religion or worldview, the **same audit sequence** applies:

```
CLAIM
→ DEFINITION
→ PRIMARY SOURCE
→ ORIGINAL LANGUAGE
→ TEXTUAL EVIDENCE
→ MANUSCRIPT / WITNESS
→ TRANSMISSION
→ HISTORICAL CONTEXT
→ INTERPRETATION
→ THEOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT
→ PHILOSOPHICAL ARGUMENT
→ COUNTERARGUMENT
→ STEELMAN
→ LIMITATION
→ CONFIDENCE
```

**No tradition receives automatic epistemic privilege.**

The audit tests whether the system's methodological standards are applied symmetrically — not whether the evidence quantity is identical (which naturally varies by historical circumstance), but whether the *rules* are identical.

### 1.1 Key separation

| Level | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | "What does the tradition teach?" | "Christianity teaches Trinity" |
| Philosophical | "Is the teaching coherent?" | "Is Trinity metaphysically coherent?" |
| Theological | "Is the teaching true?" | "Trinity is true" |

The system must **never collapse these levels**.

### 1.2 The Sunni standpoint rule

Polya's theological standpoint remains **Sunni Islam / Ahlus Sunnah wal Jama'ah**. However:

- SUNNI THEOLOGICAL EVALUATION ≠ HISTORICAL FACT
- SUNNI THEOLOGICAL CONCLUSION ≠ NEUTRAL ACADEMIC CONSENSUS

The system must be able to say:
- "Historically established, but not sufficient for the theological conclusion."
- "Strong theological argument, but historical evidence is insufficient."
- "Traditional Islamic claim, historically disputed."

### 1.3 Immutability compliance

This step does NOT:
- redo STEP 100–148
- modify specialist files
- modify production files
- perform recovery on prior steps
- begin STEP 150
- update KNOWLEDGE_INDEX.md before PASS FINAL
- silently fix prior-step errors (findings are recorded as AUDIT FINDING)

If a prior-step issue is found, it is **recorded as an audit finding**, not silently repaired.

## 4. CORE SYMMETRY AUDIT

### 4.1 Methodological symmetry principle

"Symmetry" means **equal standards**, NOT artificially equal evidence.

Historical circumstances naturally produce different quantities of evidence. The audit tests whether the *rules* for evaluating evidence are identical, not whether every tradition has the same number of manuscripts or witnesses.

### 4.2 Symmetry rules

| Rule | Christian application | Islamic application | Jewish application | Skeptical application |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Miracle claims require historical-source analysis | Gospel miracles analyzed via source criticism | Qur'anic miracles / Hadith miracles analyzed via source criticism | Biblical miracles (Hebrew Bible) analyzed via source criticism | All miracle claims subject to same threshold |
| Oral/written transmission requires isnad/criticism | Gospel transmission chain analyzed | Hadith isnad/matn analyzed | Mishnah-to-Talmud transmission analyzed | All transmission chains subject to same criteria |
| Manuscript claims require genealogical analysis | NT MSS genealogy analyzed | Qur'an MSS genealogy analyzed | HB MSS genealogy analyzed | All MSS claims subject to genealogical test |
| Commentary distinguished from scripture | Church Fathers ≠ Biblical text | Tafsir ≠ Qur'anic text | Talmud ≠ Tanakh text | All commentary-text distinctions maintained |
| Later doctrine not automatically projected backward | Nicene Trinity not read into 1st-century text | Later Sunni/Shi'i doctrine not read into 7th-century text | Later rabbinic doctrine not read into pre-70 CE text | No theological conclusion projected backward |
| Burden of proof applies to all | Christian claims bear burden | Islamic claims bear burden | Jewish claims bear burden | Skeptical claims bear burden |

### 4.3 Anti-asymmetry rules

The following are explicitly forbidden:

1. Accepting Christian favorable evidence while rejecting analogous Islamic evidence (→ DS-01)
2. Accepting Islamic favorable evidence while rejecting analogous Christian evidence (→ DS-02)
3. Dismissing a late Christian source while accepting a late Islamic source (→ DS-03)
4. Dismissing a late Islamic source while accepting a late Christian source (→ DS-04)
5. Applying manuscript uncertainty asymmetrically (→ DS-05)
6. Applying translation uncertainty asymmetrically (→ DS-06)
7. Applying historical skepticism asymmetrically (→ DS-07)
8. Applying miracle skepticism asymmetrically (→ DS-08)
9. Applying prophecy standards asymmetrically (→ DS-09)
10. Treating doctrinal development as corruption for one tradition but legitimate for another (→ DS-10)

### 6.2 DS-01 – DS-20

---

### DS-01

**DOUBLE_STANDARD:** Christian favorable evidence accepted while analogous Islamic evidence rejected.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether Christian patristic testimony is accepted as historical evidence while analogous Islamic hadith testimony (with comparable chain) is rejected as unreliable.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether Islamic hadith is accepted as historical while analogous Christian patristic testimony is dismissed.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether rabbinic testimony is accepted while analogous patristic or hadith testimony is dismissed.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether the skeptic accepts favorable secular evidence while rejecting comparable religious evidence.

**RESULT:** PASS — The system applies the same source-critical standards to patristic testimony, hadith, and rabbinic tradition. Neither is accepted/rejected on the basis of tradition identity.

**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument that accepts patristic testimony while rejecting hadith (or vice versa) must justify the asymmetry with source-critical reasoning, not tradition identity.

---

### DS-02

**DOUBLE_STANDARD:** Islamic favorable evidence accepted while analogous Christian evidence rejected.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether Christian manuscript evidence is dismissed as "late" while Islamic manuscript evidence of comparable date is accepted.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether Islamic manuscript evidence is accepted as proof of preservation while Christian manuscript evidence of comparable quantity/date is dismissed.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether Jewish manuscript evidence is treated symmetrically.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether the skeptic's skepticism is applied uniformly.

**RESULT:** PASS — Manuscript evidence is evaluated by date, genealogical relationship, and textual significance, not by tradition identity.

**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument that claims Qur'anic preservation from manuscript quantity must apply the same logic to NT manuscript quantity, and vice versa. Neither quantity alone proves preservation.

---

### DS-03

**DOUBLE_STANDARD:** Christian late source dismissed but Islamic late source accepted.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether a 4th-century Christian source (e.g., Eusebius on early church) is dismissed as "too late" while a 9th-century Islamic source (e.g., Ibn Hisham's Sira) is accepted as reliable for 7th-century events.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether Ibn Hisham is accepted while Eusebius is dismissed for comparable temporal distance.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether late midrashic sources are treated symmetrically.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether the skeptic applies temporal distance consistently.

**RESULT:** PASS — Temporal distance is evaluated by actual years between event and source, source dependency chains, and genre, not by tradition identity. Ibn Hisham (~200 years after Muhammad) and Eusebius (~300 years after Jesus) are both evaluated as late but usable with appropriate caution.

**SEVERITY:** HIGH

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument that dismisses Eusebius for lateness while accepting Ibn Hisham must justify the distinction with source-critical reasoning.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether multiple Christian scholars citing the same manuscript/edition are counted as independent.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether multiple Islamic scholars citing the same tafsir/hadith collection are counted as independent.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether multiple Jewish scholars citing the same midrash are counted as independent.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether the skeptic detects scholarly dependency.

**RESULT:** PASS — Scholarly independence is evaluated by checking whether scholars use independent primary sources or all depend on the same edition/manuscript/translation.

**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument claiming "three scholars independently prove X" must verify that the three scholars do not all depend on the same underlying source.

---

### DS-19

**DOUBLE_STANDARD:** Same manuscript counted multiple times.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether the same NT manuscript is counted under different library signatures as multiple witnesses.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether the same Qur'an manuscript is counted under different catalog entries as multiple witnesses.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether the same DSS fragment is counted multiple times.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether the skeptic detects manuscript duplication.

**RESULT:** PASS — The system uses STEP 148's manuscript-separation audit: each manuscript is counted once, regardless of how many catalog entries or citations reference it.

**SEVERITY:** HIGH

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument claiming "N manuscripts attest X" must verify that the N manuscripts are physically and genealogically distinct.

---

### DS-20

**DOUBLE_STANDARD:** Opposing evidence held to higher evidential threshold.

**CHRISTIAN_TEST:** Check whether evidence opposing Christianity is held to a higher threshold than evidence supporting it.

**ISLAMIC_TEST:** Check whether evidence opposing Islam is held to a higher threshold than evidence supporting it.

**JEWISH_TEST:** Check whether evidence opposing Judaism is held to a higher threshold.

**SKEPTICAL_TEST:** Check whether evidence opposing skepticism is held to a higher threshold.

**RESULT:** PASS — The same evidential threshold is applied to supporting and opposing evidence. The system does not raise the bar for inconvenient evidence.

**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL

**REMEDIATION:** Any argument that applies a higher evidential threshold to opposing evidence must justify the asymmetry with methodological reasoning.

## 9. TEXTUAL SYMMETRY AUDIT

### 9.1 Three texts compared

| Category | Hebrew Bible (HB) | New Testament (NT) | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composition date | ~12th–2nd century BCE (traditional); ~7th–2nd BCE (critical) | ~50–120 CE | ~610–632 CE |
| Earliest witnesses | DSS (~3rd century BCE) | P52 (~125–175 CE), P46, P66, P67, P75 | Sana'a palimpsest (~7th century), Ṣanʿāʾ 01, Birmingham folio (~7th century) |
| Manuscript diversity | MT, SP, LXX, DSS variants | Alexandrian, Western, Byzantine text-types | Regional readings (Ahruf/Qira'at), Sana'a variant readings |
| Textual variants | Significant (DSS vs MT differences, MT vs LXX) | Significant (~400,000 variants across ~6,000 MSS); ~99% are trivial | Regional variant readings (qira'at); Sana'a lower text variants; scope debated |
| Critical editions | BHS/BHQ (MT-based); eclectic editions | NA28/UBS5 (eclectic); Textus Receptus (Byzantine) | Cairo edition (1924); no critical edition in Western sense |
| Translation history | LXX (3rd century BCE); Vulgate; many modern | Many versions from 2nd century onward | Multiple early translations; many modern |
| Canon formation | Progressive (Torah → Prophets → Writings); finalized ~1st–2nd century CE | Progressive (4 gospels, Pauline corpus, catholic epistles, Revelation); ~2nd–4th century | The Qur'an as a text was compiled early (Uthmanic recension ~650s); the *canon* of qira'at developed later |
| Oral/written transmission | Oral-to-written for Torah tradition; scribal transmission | Oral tradition → written gospels; scribal transmission | Oral recitation → written compilation; scribal transmission |
| Scribal transmission | Masoretic scribes (meticulous, ~6th–10th century CE); earlier scribes varied | Christian scribes (varied quality, some trained, some not) | Qur'an copyists (trained, but early copies show orthographic variation) |
| Editorial reconstruction | DSS show textual plurality; MT is one textual tradition | Eclectic reconstruction from thousands of MSS; no single MS is the "original" | Uthmanic recension is the dominant textual tradition; Sana'a suggests pre-Uthmanic variation |
| Surviving manuscripts | ~3,000+ HB MSS (including DSS fragments) | ~6,000+ Greek MSS + 10,000+ versions | ~20+ early Qur'an MSS/palimpsests; thousands of later copies |
| Earliest recoverable text | DSS period (~3rd century BCE) for portions; MT (~7th–10th century CE) for complete | ~2nd century CE for fragments; ~4th century for complete NT | ~7th century for fragments; ~8th century for complete Qur'an |

### 9.2 Forbidden reductions

The system explicitly forbids:

| Forbidden equation | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| "number of manuscripts" = preservation quality | Quantity ≠ genealogical independence; 6,000 NT MSS may derive from fewer text-types |
| "number of variants" = corruption | Most variants are trivial (spelling, word order); significance matters, not count |
| variant = corruption | A variant may be a legitimate alternative reading, not an error |
| canon = text | Canon formation is about which books; textual criticism is about the wording within books |
| critical edition = autograph | A critical edition is a reconstruction, not the original |
| oldest manuscript = original | The oldest MS may contain a corrupt reading |
| majority reading = original | The majority may preserve a later Byzantine reading |
| traditional reading = earliest reading | Tradition may preserve a harmonized or standardized reading |

### 9.3 Textual symmetry rule

The same categories — composition, witnesses, variants, editions, canon, transmission, reconstruction — are applied to all three texts. No text receives preferential treatment in any category.

### 10.2 Historical symmetry principle

"Do not require identical quantity of evidence where historical circumstances naturally differ."

"Symmetry means **equal standards**, NOT artificially equal evidence."

The 1st-century Roman world produced more written records than 7th-century Arabia. This does NOT mean Islamic evidence is inherently weaker — it means the historical circumstances differ. The *rules* for evaluating evidence are the same; the *quantity* naturally varies.

### 10.3 Historical vs theological

The system maintains:
- HISTORICAL EVENT ≠ THEOLOGICAL TRUTH
- HISTORICAL ATTESTATION ≠ THEOLOGICAL AUTHENTICATION

A historical claim ("Muhammad lived in 7th-century Arabia") is assessed differently from a theological claim ("Muhammad is a prophet of God").

## 12. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE AUDIT

### 12.1 Using STEP 148 methodology

The source independence audit applies the STEP 148 graph methodology:

| Independence dimension | What is checked |
|---|---|
| Physical independence | Are the sources physically distinct (different manuscripts, different objects)? |
| Editorial independence | Were they edited/compiled by different editors? |
| Textual independence | Do they derive from different textual traditions? |
| Genealogical independence | Do they share a common ancestor in the transmission chain? |
| Argumentative independence | Do they make independent arguments, or repeat the same argument? |

| Pattern | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| INDEPENDENT | Sources are physically, textually, and genealogically distinct |
| PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT | Sources share some dependency but contain independent material |
| DEPENDENT | One source is derived from another |
| UNKNOWN | Dependency relationship cannot be determined |

## 15. FALLACY DATABASE CROSS-AUDIT (STEP 147)

### 15.1 Consistency test

The fallacy cross-audit tests whether fallacy labels are applied **consistently** across traditions.

### 15.2 Critical distinctions

| Distinction | Rule |
|---|---|
| DISAGREEMENT ≠ FALLACY | A theological disagreement is not a logical error |
| WEAK EVIDENCE ≠ FALLACY (automatically) | Insufficient evidence is not a fallacy unless it is presented as sufficient |
| UNSUPPORTED CLAIM ≠ FORMAL FALLACY (automatically) | An unsupported claim is unsupported, not necessarily a formal fallacy |
| THEOLOGICAL DISAGREEMENT ≠ LOGICAL ERROR (automatically) | Rejecting another tradition's theology is not a logical error |

### 15.3 When to classify as fallacy

Only classify as fallacy when the **reasoning structure** or **argumentative method** warrants it — not when the conclusion is theologically disagreeable.

Example: "The Qur'an denies the crucifixion; therefore, Islam is false" is a theological disagreement, not automatically a fallacy. But "The Qur'an denies the crucifixion; therefore, the crucifixion did not happen" commits argument-from-authority if no historical evidence is cited.

## 17. CIRCULARITY AUDIT

### 17.1 Circularity patterns detected

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Bible → Christian doctrine → Bible | The doctrine is read back into the text it claims to derive from | "John 1:1 teaches Trinity" → "Trinity is true" → "Therefore John 1:1 is correctly interpreted" |
| Qur'an → Tafsir → Qur'an | The tafsir is read back into the Qur'an it claims to interpret | "Qur'an 4:34 means 'beat'" (from tafsir) → "Therefore the Qur'an says 'beat'" → "Therefore the tafsir is correct" |
| Hadith → theology → hadith | A theological doctrine is supported by a hadith, and the hadith's authority depends on the theology | "Imamah is proven by hadith" → "Hadith are authoritative because of divine guidance" → "Imamah is proven" |
| Creed → interpretation → creed | A creedal formulation determines interpretation, and the interpretation supports the creed | "Chalcedon says two natures" → "Therefore Christ has two natures" → "Therefore Chalcedon is correct" |
| Patristic author → later tradition → same patristic author | A later tradition cites a Father, and the Father's authority is established by the later tradition | "Augustine says X" (via later medieval tradition) → "The medieval tradition is reliable because it cites Augustine" → "Augustine says X" |
| Modern apologetic source → another apologetic source → original apologetic claim | Apologetic sources cite each other without returning to primary evidence | "Source A claims X" → "Source B confirms A" → "Source C confirms B" → but A, B, C all lack independent primary evidence |

### 17.2 Circularity rule

Circularity does not automatically make a tradition false. It means the graph cannot be presented as independent external corroboration. A circular chain may still preserve genuine tradition, but it cannot be counted as independent confirmation.

### 17.3 Circularity vs tradition

| Legitimate | Illegitimate |
|---|---|
| "The Qur'an says X, and the tafsir explains X" (descriptive) | "The Qur'an says X (per tafsir), and the tafsir is correct because it agrees with the Qur'an" (circular proof) |
| "Augustine taught X" (historical) | "Augustine taught X, and X is true because Augustine taught it" (circular authority) |

### Q149-CT01

**ID:** Q149-CT01
**CLAIM:** The Trinity is the true nature of God.
**DOMAIN:** God / Tawhid vs Trinity (Domain 1–2)
**TRADITIONS_COMPARED:** Christianity (Trinitarian), Islam (Tawhid), Judaism (monotheism), Skeptical (metaphysical critique)
**METHODOLOGY:** Philosophical coherence + historical development + source-critical analysis of Biblical texts
**PRIMARY_EVIDENCE:** Biblical texts (John 1:1, Matthew 28:19, 2 Corinthians 13:14); Nicene Creed (325 CE); Chalcedonian Definition (451 CE); philosophical articulation (Augustine, Aquinas, Cappadocians)
**SOURCE_INDEPENDENCE:** Biblical sources: PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT (Matthew/Luke depend on Mark for gospel narrative; John is more independent; Paul is independent of gospels). Creeds: DEPENDENT on Biblical interpretation. Philosophical articulation: DEPENDENT on creedal framework.
**COUNTERARGUMENT:** Tawhid (Islamic): God is absolutely one, without persons or divisions. The Trinity introduces complexity into the divine essence. The Shema (Deut 6:4) affirms absolute oneness. The Trinity is a later philosophical development not present in the earliest Christian texts.
**STEELMAN:** The strongest Trinitarian formulation: God is one *being* (ousia) in three *persons* (hypostases). This is not tritheism because the three share one divine essence. The Cappadocian formulation distinguishes ousia from hypostasis, solving the "one and three" tension. The relational ontology is philosophically coherent: personhood is relational, and a God who is love (1 John 4:8) requires relational plurality.

**STEELMAN:** The strongest formulation: Absence of evidence IS evidence of absence when: (1) the event is the type that should leave evidence, (2) we have looked in the right places, (3) the evidence has not been destroyed. The more we expect evidence and fail to find it, the stronger the inference to absence. For example, the absence of contemporary Roman records of the darkness at the crucifixion (an event that, if it occurred, would have been noted) is evidence against that event.
**DOUBLE_STANDARD_TEST:** DS-12 (argument from silence). RESULT: PASS — The absence-of-evidence principle is applied symmetrically to all traditions.
**FALLACY_RISK:** IF-09 (argument from ignorance) — if absence of evidence is treated as proof of absence without the expectation principle.
**CONFIDENCE:** PROBABLE (the principle is valid with qualifications) — The system applies the expectation principle uniformly.
**LIMITATION:** The expectation principle must be applied with caution — the absence of expected evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.
**AUDIT_RESULT:** PASS — The system applies the same absence-of-evidence logic to all traditions. Absence is weighted by expectation, not by tradition identity.

### Q149-CS01

**ID:** Q149-CS01
**CASE:** Trinity vs Tawhid
**QUESTION:** Which conception of God is more philosophically coherent and historically grounded?
**TRADITIONS:** Christianity (Trinitarian), Sunni Islam (Tawhid), Judaism (monotheism), Skeptical
**CHRISTIAN_VIEW:** God is one being (ousia) in three co-equal, co-eternal persons (hypostases): Father, Son, Holy Spirit. The Cappadocian formulation distinguishes ousia from hypostasis. The Trinity is rooted in the NT and developed through Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381).
**ISLAMIC_VIEW:** God is absolutely one (ahad), without partners, persons, or divisions. Tawhid is the core of Islamic theology. The Qur'an (112:1-4, 4:171, 5:73) explicitly rejects Trinity. The Trinity is shirk (associating partners with God).
**JEWISH_VIEW:** God is one (Deut 6:4). The Trinity violates Jewish monotheism. The Shema affirms absolute oneness. Any plurality in the Godhead is incompatible with Jewish theology.
**SKEPTICAL_VIEW:** Both Trinity and Tawhid are theological constructs not amenable to empirical verification. The philosophical coherence of both is debated. The problem of divine attributes (simplicity vs. attributes) affects both.
**PRIMARY_EVIDENCE:** Biblical texts (John 1:1, Matt 28:19, 2 Cor 13:14); Qur'an (112, 4:171, 5:73); philosophical articulations (Augustine, Aquinas, Ash'ari, Maturidi)
**SOURCE_INDEPENDENCE:** Biblical: PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT. Qur'anic: primary. Philosophical: DEPENDENT on theological framework.
**CROSS_ANALYSIS:** The system applies the same three levels (descriptive, philosophical, theological) to both. Descriptively: Christianity teaches Trinity, Islam teaches Tawhid. Philosophically: both face internal debates (Trinity: social vs. relational vs. modal; Tawhid: divine attributes problem, simplicity vs. attributes). Theologically: each tradition affirms its own position. The system does not declare one "proven" by philosophical argument alone. The double-standard test (DS-10) verifies that doctrinal development is treated symmetrically.
**RESIDUAL_ISSUE:** The philosophical coherence of Trinity (three persons, one being) vs. Tawhid (divine simplicity vs. attributes) remains genuinely disputed. No side has achieved philosophical consensus.
**CONFIDENCE:** DISPUTED
**LIMITATION:** Philosophical coherence does not establish theological truth; historical development does not settle validity.

The Jewish model (teshuvah) is consistent with the Hebrew Bible's emphasis on repentance. The system does not declare one model "proven."
**RESIDUAL_ISSUE:** The coherence of penal substitution (punishing the innocent for the guilty) and the Islamic alternative (how does God forgive without atonement?) both face philosophical questions.
**CONFIDENCE:** DISPUTED
**LIMITATION:** Soteriology is theological, not historical.

### SM-01

**ID:** SM-01
**PERSPECTIVE:** Strongest Christian apologetic use of the evidence graph
**FORMULATION:** The Christian apologetic position can be formulated as a cumulative case: (1) The NT is well-attested textually (~6,000 MSS, early papyri). (2) The historical Jesus claimed unique authority, was crucified, and was believed by his earliest followers to be risen. (3) The resurrection is the best explanation of the minimal facts (crucifixion, empty tomb, appearances, transformation of disciples, early belief). (4) The Trinity developed organically from NT seeds. (5) The incarnation is philosophically coherent (two-natures Christology). (6) The spread of Christianity under adverse conditions supports its truth. (7) Fulfilled prophecy supports divine orchestration. This cumulative case makes Christianity more probable than not.

**WHAT IT EXPLAINS WELL:** The rise of early Christianity, the transformation of the disciples, the historical Jesus' impact, the textual attestation of the NT, the coherence of Trinitarian theology.
**WHAT IT STRUGGLES WITH:** The problem of evil, the silence of external sources for the resurrection, the lateness of some NT books, the development of Trinity over 300 years, the variant readings in the NT, the apparent absence of clear Trinitarian formulae in the Synoptics.
**WHAT EVIDENCE IT RELIES ON:** NT texts, early Christian creeds, patristic testimony, manuscript evidence, historical Jesus research, philosophical arguments.
**WHAT ASSUMPTIONS IT REQUIRES:** (1) The resurrection is a historical event, (2) the NT is a reliable witness, (3) the Trinity is coherent, (4) doctrinal development can be organic, (5) the "best explanation" inference is valid for supernatural events.

**GRAPH INTERPRETATION:** The same evidence graph (NT MSS, early creeds, patristic sources) can be interpreted as: (a) evidence for divine revelation (Christian apologetic reading), or (b) evidence of a developing theological movement (skeptical reading). **GRAPH INTERPRETATION:** The same evidence graph (Qur'an MSS, sira/hadith, non-Muslim sources) can be interpreted as: (a) evidence for divine revelation (Islamic apologetic reading), or (b) evidence of a 7th-century religious movement (skeptical reading). ### SM-05

**ID:** SM-05
**PERSPECTIVE:** Strongest maximalist preservation / anti-preservation argument
**FORMULATION:** This perspective focuses on the textual preservation question from both sides:

**Maximalist preservation (Islamic):** The Qur'an's preservation is unique among sacred texts because: (1) mass oral transmission (tawatur) from the first generation, (2) immediate written recording, (3) Uthmanic standardization within ~20 years of Muhammad's death, (4) the convergence of qira'at on a single rasm. No other sacred text has this combination. This establishes the Qur'an as the preserved word of God.

**Anti-preservation (skeptical):** No sacred text has been perfectly preserved: (1) The Qur'an has the Sana'a palimpsest, regional readings, and destroyed variant codices. (2) The NT has ~400,000 variants. (3) The HB shows DSS-MT differences. (4) All texts show scribal activity, editorial processes, and transmission history. The claim of perfect preservation is theological, not manuscript-attested. If God intended perfect preservation, the evidence would show zero variation — but it does not.

**WHAT IT EXPLAINS WELL:** The maximalist explains the remarkable stability of the Uthmanic text-type. The anti-preservationist explains the presence of variants in all traditions.
**WHAT IT STRUGGLES WITH:** The maximalist struggles with the Sana'a lower text and the variant codex reports. The anti-preservationist struggles with the degree of stability shown in early MSS.
**WHAT EVIDENCE IT RELIES ON:** Manuscript evidence (Sana'a, Birmingham, early NT papyri, DSS), hadith on collection, textual criticism.
**WHAT ASSUMPTIONS IT REQUIRES:** Maximalist: tawatur guarantees exact preservation; Uthmanic text = original recitation. Anti-preservationist: any variation disproves perfect preservation; theological claims must be textually verified.

**GRAPH INTERPRETATION:** The same evidence graph (all MSS, all traditions) can be interpreted as: (a) evidence for Qur'anic uniqueness (maximalist reading), or (b) evidence that no text is perfectly preserved (anti-preservationist reading). The difference is not in the evidence nodes but in the inferential steps from "the MSS show stability + some variation" to "the text is perfectly preserved" (theological) vs. "the text has a transmission history" (historical).

## 27. ERROR REGISTER (E1–E20)

> Each error entry contains: `ERROR_ID | ERROR | DETECTION_METHOD | EXAMPLE | SEVERITY | REMEDIATION`

---

### E1

**ERROR_ID:** E1
**ERROR:** Confirmation bias
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether only favorable evidence is cited and unfavorable evidence is omitted or minimized, for any tradition.
**EXAMPLE:** An argument cites all evidence for the Qur'an's preservation while omitting the Sana'a palimpsest; or cites all evidence for the resurrection while omitting the absence of external attestation.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Require that both favorable and unfavorable evidence be presented. The system must include the counterargument and steelman for every major claim.

---

### E2

**ERROR_ID:** E2
**ERROR:** Double standard
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Apply DS-01–DS-20 tests. Check if the same methodological rule is applied differently to different traditions.
**EXAMPLE:** Accepting patristic testimony as historical while dismissing hadith as unreliable, without source-critical justification for the asymmetry.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the same source-critical, manuscript-critical, and historical-critical standards to all traditions. Justify any asymmetry with methodological reasoning.

---

### E3

**ERROR_ID:** E3
**ERROR:** Source laundering
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Trace each cited source back to its primary origin. Check whether multiple "sources" trace back to the same underlying source.
**EXAMPLE:** Citing "multiple websites" or "multiple scholars" that all reproduce the same edition or argument as independent confirmation.
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Verify source independence via STEP 148 methodology (physical, editorial, textual, genealogical, argumentative, chronological independence).

---

### E4

**ERROR_ID:** E4
**ERROR:** Citation laundering
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Trace citation chains. Check whether sources cite each other without returning to primary evidence.
**EXAMPLE:** Source A cites Source B, Source B cites Source C, Source C cites Source A — none citing primary evidence.
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Require that each claim trace back to a primary source (E1–E3 in the evidence classification). Flag circular citation chains.

---

### E5

**ERROR_ID:** E5
**ERROR:** Circular reasoning
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Identify claims where the conclusion is used as a premise. Check for: Bible → doctrine → Bible, Qur'an → tafsir → Qur'an, hadith → theology → hadith.
**EXAMPLE:** "The Qur'an is God's word because Muhammad is a prophet, and Muhammad is a prophet because the Qur'an says so."
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Identify the circular chain. Circular chains may preserve genuine tradition but cannot be counted as independent external corroboration.

---

### E6

**ERROR_ID:** E6
**ERROR:** Anachronism
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether later doctrinal categories, terminology, or concepts are projected into earlier sources.
**EXAMPLE:** Reading Chalcedonian two-natures Christology into Paul's letters; reading developed Ash'ari theology into the Qur'an; reading later rabbinic halakha into the biblical period.
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Distinguish between what the earliest sources actually say and what later tradition says they mean. Apply the anti-retrodiction rule (AE-26).

---

### E7

**ERROR_ID:** E7
**ERROR:** Category error
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether a historical claim is presented as a theological truth, or a theological claim is presented as a historical fact.
**EXAMPLE:** "The resurrection occurred" (historical claim) presented as "Jesus is God" (theological claim) without an explicit inference step. Or "The Qur'an says X" (textual claim) presented as "X is true" (theological claim).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Maintain the separation: FACT, INTERPRETATION, THEOLOGICAL CLAIM, HISTORICAL CLAIM, TEXTUAL CLAIM. Each claim must be classified correctly.

---

### E8

**ERROR_ID:** E8
**ERROR:** Translation bias
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether translation choices favor a particular theological position without acknowledging the range of possible renderings.
**EXAMPLE:** Translating John 1:1c as "the Word was God" (definite) vs "the Word was divine" (qualitative) without acknowledging the anarthrous predicate nominative. Translating Qur'an 4:34 as "beat" vs "separate" without acknowledging the lexical range.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Present the lexical range, syntactic analysis, and textual context. Acknowledge alternative translations and their theological implications.

---

### E9

**ERROR_ID:** E9
**ERROR:** Manuscript overclaim
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether manuscript evidence is cited as proving more than it can establish.
**EXAMPLE:** Claiming that ~6,000 NT MSS prove perfect preservation (they don't — they show a variant transmission history). Claiming that early Qur'an MSS prove perfect preservation (they show stability of the Uthmanic text-type but not necessarily the original recitation).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Manuscript evidence establishes what the MSS show — textual existence, stability or variation of a text-type. It does not establish perfect preservation or divine origin without additional theological premises.

---

### E10

**ERROR_ID:** E10
**ERROR:** Historical overclaim
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether historical evidence is cited as establishing more than the sources can support.
**EXAMPLE:** Claiming the resurrection is "historically proven" when the sources are internal, written decades later, and the event is supernatural. Claiming Muhammad's prophethood is "historically established" when it is a theological category.
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Historical method establishes what the sources attest — existence, basic events, early belief. It does not establish supernatural claims or theological conclusions.

---

### E11

**ERROR_ID:** E11
**ERROR:** Theological overclaim
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether theological reasoning is presented as historical or philosophical proof.
**EXAMPLE:** "The Trinity is true because the Bible says so" (theological, not historical). "Tawhid is true because the Qur'an is inimitable" (theological, not historically proven).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Separate theological reasoning (E8/E9) from historical evidence (E1–E4) and philosophical argumentation (E9). Do not present one as the other.

---

### E12

**ERROR_ID:** E12
**ERROR:** Argument overreach
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether the conclusion exceeds what the premises support.
**EXAMPLE:** From "early Christians believed in the resurrection" to "the resurrection occurred" (exceeds the premise). From "the Qur'an is well-preserved" to "the Qur'an is God's word" (exceeds the premise).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Mark each inferential step explicitly. If the conclusion requires an additional premise (e.g., "God raised Jesus"), state that premise.

---

### E13

**ERROR_ID:** E13
**ERROR:** False equivalence
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether two things are equated that are not actually equivalent.
**EXAMPLE:** Equating oral tawatur (mass communal memorization) with individual oral tradition (gospel oral tradition) without distinguishing the type. Equating NT manuscript quantity with Qur'an manuscript quantity without distinguishing genealogical relationships.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Distinguish the specific type, context, and controls of each tradition. Do not equate without justification.

---

### E14

**ERROR_ID:** E14
**ERROR:** False symmetry
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether symmetry is forced by weakening a strong argument or strengthening a weak one.
**EXAMPLE:** Weakening the resurrection evidence to match the i'jaz evidence; or strengthening the i'jaz evidence to match the resurrection evidence, simply to achieve apparent parity.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Symmetry means equal standards, not equal conclusions. If one position has stronger evidence, state that. Do not manufacture equivalence.

---

### E15

**ERROR_ID:** E15
**ERROR:** Strawman
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether the opposing position is represented at its weakest rather than its strongest.
**EXAMPLE:** Representing the Trinity as "three gods" (tritheism, not Trinitarianism). Representing Tawhid as "God is just one in number" (not capturing absolute uniqueness). Representing naturalism as "nothing exists" (not capturing the nuanced position).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Construct the steelman. Represent the opposing position at its strongest before critiquing it.

---

### E16

**ERROR_ID:** E16
**ERROR:** Weakman
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether a weak version of the opposing argument is selected for refutation while ignoring the strong version.
**EXAMPLE:** Selecting a poorly argued case for the Qur'an's preservation while ignoring the strongest tawatur argument. Selecting a poorly argued case for the resurrection while ignoring the minimal facts approach.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Address the strongest version of the opposing argument (steelman requirement).

---

### E17

**ERROR_ID:** E17
**ERROR:** Selective skepticism
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether skepticism is applied to opposing evidence but not to supporting evidence.
**EXAMPLE:** Being maximally skeptical of the hadith chain while accepting the patristic chain without equivalent scrutiny. Being skeptical of Biblical miracles while accepting Islamic miracles.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the same level of skepticism to supporting and opposing evidence (DS-20).

---

### E18

**ERROR_ID:** E18
**ERROR:** Selective credulity
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether favorable evidence is accepted with insufficient scrutiny while unfavorable evidence is met with maximum skepticism.
**EXAMPLE:** Accepting the i'jaz argument without independent aesthetic criteria while demanding extraordinary evidence for the resurrection.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the same evidential threshold to all claims regardless of whether they support or oppose one's position.

---

### E19

**ERROR_ID:** E19
**ERROR:** Absence-of-evidence fallacy
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether absence of evidence is treated as proof of absence without the expectation principle.
**EXAMPLE:** "There is no archaeological evidence for Muhammad's prophethood, therefore he was not a prophet" (absence of expected archaeological evidence for a theological claim is not meaningful). "There is no Roman record of the darkness at the crucifixion, therefore it did not occur" (this IS meaningful, because we would expect a record).
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the expectation principle: absence of evidence is evidence of absence only when we would expect evidence if the event occurred.

---

### E20

**ERROR_ID:** E20
**ERROR:** Evidence-of-absence fallacy
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether the presence of evidence for one thing is treated as evidence against another, without a logical connection.
**EXAMPLE:** "The NT has many manuscripts, therefore the Qur'an is not preserved" (manuscript quantity for one text says nothing about another). "The Qur'an denies the crucifixion, therefore Christianity is false" (a theological denial is not historical counter-evidence).
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Verify that the evidence cited is logically relevant to the conclusion drawn. Do not infer absence from irrelevant presence.
