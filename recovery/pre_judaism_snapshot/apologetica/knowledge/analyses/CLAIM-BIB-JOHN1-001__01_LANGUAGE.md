* **ANALYSIS_TYPE:** LINGUISTIC / MORPHOLOGICAL / SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS
* **QUESTION:** What do the morphology, syntax, and article usage of John 1:1 (specifically *Theos* in the final clause) indicate grammatically?
* **EVIDENCE_USED:** BibleHub Interlinear parsing data: *Theos* is parsed as N-NMS (Noun - Nominative Masculine Singular), anarthrous (lacking the definite article *ho*), functioning as a predicate nominative preceding the copulative verb *ēn* and the subject *ho Logos*.
* **ANALYSIS:** 
  1. *En archē ēn ho Logos*: Prepositional phrase with dative noun (*en archē*), imperfect active verb (*ēn*), articular subject (*ho Logos*).
  2. *Kai ho Logos ēn pros ton Theon*: Conjunction, articular subject (*ho Logos*), imperfect verb (*ēn*), preposition *pros* with accusative article and noun (*ton Theon*), establishing relational distinction between the Word and God.
  3. *Kai Theos ēn ho Logos*: Conjunction, anarthrous predicate nominative (*Theos*), imperfect verb (*ēn*), articular subject (*ho Logos*). According to Colwell's Rule and standard Greek grammar (e.g., Wallace, Smyth), anarthrous predicate nominatives preceding the verb are frequently qualitative in semantic focus (describing the nature or characteristics of the subject), though context dictates whether they are definite or qualitative.
* **COUNTERARGUMENT:** While some non-Trinitarian interpretations argue that anarthrous *Theos* translates indefinitely ("a god"), Greek grammarians note that anarthrous predicate nouns preceding the copulative verb are primarily qualitative (emphasizing divine essence/attribute) rather than indefinite, given the articular subject *ho Logos* and the preceding articular *ton Theon*.
* **LIMITATIONS:** Grammar alone describes syntactic structure and semantic range; it does not automatically resolve broader theological metaphysics without historical and contextual exegesis.
