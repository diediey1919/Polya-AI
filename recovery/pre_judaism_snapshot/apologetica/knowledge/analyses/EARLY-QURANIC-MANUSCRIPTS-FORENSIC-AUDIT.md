# EARLY QUR'ANIC MANUSCRIPTS FORENSIC AUDIT (STEP 102)

## Systematic Manuscript Overview — Physical Evidence, Dating, Textual Content, and Historical Implications

**Primary Question:** What can early Qur'anic manuscripts actually establish about the history and transmission of the Qur'anic text?

**Method:** Forensic manuscript analysis, codicology, palaeography, radiocarbon dating audit, textual comparison, and scholarly model comparison. Every conclusion built from physical evidence, not presupposition.

**Critical Distinctions Maintained Throughout:**
- PHYSICAL OBJECT ≠ TEXTUAL WITNESS ≠ ORIGINAL MANUSCRIPT ≠ AUTOGRAPH
- DATE OF COMPOSITION ≠ DATE OF TRANSMISSION ≠ DATE OF CANONIZATION
- DATE OF RASM STANDARDIZATION ≠ DATE OF READING TRADITION
- PARCHMENT DATE ≠ WRITING DATE
- INSCRIPTIONAL EVIDENCE ≠ MANUSCRIPT EVIDENCE
- TEXTUAL CONTINUITY ≠ PERFECT PRESERVATION
- TEXTUAL VARIATION ≠ CORRUPTION

**Scope Boundary:** This is the SYSTEMATIC OVERVIEW of early Qur'anic manuscripts. It does NOT perform the full specialist audit of any single manuscript. STEP 103 will audit the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest in depth. STEP 104 will audit the Birmingham radiocarbon dating in depth.

## A. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### What Early Qur'anic Manuscripts Establish

**ESTABLISHED:**
- Early Qur'anic manuscripts on parchment survive from the 7th–8th centuries CE.
- The Birmingham folios have radiocarbon dates as early as 568 CE (parchment).
- The Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest lower text has parchment radiocarbon date before 671 CE (95%).
- The Tübingen manuscript has parchment radiocarbon date 649–675 CE (95.4%).
- Early manuscripts substantially preserve a consonantal tradition compatible with the later standardized Qur'anic text.
- Textual variants exist in early manuscripts, ranging from orthographic to substantive.
- The Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest lower text represents a non-Uthmanic textual tradition.
- The Dome of the Rock inscriptions (692 CE) contain Qur'anic material.

**STRONG EVIDENCE:**
- Multiple independent physical manuscripts from the 7th–8th centuries survive.
- The Uthmanic rasm was substantially established by the late 7th century.
- Palaeographic dating generally converges with radiocarbon ranges, though not always precisely.
- The textual tradition shows substantial continuity from earliest surviving manuscripts to later standardized text.

**PROBABLE:**
- The Qur'anic text was in circulation in recognizable form by the mid-to-late 7th century.
- Multiple scribal hands contributed to early codex production.
- A degree of textual standardization occurred before the surviving manuscripts were produced.

**DISPUTED:**
- Whether early manuscripts represent independent textual traditions or descend from a common exemplar.
- The significance of textual variants in the lower text of the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest.
- Whether the radiocarbon ranges prove composition during Muhammad's lifetime.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:**
- That any surviving manuscript is an autograph (written by Muhammad or Uthman personally).
- That any surviving manuscript proves the exact original revelation text.
- That radiocarbon dating of parchment proves the date of writing.
- That all early manuscripts are textually identical.
- That all early manuscripts are textually different in substantive ways.

## D. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE

### D.1 Physical Independence

Every manuscript listed above is a **physically independent object**. They are stored in different repositories, on different parchment leaves, in different codices. No two are the same physical object.

### D.2 Textual Independence

**Physical independence does NOT automatically equal textual independence.**

Two manuscripts may descend from the same exemplar, in which case their agreement does not constitute two independent witnesses to the text. The genealogical relationships between early Qur'anic manuscripts are largely NOT ESTABLISHED.

## F. DATING FORENSICS

### F.1 Radiocarbon Dating Principles

**What radiocarbon dating measures:**
- The decay of Carbon-14 in organic material
- For parchment: the death of the animal whose skin was used
- NOT the date of writing, inscription, or text composition

**What radiocarbon dating does NOT measure:**
- Date of ink application
- Exact writing event
- Date of text composition
- Date of codex production
- Date of recitation tradition

**Critical Issues:**
- **Old-animal problem:** The animal may have lived for years before slaughter
- **Storage problem:** Parchment may have been stored for years/decades before use
- **Reuse problem:** Parchment may have been recycled (especially in palimpsests)
- **Reservoir effects:** Possible but not well-documented for Qur'anic parchments

### F.4 Critical Dating Conflation Audit

**CONFLATION 1:** "Parchment dates to X" → "Qur'an was written in year X"
- **Status:** UNSAFE. Parchment date ≠ writing date.

**CONFLATION 2:** "Birmingham C14 range 568–645" → "Muhammad-era manuscript"
- **Status:** UNSAFE. The range includes pre-Muhammad dates; the upper bound extends 13 years after Muhammad's death. Palaeography suggests late 7th century.

**CONFLATION 3:** "Ṣanʿāʾ lower text parchment before 671" → "Lower text written before 671"
- **Status:** NOT ESTABLISHED. The parchment was produced before 671 (95%); the writing could have occurred later.

**CONFLATION 4:** "Radiocarbon date = exact date"
- **Status:** UNSAFE. Radiocarbon provides probability ranges, not exact dates.

## G. PALAEOGRAPHY

### G.1 Hijazi Script

**Definition:** An early Arabic script style characterized by:
- Angular letter forms
- Elongated horizontal strokes
- Rightward slant with vertical emphasis
- Thin, slender lines
- Absence of systematic diacritical marks

**Key Features:**
- **Ductus:** Generally flowing with angular terminals
- **Letter Forms:** Distinctive shapes for alif, lam, 'ayn, kaf
- **Ligatures:** Variable connection patterns
- **Spacing:** Irregular; sometimes scriptio continua tendencies

**Variability:**
- Multiple sub-styles within "Hijazi"
- Different scribal hands show different characteristics
- Regional and temporal variations

### G.2 Early Kufic Script

**Definition:** A more formalized Arabic script, developing from Hijazi, characterized by:
- Angular, rigid letter forms
- Formal structure
- Greater regularity than Hijazi
- Used in later early Qur'anic manuscripts (8th–9th centuries)

**Key Features:**
- More geometric than Hijazi
- Open form of 'ayn
- Occasional confusion between kaf and ta'
- Elongation of horizontal strokes

### G.3 Diacritical Marks and Vowel Notation

**Early Manuscripts (7th century):**
- Minimal consonantal diacritics (i'jam)
- No systematic vocalization
- Short vowels largely absent
- Hamza notation largely absent

**Development:**
- Consonantal diacritics introduced gradually (Abū al-Aswad al-Duʾalī, c. 688 CE traditionally)
- Systematic vocalization developed 8th century onward
- Full pointing system not standardized until later

### G.4 Palaeographic Dating Limitations

**Palaeography is NOT mathematically exact.**

- Palaeographic dates are estimates based on comparison with dated exemplars
- Few securely dated early Arabic manuscripts exist for comparison
- Script development is gradual and varies by region, scribe, and context
- A palaeographic date of "late 7th century" may have a range of ±50 years or more
- Palaeographic dating cannot provide the same precision as radiocarbon dating (though both have limitations)

### G.5 Palaeographic vs Scientific Date

| Type | What It Dates | Precision | Limitation |
|------|--------------|-----------|------------|
| Radiocarbon | Organic material (parchment) | ±25–100 years | Dates animal death, not writing |
| Palaeography | Script style | ±50–100 years | Based on comparison; few dated exemplars |
| Codicology | Physical features | Variable | Dependent on comparative material |
| Ink Analysis | Ink composition | Variable | Limited data for early Qur'ans |
| Archaeological | Context | Variable | Few Qur'anic MSS from controlled excavations |

### H.1 Folio and Quire Structure

**Birmingham:** 2 bifolios (4 leaves); quire structure not determined from fragment.
**Ṣanʿāʾ:** ~80 folios; quire structure documented in scholarship.
**Tübingen:** ~77 folios; partial codex.
**Parisino-petropolitanus:** 70+ folios; quire structure documented by Déroche.
**Topkapi:** 408 folios; near-complete codex; quire structure documented.
**Samarkand:** 353 folios; fragmentary codex.

### H.2 Ruling and Page Layout

- Early manuscripts show evidence of ruling (guide lines for text)
- Layout varies: some manuscripts have more lines per page than others
- Text area dimensions vary significantly between manuscripts

### H.3 Parchment Preparation

- Parchment = treated animal skin (typically sheep, goat, or calf)
- Preparation involves soaking, scraping, stretching, drying
- Quality varies between manuscripts
- The Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest demonstrates parchment reuse (lower text erased, upper text written)

### H.4 Scribal Hands

**Parisino-petropolitanus:** 5 copyists identified (Déroche)
**Topkapi:** Evidence of different hands; folios 2r–6v and 11 possibly later additions (Halaseh 2024)
**Ṣanʿāʾ:** Multiple hands in lower text documented

### H.5 Corrections and Marginal Additions

- Corrections present in several manuscripts
- Erasures and overwriting documented
- Marginal additions in some manuscripts
- Replacement leaves in some codices

### H.6 Palimpsest Structure (Ṣanʿāʾ)

**CRITICAL:**
- **Upper text (scriptio superior):** Standard Qur'anic tradition (Hijazi script)
- **Lower text (scriptio inferior):** Non-standard Qur'anic tradition (Hijazi script)
- **Process:** Lower text was washed/scraped off; upper text written over
- **Both texts are Qur'anic** — this is NOT a case of non-Qur'anic text overwritten with Qur'an
- **The lower text is NOT automatically the earliest possible Qur'an** — it represents one textual tradition that was superseded
- **The upper text is NOT automatically a correction of errors** — it represents a different textual tradition

### I.2 Textual Variant Classification System

**CATEGORY 1: Orthographic / Spelling Variation**
- Differences in spelling that do not affect pronunciation or meaning
- Example: alif variations, ta marbuta representation
- Most common type in early manuscripts

**CATEGORY 2: Script / Notation Variation**
- Differences in letter forms, dotting, or notation
- Example: presence/absence of i'jam (consonantal dots)
- Reflects scribal practice, not textual difference

**CATEGORY 3: Reading Notation**
- Differences in reading marks (vowel dots, shadda, sukun)
- May affect pronunciation but not consonantal skeleton
- Related to qira'at traditions

**CATEGORY 4: Lexical Variation**
- Different word choices
- Example: Ṣanʿāʾ lower text Q 2:87 "wa-qaffaynā 'alā āthārihi" vs standard "wa-qaffaynā min ba'dihi"
- Potentially significant; must be evaluated case by case

**CATEGORY 5: Morphological / Grammatical Variation**
- Different grammatical forms
- Example: different verb conjugations or noun declensions
- May affect meaning

**CATEGORY 6: Word-Order Variation**
- Different ordering of words within a verse
- Documented in Ṣanʿāʾ lower text
- Potentially significant

**CATEGORY 7: Addition / Omission**
- Extra words or missing words
- Documented in Ṣanʿāʾ lower text and reported in companion codices
- Potentially significant

**CATEGORY 8: Substantive Textual Variation**
- Variations that change meaning
- Must be verified on a case-by-case basis
- Cannot be assumed from orthographic difference alone

### I.3 Key Textual Observations

**Birmingham:** Conforms closely to standard text; minor orthographic variants.

**Ṣanʿāʾ lower text:** Contains significant variants:
- Different surah ordering (does not follow standard sequence)
- Lexical substitutions (e.g., Q 2:87)
- Estimated 25× more variants than reported for Ibn Mas'ud codex
- Both upper and lower text are Qur'anic — NOT non-Qur'anic material

**Ṣanʿāʾ upper text:** Conforms closely to standard text; minor orthographic variants.

**Topkapi:** Conforms to standard rasm; some non-canonical variants documented (Halaseh 2024); some scribal errors.

**Parisino-petropolitanus:** Many "mere orthographic differences" (Déroche); some differences not explained by orthography alone.

**Samarkand:** Conforms to Uthmanic rasm tradition; no vocalization or diacritical marks.

### I.4 Variant Significance Assessment

**CRITICAL:** Do not call an orthographic difference a substantive textual variant.

Most variants in early manuscripts are Category 1 (orthographic) or Category 2 (notation). Substantive variants (Categories 4–8) exist but are less common and concentrated in the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text.

## J. RASM BOUNDARY

### J.1 Definitions

**Rasm (رسم):** The consonantal skeleton of the Qur'anic text — the bare letter forms without vowel marks, diacritical dots, or other orthographic aids. Literally "trace" or "outline."

**Orthography:** The system of writing, including spelling conventions, letter forms, and notation. Orthography includes but is broader than rasm.

**Reading (qirāʾah, pl. qirāʾāt):** A specific tradition of reciting the Qur'an, which interprets the rasm in a particular way through vocalization and sometimes consonantal differentiation.

**Qirāʾāt (قراءات):** The canonical reading traditions. Traditionally seven (Ibn Mujāhid, d. 936 CE), later expanded to ten.

### J.2 Critical Distinctions

**Rasm ≠ Complete Reading**
- The rasm is the consonantal skeleton
- A reading interprets the rasm through vocalization and consonantal differentiation
- Multiple readings can be compatible with the same rasm

**Different Spelling ≠ Different Revelation**
- Orthographic variations may reflect scribal conventions, not different source texts
- Spelling standardization developed over time

**Different Reading ≠ Corruption**
- The qirāʾāt tradition acknowledges multiple valid readings
- Variants within the qirāʾāt framework are considered legitimate

**Same Rasm ≠ Identical Historical Pronunciation**
- The bare rasm is ambiguous
- Different communities may have pronounced the same rasm differently
- Historical pronunciation cannot be recovered from rasm alone

### J.3 Rasm Uthmani

**Traditional Account:**
- Uthman commissioned a standardized rasm (c. 650 CE)
- This rasm was designed to be compatible with multiple dialectal readings (ahruf)
- The rasm was intentionally ambiguous in some places to preserve flexibility

**Manuscript Evidence:**
- Early manuscripts show substantial agreement in their consonantal skeleton
- Minor orthographic variations exist
- The rasm tradition appears to have been substantially established by the late 7th century
- Full standardization of orthographic details took longer

## K. MANUSCRIPT VS CRITICAL TEXT

### K.1 Six-Layer Distinction

| Layer | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| MANUSCRIPT READING | What is physically written on the parchment | The actual ink marks on Birmingham Mingana 1572a |
| TRANSCRIPTION | Scholar's reading of the manuscript | A diplomatic transcription of the manuscript |
| NORMALIZATION | Standardized representation of the text | Adding modern orthographic conventions |
| CRITICAL EDITION | Reconstructed text from multiple witnesses | Corpus Coranicum text |
| TRANSLATION | Rendering in another language | English translation |
| INTERPRETATION | Understanding of meaning | Theological exegesis |

### K.2 Critical Rules

- A modern normalized transcription must NOT be presented as the physical manuscript itself.
- If a manuscript is compared with a modern Qur'an, state exactly what is being compared.
- The "standard Qur'an" used for comparison is itself a product of a specific reading tradition (typically Hafs 'an 'Asim) and modern editorial conventions.
- Differences between a manuscript and the "standard Qur'an" may reflect:
  - Genuine textual variants
  - Different reading traditions
  - Different orthographic conventions
  - Editorial normalization in the modern text

## L. PHYSICAL VS TEXTUAL INDEPENDENCE

### L.1 Critical Distinction

**Physical Independence:** Two manuscripts are physically different objects (different parchment, different repository, different scribal production).

**Textual Independence:** Two manuscripts represent independent textual traditions (descended from different exemplars, not copied from each other or from a common source).

**CRITICAL RULE:** Physical independence does NOT automatically equal textual independence. Two physically separate manuscripts may descend from the same exemplar.

**Likewise:** Textual agreement does NOT automatically prove common copying. Two manuscripts may agree because they faithfully transmit the same tradition, not because one copied the other.

## N. BIRMINGHAM CASE STUDY

### N.1 Manuscript Identity
- **Name:** Birmingham Quran (Mingana Arabic 1572a)
- **Repository:** Cadbury Research Library, University of Birmingham
- **Type:** Fragment (2 bifolios, 4 leaves)
- **Content:** Surahs 18:17–31 and 19:91–20:40

### N.2 Dating Evidence
- **Radiocarbon:** 568–645 CE (95.4% confidence, Oxford)
- **Palaeography:** Late 7th century (Déroche, George)
- **Critical:** The radiocarbon range extends before Muhammad's birth (c. 570 CE) and 13 years after his death (632 CE). Palaeographic dating suggests a later date than the radiocarbon range midpoint.

### N.3 Textual Content
- Conforms closely to standard Uthmanic text
- Minor orthographic variants
- No substantive textual differences documented

### N.4 What Birmingham Establishes
- Parchment from the mid-6th to mid-7th century was used for a Qur'anic text
- The text on this parchment conforms to the standard tradition
- Early Qur'anic manuscripts existed in a form recognizable from the later standardized text

### N.5 What Birmingham Does NOT Establish
- That the text was written during Muhammad's lifetime (the C14 range includes pre-Muhammad dates)
- That this is the oldest Qur'an manuscript (other manuscripts have comparable or overlapping dates)
- That the parchment date equals the writing date
- That the standard text was the only text in circulation (cf. Ṣanʿāʾ lower text)

### N.6 Critical Assessment
The Birmingham manuscript is a genuine early Qur'anic fragment. Its radiocarbon date is significant but must be interpreted with appropriate caution: the parchment date is not the writing date, and the wide range spans both pre-Islamic and early Islamic periods.

## O. ṢANʿĀʾ CASE STUDY BOUNDARY

### O.1 Manuscript Identity
- **Name:** Ṣanʿāʾ Palimpsest (DAM 01-27.1)
- **Repository:** Dār al-Makhṭūṭāt, Sana'a, Yemen
- **Type:** Palimpsest (~80 folios)
- **Content:** Both layers Qur'anic

### O.2 Palimpsest Concept
- A palimpsest is a manuscript where the original text was erased and overwritten
- The lower text (scriptio inferior) is the original
- The upper text (scriptio superior) is the overwriting
- Both can be recovered through imaging techniques (UV, multispectral)

### O.3 Upper / Lower Text Distinction
- **Lower text:** Non-standard Qur'anic tradition; different surah ordering; significant textual variants
- **Upper text:** Standard Qur'anic tradition; conforms closely to Uthmanic rasm
- **Both are Qur'anic** — neither layer contains non-Qur'anic material

### O.4 Broad Dating
- **Lower text parchment:** 578–669 CE (95% radiocarbon)
- **Lower text writing:** NOT ESTABLISHED precisely; compatible with mid-7th century
- **Upper text:** Late 7th–early 8th century (palaeographic)

### O.5 Why It Matters
- The only known extant copy from a textual tradition beside the standard Uthmanic one
- Demonstrates that non-Uthmanic textual traditions existed in the early period
- Shows that the Qur'anic text was not completely uniform before standardization
- Provides the most important physical evidence for the existence of variant Qur'anic traditions

### O.6 Boundary Note
**The complete Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest forensic audit is reserved for STEP 103.** This section establishes only the identity, palimpsest concept, dating, and significance.

## R. DOME OF THE ROCK INSCRIPTION BOUNDARY

### R.1 Inscriptional Evidence vs Manuscript Evidence

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION:**

The Dome of the Rock inscriptions are **monumental inscriptions**, not **manuscript witnesses**. They serve a different function:

| Feature | Inscription | Manuscript |
|---------|------------|------------|
| Function | Public, monumental, theological/political | Private/liturgical, scriptural |
| Context | Building decoration | Codex production |
| Completeness | Selective quotations | Attempt at complete text |
| Material | Stone/mosaic | Parchment/papyrus |
| Dating | Internally dated (692 CE) | Requires scientific/palaeographic dating |

### R.2 What the Inscriptions Establish
- Qur'anic material was in circulation by 692 CE
- Key theological passages (Q 112, Q 19, Q 3, Q 4) were considered authoritative
- The text was sufficiently stable to be inscribed in a public monument
- Some textual variants exist even in the inscriptional record

### R.3 What the Inscriptions Do NOT Establish
- The complete text of the Qur'an existed by 692 CE (inscriptions are selective)
- The date of Qur'anic composition (inscriptions show circulation, not origin)
- The relationship to any specific manuscript tradition

### R.4 Variants in the Inscriptions
- Some passages show minor variations from the standard Qur'anic text
- These variants demonstrate that even by 692 CE, the text was not letter-for-letter identical in all contexts
- This does NOT prove corruption; it proves that textual variation existed

## V. MODERN SCHOLARLY MODELS

### MODEL A: Early Stabilization / Early Canonization

**Description:** The Qur'anic text was substantially fixed during or shortly after Muhammad's lifetime, and the Uthmanic standardization preserved an already stable text.

**Strongest Evidence:**
1. Early manuscripts (Birmingham, Tübingen) have radiocarbon dates overlapping with Muhammad's lifetime or shortly after.
2. The Dome of the Rock inscriptions (692 CE) contain recognizable Qur'anic text.
3. The upper text of the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest conforms to the standard tradition.
4. Multiple early manuscripts show substantial agreement in their consonantal skeleton.

**Weakest Point:**
1. The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text demonstrates that a significantly different textual tradition existed.
2. The radiocarbon ranges are wide and do not prove composition during Muhammad's lifetime.
3. Companion codex variants are reported in literary sources.
4. The destruction of alternative codices eliminated evidence.

**Manuscript Implications:** Early manuscripts should show near-uniformity.
**Chronological Assumptions:** Text fixed c. 632–650 CE.
**Source Assumptions:** Islamic traditional sources substantially reliable.
**Confidence:** MODERATE

### MODEL B: Gradual Stabilization

**Description:** The Qur'anic text was substantially established in the early period but underwent gradual refinement, standardization, and selection over several decades.

**Strongest Evidence:**
1. Early manuscripts show substantial agreement but also minor variants.
2. The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text shows a non-standard tradition that was eventually superseded.
3. The development of diacritical marks and vocalization over time.
4. The existence of multiple reading traditions (qira'at) compatible with the same rasm.

**Weakest Point:**
1. Difficult to determine exactly what was "gradual" vs what was "early."
2. The evidence is compatible with both early and late stabilization.
3. Limited number of early manuscripts for detailed comparison.

**Manuscript Implications:** Early manuscripts should show both agreement and variation.
**Chronological Assumptions:** Text substantially fixed c. 650–700 CE, with ongoing refinement.
**Source Assumptions:** Islamic sources partially reliable; require critical evaluation.
**Confidence:** MODERATE TO HIGH

### MODEL C: Later Standardization / Revisionist Models

**Description:** The Qur'anic text was not fully standardized until later (e.g., Abd al-Malik period, late 7th–early 8th century), and the traditional narrative is largely a later construction.

**Strongest Evidence:**
1. The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text shows significant textual variation.
2. Non-Muslim sources show no detailed awareness of the Qur'an until c. 730.
3. Islamic sources have a 200+ year gap from the events they describe.
4. The Dome of the Rock inscriptions (692 CE) show some variants from the standard text.

**Weakest Point:**
1. Early manuscripts (Birmingham, Tübingen) have radiocarbon dates that are difficult to reconcile with a very late standardization.
2. The Abd al-Malik theory (Shoemaker) is contradicted by the early radiocarbon dates.
3. The upper text of the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest conforms to the standard tradition.
4. Multiple independent manuscripts show substantial agreement.

**Manuscript Implications:** Early manuscripts should show significant variation and late convergence.
**Chronological Assumptions:** Text not fixed until c. 700+ CE.
**Source Assumptions:** Islamic sources largely unreliable for early period.
**Confidence:** LOW TO MODERATE (weakened by early radiocarbon dates)

### Model Comparison

| Feature | Model A (Early) | Model B (Gradual) | Model C (Late) |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Text fixed | c. 632–650 | c. 650–700 | c. 700+ |
| Uthmanic standardization | Historical fact | Partially historical | Later retrojection |
| Ṣanʿāʾ lower text | Anomalous copyist error | Evidence of early variation | Evidence of late standardization |
| Birmingham C14 | Supports early date | Compatible | Must be explained away |
| Dome of the Rock | Supports early circulation | Compatible | Compatible (shows early circulation, not composition) |
| Non-Muslim sources | Gap is irrelevant | Gap is expected | Gap is evidence |
| Islamic sources | Largely reliable | Partially reliable | Largely unreliable |

## X. STEELMAN TRADITIONAL ACCOUNT

### Steelman A: Traditional Sunni Preservation/Transmission Interpretation

**Strongest Manuscript Evidence:**

1. **Early circulation:** Multiple manuscripts from the 7th–8th centuries demonstrate that the Qur'anic text was in wide circulation very early. Birmingham (C14 568–645), Ṣanʿāʾ upper text, and Tübingen (C14 649–675) all point to early circulation.

2. **Substantial textual agreement:** The majority of early manuscripts (Birmingham, Tübingen, Ṣanʿāʾ upper text, Topkapi, Samarkand, Parisino-petropolitanus) conform substantially to the standard Uthmanic rasm. This is consistent with a successful standardization process.

3. **Dome of the Rock (692 CE):** Qur'anic text inscribed in a public monument within 60 years of Muhammad's death demonstrates that the text was considered authoritative and sufficiently stable for monumental use.

4. **Ṣanʿāʾ upper text conformity:** Even in Yemen, far from the centers of Islamic power, the upper text of the palimpsest conforms to the standard tradition, suggesting effective standardization.

5. **Absence of radically different codices:** Despite the traditional account of companion codices, no surviving manuscript presents a radically different Qur'an. The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text, while different, is still recognizably Qur'anic.

**Strongest Argument:**
The manuscript evidence is consistent with the traditional account: a text that was substantially fixed in the early period, standardized under Uthman, and transmitted with substantial fidelity. The early dates of surviving manuscripts, their broad agreement, and the Dome of the Rock inscription all support this model.

**Strongest Weakness:**
The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text demonstrates that a significantly different textual tradition existed. The traditional account does not fully explain this. No companion codex survives physically. The Islamic sources have a 200+ year gap.

## Y. STEELMAN CRITICAL RECONSTRUCTION

### Steelman B: Critical Manuscript-Historical Interpretation

**Strongest Manuscript Evidence:**

1. **Ṣanʿāʾ lower text:** Demonstrates that non-Uthmanic textual traditions existed, with different surah ordering, lexical variants, and word-order differences. This is physical evidence of textual plurality.

2. **Textual variants in all manuscripts:** Even manuscripts that conform to the standard tradition contain variants (orthographic, and in some cases beyond orthographic). "Perfect preservation" in a letter-for-letter sense is not demonstrated.

3. **Multiple scribal hands:** Parisino-petropolitanus (5 hands), Topkapi (multiple hands, later replacements). Manuscript production was not a single authoritative event but a distributed process.

4. **Dating tensions:** Radiocarbon and palaeographic dates do not always agree (Tübingen, Birmingham), creating uncertainty about exact chronology.

5. **Uthmanic attribution failures:** Neither Topkapi nor Samarkand can be demonstrated to be Uthman's personal manuscript. The traditional attributions are legendary.

**Strongest Argument:**
The manuscript evidence reveals a more complex textual history than the traditional account suggests. The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text is physical proof of textual plurality. Variants in all manuscripts show that the transmission was not perfectly uniform. The Uthmanic standardization was a selection process, not a neutral preservation.

**Strongest Weakness:**
The critical reconstruction cannot provide a complete alternative narrative. The early radiocarbon dates are difficult to reconcile with very late standardization models. The majority of manuscripts conform substantially to the standard tradition. The critical position may underestimate the effectiveness of early standardization.

### Common Ground
- The Qur'anic text existed in recognizable form by the late 7th century.
- A standardization process occurred.
- Early manuscripts show substantial agreement.
- Textual variation existed in the early period.
- No surviving manuscript is the autograph.

### Genuine Disagreement
- The significance of the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text (anomalous vs representative).
- Whether "substantial agreement" means "effective preservation" or "effective suppression of alternatives."
- The dating of the earliest manuscripts (how early is "early"?).
- The reliability of the traditional narrative.

### Unresolved Evidence
- The exact writing dates of the earliest manuscripts.
- The genealogical relationships between manuscripts.
- The full content of the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text (only partially reconstructed).
- The content of the destroyed companion codices.

## Z. APOLOGETIC CONTROLS

### Z.1 Symmetry Rules

**Apply the same rules used for Biblical manuscript analysis.**

| Rule | Biblical Application | Qur'anic Application |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Old manuscript ≠ proof of divine preservation | P52 (c. 125 CE) does not prove NT divine origin | Birmingham (C14 568–645) does not prove Qur'an divine origin |
| Variant manuscript ≠ proof of corruption | NT variants do not prove NT corruption | Ṣanʿāʾ lower text does not prove Qur'an corruption |
| Textual criticism addresses history, not theology | NT textual criticism ≠ attack on Christianity | Qur'anic textual criticism ≠ attack on Islam |
| Standardization ≠ divine action | NT canon formation ≠ proof of divine guidance | Uthmanic standardization ≠ proof of divine guidance |

### Z.2 Prohibited Moves

1. **Do NOT say:** "Old manuscript = proof of divine preservation."
2. **Do NOT say:** "Variant manuscript = proof of corruption."
3. **Do NOT use** Christian manuscript problems as proof of Qur'anic problems.
4. **Do NOT use** Qur'anic manuscript continuity as proof of Christianity being false.
5. **Manuscript evidence addresses textual history.** It does not by itself establish divine origin, prophethood, inspiration, or theological truth.

## AA. EVIDENCE GAPS

### AA.1 Critical Gaps

1. **No complete 7th-century Qur'an codex survives.** The earliest near-complete codex (Topkapi) dates to the 8th century.
2. **No companion codex survives physically.** We rely on literary reports for their content.
3. **Radiocarbon dates parchment, not writing.** The exact writing dates are NOT ESTABLISHED.
4. **Ink dating not available** for most early Qur'anic manuscripts.
5. **Genealogical relationships** between manuscripts are NOT ESTABLISHED.
6. **The Ṣanʿāʾ lower text** is only partially reconstructed.
7. **No contemporary written Islamic source** describes the standardization process in detail.
8. **Few securely dated early Arabic manuscripts** exist for palaeographic comparison.

### AA.2 Implications

These gaps mean:
- Many historical claims about early Qur'anic manuscripts are PROBABLE or DISPUTED, not ESTABLISHED.
- Neither the traditional account nor the critical reconstruction can be fully verified.
- Theological conclusions about preservation or corruption cannot be established from manuscript evidence alone.

## AC. FINAL SYNTHESIS

### What Do Early Qur'anic Manuscripts Establish, and What Do They Not Establish?

**ESTABLISHED:**
- Early Qur'anic manuscripts on parchment survive from the 7th–8th centuries CE.
- Multiple physically independent manuscripts exist from this period.
- These manuscripts substantially preserve a consonantal tradition compatible with the later standardized Qur'anic text.
- Textual variants exist in early manuscripts (orthographic and, in the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text, substantive).
- The Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest lower text represents a non-Uthmanic textual tradition.
- The Dome of the Rock inscriptions (692 CE) contain Qur'anic material.
- No surviving manuscript can be demonstrated to be an autograph.

**STRONG EVIDENCE:**
- The Uthmanic rasm was substantially established by the late 7th century.
- The Qur'anic text was in wide circulation by the late 7th century.
- Multiple scribal hands contributed to early codex production.
- Textual standardization was effective across a wide geographic area.

**PROBABLE:**
- The Qur'anic text was in circulation in recognizable form by the mid-to-late 7th century.
- A degree of textual standardization occurred before the surviving manuscripts were produced.
- The traditional narrative of Uthmanic standardization has a historical core.

**PLAUSIBLE:**
- Writing of Qur'anic material occurred during Muhammad's lifetime.
- The Abu Bakr collection narrative has a historical basis.
- Companion codices existed and differed from the Uthmanic standard.

**DISPUTED:**
- Whether early manuscripts represent independent textual traditions.
- The significance of the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text variants.
- The exact dating of the earliest manuscripts.
- Whether the Uthmanic text preserved all authentic revelation.

**UNCERTAIN:**
- The exact writing dates of the earliest manuscripts.
- The genealogical relationships between surviving manuscripts.
- The full content of destroyed companion codices.
- The relationship between surviving manuscripts and any hypothetical autograph.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:**
- That any surviving manuscript is an autograph.
- That radiocarbon dating proves composition during Muhammad's lifetime.
- That the Qur'anic text was perfectly preserved letter-for-letter.
- That the Qur'anic text was substantially corrupted.
- That all early manuscripts are identical.
- That all early manuscripts are fundamentally different.

### Critical Distinctions Maintained

**TEXTUAL CONTINUITY ≠ PERFECT PRESERVATION**
- The manuscripts show substantial continuity with the standard text.
- This is not the same as perfect, letter-for-letter preservation of an autograph.
- Variants exist; the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text shows significant variation.

**TEXTUAL VARIATION ≠ CORRUPTION**
- Variants exist in early manuscripts.
- This is not the same as "corruption" (a theological category implying deliberate falsification).
- Variants may reflect scribal practices, reading traditions, or genuine textual plurality.
