# HISTORICAL JESUS CHRISTOLOGY FORENSIC AUDIT

* **AUDIT_ID:** HISTORICAL-JESUS-CHRISTOLOGY-FORENSIC-AUDIT
* **STEP:** 92
* **TARGET:** Jesus → Earliest Movement → Paul → Gospels — baseline before later Christian doctrine
* **PERFORMED:** 2026-09-05
* **AUDITOR:** Polya Apologetica Knowledge System (Audit Module)
* **INTEGRATES:** STEP 89 (Second Temple Judaism / Historical Jesus / Earliest Christianity), STEP 90 (Pauline Christology), STEP 91 (Early Christology Cross-Corpus), John 1:1 language/exegesis/manuscript audits

> **PURPOSE:** Reconstruct — within the strict limits of the historical evidence stored in this system — what can actually be supported about Jesus of Nazareth: his identity, his reported claims, his relation to God, Torah, Kingdom, forgiveness, authority, miracles, prayer, crucifixion, resurrection expectation, and post-Easter interpretation. This audit neither proves nor disproves divinity; it separates TEXTUAL FACT → HISTORICAL INFERENCE → CHRISTOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION → DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT at every step.

---

## 0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Historical method, applied to the evidence stored in this system, establishes the following with descending confidence:

* **HISTORICALLY SECURE:** Jesus was a 1st-century Galilean Jew who was baptized in the context of John the Baptist, proclaimed the Kingdom of God, taught with prophetic authority within Judaism, was crucified by Roman authority under Pontius Pilate (principal stored non-Christian evidence: Tacitus, Annals 15.44), and whose followers very early proclaimed his resurrection (1 Cor 15:3–5, received tradition). Paul states explicitly that he *received* (παρέλαβον) and *passed on* (παρέδωκα) this tradition.
* **STRONGLY SUPPORTED:** Jesus taught in parables and aphorisms; associated with marginal figures; disputed Torah interpretation (Sabbath, purity, divorce); performed reported healings/exorcisms (claim-level, not supernatural proof); understood himself in relationship to God as Father; used "Son of Man" as a distinctive self-reference; and drew eschatological-expectation around his ministry.
* **DISPUTED:** Whether Jesus explicitly identified himself as Messiah during his lifetime; whether "Son of Man" was an eschatological figure-designation or an idiomatic self-reference; whether his forgiveness pronouncements implied an explicit claim of divine identity; the exact content of his self-understanding.
* **NOT ESTABLISHED (by stored evidence):** Any explicit pre-Easter "I am God" claim; any direct equation of Gospel "Son of God" with Nicene "God the Son" in the historical layers; high Christology as a demonstrable feature of Jesus's *lifetime* (as opposed to the post-Easter movement, where it is well attested within ~20 years).
* **BOUNDARY:** Historical method establishes that followers *believed and proclaimed* resurrection and divinity; it cannot by itself establish the occurrence of the resurrection as a supernatural event or Jesus's metaphysical ontology. Those are theological conclusions requiring additional premises.

**Crucifixion correction (STEP 89R integrated):** Josephus *Antiquities* XX.9.1 concerns the death of James, "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ," in a Jerusalem high-priestly context — it does **not** serve as independent confirmation of Jesus's own crucifixion. Tacitus *Annals* 15.44 is the principal stored non-Christian Roman evidence that "Christus" was executed under Pontius Pilate during Tiberius's reign. The Testimonium Flavianum (*Ant.* XVIII.3.3) is transmitted but its authenticity is DISPUTED (multiple scholarly models, stored as CLAIM-HIST-JOSEPH-003).

## 2. FOUR-LAYER MODEL

| Layer | Content | Examples in Store | Epistemic Function |
|-------|---------|-------------------|--------------------|
| **LAYER A** | Historical Jesus (c. 4 BCE–30/33 CE) | Reconstructed via Gospels critically, Tacitus 15.44, Paul's incidental data | What Jesus did/said as historical reconstruction; NOT raw Gospel transcript |
| **LAYER B** | Earliest Jesus Movement (c. 30–50 CE) | 1 Cor 15:3–5 received formula; Aramaic Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22); Phil 2:6–11 hymn (probable pre-Pauline); Gal 1–2 Jerusalem contacts | Earliest attested beliefs & devotion, before/independent of Gospel composition |
| **LAYER C** | Evangelists / Pauline interpretation (c. 50–110 CE) | Pauline letters (P46 witness); Mark, Matthew, Luke–Acts, John (P66/P75 witnesses); narrative & theological redaction | Interpretive presentation of Jesus; theology of the authors |

Failure to observe this boundary is the dominant source of anachronism in both popular apologetics and popular skepticism.

## 3. HISTORICAL METHOD — CRITERIA AND THEIR LIMITS

Criteria used (with explicit caveats):

1. **Multiple attestation:** independent streams (Paul; Mark; Q-material shared by Matthew/Luke; M/L special material; Johannine tradition). *Caveat: multiple attestation establishes breadth of tradition, not automatic historicity — a widely spread claim can still be false. It raises the bar for wholesale invention.*
2. **Contextual credibility:** fits 1st-century Galilean/Judean Judaism (stored basis: Josephus on sectarian plurality; CLAIM-HIST-JOSEPH-001).
3. **Embarrassment:** tradition that would embarrass the early church (e.g., Jesus's baptism by John; crucifixion as a criminal; disciples' failure and flight; Mark 13:32 ignorance of the day). *Caveat: embarrassment is a strong indicator but not proof; communities can preserve embarrassing material for other reasons.*
4. **Coherence:** sayings/events cohere with the overall reconstructed profile.
5. **Linguistic/contextual plausibility:** Aramaic background, Palestinian settings, Jewish idiom.
6. **Early attestation:** tradition attested within ~20–30 years of events (1 Cor 15:3–5; Phil 2 hymn; Maranatha).
7. **Independent attestation (only where demonstrable):** Tacitus (Roman, non-Christian); Paul (independent of Gospels); Mark vs Q vs John. *Caveat (from STEP 91 §23): different documents ≠ automatic genealogical independence; P66/P75/Sinaiticus/Vaticanus are physically distinct but genealogical independence is not assumed.*

**Do NOT claim:** "multiple attestation = automatically historical." Every criterion is cumulative, probabilistic, and defeasible.

## 4. JESUS AND SECOND TEMPLE JUDAISM

### 4.1 Context (stored basis)

Josephus (SRC-HIST-JOSEPH-001) provides evidence for 1st-century Judea: sectarian diversity (Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealot-adjacent movements), Roman administration, Temple-centered religion, messianic/apocalyptic currents. The Testimonium Flavianum (XVIII.3.3) is transmitted but DISPUTED; XX.9.1 attests "James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ" — early external (non-Christian Jewish) evidence of the movement's existence.

### 4.2 Jesus within Judaism — Audit

| Element | Evidence in Store | Assessment |
|---------|-------------------|------------|
| Jewish identity | Galilean Jew; Hebrew Bible assumes; Torah debate traditions; Temple participation reported | SUPPORTED (high confidence) |
| Jewish monotheism | One God of Israel; Jesus's reported prayers, Scripture citation | SUPPORTED (contextual) |
| Temple | Reported Temple action (Mark 11//John 2), Temple teaching; consistent with 2nd-Temple Judaism | SUPPORTED (multiple attestation at claim-level) |
| Torah / Sabbath / purity | Controversies reported (Mark 2–3; 7; 10) | SUPPORTED (claim-level; interpretation DISPUTED — see §12) |
| Messianism | Post-Easter title earliest; Gospel presentation later | DISPUTED at Layer A |
| Apocalypticism | Kingdom proclamation, eschatological sayings | SUPPORTED at Layer A (multiple independent streams) |
| Resurrection belief | Pharisaic-style resurrection expectation present in Second Temple Judaism | SUPPORTED (contextual; see §17) |
| Sectarian diversity | Josephus attests pluralism | SUPPORTED |

**Answer:** The historical Jesus is most plausibly understood within 1st-century Jewish society. **CONTROL:** "Therefore Jesus could not be divine" is NOT entailed — Jewish context constrains *language and categories*, not metaphysical possibilities. Vice versa, Jewish context does not prove divinity.

## 5. KINGDOM OF GOD

### 5.1 Audit

The Kingdom of God (βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ) is the most frequently attested theme of Jesus's preaching in the Synoptic tradition, appearing across multiple independent streams (Mark, Q, special material). It is present in the earliest Gospel layer; Pauline letters mention "kingdom" less centrally (Rom 14:17; 1 Cor 4:20; 6:9-10; 15:24,50) — a point of *emphasis change*, not contradiction (STEP 90).

| Dimension | Evidence | Assessment |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Proclamation of Kingdom as central | Synoptic multiple attestation | STRONGLY SUPPORTED (Layer A, high scholarly consensus) |
| Present dimension | "The kingdom of God has come near" (Mark 1:15); exorcism sayings (Luke 11:20//Matt 12:28, Q) | SUPPORTED |
| Future dimension | Kingdom prayers (Matt 6:10//Luke 11:2), kingdom parables, eschatological sayings | SUPPORTED |
| Repentance | Mark 1:15; parable tradition | SUPPORTED (claim-level) |
| Judgment | Parables; eschatological Son of Man sayings | SUPPORTED (claim-level) |
| Restoration of Israel | Twelve disciples (Mark 3:14), tribal restoration echoes | SUPPORTED (multiple attestation) |

**Classification: PROBABLE — "Jesus was an eschatological Kingdom-proclaimer" is among the most secure historical reconstructions in Jesus research.** *Caveat: theological reconstruction (e.g., "present/future tension," "inaugurated eschatology") is scholarly interpretation, not a direct quotation of Jesus. Exact wording of any Kingdom saying is NOT ESTABLISHED as verbatim ipsissima verba.*

### 6.2 Separation of Layers

- **Layer A:** Jesus of Nazareth, Galilean prophet/healer, was executed on a charge relating to kingship/messianic claims (Roman "King of the Jews" charge is historically PROBABLE).
- **Layer B:** Within a decade or two, believers confessed "Jesus is the Christ/Messiah" (title embedded in pre-Pauline formulae).
- **Layer C:** The evangelists narrate Jesus's *self*-identification as Messiah under conditions of secrecy and partial disclosure.
- **Layer D:** Messiah becomes one confessed title among a developing set (Son, Lord, Word, God) in conciliar theology.

**Do NOT claim** "Peter's confession proves Jesus claimed Messiahship" (Layer C → A requires argument); **do NOT claim** "Messianic secrecy proves Jesus never claimed it" (redactional reading).

## 7. SON OF MAN

### 7.1 Terminology

Greek: ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου (ho huios tou anthrōpou). Aramaic background: bar enosh / bar nasha ("son of man" = human being idiom). In the Gospels, "Son of Man" is overwhelmingly a self-reference of Jesus (approximating 80 occurrences in the Synoptics; John ~13). Daniel 7:13-14 presents "one like a son of man" receiving dominion — the standard intertext for eschatological Son of Man sayings.

### 7.2 Distinctions

| Sense | Evidence | Assessment |
|-------|----------|------------|
| 1. Generic "human being" | Ezekiel passim; Psalm 8:4; Hebrew/Aramaic idiom | ESTABLISHED (linguistic fact) |
| 2. Self-reference | Jesus uses "Son of Man" as surrogate first person | STRONGLY SUPPORTED (independent streams; **not** used by others speaking of Jesus in the Synoptics — meaning the idiom is distinctive) |
| 3. Eschatological figure | Mark 8:38; 13:26; 14:62 (coming with clouds — Daniel 7 echo); Q sayings (Luke 12:8//Matt 10:32-33) | SUPPORTED for the *sayings*; whether the *designation* was eschatological is DISPUTED |
| 4. Daniel 7 interpretation | Mark 14:62 explicitly joins Son of Man + power + clouds | PROBABLE intertext (LXX/MT Daniel traditions) |
| 5. Later Christian interpretation | Patristic/conciliar reading of Son of Man as divine-human title | Layer D; NOT automatically Layer A |

### 7.3 Classification

- "Son of Man = divine title" → **NOT ESTABLISHED** (the idiom's base meaning is human; divinity must be argued from context, not the phrase).
- "Son of Man = merely human title" → **NOT ESTABLISHED** (the phrase is never used merely generically of Jesus; eschatological contexts imply exalted authority).
- So-called "Son of Man problem": three-fold pattern (earthly authority Mark 2:10,28; suffering Mark 8:31; coming glory Mark 8:38, 13:26, 14:62) — a widely noted scholarly construction. **Confidence: the *sayings* are multiple-attested; the *pattern* is interpretation.**

**PASS / DISPUTED.**

## 8. SON OF GOD

### 8.1 Jewish Background

- **Psalm 2:7:** "You are my son; today I have begotten you" — royal adoption idiom for the (Davidic) king. Echoed at Jesus's baptism (Mark 1:11) and transfiguration (Mark 9:7).
- **Israel as son:** Exod 4:22; Hos 11:1 (quoted in Matt 2:15 — redactional).
- **Davidic sonship:** 2 Sam 7:14 ("I will be his father, and he shall be my son"); royal messianism.
- **Righteous/angelic sonship:** Wisdom literature; intertestamental texts (not stored as source records; noted as contextual background, LIMITED).

### 8.2 Gospel Usage

| Passage (Layer C) | Context | Reading |
|-------------------|---------|---------|
| Mark 1:11 / 9:7 | Baptism/Transfiguration voice | Ps 2:7 royal; possibly Isa 42:1 servant |
| Mark 3:11 / 5:7 (demons) | Acclamation by demons | Confession language; Mark redactionally uses it |
| Mark 14:61–62 | High-priest question "Are you the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?" | Messianic-Davidic frame; answer alludes to Ps 110:1 + Dan 7:13 |
| Matt 16:16 | Peter's confession | "Christ, the Son of the living God" |
| Matt 27:54 | Centurion: "Truly this was God's son" | Polyvalent: Roman idiom, divine-man concept, or confession |
| Luke 1:32,35 | Annunciation | Davidic throne + "Son of God" as conception |

### 8.3 Critical Distinction

- **"Son of God"** in Second Temple Jewish context = primarily **royal-messianic / covenantal** category (Psalm 2; 2 Sam 7), not a claim of metaphysical identity.
- **"God the Son"** = later Trinitarian formula (Layer D).
- Retrojecting "God the Son" onto Gospel "Son of God" is anachronism (STEP 91 §18 Trinity Control).
- Conversely, in *Paul*, "Son of God" carries divine-agency weight (Rom 1:4 "designated Son of God in power"; Gal 4:4–5; Rom 8:3 "sending his own Son") — Layer C Pauline development.

**Classification: PASS — "Son of God" demarcated as layered term (royal/Davidic → confessional → ontological).**

## 9. LORD / KYRIOS

### 9.1 Range of Meanings (Koine)

1. **Ordinary polite address / vocative:** "sir/master" — Kyrie (Matt 8:6; John 4:11 without theological intent).
2. **Social master:** owner of slaves/property (Mark 13:35 "master of the house"; parables — Matt 21:40; Luke 12:36-37).
3. **Teacher/rabbi surrogate:** disciples addressing Jesus (Mark 4:38; 9:17?).
4. **Messianic/Davidic title:** Ps 110:1 "The Lord says to my Lord" — used by Jesus in Mark 12:36-37 (explicating Messiah as Lord).
5. **Divine reference / YHWH substitution:** LXX renders YHWH as κύριος; Rom 10:9,13 applies Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5) "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord" to Jesus (STEP 90 §Rom 10:9-13) — the strongest stored case for κύριος-as-divine in Paul.
6. **Christian confession formula:** "Jesus is Lord" (1 Cor 12:3; Rom 10:9; Phil 2:11 confession "Jesus Christ is Lord").

### 9.2 Control

- Context determines meaning; **NOT every κύριος = YHWH** (e.g., parabolic masters, polite vocatives).
- **NOT every κύριος = merely human** (Rom 10:13; Phil 2:11 function within divine-identity texts).
- The YHWH-application texts (Rom 10:9–13; Phil 2:10–11) are Layer B/C Pauline; their application to *Jesus of Nazareth* as self-designation is **NOT ESTABLISHED** at Layer A.

**Classification: PASS — kyrios handled distributionally.**

## 12. FORGIVENESS OF SINS

### 12.1 Relevant Tradition

- Mark 2:1-12 (paralytic): "Son, your sins are forgiven" (2:5) → scribes: "Who can forgive sins but God alone?" (2:7) → healing as validation (2:10-11: "that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins").
- Luke 7:36-50 (sinful woman): "Your sins are forgiven" (7:48); "your faith has saved you; go in peace" (7:50).
- Eschatological forgiveness sayings: Lord's Prayer petition (Matt 6:12//Luke 11:4); Mark 11:25.

### 12.2 Textual Observation vs Theological Inference

| Level | Content | Status |
|-------|---------|--------|
| TEXTUAL OBSERVATION | The Synoptic tradition presents Jesus pronouncing forgiveness of sins directed at persons | SUPPORTED (multiple attestation: Mark 2; Luke 7; Q prayer tradition) |
| THEOLOGICAL INFERENCE (option A) | Pronouncing forgiveness presupposes divine authority (God alone forgives — Mark 2:7 scribal objection) | Possible; the objection is itself *within the narrative* — it shows the earliest tradents recognized the tension |
| THEOLOGICAL INFERENCE (option B) | Jesus exercises *delegated/authorized* agency (like a prophetic announcement: "the kingdom... comes near"; Isaiah servant; Nicene-era categories absent) | Possible; Jewish agency/prophetic announcement parallels exist |
| THEOLOGICAL INFERENCE (option C) | Forgiveness is *eschatological proclamation* of God's mercy anticipated in the Kingdom's arrival | Possible; coherent with Kingdom-centered preaching (STEP 92 §5) |

### 12.3 Controls

- "Forgiving sins proves Jesus is God" → **UNSAFE** (would collapse Mark 2 into a proof-text; ignores agency/prophetic parallels; STEP 91 §19 divine-identity rule).
- "Forgiving sins proves Jesus is only a prophet" → **UNSAFE** (would ignore that the tradition *itself* flags the divine prerogative; the storytellers recognized the claim's weight).
- Mark 2:10 attaches "authority on earth" to the **Son of Man** — the title whose semantics are DISPUTED (§7).

**Classification: PASS — the tradition claims sin-forgiveness authority; its *interpretation* is DISPUTED among the three options.**

### 13.2 Controls

- Historical method CAN establish: the *claims existed*, were *early*, and were *widespread*, and that Jesus was *reputed* a healer/exorcist.
- Historical method CANNOT establish: that supernatural causation occurred. The resurrection is the paradigm case (§17 §F).
- The Beelzebul retort (Mark 3:22-27//Matt 12:24-32//Luke 11:15-23, Q) is a strong *non-Christian-attested* datum that healings were not fabricated by the church — opponents operated within the claim-space.

**Classification: PASS.**

- Later worship of Jesus (Layer B/C, c. 30-60 CE, attested in Paul) is **NOT direct evidence** of Jesus's own self-understanding (Layer A). The leap "they worshipped him → he must have known/claimed it" is an inference, not a datum (STEP 91 §16).
- The leap "Jesus prayed to God → Jesus was merely a prophet" is equally an inference (see §10.2 double control).

**Classification: PASS.**

### 15.2 Christian-Stream Evidence (Layer B/C)

- 1 Cor 15:3 received formula: "Christ died for our sins... was buried" (earliest written attestation, c. 53–55 CE, received from earlier tradition).
- Gal 3:1: "Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified" — Paul's incidental datum.

- Phil 2:8: "became obedient to death, even death on a cross" (hymnic).

### 15.3 Historical Assessment

- Crucifixion: **ESTABLISHED** (Tacitus + Paul + Gospel tradition; embarrassment criterion — a crucified Messiah was a scandal, and the movement nonetheless proclaimed crucifixion as central).
- Pilate's involvement: PROBABLE (Tacitus; Gospels; Pilate's attested historicity as prefect).
- "King of the Jews" charge: PROBABLE (distinctiveness; embarrassment).
- Political/religious context: Jesus's Temple action and Kingdom preaching fit Roman/Judaean conflict scenario (Josephus context, CLAIM-HIST-JOSEPH-001).

**Classification: PASS — crucifixion is the single most historically secure event in Jesus's life after his existence itself.**

## 23. CLAIM TESTS (A–T)

### Claim A: "Jesus was a historical Jewish figure."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** Jesus's existence as a 1st-century Galilean Jew is the starting point of virtually all critical historical scholarship and is coherently supported by the stored evidence.

**Evidence:** Tacitus Annals 15.44 (Christus executed under Pilate, CLAIM-HIST-TAC-002); Josephus Ant. XX.9.1 (James, "brother of Jesus who was called Christ," CLAIM-HIST-JOSEPH-002); Paul's incidental references (Gal 1:19; 1 Cor 15:3-5; Rom 1:3 — "born of the seed of David"); Gospel tradition; contextual fit with Josephus's Judea (CLASHIST-JOSEPH-001).

**Reason:** Multiple independent streams (Roman, Jewish, Christian) converge on a historical figure; no serious scholarship denies existence.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH.

**Limitations:** No contemporary (30s CE) non-Christian source exists; all our witnesses postdate the events; "historical figure" ≠ anything about his divine/metaphysical status.

---

### Claim B: "Jesus proclaimed the Kingdom of God."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Kingdom of God is the best-attested theme of Jesus's preaching, crossing multiple independent streams.

**Evidence:** Mark 1:15; Q traditions (Matt 6:10//Luke 11:2; Matt 11:5//Luke 7:22); parables (Mark 4; Matt 13; Luke 15); Jesus's own reported contrast of his message with John the Baptist (Matt 11:11-13; Luke 16:16).

**Reason:** Multiple attestation + contextual credibility + coherent fit with early Jewish apocalyptic — the Kingdom theme is nearly undisputed in historical Jesus research.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Stored evidence for exact Kingdom-sayings wording is LIMITED BY STORED EVIDENCE (no dedicated Gospel source records for Q/Mark passages in this store; passages referenced via the audit chain, not primary source records). "Proclaimed the Kingdom" is historical; the *theological content* of the Kingdom (present/future, inaugurated eschatology) is interpretation.

---

### Claim C: "Jesus identified as Messiah."
**Classification:** DISPUTED

**Finding:** The earliest secure attestation of "Christ/Messiah" as a title for Jesus is post-Easter confession (Paul; 1 Cor 15:3; Rom 1:3-4). Whether Jesus himself made explicit messianic self-identification is disputed.

**Evidence:** Rom 1:3-4 (probable pre-Pauline formula: Davidic descent + "designated Son of God in power by resurrection"); Gospels present confessions by others (Mark 8:29) and trial exchange (Mark 14:61-62); messianic-secrecy motif (Mark 1:34; 8:30); inscription "King of the Jews" (Mark 15:26).

**Reason:** The title's earliest usage is confessional; the Gospels' portrayal of Jesus's own claims is redactionally shaped and complicated by secrecy; crucifixion charge suggests messianic implication but not self-identification.

**Confidence:** DISPUTED — both "Jesus self-identified discreetly" and "title first applied after Easter" have serious scholarly support.

**Limitations:** No non-confessional Layer-A datum directly reports Jesus's own messianic self-designation; Layer C narrative presentation cannot be read as transcript.

---

### Claim D: "Jesus clearly claimed to be God in explicit terms."
**Classification:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Finding:** No unambiguously explicit "I am God" claim by Jesus is established in the Synoptic layers.

**Evidence:** Synoptic consciousness texts (Matt 11:27; Mark 14:62) assert authority and roles, not the predicate "I am God"; explicit divine self-language ("I AM" sayings, John 8:58; 10:30) is Johannine (Layer C2).

**Reason:** The explicit predicate-claim belongs to the Johannine layer; historical method cannot certify Johannine speeches as verbatim Layer-A reports; Synoptic sayings are ambiguous.

**Confidence:** HIGH that explicit Layer-A claim is NOT ESTABLISHED.

**Limitations:** Absence of explicit claim ≠ absence of implicit self-understanding; John's testimony could preserve authentic tradition, but that is theological/historical argument, not established fact.

---

### Claim E: "Jesus claimed unique divine authority."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Jesus is reported to have exercised and claimed authority in ways associated (in the tradition) with divine prerogatives.

**Evidence:** Forgiveness of sins (Mark 2:5-10); temple action (Mark 11:15-19); Sabbath lordship (Mark 2:28); "but I say to you" antitheses (Matt 5:21-48); eschatological Son of Man judge sayings (Mark 8:38; 14:62).

**Reason:** Multiple attestation at claim-level; the tradition itself flags the tension (Mark 2:7 scribal objection: "Who can forgive sins but God alone?").

**Confidence:** HIGH that authority-claims are reported; DISPUTED whether they amount to *divine* authority as opposed to eschatological/prophetic agency.

**Limitations:** "Unique divine authority" is an interpretation of the reported praxis; the reporting of claims is not proof of their ontological ground.

---

### Claim F: "Jesus used Son of Man as an eschatological self-reference."
**Classification:** DISPUTED

**Finding:** Jesus's use of "Son of Man" as self-reference is strongly supported; the *eschatological-figure* content is disputed.

**Evidence:** Distinctive idiom (Gospels: Son of Man on Jesus's lips, rarely others); Daniel 7:13-14 background; eschatological sayings (Mark 8:38; 13:26; 14:62; Luke 12:8//Matt 10:32-33).

**Reason:** The self-referential use is independently attested; whether the phrase designated an eschatological figure (Daniel 7) or was an idiomatic self-reference later given eschatological content is the classic "Son of Man problem."

**Confidence:** HIGH for self-reference; DISPUTED for eschatological content.

**Limitations:** Aramaic background (bar enosh/bar nasha) is reconstructed, not directly attested; two of three Son of Man sayings-classes (suffering, glory) are debated as to authenticity.

---

### Claim G: "Son of God in the Gospels means God the Son."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** "Son of God" in the Gospels functions within royal-messianic/Jewish categories (Psalm 2; 2 Sam 7), not Nicene "God the Son."

**Evidence:** Ps 2:7 background (Mark 1:11; 9:7); Davidic-sonship framing (2 Sam 7:14; Matt 1; Luke 1:32-35); confession scenes (Mark 14:61-62; Matt 16:16).

**Reason:** The title's semantic range in the Gospels is primarily royal-messianic; "God the Son" is a 4th-century Trinitarian formulation (STEP 91 §20 Trinity Control: technical terminology = LATER FORMULATION).

**Confidence:** HIGH that direct equation is anachronistic.

**Limitations:** The title *later* acquires ontological weight; a Gospel author may have intended more than royal sonship (e.g., Matt 11:27; Mark 14:62 allusions), but that is argued per-passage, not automatic.

---

### Claim H: "Every use of Kyrios for Jesus means YHWH."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** κύριος has a wide Koine range (polite address, master, Davidic title, divine reference); context controls meaning.

**Evidence:** Vocatives (Matt 8:6; John 4:11); parabolic masters (Matt 21:40; Luke 12:36-37); Ps 110:1 exegesis (Mark 12:36-37); YHWH-text applications (Rom 10:9-13; Phil 2:9-11).

**Reason:** STEP 90 Claim O established that kyrios-YHWH equivalence requires contextual support; blanket equivalence is linguistically untrue in both directions.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** The reverse claim (no κύριος ever means YHWH) is equally UNSAFE; Rom 10:13 and Phil 2:10-11 are strong YHWH-application cases at Layer C.

---

### Claim I: "Jesus's forgiveness of sins demonstrates divine identity."
**Classification:** DISPUTED

**Finding:** The forgiveness tradition (Mark 2:1-12; Luke 7:48-50) demonstrates that Jesus's pronouncements were early understood as theologically weighty; whether they *demonstrate* divine identity is interpretation.

**Evidence:** Mark 2:5-7 (pronouncement + scribal objection); Mark 2:10-11 (Son of Man "authority on earth").

**Reason:** Three interpretations are representable (divine prerogative; delegated/prophetic agency; eschatological proclamation of God's mercy). The text supports the *claim*; the *ground* is inference.

**Confidence:** HIGH for claim-existence; DISPUTED for divine-identity proof.

**Limitations:** Mark 2:7's objection is embedded in the narrative (theologoumenon), not independent testimony; "Son of Man" semantics affect the inference.

---

### Claim J: "Jesus distinguished himself from God."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** The tradition reports Jesus praying to, obeying, and speaking of the Father as distinct from himself.

**Evidence:** Gethsemane (Mark 14:36); "Why do you call me good? No one is good but God alone" (Mark 10:18); "not even the Son, but the Father" (Mark 13:32); Lord's Prayer addressed to the Father (Luke 11:2).

**Reason:** Multiple streams report dependence/distinction language; embarrassment criterion supports authenticity of the ignorance saying (Mark 13:32).

**Confidence:** HIGH that distinction language is reported; the interpretation is open (see Claim K).

**Limitations:** "Distinguished" is a textual-relation observation, not a metaphysical verdict; the same tradition also contains unity/sonship language.

---

### Claim K: "Jesus's distinction from God proves he was not divine."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Distinction-language does not entail non-divinity; agency, sending, and intra-divine distinction are all compatible with later doctrine.

**Evidence:** Jewish agency theology (sheluḥim); Nicene doctrine itself maintains divine Person distinction; Phil 2:6-11 combines "form of God" with obedience/self-emptying.

**Reason:** The inference "distinct → not divine" is a category error that both Jewish agency categories and later Trinitarian logic reject (STEP 91 §19: distinction ≠ non-divinity).

**Confidence:** HIGH that the inference is invalid as stated.

**Limitations:** The opposite inference ("distinction proves divinity") is equally invalid; the honest result is that distinction-language is compatible with multiple metaphysical models.

---

### Claim L: "Jesus's unity language proves Nicene equality."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Unity/sonship language (John 10:30; 14:9-11; Matt 11:27) does not automatically express 4th-century homoousios-equality.

**Evidence:** John 10:30 ("I and the Father are one") is Johannine (Layer C2); its historical reach to Jesus is DISPUTED; union-of-will and agency readings are attested in scholarly literature; Nicene ousia language is absent from all Gospels.

**Reason:** STEP 91 §20 Trinity Control: early evidence PRESENT, technical formulation LATER. Claiming John 10:30 "equals" homoousios smuggles 4th-century metaphysics into a 1st-century text.

**Confidence:** HIGH that direct equation is anachronistic.

**Limitations:** The Council did *see* John 10:30 as supportive — reception history matters (Nicaea exegesis) — but reception is not the same as the text's original meaning; both claims must be kept separate.

---

### Claim M: "Jesus's crucifixion is historically established."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate is among the most secure facts in the store.

**Evidence:** Tacitus Annals 15.44 (principal stored non-Christian witness, CLAIM-HIST-TAC-002: "extreme penalty... ## 24. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### SIDE A — "Jesus's divine status was already embedded in his own historical self-understanding and earliest movement."

**Strongest case:**

1. **Reported authority is extraordinary, not merely prophetic:** Jesus forgives sins (Mark 2:5-10) — the tradition itself records the theological shock ("Who can forgive sins but God alone?"); acts on the Temple (Mark 11); claims Sabbath lordship (Mark 2:28); speaks in "I" antitheses against transmitted Torah interpretation (Matt 5:21-48 — assuming Q/Matthean core authenticity).
2. **Distinctive filial language:** "Abba" (Mark 14:36) + "the Son" as unique counterpart of "the Father" (Matt 11:27//Luke 10:22; Mark 13:32) — the Father/Son pairing is a *self-referential* category, not a community-accolade category.
3. **Eschatological self-placement:** Son of Man sayings (Mark 8:38; 14:62) place Jesus at the judgment — an eschatological role normally reserved for God or God's agent (Daniel 7).
4. **The earliest movement's devotion is anomalously early and Aramaic:** Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22), baptism into Christ's name (1 Cor 1:13), "calling on the name of the Lord" (Rom 10:12-13), hymn (Phil 2:6-11) — devotion to Jesus as Lord within ~20 years of the crucifixion, in the *same language* as the first disciples. Explanations that make this a late Pauline invention fail against Gal 1-2 (contact with Jerusalem) and 1 Cor 15:11 ("whether I or they, so we preach").
5. **No martyrdom-cult or teacher-cult parallel** in Second Temple Judaism produced divine worship of a teacher; the closest analogues (Enoch, Melchizedek, Wisdom) involve exalted *figures*, and their veneration does not match NT-level devotional practice (STEP 91 §§14-16).
6. **The tradition's embarrassment cuts across:** crucifixion, baptism by John, ignorance sayings, disciples' failure — the church preserved material that undercuts triumphalism; wholesale christologizing invention is harder to square with this editorial behavior.

**Verdict on A:** Strongest where it claims *post-resurrection continuity with very early* (within years) devotion and *distinctive self-referential claims* (authority, filial pairing, eschatological role). The inference "therefore his own lifetime contained explicit divine self-identity" is A's *weakest point* — it stretches beyond stored evidence (Claim P: NOT ESTABLISHED).

---

### SIDE B — "Jesus's original historical profile was primarily Jewish, messianic, prophetic, and eschatological; stronger divine Christology developed after Easter."

**Strongest case:**

1. **Kingdom over christology:** The dominant, multiple-attested content of Jesus's preaching is the Kingdom of God, not his own person — an essentially theocentric program (STEP 92 §5). Paul's letters, by contrast, are christocentric (STEP 90 §inherited vs original: shift from Kingdom to King).
2. **The earliest kerygma is exaltation-shaped:** Rom 1:3-4 ("designated Son of God *in power by resurrection*") and Acts-style proclamation (Layer C narrative) make resurrection the *vindication/installation* event — a functional, two-stage model (human Jesus → exalted Lord), not a pre-existent divine being entering the world.
3. **Distinction and subordination language is early and persistent:** Mark 10:18 ("no one good but God"), Mark 13:32 (ignorance of the day), Gethsemane (Mark 14:36), prayer itself — if the earliest strand already contains these, and the latest explicit strand (John) contains equality language, the trajectory is *from* distinction *toward* equality, i.e., development.
4. **Title-history supports development:** "Messiah" and "Son of God" begin as royal-Davidic categories (Ps 2; 2 Sam 7) and acquire ontological weight only over centuries; "Lord" begins as address and becomes YHWH-application only in Pauline/confessional usage (STEP 92 §§8-9; STEP 91 §18).
5. **Genre controls:** The Gospels are confession-shaped narrative (Mark: "beginning of the gospel"; John: "written that you may believe" 20:31) — not neutral biography; their high elements are authorial theology (Layer C), not transcript (Layer A).
6. **Skeptical controls:** No Q saying, no authentic-Synoptic datum, is *unambiguously* an explicit divine self-claim; every candidate (Matt 11:27; Mark 14:62) is ambiguous or disputed; the unambiguous ones are Johannine and late.

**Verdict on B:** Strongest where it demonstrates *development in explicitness* (functional → ontological; hidden → open) and *theocentric Kingdom-orientation* of the earliest Jesus tradition. B's *weakest point*: explaining the *speed and shape* of early devotion (Maranatha, YHWH-text application within ~20 years) as mere post-Easter creativity without any root in Jesus's own impact — that requires a second act of explanation that B often leaves implicit.

---

### Where neither side can establish:

- The exact psychological content of Jesus's own self-understanding (prophet? Messiah? unique Son? all? none?) — Layer A is inferential, not introspective.
- Whether Matt 11:27 / Mark 14:62 are authentic Jesus sayings or community formulations.
- The precise origin (Aramaic milieu? Hellenistic? both?) and dating of the earliest high-Christology fragments.
- Any direct bridge from "early devotion" to "divine ontological identity" without invoking theological premises.

## 27. FINAL SYNTHESIS

### 27.1 Answers to the Ten Questions

**1. What is historically secure?**
Jesus was a 1st-century Galilean Jew, baptized in John's movement, who proclaimed the Kingdom of God, taught authoritatively within Judaism (parables, aphorisms, Torah controversies), was known as healer/exorcist (claim-level), drew conflict (Temple action), and was crucified under Pontius Pilate on a kingship-charge (Tacitus + Paul + Gospels). His followers, within about two decades (per Paul's received traditions), confessed him Messiah and Lord, proclaimed his resurrection, and rendered him devotional honors.

**2. What is strongly supported?**
Kingdom-proclamation as his central message; "Abba"/Father relation-language; Son of Man as his distinctive self-reference; rigorist Torah interpretation (divorce, mercy, love-command); sin-forgiveness pronouncements as early tradition; the eschatological horizon of his ministry; the movement's early binitarian/devotional fidelity within Jewish monotheism.

**3. What is probable?**
The historical core of the Temple action and the "King of the Jews" charge; Daniel-7 echo in his eschatological speech (if Son of Man sayings are authentic); widespread early devotion to Jesus as Lord in the first two decades; pre-Pauline origin of the 1 Cor 15:3-5 formula and (probable) Phil 2 hymn.

**4. What is disputed?**
Messianic self-identification in his lifetime; Son of Man semantics (idiom vs eschatological figure); whether forgiveness pronouncements claimed divine prerogative vs delegated/eschatological authority; authenticity of Matt 11:27 and suffering-Son-of-Man sayings; the point at which "divine identity" became explicit ontology; the psychological content of his self-understanding.

**5. What is uncertain?**
Exact dates (30 vs 33 CE); the exact compositional history of pre-Pauline fragments; the precise pace/shape of the earliest resurrection-proclamation; the transmission chain between Jesus's sayings and their Gospel forms; the regional dynamics of the movement.

**6. What belongs to post-Easter interpretation?**
Explicit titles of exaltation ("designated Son of God in power by resurrection," Rom 1:4); confession-formulas ("Jesus is Lord"); resurrection-proclamation as gospel-content; devotion (Maranatha, baptism into Christ's name, invocation); the identification of resurrection as vindication. (Attested Layer B, i.e., within years — not "late," but post-Easter.)

**7. What belongs to Pauline development?**
Justification by faith apart from Torah-works for Gentiles; cosmic substitutionary atonement language; explicit YHWH-text application (Rom 10:13; Phil 2:10-11); creation-agency formulation (1 Cor 8:6); the shift from Kingdom-proclamation to proclamation of the King; the Gentile mission framework (STEP 90: INHERITED + DEVELOPED).

**8. What belongs to Johannine development?**
Logos theology (John 1:1-18); explicit pre-existence and "I AM" self-designations; open messianic self-claim in narrative; Father-Son unity formulations (10:30; 17:5); Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28). Layer C2 — explicitation, not necessarily invention of the concepts attested earlier in Paul.

**9. What belongs to later conciliar doctrine?**
homoousios (Nicaea 325); three-hypostasis Triad formulation (Cappadocian synthesis; Constantinople 381); two-natures metaphysics (Chalcedon 451); technical ousia/prosopon vocabulary; conciliar authority as such (STEP 70-88 chain).

**10. What cannot be projected backward?**
Nicene/Chalcedonian terminology onto Gospel passages; Johannine "I AM" as automatic Layer-A transcript; Pauline Christology as automatic Jesus-quotation; later devotion as direct evidence of Jesus's own self-understanding; layer-D categories as proof of layer-A claims (STEP 92 §22 Anti-Retrojection Control).

## 28. FINAL CONCLUSION (CALIBRATED)

**HISTORICALLY SECURE:** Jesus existed; was a Galilean Jew; proclaimed the Kingdom of God; taught within Judaism with prophetic authority; was reputed a healer/exorcist; drew temple/authority conflict; was crucified under Pontius Pilate; his followers within ~20 years proclaimed his resurrection (received formula in 1 Cor 15:3-11) and rendered him devotional honors (Maranatha; baptism into Christ's name; confession of Lordship) within a Jewish monotheistic frame.

**STRONGLY SUPPORTED:** Distinctive "Abba"/Father language; Son of Man as his self-reference; sin-forgiveness pronouncements as early tradition; rigorist-merciful Torah interpretation; eschatological orientation; early binitarian devotion (Layer B); Paul's reception of core tradition and coordination with Jerusalem.

**PROBABLE:** Temple-action historical core; "King of the Jews" charge; Daniel-7 echo in eschatological Son of Man sayings; pre-Pauline origin of 1 Cor 15:3-5 and Phil 2:6-11 hymn; high Christology as *received* by Paul (hence pre-dating his letters).

**DISPUTED:** Messianic self-identification in his lifetime; Son of Man semantics; the ground of forgiveness-authority (divine prerogative vs agency vs eschatological proclamation); authenticity of Matt 11:27 and suffering-Son-of-Man sayings; the exact distribution of divine-identity language between Jesus himself and post-Easter interpretation; the exact dating of Jesus events.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** Any explicit "I am God" claim by the historical Jesus (Layer A); "Son of God" = "God the Son" in the Gospels; every κύριος = YHWH; high Christology as a demonstrable feature of Jesus's *lifetime*; the resurrection as an event (Layer F); Jesus's metaphysical ontology.

**THEOLOGICAL CONCLUSION REQUIRING ADDITIONAL PREMISES:** That Jesus was/is divine; that he was eternally pre-existent; that he is homoousios with the Father; that the resurrection occurred as a physical event; that his sin-forgiveness was authorized by divine identity. Each of these is *consistent with* the historical evidence (none is contradicted by it) and each is *supported by specific texts and traditions* — but each requires premises beyond what historical method alone can supply (revelation, faith, theological interpretation of Scripture). The honest audit result: history establishes the *earliest belief and devotion*; theology interprets that belief.
