# AUDIT REPORT: STEP 71 — ARIUS / ARIAN CONTROVERSY PRIMARY-EVIDENCE AUDIT

## 1. SOURCE IDENTITY & AUTHENTICITY
* **Source ID:** SRC-THEO-002
* **Title:** Surviving Fragments and Letters of Arius (c. 318–325 CE)
* **Authenticity Status:** PROBABLE / FRAGMENTARY. Preserved primarily through opponents (Athanasius, Epiphanius, Theodoret). While context is polemical, modern scholarship (Opitz, Williams) accepts key preserved statements (e.g., *Letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia*, *Thalia* fragments) as genuine reflections of Arius's core theology.

## 2. CORE CHRISTOLOGICAL CLAIMS OF ARIUS
* **Relation to the Father:** Arius maintained that the Father alone is unbegotten (agennetos), eternal, and without beginning. The Son (*Logos* / *Sophia*) was created or begotten by the Father's will *before times and ages*, thus having a beginning of existence ("there was when he was not" / *ōte ouk ēn*).
* **Ontological Status:** The Son is a creature (*ktisma* / *poiēma*), distinct in substance (*heteros kat' ousia*) from the Father, mutable by nature, yet exalted above all other creatures as the instrument through whom God created the universe.
* **Is Arius "just human"?** **FALSE / CARICATURE.** Arius explicitly affirmed that the Son pre-existed creation, was begotten before time, created the worlds, and was rightly accorded honor/worship as God's unique agent. He did *not* view Jesus as a mere human or ordinary prophet.

## 3. NICENE COMPARISON
* **Arius:** Asserted the Son had a beginning ("there was when he was not") and was created out of nothing (*ex nihilo*), distinct in substance from the Father.
* **Nicaea (325 CE):** Condemned these specific propositions in the anathemas, affirming instead that the Son is eternally begotten, *not made*, "of one substance" (*homoousios*) with the Father, and true God from true God.

## 4. CLAIM CLASSIFICATIONS (APOLOGETIC TEST)
A. "Arius believed Jesus was merely a human." — **UNSAFE / UNSUPPORTED** (Caricature; Arius affirmed high pre-existence and cosmic status).
B. "Arius denied every form of Christ's divinity." — **SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION** (He affirmed a subordinate, creaturely divine status/title, but denied *true, uncreated, eternal* deity equal to the Father).
C. "Arius regarded the Son as subordinate to the Father." — **SAFE** (Central to his position).
D. "Arius's position differed materially from Nicene homoousios." — **SAFE** (The exact point of the controversy).
E. "Nicaea created the concept of Christ's divine status from nothing." — **UNSAFE / UNSUPPORTED** (High pre-existent Christology existed long before Nicaea in Irenaeus and the New Testament).
F. "Arius and Nicaea disagreed over the Son's relation to the Father." — **SAFE** (Accurate historical finding).
G. "All fourth-century non-Nicene Christians held exactly Arius's position." — **UNSAFE / UNSUPPORTED** (Fourth-century anti-Nicene coalition included diverse positions: Arian, Eunomian/Anomoean, Semi-Arian/Homoiousian, and Homoian).
H. "Arius's own surviving evidence should be distinguished from later anti-Arian descriptions." — **SAFE** (Methodological necessity).

## 5. JOHN 1:1 BOUNDARY
* Arius interpreted John 1:1 (*"And the Word was God"*) by arguing that the *Logos* is God only by participation and adoption/creation, distinguishing the unbegotten true God (the Father) from the begotten/created God (the Son). This is recorded as an *interpretation/reception* layer, not as the grammatical meaning of the text itself.
