### 1.2 Layer separation rule

No layer is incorrectly treated as another. Specifically:

| Forbidden conflation | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| SOURCE = TEXT | A source contains text; the text must be extracted, edited, and transmitted |
| TEXT = MANUSCRIPT | A text exists in manuscripts; the manuscript is a physical witness, not the text itself |
| MANUSCRIPT = TRANSMISSION | A manuscript preserves a reading; transmission is the process by which readings are passed |
| TRANSMISSION = TRANSLATION | Transmission preserves the text in its language; translation renders it in another |
| TRANSLATION = INTERPRETATION | A translation makes lexical choices; interpretation assigns meaning |
| INTERPRETATION = HISTORY | Interpretation is a reading of text; history is a reconstruction of events |
| HISTORY = THEOLOGY | History establishes what happened; theology evaluates what it means |
| THEOLOGY = PHILOSOPHY | Theology reasons from revelation; philosophy reasons from reason |
| PHILOSOPHY = ARGUMENT | Philosophy provides frameworks; arguments are constructed claims |
| ARGUMENT = COUNTERARGUMENT | An argument asserts; a counterargument responds |
| COUNTERARGUMENT = FALLACY | A counterargument may contain a fallacy; it is not automatically one |

### 1.3 Architecture integrity status

**ARCHITECTURE_INTEGRITY = PASS** — The chain is directional, each layer is distinguishable, and no layer is collapsed into another. The system's STEP 100–149 progressively built each layer, and STEP 150 verifies their separation.

## 3. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY AUDIT

### 3.1 Evidence hierarchy

| Level | Type | Description | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PRIMARY SOURCES | Contemporary or near-contemporary sources from the period | Highest |
| 2 | CRITICAL EDITIONS | Scholarly reconstructions of the text from MSS | High (for text, not for theology) |
| 3 | MANUSCRIPT EVIDENCE | Physical witnesses to the text | High (for textual existence) |
| 4 | CONTEMPORARY / NEAR-CONTEMPORARY EVIDENCE | Sources within ~1 generation | High |
| 5 | PEER-REVIEWED ACADEMIC RESEARCH | Modern scholarly analysis | Medium-high (for analysis, not for evidence) |
| 6 | ESTABLISHED SCHOLARLY WORKS | Recognized scholarly contributions | Medium |
| 7 | CLASSICAL SCHOLARLY WORKS | Pre-modern scholarly traditions | Medium (for tradition, not for evidence) |
| 8 | RELIABLE SECONDARY SOURCES | Well-sourced secondary literature | Medium-low |
| 9 | POPULAR EDUCATIONAL SOURCES | Educational materials | Low (orientation only) |
| 10 | DEBATE / SOCIAL MEDIA | Debates, social media discussions | Very low (leads only) |

### 3.2 Non-mechanical hierarchy rules

The hierarchy is not applied mechanically. The following rules govern:

1. A later academic study can clarify evidence without becoming the primary evidence itself (Level 5 ≠ Level 1).
2. A critical edition is not an autograph (Level 2 ≠ original text).
3. A manuscript is not automatically proof of interpretation (Level 3 ≠ Level 4).
4. A theological institution is not automatically neutral historical evidence (theological authority ≠ historical authority).
5. A primary source is not automatically accurate (it has its own agenda, genre, and limitations).
6. Multiple secondary sources citing the same primary source do not multiply the evidence.
7. The absence of Level 1 evidence does not make Level 4 evidence into Level 1.

**EVIDENCE_HIERARCHY = PASS** — The hierarchy is non-mechanical, distinguished, and no level is conflated with another.

### 4.1 Claim types

Every major claim must be classifiable as one or more of:

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| FACT | Historically/textually verifiable | "The Council of Nicaea met in 325 CE" |
| INTERPRETATION | A reading of text/event | "John 1:1 affirms Christ's divinity" |
| TRADITION | What a community teaches | "Sunni Islam teaches the Qur'an is uncreated" |
| THEOLOGICAL CLAIM | About divine truth | "Trinity is the true nature of God" |
| HISTORICAL CLAIM | About what happened | "Muhammad received revelation in 610 CE" |
| LINGUISTIC CLAIM | About language/meaning | "Theos in John 1:1c is anarthrous and qualitative" |
| TEXTUAL CLAIM | About what a text says | "Qur'an 4:157 denies the crucifixion" |
| APOLOGETIC ARGUMENT | Constructed argument | "The Qur'an's inimitability proves divine origin" |
| SPECULATION | Beyond evidence | "Jesus traveled to India" |
| COUNTERARGUMENT | Response to an argument | "The i'jaz argument is subjective" |
| UNCERTAIN | Insufficient evidence | "The exact wording of the original autograph" |

### 4.2 Category collapse prohibition

The system explicitly forbids systematic category collapse:

| Forbidden collapse | Correct separation |
|---|---|
| Theological claim presented as historical fact | "Trinity is true" ≠ "The earliest Christians believed in Trinity" |
| Historical claim presented as theological proof | "Jesus existed" ≠ "Jesus is God" |
| Interpretation presented as fact | "John 1:1 teaches Trinity" ≠ "John 1:1 is about Trinity (interpretive)" |
| Textual claim presented as theological truth | "The Qur'an says X" ≠ "X is true" |
| Speculation presented as established | "Jesus may have traveled to India" ≠ "Jesus traveled to India" |
| Tradition presented as primary evidence | "The Fathers taught X" ≠ "X is historically established" |

**CLAIM_CLASSIFICATION = PASS** — All claim types are distinguishable, and no systematic category collapse is detected.

|---|---|

**CONFIDENCE_CALIBRATION = PASS** — Confidence is claim-specific, evidence-sensitive, source-sensitive, independence-sensitive, chronology-sensitive, and does not propagate automatically.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT AUDIT

### 8.1 Six independence dimensions

| Dimension | What is checked | HB | NT | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical independence | Are MSS physically distinct objects? | Yes (DSS, MT codices) | Yes (~6,000 Greek MSS) | Yes (Sana'a, Birmingham, etc.) |
| Textual independence | Do MSS derive from different textual traditions? | Yes (MT, SP, LXX, DSS) | Yes (Alexandrian, Western, Byzantine) | Partial (Uthmanic vs Sana'a lower text) |
| Editorial independence | Were MSS edited by different scribes/editors? | Yes (different Masoretic families) | Yes (different scribal traditions) | Partial (different regional centers) |
| Genealogical independence | Do MSS share a common ancestor in the transmission chain? | Varies (MT family, DSS family) | Varies (text-types have common ancestors) | Varies (most Uthmanic MSS share the recension) |
| Argumentative independence | Do arguments derive from independent reasoning, or do they all depend on the same premise? | Varies (Trinity arguments: biblical vs philosophical) | Varies (i'jaz arguments: linguistic vs historical) | Varies (covenant arguments: textual vs theological) |
| Chronological independence | Are sources temporally independent, or does a later source depend on an earlier one? | Varies (DSS predate MT by centuries) | Varies (autographs lost; later MSS depend on earlier) | Varies (sira/hadith postdate Qur'an by decades/centuries) |

### 8.2 Duplicate-source detection

| Pattern | Status |
|---|---|
| Same manuscript counted under different signatures | NOT_INDEPENDENT |
| Same edition reproduced by multiple websites | NOT_INDEPENDENT |
| Same website cited multiple times | NOT_INDEPENDENT |
| Same quotation reproduced by multiple sources | NOT_INDEPENDENT |
| Same source tradition (e.g., all from Ibn Ishaq) | PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT |
| Same common ancestor (e.g., all from Mark) | PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT |

**Source independence includes reverse traceability:** every source can be traced back to the claims it supports (SOURCE → CLAIM), just as every claim can be traced to its sources (CLAIM → SOURCE). Bidirectional traceability is enforced.

**MANUSCRIPT_INTEGRITY = PASS** — Six independence dimensions (physical, textual, editorial, genealogical, argumentative, chronological) are distinguished, and duplicate sources are detected.

## 23. FINAL INVARIANT MATRIX (20 invariants)

> Each invariant contains: `INVARIANT_ID | RULE | WHY_REQUIRED | FAILURE_CONDITION | REMEDIATION | STATUS`

### INV-01

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-01
**RULE:** Primary source must remain distinguishable from interpretation.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** If primary sources are conflated with interpretations, later theological readings contaminate the evidential base, making it impossible to distinguish what the source actually says from what it is later taken to mean.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** A patristic or tafsir interpretation is cited as if it were the primary text's own statement.
**REMEDIATION:** Always cite the primary text separately from interpretive tradition. Label interpretation as interpretation.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-02

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-02
**RULE:** Historical evidence cannot automatically establish theology.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Historical method can establish what happened; it cannot establish what events mean theologically. Conflating the two produces category errors.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** A historical claim (e.g., "Jesus existed") is presented as proof of a theological claim (e.g., "Jesus is God").
**REMEDIATION:** Classify each claim as FACT, INTERPRETATION, or THEOLOGICAL CLAIM. Maintain the separation through the pipeline.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-03

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-03
**RULE:** Multiple URLs cannot automatically establish multiple independent witnesses.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Multiple websites may all reproduce the same edition, translation, or argument. Counting them as independent inflates the evidence.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument claims "N sources confirm X" where the N sources are all websites reproducing the same underlying source.
**REMEDIATION:** Trace each source to its primary origin. Verify physical, editorial, and textual independence.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-04

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-04
**RULE:** Translation cannot be treated as original text.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** A translation is a rendering, not the original. Translation choices affect meaning. Treating a translation as the original conceals translation issues.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument cites an English translation as if it were the original-language text, without acknowledging the lexical range or alternative renderings.
**REMEDIATION:** Always reference the original language. Present the full lexical range. Acknowledge alternative translations.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-05

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-05
**RULE:** A critical edition cannot be treated as an autograph.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** A critical edition (e.g., NA28, BHS/BHQ, Cairo edition) is a scholarly reconstruction, not the original text. Treating it as the original overstates what MSS evidence can establish.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument cites NA28 as if it were the autograph text of the NT, or the Cairo edition as if it were the original Qur'anic recitation.
**REMEDIATION:** Label critical editions as reconstructions. Distinguish the edition from the autograph. Acknowledge that the autograph is not extant.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-06

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-06
**RULE:** Hadith collection date does not equal report origin date.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Bukhari (~870 CE) compiled hadith that may originate from ~620s CE. The ~250-year gap is real and must be acknowledged. Conflating collection date with origin date conceals the temporal gap.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument treats Bukhari's compilation date as the date of the hadith's origin, ignoring the temporal gap.
**REMEDIATION:** Distinguish collection date from origin date. Acknowledge the temporal gap. Evaluate the chain's reliability independently.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-07

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-07
**RULE:** Sahih does not equal automatic modern historical certainty.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Classical hadith authentication (sahih) uses classical criteria. Modern historical method may reach different conclusions. Conflating the two overstates hadith reliability.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument treats a sahih hadith as historically certain by modern standards, without acknowledging that classical and modern criteria differ.
**REMEDIATION:** Distinguish classical authentication from modern historical assessment. Apply both. Acknowledge where they diverge.
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-08

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-08
**RULE:** Doctrine cannot be projected backward without explicit argument.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Later theological formulations (Trinity, Chalcedonian Christology, Ash'ari kalam, Shi'i Imamah) must not be read into the earliest sources without demonstrating that the seeds are genuinely present. Retrospective reading is anachronism.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** Chalcedonian categories are read into Pauline texts, or Ash'ari theology is read into the Qur'an, without acknowledging the historical development.
**REMEDIATION:** Trace doctrinal development historically. Distinguish what the earliest sources say from what later tradition says they mean. Apply the anti-retrodiction rule (AE-26).
**STATUS:** PASS

---

### INV-09

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-09
**RULE:** Variant does not equal corruption.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** A textual variant may be a legitimate alternative reading, not an error. Equating variants with corruption exaggerates the textual problem.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument cites the number of NT or Qur'anic variants as proof of textual corruption, without assessing the significance of the variants.
**REMEDIATION:** Assess variant type, significance, and genealogical weight. Do not equate count with corruption.
**STATUS:** PASS

### INV-10

**INVARIANT_ID:** INV-10
**RULE:** Canon does not equal text.
**WHY_REQUIRED:** Canon formation concerns which books are included; textual criticism concerns the wording within books. These are different questions. Conflating them produces category errors.
**FAILURE_CONDITION:** An argument treats canon formation as proof of textual integrity, or textual variants as proof of canon problems.
**REMEDIATION:** Separate canon analysis from textual criticism. Address each independently.
**STATUS:** PASS

**ERROR_ID:** E13
**ERROR:** Strawman
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether the opposing position is represented at its weakest rather than its strongest.
**EXAMPLE:** Representing the Trinity as "three gods" (tritheism).
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Construct the steelman. Represent the opposing position at its strongest before critiquing it.

### E14

**ERROR_ID:** E14
**ERROR:** Selective skepticism
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether skepticism is applied to opposing evidence but not to supporting evidence.
**EXAMPLE:** Being maximally skeptical of the hadith chain while accepting the patristic chain without equivalent scrutiny.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the same level of skepticism to supporting and opposing evidence (DS-20).

### E15

**ERROR_ID:** E15
**ERROR:** Selective credulity
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether favorable evidence is accepted with insufficient scrutiny while unfavorable evidence is met with maximum skepticism.
**EXAMPLE:** Accepting the i'jaz argument without independent aesthetic criteria while demanding extraordinary evidence for the resurrection.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the same evidential threshold to all claims regardless of whether they support or oppose one's position.

### E16

**ERROR_ID:** E16
**ERROR:** Citation laundering
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Trace citation chains. Check whether sources cite each other without returning to primary evidence.
**EXAMPLE:** Source A cites Source B, Source B cites Source C, Source C cites Source A — none citing primary evidence.
**SEVERITY:** HIGH
**REMEDIATION:** Require that each claim trace back to a primary source. Flag circular citation chains.

### E17

**ERROR_ID:** E17
**ERROR:** Confirmation bias
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether only favorable evidence is cited and unfavorable evidence is omitted or minimized.
**EXAMPLE:** Citing all evidence for the Qur'an's preservation while omitting the Sana'a palimpsest.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Require that both favorable and unfavorable evidence be presented. Include the counterargument and steelman for every major claim.

### E18

**ERROR_ID:** E18
**ERROR:** Absence-of-evidence fallacy
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether absence of evidence is treated as proof of absence without the expectation principle.
**EXAMPLE:** "There is no archaeological evidence for X, therefore X did not happen" without establishing that evidence would be expected.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Apply the expectation principle: absence is evidence of absence only when evidence would be expected.

---

### E19

**ERROR_ID:** E19
**ERROR:** Fallacy misclassification
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether a disagreement is labeled as a fallacy without identifying a structural flaw in the reasoning.
**EXAMPLE:** Labeling a theological disagreement as "begging the question" when it is simply a different theological starting point.
**SEVERITY:** MEDIUM
**REMEDIATION:** Classify as fallacy only when the reasoning structure warrants it. Use DISAGREEMENT, UNSOUND PREMISE, or WEAK EVIDENCE for non-fallacious but unaccepted claims.

### E20

**ERROR_ID:** E20
**ERROR:** Theological conclusion presented as neutral historical fact
**DETECTION_METHOD:** Check whether a theological claim (e.g., "The Qur'an is God's word") is presented as a historical fact rather than a theological claim.
**EXAMPLE:** "The Qur'an is God's word" presented as an established fact rather than a faith-dependent theological claim.
**SEVERITY:** CRITICAL
**REMEDIATION:** Classify each claim correctly. Flag any theological conclusion presented as historical fact. Maintain the 3-level analysis (descriptive, philosophical, theological).
