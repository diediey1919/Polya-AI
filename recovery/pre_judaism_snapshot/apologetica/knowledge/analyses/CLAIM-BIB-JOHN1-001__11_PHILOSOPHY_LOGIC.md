# SPECIALIST ANALYSIS: CLAIM-BIB-JOHN1-001__11_PHILOSOPHY_LOGIC.md

* **CLAIM_ID:** CLAIM-BIB-JOHN1-001
* **SOURCE_ID:** SRC-BIB-002
* **SPECIALIST_ID:** 11_PHILOSOPHY_LOGIC
* **ANALYSIS_TYPE:** ONTOLOGICAL / LOGICAL ANALYSIS
* **QUESTION:** What are the logical and ontological implications of attributing divine identity (*Theos*) to the *Logos* while maintaining relational distinction (*pros ton Theon*)?
* **EVIDENCE_USED:** Syntactic pairing of John 1:1b and 1:1c.
* **ANALYSIS:** 
  1. *Law of Non-Contradiction & Identity*: Logically, if A (*ho Logos*) is *pros* B (*ton Theon*), A is not identical in personhood to B. Yet if A is *Theos*, A shares the ontological attributes and category of B.
  2. *Ontological vs. Relational Categories*: Classical theology resolves this by distinguishing between *ousia* (essence/divinity) and *hypostasis* (personhood), allowing the *Logos* to share divine essence while maintaining relational distinction.
* **COUNTERARGUMENT:** Critics argue this introduces ontological paradoxes or polytheistic tendencies unless strictly subordinated within the Godhead (Arianism/Subordinationism).
* **LIMITATIONS:** Philosophical ontology abstracts from the historical and linguistic data of the text.
* **CONFIDENCE:** PROBABLE
