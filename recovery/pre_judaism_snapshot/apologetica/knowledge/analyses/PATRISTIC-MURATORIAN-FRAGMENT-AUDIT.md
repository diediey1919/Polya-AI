# AUDIT REPORT: STEP 67 — MURATORIAN FRAGMENT CANON-HISTORY AUDIT

## 1. SOURCE IDENTITY & RECORD
* **Source ID:** SRC-PAT-003
* **Title:** Muratorian Fragment (*Canon Muratorianus*)
* **Author / Attribution:** UNKNOWN / UNATTRIBUTED (Strictly maintained per instruction; rejected simplistic attribution to Hippolytus or others as uncertain).
* **Date:** Disputed scholarly range: c. 170–200 CE (traditional late 2nd century) vs. c. 350–400 CE (revisionist 4th century). Surviving manuscript is an 8th-century Latin copy in Codex Ambrosianus J. 101 inf., translated from a lost Greek original. Discovered and published by Muratori in 1740.

## 2. CANONICAL CONTENTS & FINDINGS
* **Four Gospels:** Begins abruptly mentioning Luke as the third book of the Gospel (implying Matthew and Mark preceded in the missing top lines); explicitly names John as the fourth Gospel. Demonstrates early or mid-period fourfold Gospel reception.
* **Acts & Paul:** Lists Acts of the Apostles and thirteen Pauline Epistles (explicitly naming Philemon, Titus, two to Timothy; omitting Hebrews; noting and rejecting Marcionite forged epistles to the Laodiceans and Alexandrians).
* **Other Books:** Accepts Jude, two epistles of John, Wisdom of Solomon, Apocalypse of John (Revelation), and Apocalypse of Peter (with qualification that some refuse to read it in church). Rejects writings of Valentinus, Marcion, and Basilides. Omits James, 1-2 Peter, and 3 John (though omission is fragmentary, not definitive proof of rejection).

## 3. COMPARISON: IRENAEUS (c. 180) vs MURATORIAN (Disputed) vs ATHANASIUS (367)
* **Irenaeus (~180 CE):** Explicitly defends fourfold Gospels and extensive Pauline corpus; patristic framework in Gaul.
* **Muratorian Fragment (Disputed c. 170–400):** Provides a western/regional bibliographic list detailing accepted and rejected books; shares core fourfold Gospel and Pauline emphasis with Irenaeus, but exhibits distinct marginal boundaries (e.g., accepting Apocalypse of Peter, omitting James/Hebrews).
* **Athanasius (367 CE):** Formalizes the exact 27-book canon in Alexandria with rigid boundaries excluding apocryphal apocalypses.
* **Synthesis:** These three sources do *not* form a single uniform list; they illustrate regional diversity, gradual consolidation, and shifting boundaries across different geographical centers before ultimate 4th/5th-century convergence.

## 4. BOUNDARIES: CONSTANTINE, TEXT, AUTHORSHIP, THEOLOGY
* **Constantine Assessment:** The Muratorian Fragment predates Constantine (under traditional dating) or is roughly contemporary with fourth-century developments (under revisionist dating), but in neither case was Constantine involved in creating or selecting its contents.
* **Textual Boundary:** The Muratorian Fragment is a canonical/bibliographic list, *not* a biblical manuscript or textual critical witness. It gives no Greek text or variant readings.
* **Authorship Boundary:** Inclusion of a letter does not prove traditional historical authorship; it proves canonical/ecclesial recognition.
* **Theological Boundary:** Listing books does not independently prove theological dogma (Trinity, resurrection).
