## A. OBJECTIVE & CORE METHOD

Forensic historical audit of the emergence and development of early Muslim communal, political, theological, and sectarian identities: proto-Sunni formations, proto-Shi'a formations, pro-'Alid movements, Kharijite formations, Ibadi development, Umayyad political-religious identity, early communal terminology, and later Sunni/Shi'i historiographical reconstruction.

Central methodological question: WHEN did later sectarian identities become historically visible as distinct communities with articulated theologies and institutions? Modern Sunni/Shi'a categories in fully developed form are NOT assumed for the first decades of Islam.

Method: every proposition is tested across chronological layers (Section C), terminology is dated before use (Section D), political loyalty is separated from sectarian identity (Section B), and every major narrative carries EVENT DATE / EARLIEST TRACE / EARLIEST WRITTEN FORM / LATER COMPILATION / LATER DOCTRINAL FORM (Section Z).

Core principle: EARLY POLITICAL LOYALTY ≠ LATER SECTARIAN IDENTITY. PRO-'ALID ≠ AUTOMATICALLY TWELVER SHI'A. ALID SUPPORT ≠ IMAMOLOGY. KHARIJITE REBELLION ≠ FULLY DEVELOPED IBADISM. EARLY SUNNI-LIKE TRADITIONISM ≠ FULLY FORMULATED LATER SUNNISM.

## B. LOYALTY-IDENTITY SEPARATION FRAMEWORK

Seven distinct phenomena, never inferred automatically from one another:

1. **POLITICAL LOYALTY** — support for a claimant (Ali, Mu'awiya, Ibn al-Zubayr) in a concrete conflict.
2. **POLITICAL REVOLT** — armed rising (Tawwabun 65/685, Mukhtar 66-67/685-687, Zayd 122/740).
3. **COMMUNAL IDENTITY** — self-designation as a distinct group (Shi'at 'Ali, Ahl al-Sunna wa'l-Jama'a).
4. **RITUAL PRACTICE** — mourning rites, communal prayer markers, pilgrimage patterns.
5. **LEGAL SCHOOL** — madhhab affiliation (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali; Zaydi/Imami/Ibadi fiqh).
6. **THEOLOGY** — articulated doctrine (nass, 'isma, irja', qadar positions).
7. **SECTARIAN INSTITUTION** — scholars, courts, endowments, dynastic patronage.

A 1st-century Kufan who fought for Ali at Siffin (1) is not thereby a Twelver theologian (6) or a member of a later sectarian institution (7).

## AA. SOURCE BARRIER

No contemporaneous [L2-L4] narrative prose survives; earliest histories are [L8-L9] compilations (Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Hisham, Abu Mikhnaf via Tabari, Waqidi, Sayf ibn Umar — each with own reliability profile, STEP 116). Oral transmission + lost early notebooks (e.g. 'Urwa corpus claims) are real phenomena but do not self-authenticate. Lost-work claims (e.g. "Abu Mikhnaf had monographs") are evaluated by later citation density, not assumed. Late surviving manuscript ≠ late invention automatically; claimed early origin ≠ authenticity automatically.

## AD. CRITICAL STEELMAN

Strongest historical-critical model: early succession crises and civil wars [L2-L4] generated competing communal memories; Kufan, Syrian, Medinan, and Kharijite storytellers preserved partisan layers; 2nd-3rd/8th-9th-century scholars organized these into coherent madhhab histories; heresiographers [L10] hardened fluid loyalties into fixed sects. Tested against earliest evidence: PASSES for the political core (material + external corroboration, STEP 117-118); REQUIRES QUALIFICATION where it implies invention (Ghadir breadth, Karbala anchoring, and Siffin poetry resist pure-invention readings). Development ≠ fabrication.
