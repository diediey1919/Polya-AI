A. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Conduct a forensic historical and epistemological audit of Sunni hadith transmission, authentication, canonization, and authority.

Primary focus:
- Early Sunni hadith transmission
- Musannaf literature and early notebooks
- Isnad, matn, and narrator criticism (Jarh wa Ta'dil)
- Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, and the Six Books (Kutub al-Sittah)
- Muwatta' of Malik, Musnad Ahmad, and Sunan collections
- Hadith canon formation and Sunni authority theory.

The objective is NOT to prove Sunni hadith is authentic or inauthentic as a whole, but to determine historical reconstructibility, methodological development, authentication criteria, canon formation, and the distinction between classical and modern historical analysis.

E. EARLY WRITTEN HADITH
==================================================

Evidence for Sahifa, notebooks (kutub), and companion records indicates that writing coexisted with oral transmission from the late 1st/7th century onwards, though formal state-sponsored or widely distributed compendia materialized primarily in the 2nd/8th century.

==================================================
F. AL-ZUHRI
==================================================

Muhammad ibn Shihab al-Zuhri (d. 124 AH / 742 CE) evaluated through competing models: major early Umayyad-associated transmitter vs. retrospective literary construction. Survives as a pivotal common link in early isnad networks.

==================================================
G. MALIK AND AL-MUWATTA'
==================================================

Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH) and al-Muwatta'. Distinguishes prophetic hadith from companion practice (mawquf), successor reports (maqtu'), and Medinan consensus ('amal ahl al-Medina).

==================================================
H. MUSANNAF LITERATURE
==================================================

'Abd al-Razzaq al-San'ani (d. 211 AH) and Ibn Abi Shaybah (d. 235 AH). Musannaf structure reveals thematic legal organization preserving 2nd-century materials prior to the canonical Jami' format.

==================================================
I. MUSNAD AHMAD
==================================================

Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 241 AH). Compilation arranged by companion isnads. Subject to subsequent additions and structural editing by his son 'Abd Allah and later transmitters.

==================================================
J. SAHIH AL-BUKHARI
==================================================

Muhammad b. Ismail al-Bukhari (d. 256 AH / 870 CE). Audit of selection criteria, chapter headings (fiqh al-Bukhari), repeated reports, mu'allaqat, and transmission through al-Firabri. Distinguishes canonical importance from absolute historical certainty of every single matn.

==================================================
K. SAHIH MUSLIM
==================================================

Muslim b. al-Hajjaj (d. 261 AH / 875 CE). Methodological emphasis on isnad arrangement, introduction on transmission ethics, and clear hierarchy of narrator reliability.

==================================================
L. SIX BOOKS (KUTUB AL-SITTAH)
==================================================

Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah. Compiled across the 3rd/9th century and gradually recognized as a canonical corpus through 4th-6th century scholarly consensus.

==================================================
M. CANONIZATION
==================================================

Distinction between composition (3rd/9th c.) and canonization (5th-7th/11th-13th c.). Regional differences in reception (e.g., initial resistance to Ibn Majah or Tirmidhi in certain western provinces).

==================================================
N. JARH WA TA'DIL
==================================================

Narrator criticism evaluating memory (dabt), uprightness ('adala), sectarian bias, tadlis (concealment of transmission flaws), and chronological plausibility.

==================================================
O. SECTARIAN NARRATORS
==================================================

Evaluation of Shi'i, Kharijite, Qadari, and Murji'i narrators in Sunni isnads. Classical rule: trustworthiness in transmission (sidq/dabt) overrode theological deviance, provided the narrator did not fabricate to support dogma.

==================================================
P. HADITH AUTHENTICATION
==================================================

Epistemological separation between classical authentication (sahih within Sunni methodological rules) and historical authentication (verifiable provenance through historical-critical methodology).

==================================================
Q. ISNAD & MATN ANALYSIS
==================================================

Rigorous application of isnad-cum-matn analysis (Juynboll, Motzki) to identify common links, partial common links, and divergent matns.

==================================================
R. SUNNI AUTHORITY THEORY
==================================================

Evolution of legal theory regarding the binding authority of Khabar al-Wahid (isolated reports) vs. Mutawatir (mass-transmitted reports) from Shafi'i onwards.
