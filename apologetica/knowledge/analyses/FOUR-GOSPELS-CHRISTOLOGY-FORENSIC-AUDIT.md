* **AUDIT_ID:** FOUR-GOSPELS-CHRISTOLOGY-FORENSIC-AUDIT
* **STEP:** 93
* **TARGET:** Mark → Matthew → Luke → John — Jesus tradition → Gospel Christology → Early Christian development
* **PERFORMED:** 2026-09-05
* **AUDITOR:** Polya Apologetica Knowledge System (Audit Module)
* **INTEGRATES:** STEP 92 (Historical Jesus Christology), STEP 91 (Early Christology Cross-Corpus), STEP 90 (Pauline Christology), STEP 89 (Second Temple Judaism / Historical Jesus), existing John 1:1 language/exegesis/manuscript audits

> **PURPOSE:** Forensically audit the Christology of each of the four Gospels — what each says about Jesus, what is shared tradition vs distinctive presentation, whether Gospel differences constitute contradiction, whether John "invented" divine Christology, whether the Synoptics are genuinely "lower", and how all of this relates to the Historical Jesus baseline (STEP 92) and to Nicene/conciliar formulation (STEP 70-88 chain).

## 0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

* The four Gospels share a substantial common Christological substructure: Jesus as Messiah/Christ, crucified and raised, exercising divine-type authority (forgiveness, healing, eschatological judgment, authority over Torah interpretation), in unique filial relation to God as Father.
* Mark is the earliest Gospel (conventional chronology) and is NOT "low Christology" — it contains forgiveness (2:5-10), Son of Man judgment authority (8:38; 13:26; 14:62), divine audition at baptism (1:11), demons recognizing Jesus (1:24; 3:11; 5:7), and "Son of God" confession (15:39). What Mark lacks is *explicit* pre-existence and incarnational language — absence ≠ denial (STEP 93 §20 est.).
* Matthew adds: Emmanuel ("God with us," 1:23), worship scenes (2:11; 14:33; 28:9,17), the triadic baptismal formula (28:19), and heightened divine-authority discourse (11:25-27; 28:18). The triadic formula is early triadic *language*, NOT a Nicene proof: it lacks homoousios/ousia entirely (STEP 93 §27).
* Luke-Acts adds: Spirit-driven narrative, prayer emphasis, explicit exaltation-by-resurrection Christology (Acts 2:36; 13:33), universal salvation scope. Luke's omissions (no Logos, no "I AM") are NOT denials.
* John is the most *explicit*: Logos (1:1-18), "I AM" sayings (8:24,28,58; 13:19), pre-existence (1:1-2; 8:58; 17:5), divine predicates (20:28 "My Lord and my God"), explicit Father/Son unity (10:30,38; 14:9-11). Witnessed by P66/P75 (c. 200 CE) — manuscript evidence for the *text*, not for its composition date.
* **FINDING on "John invented divine Christology":** NOT SUPPORTED. Explicit high Christology in John is anticipated by Pauline received traditions (1 Cor 8:6; Phil 2:6-11; Rom 10:9-13), early devotion (Maranatha, 1 Cor 16:22), and Synoptic authority/elevation material. John's distinctiveness is *explicitness and literary-theological articulation*, not *origin*. However, overcorrecting to "John added nothing" is equally unsupported: John contains distinctive conceptual development (Logos incarnate; "I AM" self-revelation; pre-existence made explicit).
* **Historical Jesus relation (STEP 92 baseline):** Gospel Christology develops and articulates the post-Easter confession; it is neither verbatim transcript of Jesus's self-understanding nor unhistorical invention. The movement preserved authentic tradition *and* developed it — both are true, per-layer.
* **Nicene relation:** Gospel Christology = high Christology in narrative/devotional/Jewish categories; Nicene theology = later technical formalization (homoousios, hypostasis, 381 spirit-clause, 451 two-natures). Neither "Gospel = Nicaea" nor "Nicaea = invention from nothing" is acceptable.

## 1. GOSPEL LAYER CONTROL

For EVERY Gospel, these layers are kept strictly separate:

| Layer | Content | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| A. Historical Jesus reconstruction | Critical reconstruction of the Galilean prophet (STEP 92) | Kingdom proclamation; Abba; crucifixion under Pilate |
| B. Gospel narrative | The story as told by the evangelist (selection, order, framing) | Mark's secrecy motif; Matthew's birth narrative; Luke's journey narrative; John's sign sequence |
| C. Evangelist's theological presentation | The author's christological point of view and redaction | Mark 1:1 "Son of God"; Matt 28:18-20; Luke 24:44-49; John 20:31 |
| D. Later patristic interpretation | 2nd-century+ reading (Irenaeus c. 180 CE) | Irenaeus on the fourfold Gospel & Logos |
| E. Conciliar doctrine | 4th-5th century definitions | Nicaea homoousios; Chalcedon two natures |

**RULES:** John 1:1 is Johannine textual evidence (witnessed by P66/P75/Sinaiticus/Vaticanus/SBLGNT) — NOT automatically a historical-Jesus quotation. No Gospel text proves conciliar doctrine by itself, and no conciliar definition proves a Gospel's intent.

### 4.2 Mark — Answers

**Does Mark have high Christology?** YES — but "high" must be defined by *indicators present in Mark* (divine-era authority, forgiveness, eschatological judgment, "Son of God" disclosures, royal/Ps-2 sonship, YHWH-power acts, Ps 110:1 lordship) rather than by Johannine/Nicene categories. Mark's is a *narrative/functional* high Christology, not an ontological one in explicit terms.

**Does Mark have pre-existence Christology?** NOT ESTABLISHED — no explicit pre-existence text. "Mark lacks Logos" ≠ "Mark has low Christology" (STEP 93 §20, §31 anti-bias).

**Does Mark describe Jesus as divine?** DISPUTED — Mark uses God-associated functions (calming chaos-waters; forgiveness; eschatological judge; 14:62 "right hand of Power") but never states "Jesus is God" and preserves distinction sayings (10:18; 13:32). The text is *compatible with* divine-agency and divine-identity readings; it does not *require* either in 4th-century terms.

### 5.2 Critical Rule — Matthew 28:19

The triadic formula "Father, Son, Holy Spirit" is:
* **Triadic language** — threefold divine name into which baptism is administered; Trinitarian *pattern* in nuce.
* **NOT automatic Nicene Trinity proof:** (1) no homoousios, ousia, hypostasis, or "one substance" vocabulary; (2) it is a baptismal formula in narrative, not a doctrinal definition; (3) Eusebius tradition suggests shorter variant ("in my name") existed; (4) the full triadic form is present in the transmitted Greek text (Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, critical editions) — as transmitted, it IS triadic; but "triad" ≠ 381-CE Trinity. **Classify: STRONG EVIDENCE of early triadic baptismal practice (Layer C); NOT a Nicene proof-text (STEP 93 §27; STEP 91 §20).**

### 5.3 Matthew — Answers

**Divine elements?** YES — Emmanuel presence-theology, worship scenes (2:11; 14:33; 28:9,17), "all authority" (28:18), mutual-Son-knowledge (11:27), Ps-110 lordship exegesis, triadic formula (28:19). Matthew's Christology: **royal-Davidic Messiah + Emmanuel-presence + risen Lord with universal authority.** Pre-existence: NOT ESTABLISHED (11:27 implication only).

**Most explicit?** YES — without question: Logos, pre-existence, creation-agency, "I AM" absolute (8:58), Father/Son unity, Thomas's acclamation, YHWH-text application (12:41).

John's contribution is **literary-theological articulation and narrative embodiment** — a distinctive, coherent presentation — NOT the origin of the trajectory.

**CRITICAL:** "I AM" sayings and high-priestly prayer are Johannine literary construction (Layer C2); projecting them onto the historical Jesus is prohibited (STEP 92 §19, §22). Their *theological truth* is outside historical method's purview.

## 11. TEST: "JOHN INVENTED THE DIVINE JESUS"

### 11.1 Decomposition (A-F per spec)

| Step | Claim | Evidence | Status |
|------|-------|----------|--------|
| A. John is more explicit | John's explicit divine language (1:1; 8:58; 10:30; 20:28) exceeds the Synoptics | Textual comparison | SAFE |
| B. Earlier evidence exists | 1 Cor 8:6 (Shema-reframe: Jesus in creation-agency), Phil 2:6-11 (pre-existence + YHWH homage), Rom 10:9-13 (Joel-YHWH applied), Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22) | STEP 90, 91 | SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION (Phil 2 pre-Pauline dating PROBABLE not proven) |
| C. Pauline evidence predates John (conventional chronology) | Paul c. 50-62; John c. 90-110 | Chronology matrix | SAFE (with PROBABLE date status) |
| D. Early Jesus devotion predates John | Devotion attested in Paul's received tradition; worship scenes even in Synoptics (Matt 2:11; 28:9,17; Luke 24:52) | STEP 91 §14-16 | SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION |

### 11.2 Anti-overcorrection

"John added nothing new" is **UNSAFE**: John's *distinctives* are genuine — (1) Logos-incarnation as literary frame; (2) absolute "I AM" self-revelation (8:58); (3) explicit pre-incarnate glory (17:5); (4) Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28); (5) mutual-indwelling unity formulations (10:38; 14:10-11). Whether these are *new concepts* or *new articulations of earlier concepts* is DISPUTED; claiming either extreme is methodologically unsafe.

## 12. TEST: "SYNOPTICS TEACH A MERELY HUMAN JESUS"

### 12.1 Binary Rejected

The claim "Synoptics = merely human Jesus, John = divine Jesus" fails on the grid itself:

* **Authority:** Synoptics show forgiveness (Mark 2:5-10), Sabbath lordship (Mark 2:28), temple authority (Mark 11), "all authority" (Matt 28:18) — these are *divine-authority markers*, not merely-human ethics.
* **Titles:** "Son of God" confessions (Mark 1:11; 3:11; 15:39; Matt 14:33; 16:16), Ps 110 "Lord" (Mark 12:36-37), Emmanuel (Matt 1:23), worship (Matt 28:9,17; Luke 24:52).
* **YHWH-adjacent acts:** calming chaos-waters (Mark 4:35-41; Matt 8:23-27; Luke 8:22-25 — Ps 89/107 echo), walking on sea with egō eimi (Mark 6:50; Matt 14:27).
* **Forgiveness & eschatological judgment:** Son of Man judge (Mark 8:38; Matt 16:27; Luke 21:36).
* **Sonship:** unique Father/Son pairing (Matt 11:27; Luke 10:22; Mark 13:32).

### 12.2 Honest Differences

| Dimension | Synoptics | John | Genuine difference? |
|-----------|-----------|------|---------------------|
| Pre-existence | implicit/absent | explicit | YES — explicitness (development) |
| Self-designation | indirect/secrecy | open "I AM" | YES — presentation |
| Consciousness of origin | no incarnation frame | descending redeemer | YES — literary frame |
| Father/Son theological articulation | minimal | maximal | YES — development |
| Worship/veneration | present, especially post-resurrection | climactic (20:28) | DEGREE, not kind |

**CONCLUSION: CONTINUITY in the substance of devotion/authority; THEOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT in explicitness and conceptual articulation. The "two different Jesuses" framing is a false binary.**

## 16. FORGIVENESS AND AUTHORITY — CROSS-GOSPEL

### 16.1 Passages

| Gospel | Passage | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Mark | 2:1-12 | Pronouncement + scribal objection + healing validation; "Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins" |
| Matthew | 9:2-8 | Same story; crowds "glorified God, who had given such authority to men" (9:8) |
| Luke | 5:17-26 | Same story; plus Luke 7:36-50 (woman's sins forgiven) |
| John | 8:11; 20:22-23 | "Go and sin no more" (8:11); post-resurrection "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven" (20:23) |

### 16.2 Analysis

* **Jesus exercises authority (Layer B/C claim):** SAFE — the pronouncement tradition is triple-attested (Mark/Matthew/Luke) and the Gospel authors all present it as authoritative.
* **Jesus is metaphysically identical with God (inference):** DISPUTED — three representable options (STEP 92 §12.2): (a) divine prerogative exercised by God's agent with delegated authority; (b) eschatological proclamation of God's mercy arriving in the Kingdom; (c) implicit divine-identity claim (Mark 2:7's "who can forgive sins but God alone?" + 2:10 Son of Man authority). The *text* demonstrates that the tradition registered the theological weight; it does not adjudicate option (c).
* John 20:22-23 transfers forgiveness-authority to the apostles — an *ecclesiastical* development distinct from Mark 2's pronouncement.

Neither "forgiving sins proves Jesus is God" nor "forgiving sins proves Jesus is only a prophet" is SAFE (STEP 92 §12.3).

## 17. WORSHIP AND DEVOTION — CROSS-GOSPEL

### 17.1 Evidence by Layer

| Context | Evidence | Gospels |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Homage during lifetime (proskynesis as fealty) | Magi (Matt 2:2,11); leper (Matt 8:2); ruler (Matt 9:18); woman (Matt 15:25); blind men (Matt 20:20?); Mark 5:6 demoniac; Mark 15:19 mockery | Matt/Mark — Semitic/royal homage idiom; proskynesis = bowing (can be to humans in LXX, e.g., 2 Sam 14:22; 1 Kgs 1:16) |
| Acclamation | Hosanna (Mark 11:9-10; Matt 21:9; Luke 19:38; John 12:13); "blessed is the King" | All four |
| Post-resurrection worship | Matt 28:9,17 ("they worshiped him"); Luke 24:52 ("they worshiped him"); John 20:28 (Thomas) | Matt/Luke/John — resurrection-devotion explicit |
| Prayer to Jesus | Acts 7:59-60 (Stephen: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit"); 9:10-16?; 1 Cor 1:2; 16:22 (Maranatha) | Acts + Paul — post-Easter liturgical crying |
| Baptism into name | Matt 28:19 triadic; Acts 2:38; 8:16; 19:5; 1 Cor 1:13-15 | Matthew/Acts/Paul |

### 17.2 Separation Rules

* **proskynesis ≠ automatic divine ontology:** Semitic homage to kings/prophets exists (LXX usage); context determines. Conversely, post-resurrection worship-scenes (Matt 28:9,17; Luke 24:52; John 20:28) are placed where ONLY God-worship fits — the evangelists present worship *without demurral*, which is striking, but the semantic category remains contextual.
* **Lifetime homage ≠ post-resurrection devotion:** the latter is theologically advanced (calling on the name; Maranatha; baptism-into-name) and attested in Paul (Layer B) — earlier than the Gospels' final forms.
* Do not collapse homage → worship → ontology in one step; likewise do not deny devotion because the word is proskynesis (§19, §31).

## 20. RESURRECTION — CROSS-GOSPEL

### 20.1 Textual-Critical Caution: Mark 16:9-20

* **Status:** The "longer ending" (Mark 16:9-20) is ABSENT from Codex Sinaiticus (SRC-MSS-002) and Codex Vaticanus (SRC-MS-002) — the two earliest complete witnesses in the store — and from other important witnesses; it is present in later majuscules (A, D, W) and most medieval minuscules. NA28/SBLGNT mark the passage (brackets). Eusebius/Marcion-era attestation (Irenaeus quotes it, c. 180 CE) indicates the longer ending existed by the late 2nd century.
* **Consequence:** Do NOT silently treat 16:9-20 as unquestionably original Mark. If used, LABEL: "longer ending — attested from late 2nd century (Irenaeus), absent from Sinaiticus/Vaticanus."
* Additional variants: 16:8 ending with "for they were afraid"; shorter alternative ending in some witnesses (k, it, etc.).

### 20.2 Layer Separation

| Layer | Content | Status |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Empty tomb tradition | Mark 16:1-8; Matt 28:1-10; Luke 24:1-12; John 20:1-10 | Multiple-attested narrative; historicity DISPUTED per method (widely considered plausibly early; not proof) |
| Appearance tradition | Matt 28:9-10,16-20; Luke 24:13-49; John 20:11-29; 21:1-23; 1 Cor 15:5-8 | Multiple + Pauline parallel; **1 Cor 15:5-8 is the earliest stored list (received tradition)** |
| Resurrection proclamation | 1 Cor 15:3-5 (believed); Acts 2; 4; 10; 13 (narrative) | ESTABLISHED as belief |
| Theological interpretation | Vindication (Acts 2:36; Rom 1:4); new creation (2 Cor 5); glorification (John 12:23; 13:31); self-raising (John 2:19; 10:18) | DEVELOPMENT layers |
| Bodily resurrection as event | 1 Cor 15:12-20 (Paul's argument for bodily form); Gospels' narratives | NOT ESTABLISHED BY HISTORICAL METHOD (STEP 92 §16; §31 boundary) |

**CONTROLS:** empty-tomb + appearances are *narrative claims*; their historical evaluation uses STEP 92 criteria; they neither establish nor refute the event's supernatural character. Luke's ascension (24:51) and Acts 1:9 are Lukan presentation (later narrative development) — distinct from Mark's silence and John's breathing-Spirit (20:22).

## 22. GOSPEL DEVELOPMENT — MODEL

```
Historical Jesus (STEP 92 baseline)
   ↓ CONTINUITY: Kingdom-proclaimer; healer; forgiveness-authority; Abba; Son of Man; crucified
Mark (c. 65-75)
   ↓ narrative high Christology: authority, sonship, judgment; no pre-existence; abrupt ending
Matthew (c. 80-90)
   ↓ + Emmanuel, worship, 11:27, 28:18-19 triadic, judgment scenes
Luke-Acts (c. 80-95)
   ↓ + Spirit, prayer, salvation-history, exaltation/appointment Christology, universal scope
John (c. 90-110)
   ↓ + Logos, pre-existence, "I AM", Father/Son unity, explicit divine acclamation
Irenaeus (c. 180)
   ↓ canonical fourfold Gospel; Logos; apostolic continuity (SRC-PAT-001)
Nicaea (325) → Constantinople (381) → Chalcedon (451)
   ↓ technical formulation: homoousios; Spirit; two natures (SRC-THEO-001/003/005/006)
```

**CONTROLS:** Do NOT assume linear theological evolution (Matthew is not simply "more" than Mark in every dimension; Mark's 14:61-62 is as high as anything in Matthew; Luke's exaltation-framework differs from John's incarnation-framework). Evaluate each relationship as: CONTINUITY / DEVELOPMENT / LITERARY DEPENDENCE / INDEPENDENT TRADITION / UNCERTAINTY.

| Relationship | Mark→Matthew | Mark→Luke | Synoptics→John |
|--------------|--------------|-----------|----------------|
| Continuity | HIGH (shared tradition, ~90% of Mark in Matthew) | HIGH (shared Markan core) | MEDIUM (shared passion/resurrection skeleton; different sayings) |
| Development | Theological heightening + Judaic framing | Salvation-history + Gentile scope | Explicit christological articulation |
| Literary dependence | PROBABLE (Markan priority working model) | PROBABLE (Markan priority + Q) | NOT ESTABLISHED (no demonstrated John-on-Synoptics dependence; only shared tradition) |
| Independent tradition | LIMITED (Matt follows Mark closely) | LIMITED | PROBABLE for some material (Lazarus? "I AM" background? shared passion core) |
| Uncertainty | Verbal agreements vs Q | Q hypothesis itself | John's sources entirely uncertain (no Q; possible signs-source, discourses-source — hypothetical) |

"Gospel = Nicaea" and "Nicaea = invention from nothing" are both rejected (STEP 93 §27; STEP 91 §18-20). Irenaeus (c. 180 CE) is an *intermediate* witness (SRC-PAT-001: fourfold Gospel; Logos; apostolic continuity) — bridging Gospel text to later doctrine.

## 26. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE

| Type | Meaning | Gospel application |
|------|---------|--------------------|
| Physical independence | Distinct manuscript objects | P66, P75, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus = physically distinct witnesses to John (and Luke for P75); they corroborate text-transmission, NOT four independent traditions |
| Literary independence | No literary copying | Mark-Matthew-Luke: literary relationship PROBABLE (Markan priority + Q); John vs Synoptics: NOT ESTABLISHED (no demonstrated copying) — possibly independent tradition |
| Oral-tradition independence | Separate streams before writing | Q vs Mark (probable); John's traditions (possible); 1 Cor 15 vs Gospels (probable relative independence) |
| Theological independence | Distinct viewpoints | Each evangelist has a distinct theological program (Markan secrecy; Matthean fulfillment; Lukan salvation-history; Johannine signs/glory) — independence of *perspective* ≠ independence of *tradition* |
| Historical independence | Separate historical sources | Tacitus, Josephus, Paul = historically independent of Gospels (non-Gospel streams); four Gospels ≠ four eyewitnesses automatically |

**CONTROLS:** Four Gospels ≠ automatically four independent eyewitnesses (Markan priority makes 2 of 3 Synoptic largely derivative; John's independence is real but not eyewitness-guaranteed). Four Gospels ≠ one source either (distinct traditions, redactions, and theological programs demonstrable). Counting witnesses requires arguing dependence/independence per-stream (STEP 91 §23; STEP 93 §28).

## 27. CLAIM TESTS (A–T)

### Claim A: "Mark has no high Christology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Mark contains multiple high-Christological indicators: sin-forgiveness authority, Son of Man eschatological judgment, "Son of God" disclosures, Yahwistic power-acts, Ps 110:1 lordship exegesis.

**Evidence:** Mark 2:5-10 (forgiveness); 8:38; 13:26; 14:62 (Son of Man judge); 1:11; 9:7 (divine audition); 3:11; 5:7 (demonic acclamation); 4:35-41 (chaos-waters); 6:50 (egō eimi); 12:36-37 (Ps 110); 15:39 (centurion confession).

**Reason:** "High Christology" is defined by indicators (STEP 91 §3), not by genre; Mark has authority-, filial-, judgment-, and lordship-Christology in narrative form. Only explicit pre-existence/Logos are absent — absence ≠ low.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Mark's Christology is narrative/functional, not ontological-explicit; if "high" is defined *exclusively* as "explicit divine-identity statements," then Mark's is implicit — but that definition is question-begging.

---

### Claim B: "Mark teaches a merely human Jesus."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Mark's Jesus exercises divine-type authority and receives supernatural acclamation incompatible with "merely human" presentation.

**Evidence:** Forgiveness (2:5-10); stilling the sea (4:35-41 — Ps 89:9/107:29 echo); walking on water with egō eimi (6:50); demons' recognition (1:24; 3:11; 5:7); transfiguration (9:2-8); eschatological judge (8:38; 14:62).

**Reason:** These acts and acclamations exceed prophetic-human categories in the text's own frame; "merely human" is a misreading of Mark's device (Mark is restrained but not reductionist).

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Mark does not *define* Jesus's ontology; the reader must integrate the data. The centurion's "Son of God" (15:39) could be royal-filial rather than ontological.

---

### Claim C: "Matthew contains divine Christological elements."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** Matthew presents Emmanuel ("God with us"), worship, "all authority," and unique Son-knowledge.

**Evidence:** Matt 1:23 (Emmanuel); 2:2,11 (magi worship); 11:27 (mutual knowledge); 14:33 (disciples worship + "Son of God"); 28:9,17 (resurrection worship); 28:18 ("all authority in heaven and on earth").

**Reason:** Worship-scenes + authority-maximalism + presence-theology constitute divine-element content within a Jewish monotheistic frame.

**Confidence:** HIGH that divine elements are present; per-element interpretation varies.

**Limitations:** "Divine Christological elements" ≠ "Nicene theology"; Emmanuel is presence-typology; proskynesis is homage-idiom.

---

### Claim D: "Matthew 28:19 proves Nicene Trinity."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Matt 28:19 is triadic baptismal language but contains no Nicene terminology (no ousia, homoousios, hypostasis) and functions as baptismal formula, not doctrinal definition.

**Evidence:** Text: "baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." Transmitted in Sinaiticus/Vaticanus/critical editions; shorter "in my name" variant attested (Eusebius tradition). Historical reception: Nicene exegesis *used* the verse.

**Reason:** Triad ≠ Nicene Trinity as defined (325/381); the verse evidences early triadic formula (Layer C), which the later councils systematized. The leap "triadic text → 381-CE dogma" is anachronistic (STEP 93 §5.2; STEP 91 §20).

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** The verse IS the strongest early triadic datum in the Gospels and was genuinely formative; rejecting it as "Nicene proof" does not reduce its triadic content — both claims stand together.

---

### Claim E: "Luke-Acts has high Christological elements."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** Luke-Acts presents risen-Lord devotion, exaltation-royalty, worship, universal Lordship, and eschatological judge-role.

**Evidence:** Luke 24:34 ("The Lord has risen"); 24:52 (worship); Acts 2:36 (made Lord and Christ); 4:12 (salvation in no other name); 7:56 (Son of Man at right hand); 10:36 (Lord of all); 10:42 (judge of living and dead); 7:59-60 (prayer to Lord Jesus).

**Reason:** High devotion and Lord-title application pervade Luke-Acts, within an exaltation-Christology framework.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Luke's high elements are functional/exaltation-shaped (appointed Lord by resurrection), not incarnation-shaped; absence of Logos/"I AM" = presentation difference, not denial.

---

### Claim F: "John contains explicit high Christology."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** John is the most explicit Gospel: Logos, pre-existence, "I AM" absolute, divine predicates, Father/Son unity, Thomas's acclamation.

**Evidence:** John 1:1-18 (P66/P75/Sinaiticus/Vaticanus/SBLGNT); 5:18 (making himself equal with God); 8:58 (before Abraham, I am); 10:30,38 (unity/indwelling); 17:5 (pre-world glory); 20:28 (My Lord and my God).

**Reason:** Direct textual data; no special pleading needed.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH.

**Limitations:** Explicitness ≠ historical-Jesus transcript (Layer C2); concepts traceable earlier in Paul as belief.

---

### Claim G: "John invented divine Christology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** The divine-Christology trajectory predates John (Pauline received traditions, early devotion, Synoptic authority/elevation material); John's role is explicitation and articulation.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:6 (Shema-reframe creation-agency); Phil 2:6-11 (pre-existence + YHWH homage); Rom 10:9-13 (Joel 2:32 applied); Maranatha (1 Cor 16:22); Matt 28:9,17; Luke 24:52 (worship); Matt 11:27//Luke 10:22 (Q Son-knowledge).

**Reason:** "Invented ex nihilo" contradicts the pre-Johannine evidence stream; the origin-question and the explicitness-question must be separated (§13).

**Confidence:** HIGH against "invented"; DISPUTED on "how much Johannine articulation is genuinely new conceptual development."

**Limitations:** "Not invented" ≠ "nothing new"; John's conceptual-literary development (Logos frame, absolute I AM, indwelling-unity) is real and distinctive.

---

### Claim H: "Synoptics and John teach contradictory Jesuses."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** The four Christologies share a common substructure (Messiah, crucified-raised, authority, sonship) and differ in emphasis, selection, and explicitness — not in logically incompatible claims.

**Evidence:** Comparison matrix (§9) — CONTINUITY on 13+ features; differences classed as EMPHASIS/DEVELOPMENT/TENSION; no demonstrated CONTRADICTION.

**Reason:** Differences (Kingdom prominence; pre-existence explicitness; temple timing; crucifixion-day) are presentation/development, not mutual denial; John's high claims are anticipated in Synoptic authority-material and Pauline tradition.

**Confidence:** HIGH that "contradictory Jesuses" is a false binary; DISPUTED on individual tension-resolutions (e.g., 2:19 vs Synoptics temple-logion; day-of-crucifixion).

**Limitations:** Genuine tensions exist (real, not imaginary); "no contradiction" is a claim about logical incompatibility, not about historical-consistency of every detail.

---

### Claim I: "John simply copied Paul."
**Classification:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Finding:** John and Paul share Christological concepts but no demonstrated literary dependence; John's idiom (Logos, signs, I AM, discourses) is distinct from Paul's (justification, Spirit, parousia).

**Evidence:** No Pauline citation in John; conceptual parallels (1 Cor 8:6 ↔ John 1:3; Phil 2 ↔ John 17 pre-existence) but different literary forms; STEP 91 §13 classified John-Paul dependence NOT ESTABLISHED.

**Reason:** Shared tradition (early confession, hymns, Wisdom/Word background) explains convergence without copying; "simply copied" overstates an unproven relation.

**Confidence:** HIGH that copying is NOT ESTABLISHED; DISPUTED whether any dependence exists at all.

**Limitations:** Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence; both may share lost common sources.

---

### Claim J: "Paul simply copied John."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Conventional dating places Paul (c. 50-62) before John (c. 90-110); Paul could not have copied John.

**Evidence:** Chronology matrix (§2); no reference to Johannine texts in Paul; Paul's traditions are independently early (1 Cor 15:3-5 received).

**Reason:** Chronological impossibility under conventional dating; even under minority datings, no dependence evidence exists.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Both dates are PROBABLE; but the direction-of-copy claim is chronologically untenable in the standard framework.

---

### Claim K: "Early Jesus devotion predates John."
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION

**Finding:** Devotion to Jesus (prayer, invocation, baptism-into-name, hymns, confession) is attested 40-60 years before John's composition.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 1:2 (calling on the Lord's name); 11:23-26 (eucharist remembrance); 16:22 (Maranatha — Aramaic); Phil 2:6-11 (hymn); Rom 10:9-13 (confession + Joel-application); 2 Cor 12:8 (prayer to Lord).

**Reason:** Pauline letters (c. 50-62) contain received liturgical/confessional material predating John by decades.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** The exact origin-date of each devotion-fragment is inference; "predates John" ≠ "from the historical Jesus's lifetime."

---

### Claim L: "Jesus's divinity is absent before John."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Divine-identity elements (creation-agency, YHWH-text application, worship, Shema-reframe) are present in Paul and Synoptics before/independent of John.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:6; Phil 2:6-11; Rom 10:13; Matt 1:23; 14:33; 28:9,17; Mark 2:5-10; 14:62; Luke 24:52; Q-saying 11:27//10:22.

**Reason:** The claim ignores pre-Johannine texts; "divinity" is attested as belief/function well before John, even if not as explicitly as John states it.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** "Divinity present" at Layer C (documents/traditions) — at Layer A (Jesus's self-understanding) it remains NOT ESTABLISHED (STEP 92); the two must not be conflated.

---

### Claim M: "Multiple attestation proves historicity."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Multiple attestation strengthens a tradition's claim to early/wide distribution but does not automatically prove it historical.

**Evidence:** STEP 92 §3 (criteria defeasible, cumulative); examples of multiple-attested-but-questionable traditions exist in ancient literature; single-source items can be historical.

**Reason:** A widely spread claim can be false; attestation measures distribution, not truth. The criterion supports *early believability* — not *veridicality*.

**Confidence:** HIGH (methodological).

**Limitations:** The criterion is a legitimate *indicator* used in conjunction with others; "not automatic proof" ≠ "useless."

---

### Claim N: "Single attestation proves later invention."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Single-attested material (e.g., Logos-prologue; Matt 1:23 only; Luke's 24:52 worship is also singular) may be early or late; singularity alone proves nothing.

**Evidence:** John's prologue (single Gospel) contains conceptual parallels in Paul (1 Cor 8:6; Wis-tradition background) — conceptual multilocation despite single literary location; single-attested sayings (e.g., Luke 19:10) are independently plausible.

**Reason:** Selection/omission by evangelists is driven by theology and audience, not just authenticity; absence from other Gospels ≠ late.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Singleton material needs *more* scrutiny (embarrassment/coherence checks); scrutiny ≠ automatic rejection.

---

### Claim O: "Gospel differences automatically prove contradiction."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Differences in emphasis, selection, vocabulary, chronology, and explicitness are not logical contradictions; each must be classified (STEP 93 §21 A-G).

**Evidence:** Temple action (John 2 vs Synoptics end-of-ministry) — chronology/selection difference; crucifixion day (Synoptics vs John) — real tension, not demonstrated incompatibility; Kingdom-prominence (John minimal) — emphasis.

**Reason:** Contradiction requires mutual denial of a truth-claim; differences in arrangement/timing/explicitness do not meet that bar.

**Confidence:** HIGH methodologically; DISPUTED on individual cases.

**Limitations:** Some tensions ARE unresolved (day-of-Passover; 27:52-53 saints; 18:28-29 vs trial order) — "not demonstrated contradiction" is not "harmonization established."

---

### Claim P: "Gospel differences automatically prove harmony."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Differences are real and sometimes irreducible to neat harmonization; forced harmony is as problematic as forced contradiction.

**Evidence:** Temple-cleansing placement; one- vs two-trial variants across Gospels; resurrection-appearance sequences (Galilee vs Jerusalem) resist simple merging; day-of-crucifixion problem remains.

**Reason:** Each Gospel has an independent literary-theological program; harmonization requires argument (source-critical, redactional), not assertion.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Many apparent tensions DO have plausible solutions (chronological frame, distinct visits, literary devices); "not automatic harmony" ≠ "no harmony possible."

---

### Claim Q: "Gospel Christology is identical to Nicene theology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Gospels contain high Christology in Jewish narrative/cultic categories; Nicaea uses Greek metaphysical vocabulary (homoousios, ousia) absent from the Gospels.

**Evidence:** Grid §5.2/§27 matrix: ousia/hypostasis/homoousios ABSENT from all four Gospels; Matt 28:19 triadic but pre-Nicene-vocabulary.

**Reason:** Identity-claim fails on vocabulary, genre, and historical distance (Gospels c. 65-110; Nicaea 325). Development ≠ identity (STEP 91 §21).

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** Nicaea self-consciously drew on Johannine/Pauline texts; a *trajectory-relation* exists — but trajectory ≠ identity.

---

### Claim R: "Nicene Christology was invented from nothing."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Nicene formulation formalizes trajectories present in Gospels/Paul (pre-existence, creation-agency, divine sonship, worship).

**Evidence:** John 1:1-3, 8:58, 10:30; Phil 2:6-11; 1 Cor 8:6; Matt 28:19 triadic; worship-tradition; Irenaeus (SRC-PAT-001) intermediate reception.

**Reason:** "From nothing" contradicts the documentable pre-Nicene trajectory (STEP 91 Claim P rejected); councils made *terminological decisions*, not ex nihilo creation.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limitations:** The councils made genuinely new *terminological* commitments (homoousios as normative; anathemas) and selected among competing readings (Arian alternative) — "formalization of a trajectory" ≠ "predetermined outcome."

---

### Claim S: "High Christology developed before Nicene terminology."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Finding:** High Christology (devotion, worship, divine predicates, pre-existence-belief) is attested in 1st-century texts; Nicene terminology (homoousios etc.) is 4th-century.

**Evidence:** 1 Cor 8:6; Phil 2; Rom 10:9-13; Maranatha; John 1:1, 8:58, 20:28 (P66/P75 witnesses); Matt 28:19; Irenaeus c. 180.

**Reason:** Chronological sequence is documentable: high Christology in 1st-century witnesses; technical terms only in 4th-century conciliar texts.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH.

**Limitations:** "Before terminology" ≠ "identical in content to Nicene dogma"; early language was functional/Jewish, later language metaphysical.

---

### Claim T: "Gospel evidence alone settles Jesus's metaphysical ontology."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Gospel evidence attests beliefs, claims, devotion, and texts; metaphysical ontology (whether Jesus "was" God) is a theological conclusion requiring interpretation-premises beyond historical-textual analysis.

**Evidence:** STEP 92 §28 (historical/theological boundary); STEP 91 §26 (method limits); Gospel texts admit multiple coherent interpretations (agency, identity, typological) at Layer A/C boundary.

**Reason:** The texts are evidence *for beliefs*; ontology requires additional premises (revelation, theological systematics). The audit classifies claims, not the metaphysical fact itself.

**Confidence:** HIGH (methodological boundary).

**Limitations:** This does not mean the texts are irrelevant to theology — they are the church's foundational witness; it means *historical method alone* does not complete the theological argument.

## 28. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### SIDE A — "John represents a natural culmination of an already high Christological tradition."

**Strongest case:**

1. **Pre-Johannine evidence is substantial and early:** 1 Cor 8:6 (creation-agency inside a Shema-reframe, c. 53-55 CE, in received tradition's neighborhood), Phil 2:6-11 (pre-existence + every-knee-bow applied to Jesus — Isa 45:23 YHWH homage), Rom 10:9-13 (Joel 2:32 "call on the name of the Lord" = Jesus), Maranatha (Aramaic liturgical cry). Paul's letters — the earliest documents — already move Jesus into YHWH's functional space.
2. **Synoptics are already "high":** Mark 2:5-10 (forgiveness — divine prerogative flagged in-text); Mark 14:62 (Son of Man at the right hand of Power, coming on clouds — Dan 7 + Ps 110); Matt 1:23 (Emmanuel); Matt 11:27//Luke 10:22 (Q: mutual Father/Son knowledge); Matt 28:9,17 and Luke 24:52 (unambiguous worship of the risen Jesus); Matt 28:18 ("all authority" — the maximal Synoptic claim).
3. **Devotionally, the movement went "high" within ~2 decades** (Maranatha; baptism-into-name; calling-on-name) — such explosive cultic practice demands an antecedent trajectory, not a late accretion (STEP 91 §14-16).
4. **John's distinctive vocabulary maps onto earlier concepts:** Logos (Wisdom/Word tradition — Prov 8; Wis; Philo-adjacent); "I AM" (Exod 3:14 LXX idiom; Deut 32:39; Isa 43:10) — the raw materials were in the Jewish scriptural and Pauline toolbox; John *assembled* them.
5. **Irenaeus (c. 180) and the canonical reception** treated the fourfold Gospel (including John) as apostolic tradition (SRC-PAT-001) — the early church saw John as culmination-of-tradition, not innovation.
6. **The credal language of Nicaea** ("begotten before all ages... through whom all things were made") quotes John 1:3/1 Cor 8:6 near-verbatim — the councils believed they were formalizing, not inventing.

**Verdict on A:** Strongest on the *trajectory* claim: high Christology demonstrably predates John in multiple independent streams, and John's elements are assembled from available Jewish/Pauline categories. A's weakness: "natural culmination" can understate John's genuinely distinctive literary-theological *construction* (absolute I AM sayings as narrative device; the prologue's incarnational frame — no pre-Johannine text says "the Word became flesh").

---

### SIDE B — "John represents substantial theological development beyond the Synoptic and earliest Jesus traditions."

**Strongest case:**

1. **Explicitness is itself development:** No Synoptic text *states* "Jesus is God"; no pre-Pauline fragment states "the Word became flesh" or "before Abraham was, I am." John makes explicit (8:58; 20:28; 1:1,14) what earlier texts leave implicit or ambiguous. Making a claim explicit is a conceptual act.
2. **Narrative incarnation-frame is new:** The prologue (1:1-18) is unprecedented — a creation-to-incarnation christological narrative. Paul's pre-existence (Phil 2) is hymnic-functional; John's is *ontological-narrative* ("the Word became flesh and dwelt among us").
3. **The "I AM" absolute discourse-form is Johannine:** While egō eimi occurs in Mark 6:50/Matt 14:27 (self-identification on the water), John's absolute predication (8:24,28,58; 13:19) and the "I am the bread/light/gate..." series (6-11) are a rhetorical self-revelation scheme absent from the Synoptics and Paul.
4. **Soteriological and eschatological re-centering:** Kingdom → eternal life/personal union ("that they may be one... as we are one" 17:21-23); eschatology realized in encounter ("I am the resurrection and the life" 11:25). This is a *theological system*, not mere redaction.
5. **Historical-Jesus distance:** Layer A (STEP 92) does not establish that the historical Jesus made explicit divine-identity claims; John's discourse-material is Layer C2 literary construction. The *distance* between Jesus's ambivalent synoptic self-presentation and John's open "I AM" is a real historical-theological gap (40-80 years of development).
6. **Contrast with Luke's exaltation model:** Luke-Acts (2:36 "God has made him Lord and Christ") implies *appointment at resurrection*; John implies *eternal pre-existence* (17:5). These are *different christological models* — the difference is a real development between the streams, not mere explicitness of the same model.

**Verdict on B:** Strongest on the *development* claim: John adds a narrative-incarnational frame, an "I AM" discourse-scheme, an ontological re-centering, and systematic Father/Son theology that earlier texts lack. B's weakness: "substantial development" must not slide into "invention from nothing" — the components demonstrably pre-exist (A's evidence), so the question is *how much* of John is new articulation vs new conception, which is genuinely DISPUTED.

---

| Point | Side A | Side B |
|-------|--------|--------|

## 31. FINAL SYNTHESIS ($33 per spec)

1. **Shared by all/most Gospel traditions:** Messiah/Christ; crucifixion; resurrection-proclamation; Son of Man self-reference; Son of God filial/royal category; forgiveness-authority; miracle-tradition; worship-devotion; prayer to Father; Ps 110 lordship; eschatological judgment; Spirit-activity.
2. **Distinctive to Mark:** Messianic-secrecy motif; stark suffering-Son-of-Men emphasis; abrupt ending (16:8); implicit-yet-high authority Christology (5:41? no — the *question* "who then is this?" 4:41 as device); 14:61-62 self-claim-summit; the "gospel of the Son of God" frame.
3. **Distinctive to Matthew:** Emmanuel-inclusio (1:23 ↔ 28:20); magi worship; "all authority" commission + triadic baptismal formula (28:18-19); heightened Davidic genealogy; eschatological throne-judgment scenes (19:28; 25:31-46); "Kingdom of heaven" idiom; Torah-fulfillment antitheses.
4. **Distinctive to Luke:** Birth-annunciation christology (1:32-35); prayer-emphasis; Spirit/salvation-history engine; universal-Gentile scope; exaltation-appointment model (24:26,51; Acts 2:33,36); Gospel of the marginalized + "today" salvation (19:9; 23:43).
5. **Distinctive to John:** Logos-prologue (1:1-18); absolute "I AM" + "I am the..." series; signs-theology (7 semeia); explicit pre-existence (8:58; 17:5); Father/Son mutual-indwelling (10:38; 14:10-11); high-priestly prayer (17); Paraclete (14-16); Thomas-acclamation (20:28); realized eschatology (5:24; 11:25-26).
6. **Evidence predating the Gospels:** Pauline letters + received traditions (1 Cor 15:3-5; Phil 2 hymn; Rom 1:3-4; Maranatha; 1 Cor 8:6) — c. 50-62 CE; Aramaic liturgical retention; Q-material (shared Matt/Luke) likely earlier than their final forms; oral tradition (inferred).
7. **Specifically Johannine:** the *literary forms* (prologue; I AM scheme; discourse/argument structure; signs-arrangement); whether *concepts* are Johannine-origin is DISPUTED (pre-existence & creation-agency demonstrably earlier in Paul).
8. **Does John originate high Christology?** NO — NOT SUPPORTED as origin-claim (Claim G: UNSAFE); high Christology attested earlier (Paul, devotion, Synoptic-elements).
9. **Does John substantially develop it?** YES — explicitness, narrative-incarnation frame, I AM scheme, Father/Son systematization (Side B's strongest points); degree of conceptual-novelty DISPUTED.
10. **Are the Synoptics genuinely "low Christology"?** NO — they contain high-but-implicit/narrative Christology (authority, judgment, worship, sonship, YHWH-adjacent acts); "low vs high" binary rejected (STEP 93 §12, §31).
11. **How does Paul fit?** Paul is the earliest literary attestation of the high-Christology trajectory (received + developed), running parallel to and ahead of the Gospels; Gospels flesh out the narrative of the figure Paul proclaims (STEP 90).
12. **Continuity:** Messiah; Lordship-confession; death-resurrection; sonship; divine agency within monotheism; worship/devotion; forgiveness; judgment — all present from earliest attestation through the Gospels.
13. **Development:** explicitness (pre-existence, divine predicates, I AM); narrative form (from hymns/formulas to Gospels); conceptual articulation (Logos; Father/Son system; Spirit-theology); later formalization (homoousios; three hypostases; two natures).
14. **Disputed:** John's dependence on/uniqueness vs Synoptics; authenticity of Matt 11:27/Q 10:22; Son of Man semantics; day-of-crucifixion harmonization; Mark's original ending; the historical reach of "I AM" sayings; whether Johannine development is explicitation or new conception; 28:19 variant-history; Luke 23:34/22:43-44 variants.
15. **Cannot be established (by stored evidence):** exact composition dates beyond ranges; John's exact sources; the resurrection as event; Jesus's metaphysical ontology; the historical Jesus's explicit divine self-claim; the psychological origin-quality of early devotion.

## 32. FINAL CONCLUSION ($34 per spec)

**EARLIEST SHARED CHRISTOLOGY:** Jesus — Messiah/Christ, crucified, raised; Son of Man self-reference; filial relation to the Father; forgiveness-and-judgment authority; object of post-Easter devotion (Maranatha, confession, baptism-into-name, hymnody) within undivided Jewish monotheism. Attested from the earliest layer of the store (1 Cor 15:3-5 received formula; Phil 2:6-11; Rom 10:9-13) and shared across all four Gospels.

**SYNOPTIC CHRISTOLOGY:** Narrative-functional high Christology — divine-type authority (forgiveness, Sabbath lordship, stilling chaos, eschatological judge), filial sonship (Abba; 11:27//10:22), royal messianism (Ps 2; Ps 110), and post-resurrection worship (Matt 28:9,17; Luke 24:52) — without explicit pre-existence, Logos, or "I AM" formulations.

**JOHANNINE DEVELOPMENT:** The most explicit christological articulation — Logos-incarnation (1:1-18), absolute "I AM" (8:58), explicit pre-existence and pre-world glory (17:5), Father/Son mutual indwelling and unity (10:30,38; 14:9-11), Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28) — developed as *articulation and narrative embodiment* of a trajectory already attested in Paul and the Synoptic devotion-stream, with genuinely distinctive literary-theological construction.

**PAULINE CHRISTOLOGY:** The earliest written high Christology — Shema-reframing creation-agency (1 Cor 8:6), pre-existence and universal homage (Phil 2:6-11), Joel-YHWH salvation-application (Rom 10:9-13), cosmic atonement, Son of God in power (Rom 1:4) — inherited (1 Cor 15:3-5; Maranatha) and developed (justification; Gentile mission; explicit YHWH-text use). Paul and John independently articulate overlapping high Christology with no demonstrated copying (STEP 91 §11-13).

**CONTINUITY:** Messiah, Lordship, death-resurrection, sonship, divine agency within Jewish monotheism, worship-devotion, forgiveness, judgment — the substance of Christological confession is continuous from the earliest attestation (1 Cor 15; Phil 2; Maranatha) through all four Gospels to Irenaeus.

**DEVELOPMENT:** Explicitness (implicit → explicit divine predicates; Synoptics → John); literary form (formulas/hymns → narratives → theologically-systematic Gospel); conceptual articulation (Logos; Father/Son; Paraclete; eternal pre-existence); eschatological re-centering (Kingdom → life/personal union in John); later formalization in conciliar metaphysics.

**DISPUTED:** Whether John's distinctives are explicitation or new conception; authenticity of Q 11:27 (=Matt 11:27); Son of Man eschatological semantics; Mark's original ending; day-of-crucifixion harmonization; the historical reach of "I AM" to Jesus; Gospel compositional hypotheses (Q; Johannine source-theories); several textual variants (Luke 23:34; 22:43-44; Mark 1:1; 16:9-20).

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** Gospel composition dates beyond scholarly ranges; John's sources; the resurrection as event; Jesus's metaphysical ontology; Jesus's explicit divine self-claim (Layer A); direct literary dependence between John and Paul or John and the Synoptics; any Nicene-Gospel identity.

**NICENE FORMALIZATION:** The trajectory of divine Christology — attested in John's "the Word was God," Paul's Shema-reframe, the worship of the risen Jesus, and the triadic baptismal formula — was terminologically fixed as homoousios ("of one substance") at Nicaea (325), integrated with pneumatology at Constantinople (381), and given two-nature metaphysics at Chalcedon (451). Faithful formalization of a trajectory; not invention from nothing (Claim R: UNSAFE) — and not identical to the Gospel texts that preceded it (Claim Q: UNSAFE).
