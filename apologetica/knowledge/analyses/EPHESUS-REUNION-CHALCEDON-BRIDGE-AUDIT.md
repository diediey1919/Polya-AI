# EPHESUS 431 → REUNION 433 → CHALCEDON 451
## CHRISTOLOGICAL BRIDGE FORENSIC AUDIT

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This audit performs a cross-source forensic bridge analysis connecting three pivotal Christological events:

1. **Council of Ephesus (431)** — Deposition of Nestorius; approval of Cyril's Second Letter; Theotokos affirmed
2. **Formula of Reunion (433)** — Reconciliation between Cyril and John of Antioch; "union of two natures" terminology
3. **Council of Chalcedon (451)** — Definition of "one person in two natures"; four adverbs; Leo's Tome approved

**Key Findings:**
- Significant theological continuity exists across all three events, but terminology developed and became more precise
- The Twelve Anathemas received no formal conciliar approval at any of these three stages (only at Constantinople II, 553)
- Cyril's theology can be read as compatible with Chalcedon under certain terminological frameworks, but this is not automatic
- The relationship between *mia physis* (one nature) and *en duo physeisin* (in two natures) represents terminological development, not necessarily contradiction
- Later Chalcedonian or Oriental Orthodox terminology should not be retrojected into earlier stages

## I. THREE-STAGE TIMELINE

### Stage A: Ephesus 431

**Historical Context:**
- Nestorius (Archbishop of Constantinople) questioned Theotokos terminology
- Cyril of Alexandria led opposition to Nestorius
- Pope Celestine I condemned Nestorius at Roman synod (430)
- Emperor Theodosius II convoked council at Ephesus
- John of Antioch and Eastern bishops delayed in travel

**Principal Participants:**
- Cyril of Alexandria (president)
- Nestorius (accused, refused to attend)
- Memnon of Ephesus (host)
- John of Antioch (arrived late, convened rival council)
- Papal legates (arrived after Session I)

**Documents:**
- Cyril's Second Letter (*Intelligo quosdam*) — APPROVED as Nicene orthodoxy
- Cyril's Third Letter (*Cum Salvator noster*) with Twelve Anathemas — PRESENT in Acts, likely read, NO formal conciliar vote of approval
- Nestorius's Second Letter — CONDEMNED as contrary to Nicene faith
- Patristic florilegium — read as standard of orthodoxy
- Nicene Creed (325) — read as doctrinal baseline

**Theological Terminology:**
- Theotokos (God-bearer) — affirmed
- Hypostatic union — Cyril's terminology, not formally defined
- Mia physis — Cyril's formula, not conciliar terminology
- Physis, hypostasis, prosopon — used but not yet fixed in technical sense

**Formal Decisions:**
- Nestorius deposed and anathematized
- Cyril's Second Letter approved (124-126 bishops attested conformity)
- Third Letter/Twelve Anathemas present but not formally approved
- John of Antioch + 34 bishops excommunicated (not deposed)

**Unresolved Disputes:**
- Status of Twelve Anathemas (no formal approval)
- Relationship between Alexandria and Antioch (schism continued)
- Precise meaning of hypostatic union (not defined)
- Whether "two natures" language acceptable (not addressed)

**Later Reception:**
- Cyril's council eventually accepted as Third Ecumenical Council (Sixtus III, 432)
- Formula of Reunion (433) healed schism with Antioch
- Twelve Anathemas remained in limbo until Constantinople II (553)

---

### Stage B: Formula of Reunion 433

**Historical Context:**
- Alexandria and Antioch remained out of communion after Ephesus 431
- Nestorius in exile (first monastery, later Great Oasis)
- Cyril wrote defenses of Twelve Anathemas
- Theodoret of Cyrrhus wrote extensively against Cyril's Anathemas
- Paul of Emesa and Acacius of Beroea mediated
- Imperial pressure for reconciliation

**Principal Participants:**
- Cyril of Alexandria
- John of Antioch
- Paul of Emesa (mediator)
- Acacius of Beroea (mediator)
- Theodoret of Cyrrhus (likely drafter of Formula)

**Documents:**
- Cyril's Letter 39 (*Laetentur caeli*) — vehicle for Formula
- Formula of Reunion — drafted by Antiochenes, accepted by Cyril
- Cyril's Letter to Eulogius — defense of acceptance
- Cyril's Letter to Acacius — clarification of position

**Theological Terminology:**
- Union of two natures (*henōsis dyo physeōn*) — AFFIRMED
- Theotokos — AFFIRMED with qualification
- Perfect God and perfect man — AFFIRMED
- Double consubstantiality — AFFIRMED
- Distinction principle (person vs. nature predication) — AFFIRMED
- Mia physis — NOT MENTIONED
- Twelve Anathemas — NOT MENTIONED

**Formal Decisions:**
- Cyril and John reconciled
- Formula accepted as basis for communion
- Twelve Anathemas not required as condition
- Nestorius's deposition accepted

**Unresolved Disputes:**
- Whether "two natures" persist after union (ambiguous)
- Status of Twelve Anathemas (neither affirmed nor rejected)
- Precise meaning of hypostatic union vs. prosopic union
- Whether Theotokos implies full communicatio idiomatum

**Later Reception:**
- Formula read and approved at Chalcedon (451)
- Used as bridge between Cyrillian and Antiochene traditions
- Twelve Anathemas continued to be debated

---

### Stage C: Chalcedon 451

**Historical Context:**
- Eutyches controversy (448-449) — monophysitism
- Robber Council of Ephesus (449) — Eutyches vindicated, Leo's Tome rejected
- Emperor Marcian convoked new council
- Pope Leo I's Tome to Flavian (449) — key theological document
- Need to address both Nestorianism and Eutychianism

**Principal Participants:**
- Emperor Marcian and Empress Pulcheria
- Papal legates (representing Leo I)
- Dioscorus of Alexandria (Cyril's successor, deposed)
- Anatolius of Constantinople
- 600+ bishops (mostly Eastern)

**Documents:**
- Definition of Chalcedon — primary Christological statement
- Leo's Tome to Flavian — APPROVED as orthodox
- Cyril's Second Letter — REAFFIRMED
- Cyril's Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas — NOT READ as independent document
- Formula of Reunion — READ and AFFIRMED
- Nicene Creed (325) and Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed (381) — read as standards

**Theological Terminology:**
- One person/hypostasis in two natures (*en duo physeisin*) — DEFINED
- Four adverbs: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation — DEFINED
- Theotokos — AFFIRMED
- Double homoousios — AFFIRMED
- Physis, hypostasis, prosopon, ousia — TECHNICAL DEFINITIONS FIXED
- Mia physis — NOT USED (Cyril's formula not adopted)

**Formal Decisions:**
- Eutyches deposed and condemned
- Dioscorus deposed
- Definition of Chalcedon promulgated
- Leo's Tome approved
- Cyril's Second Letter reaffirmed
- Nestorius's condemnation upheld
- Theodoret and Ibas rehabilitated (after condemning Nestorius)

**Unresolved Disputes:**
- Relationship between Chalcedon and Cyril's *mia physis* formula
- Whether Chalcedon contradicted Cyril or clarified him
- Status of Twelve Anathemas (still not formally approved)
- Miaphysite vs. Dyophysite interpretations (schism would follow)

**Later Reception:**
- Accepted as Fourth Ecumenical Council by Chalcedonian churches
- Rejected by Oriental Orthodox (Miaphysite) churches
- Constantinople II (553) attempted reconciliation, reaffirmed Twelve Anathemas

## III. CORE CHRISTOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENTS

### 3A. One Christ / One Son

**Ephesus 431:**
- Cyril's Second Letter emphasizes unity: "one Christ, one Son, one Lord"
- Nestorius accused of dividing Christ into two persons
- Council condemns Nestorius's teaching as contrary to Nicene faith

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula affirms: "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Only Begotten Son of God"
- Maintains unity language
- Does not address Nestorian division explicitly (Nestorius already deposed)

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition affirms: "one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten"
- Explicitly rejects division into two persons
- Upholds Nestorius's condemnation

**Assessment:** CONTINUITY. All three stages affirm the unity of Christ's person.

---

### 3B. True God and True Man

**Ephesus 431:**
- Nicene Creed establishes Christ as "true God from true God"
- Cyril's Second Letter affirms full divinity and full humanity
- Nestorius accused of undermining unity by overemphasizing distinction

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula explicitly states: "perfect God and perfect man, consisting in a rational soul and flesh"
- Double consubstantiality: "consubstantial with the Father according to his divinity, and consubstantial with us according to his humanity"

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition states: "truly God and truly man"
- Reaffirms double consubstantiality
- Adds precision: "like us in all things, sin apart"

**Assessment:** CONTINUITY WITH DEVELOPMENT. Full divinity and humanity consistently affirmed, but Formula and Chalcedon add terminological precision.

---

### 3C. Theotokos

**Ephesus 431:**
- Theotokos central issue
- Cyril insists Mary bore God the Word incarnate
- Nestorius questioned Theotokos, preferred Christotokos
- Council affirms Theotokos (implicit in Nestorius's condemnation)

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula affirms Theotokos with qualification: "we confess the holy Virgin to be the Mother of God (Theotokon), because God the Word was incarnate and became man"
- Adds explanatory clause: "from the very conception he united the temple taken from her with himself"

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition affirms: "born of Mary the Virgin Theotokos as to the Manhood"
- Maintains Theotokos but with precision: according to humanity

**Assessment:** CONTINUITY WITH DEVELOPMENT. Theotokos consistently affirmed, but later stages add precision regarding what Theotokos entails (incarnation, union from conception).

---

### 3D. Union Terminology

**Ephesus 431:**
- Cyril uses "hypostatic union" (henōsis hypostatikē)
- Insists on one incarnate nature of God the Word (mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē)
- Rejects Nestorius's "prosopic union" as insufficient

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula uses "union of two natures" (henōsis dyo physeōn)
- Does NOT use "hypostatic union" explicitly
- Does NOT use "mia physis" formula
- Ambiguous on whether two natures persist after union

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition uses "one hypostasis" (mia hypostasis)
- Defines Christ as "in two natures" (en duo physeisin)
- Adds four adverbs: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation
- Does NOT use "mia physis" formula

**Assessment:** TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT. All three affirm union, but terminology shifts from Cyril's "hypostatic union" and "mia physis" to Chalcedon's "one hypostasis in two natures."

---

### 3E. Physis / Hypostasis / Prosopon

**Ephesus 431:**
- Cyril uses physis in archaic sense (concrete reality, akin to hypostasis)
- Cyril: "one incarnate nature" = one concrete incarnate reality
- Nestorius uses physis to mean nature with distinct properties
- Terminology not yet fixed in technical conciliar sense

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula uses "two natures" (dyo physeis)
- Does not define physis precisely
- Uses "one person" (prosōpon) but not hypostasis
- Terminology still fluid

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition fixes terminology:
  - Physis = nature (set of properties)
  - Hypostasis = person (concrete subsistence)
  - Prosopon = person (used synonymously with hypostasis)
- Christ: one hypostasis, two physeis
- Technical definitions established

**Assessment:** TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT. Same words used, but semantic range becomes more precise and technical at Chalcedon.

---

### 3F. Mia Physis vs. Two Natures

**Ephesus 431:**
- Cyril's formula: "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē)
- Cyril uses physis to mean concrete reality, not abstract nature
- Formula emphasizes unity over duality

**Reunion 433:**
- Formula does NOT use "mia physis"
- Formula uses "union of two natures"
- Cyril accepts Formula, interpreting "two natures" as referring to the two natures FROM WHICH union occurred
- Cyril continues to use "mia physis" in later writings

**Chalcedon 451:**
- Definition does NOT use "mia physis"
- Definition uses "in two natures" (en duo physeisin)
- Emphasizes duality persisting after union
- Does not explicitly reject "mia physis" but does not adopt it

**Assessment:** DISPUTED. Three interpretations possible:

1. **Contradiction:** Mia physis and two natures are fundamentally incompatible (Oriental Orthodox critique of Chalcedon)
2. **Complementarity:** Mia physis emphasizes unity, two natures emphasizes distinction; both needed (Chalcedonian interpretation)
3. **Terminological Framework Difference:** Cyril and Chalcedon use physis with different semantic ranges; under Cyril's framework, "one nature" = one concrete reality; under Chalcedon's framework, "two natures" = two sets of properties (scholarly consensus)

**Critical Observation:** The relationship cannot be reduced to simple contradiction or simple identity. The evidence shows terminological development where the same word (physis) acquired more technical precision over time.

## IV. THE TWELVE ANATHEMAS: CROSS-STAGE TRACKING

**Five-Category Framework Applied Across Three Stages:**

### Stage A: Ephesus 431

**(A) Textual Presence:** PRESENT — Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas appears in Acts

**(B) Reading:** LIKELY — placement in Acts and testimony of bishops suggest reading

**(C) Theological Endorsement:** IMPLICIT — council's Cyrilline orientation suggests endorsement, but not formally recorded

**(D) Formal Conciliar Approval:** NOT RECORDED — no comparable vote to Second Letter's 124-126 attestations

**(E) Later Conciliar Reception:** PENDING — would receive explicit reception at Constantinople II (553)

### Stage B: Reunion 433

**(A) Textual Presence:** PRESENT — remained in Cyril's corpus and documentary record

**(B) Reading:** NOT REQUIRED — Formula does not read or recite Anathemas

**(C) Theological Endorsement:** NOT GIVEN — Formula does not quote or cite Anathemas

**(D) Formal Conciliar Approval:** NOT GIVEN — Formula is bilateral agreement, not council decree

**(E) Later Conciliar Reception:** PENDING — still awaiting explicit reception

### Stage C: Chalcedon 451

**(A) Textual Presence:** PRESENT — Third Letter in documentary tradition

**(B) Reading:** NOT READ as independent document — unlike Second Letter, Third Letter not formally read

**(C) Theological Endorsement:** NOT EXPLICIT — Chalcedon does not quote or cite Anathemas

**(D) Formal Conciliar Approval:** NOT GIVEN — Chalcedon does not approve Anathemas as conciliar decree

**(E) Later Conciliar Reception:** PENDING — would receive explicit reception at Constantinople II (553)

**Cross-Stage Assessment:** The Twelve Anathemas remained textually present throughout all three stages but received no formal conciliar approval at any of them. Their status was consistently ambiguous — neither formally approved nor formally rejected — until Constantinople II (553).

## V. CYRIL ACROSS THREE STAGES

### 5A. Cyril at Ephesus 431

**Position:**
- Chief prosecutor of Nestorius
- Council president
- Insisted on hypostatic union and mia physis
- Demanded Nestorius's deposition
- Wrote Twelve Anathemas

**Key Formulations:**
- Second Letter: emphasizes unity, Theotokos, hypostatic union
- Third Letter: Twelve Anathemas attacking Nestorian division
- Mia physis formula: "one incarnate nature of God the Word"

**Outcome:**
- Nestorius deposed
- Second Letter approved
- Third Letter/Twelve Anathemas present but not formally approved
- John of Antioch's counter-council deposed Cyril

### 5B. Cyril at Reunion 433

**Position:**
- Accepted Formula of Reunion
- Reconciled with John of Antioch
- Defended acceptance in letters to Eulogius and Acacius

**Key Formulations:**
- Accepted "union of two natures" language
- Interpreted "two natures" as referring to the two natures FROM WHICH union occurred
- Continued to use "mia physis" in later writings
- Never renounced Twelve Anathemas

**Outcome:**
- Schism healed
- Formula accepted as basis for communion
- Twelve Anathemas not required as condition
- Cyril's Christology remained intact (in his interpretation)

### 5C. Cyril and Chalcedon 451

**Cyril's Status:**
- Cyril died in 444 (seven years before Chalcedon)
- Chalcedon read Cyril's Second Letter and approved it
- Chalcedon did NOT read Third Letter/Twelve Anathemas as independent document
- Chalcedon's Definition can be read as compatible with Cyril under certain interpretations

**Key Question:** Is Chalcedon Cyrillian or anti-Cyrillian?

**Three Interpretations:**

1. **Chalcedon is Cyrillian:**
   - Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter
   - Chalcedon's "one hypostasis" = Cyril's hypostatic union
   - Chalcedon's "two natures" compatible with Cyril if understood correctly
   - Chalcedon upheld Nestorius's condemnation (Cyril's primary goal)

2. **Chalcedon is Anti-Cyrillian:**
   - Chalcedon used "two natures" language Cyril resisted
   - Chalcedon did not use "mia physis" formula
   - Chalcedon rehabilitated Theodoret and Ibas (Cyril's opponents)
   - Chalcedon's terminology closer to Antiochene tradition

3. **Chalcedon is Development of Cyril:**
   - Chalcedon preserved Cyril's core insights (unity, Theotokos, anti-Nestorian)
   - Chalcedon added terminological precision (two natures, four adverbs)
   - Chalcedon addressed new controversy (Eutychianism) Cyril did not face
   - Chalcedon represents legitimate development, not contradiction

**Assessment:** The evidence supports interpretation #3 (development) but acknowledges that interpretations #1 and #2 are not without merit. The relationship is complex and cannot be reduced to simple identity or simple contradiction.

## VI. NESTORIUS ACROSS THREE STAGES

### 6A. Nestorius at Ephesus 431

**Position:**
- Archbishop of Constantinople
- Accused of denying Theotokos
- Refused to attend council (three summonses rejected)
- Claimed Cyril was "accuser and judge"

**Key Formulations:**
- Questioned Theotokos, preferred Christotokos
- Emphasized distinction between divine and human
- Accused of teaching two persons (Cyril's characterization)
- Bazaar of Heracleides (later work) defends prosopic union

**Outcome:**
- Deposed and anathematized
- Exiled (first monastery, later Great Oasis)
- Condemnation upheld by later councils

### 6B. Nestorius at Reunion 433

**Position:**
- Already deposed and in exile
- Not party to Formula of Reunion
- No evidence he accepted Formula

**Key Formulations:**
- Bazaar of Heracleides written during exile
- Defends his position as orthodox
- Claims Cyril misunderstood him

**Outcome:**
- Not rehabilitated by Formula
- Deposition accepted by both Cyril and John

### 6C. Nestorius at Chalcedon 451

**Position:**
- Still in exile (died ~450, just before Chalcedon)
- Condemnation upheld by Chalcedon
- Not rehabilitated

**Key Formulations:**
- Chalcedon explicitly rejects Nestorianism
- Definition's "one person" language anti-Nestorian
- Chalcedon maintains Nestorius's condemnation

**Outcome:**
- Nestorianism condemned
- Nestorius's deposition upheld
- Church of the East later associated with "Nestorian" label (though Nestorius himself not necessarily representative)

**Critical Distinction:** Nestorius himself must be distinguished from:
- Nestorianism (later theological tradition)
- Church of the East (historical church)
- Modern Assyrian Church of the East
- Cyril's characterization of Nestorius (polemical)
- Later conciliar condemnations

## VII. LEO'S TOME AND CYRIL

### 7A. Leo's Tome to Flavian (449)

**Context:**
- Written to Flavian of Constantinople
- Addresses Eutyches controversy (monophysitism)
- Rejected at Robber Council of Ephesus (449)
- Approved at Chalcedon (451)

**Key Formulations:**
- Emphasizes two natures with distinct properties
- Each nature retains its own operations
- Divine nature does not become human; human nature does not become divine
- Both natures belong to single person

**Terminology:**
- Uses "two natures" explicitly
- Emphasizes distinction and integrity of natures
- Less emphasis on unity than Cyril

### 7B. Leo vs. Cyril

**Agreements:**
- Both affirm one Christ, one Son
- Both affirm Theotokos
- Both reject Nestorianism
- Both reject Eutychianism
- Both affirm full divinity and humanity

**Differences:**
- Cyril emphasizes unity; Leo emphasizes distinction
- Cyril uses "mia physis"; Leo uses "two natures"
- Cyril's hypostatic union vs. Leo's two-nature Christology
- Different terminological frameworks

**Chalcedon's Reception:**
- Chalcedon approved BOTH Cyril's Second Letter AND Leo's Tome
- Chalcedon's Definition synthesizes elements from both
- Chalcedon can be read as Cyrillian (unity) and Leonine (distinction)

**Assessment:** Leo and Cyril represent different emphases within orthodox Christology. Chalcedon attempts to hold both together, though tensions remain.

## VIII. EPHESUS VS. CHALCEDON: CLAIM TESTS

### Claim A: "Chalcedon simply repeated Ephesus."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon read Cyril's Second Letter (approved at Ephesus) but added significant terminological precision (two natures, four adverbs). Chalcedon addressed Eutychianism, which Ephesus did not face.

---

### Claim B: "Chalcedon rejected Ephesus."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon explicitly reaffirmed Cyril's Second Letter and upheld Nestorius's condemnation. Chalcedon claimed continuity with Ephesus.

---

### Claim C: "Chalcedon preserved major elements of Ephesus."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter, upheld Theotokos, upheld Nestorius's condemnation, affirmed unity of Christ.

---

### Claim D: "Chalcedon developed terminology beyond Ephesus."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon introduced "in two natures" language, four adverbs, technical definitions of physis/hypostasis. Ephesus did not use this precise terminology.

---

### Claim E: "Chalcedon incorporated Cyrilline material."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon read Cyril's Second Letter, used Cyril's Theotokos language, upheld Cyril's anti-Nestorian position.

---

### Claim F: "Chalcedon completely resolved all Christological controversy."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon led to Oriental Orthodox schism (Miaphysite rejection). Constantinople II (553) needed to address Three Chapters controversy. Debates continued for centuries.

---

### Claim G: "Chalcedon created Christology from nothing."
**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Evidence:** Chalcedon explicitly claimed continuity with Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Cyril, and Leo. Chalcedon built on prior tradition.

---

### Claim H: "Ephesus and Chalcedon must be interpreted as separate historical events."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Different contexts, different participants, different controversies, different terminological precision. Treating them as identical obscures historical development.

---

### Claim I: "Later Chalcedonian terminology should not automatically be projected backward into 431."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Ephesus 431 did not use "in two natures" language, four adverbs, or technical definitions of physis/hypostasis. These developed by 451.

---

### Claim J: "Later Oriental Orthodox terminology should not automatically be projected backward into Cyril's own texts."
**Classification:** SAFE

**Evidence:** Cyril used physis in archaic sense (concrete reality), not later technical sense (abstract nature). Later Miaphysite interpretations may not reflect Cyril's original intent.

## IX. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### Position A: Chalcedonian Continuity

**Argument:** Chalcedon represents legitimate clarification and stabilization of earlier Nicene, Constantinopolitan, Cyrilline, and anti-Nestorian commitments.

**Supporting Points:**
- Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter
- Chalcedon upheld Nestorius's condemnation (Cyril's primary goal)
- Chalcedon's "one hypostasis" = Cyril's hypostatic union
- Chalcedon's "two natures" compatible with Cyril if understood as referring to properties, not separate realities
- Chalcedon addressed new controversy (Eutychianism) Cyril did not face
- Chalcedon's terminology became standard for orthodox Christology
- Constantinople II (553) reaffirmed Chalcedon while also reaffirming Cyril

**Strengths:**
- Accounts for Chalcedon's explicit claim of continuity
- Explains how Chalcedon can be both Cyrillian and Leonine
- Acknowledges legitimate doctrinal development

**Weaknesses:**
- Does not fully address why Chalcedon did not use "mia physis" formula
- Does not explain why Oriental Orthodox rejected Chalcedon as anti-Cyrillian
- May understate terminological shift from Cyril to Chalcedon

---

### Position B: Non-Chalcedonian Critique

**Argument:** Chalcedon's "two natures" formulation can be seen as introducing terminology that risks obscuring Cyril's emphasis on the unified incarnate Christ.

**Supporting Points:**
- Cyril consistently used "mia physis" formula; Chalcedon did not
- Chalcedon's "two natures" language closer to Antiochene tradition Cyril opposed
- Chalcedon rehabilitated Theodoret and Ibas (Cyril's opponents)
- Chalcedon's terminology led to schism (Oriental Orthodox rejection)
- Later Chalcedonian interpretations sometimes emphasized duality over unity
- Cyril's emphasis on single subjectivity of Logos potentially obscured by "two natures"

**Strengths:**
- Accounts for Oriental Orthodox rejection of Chalcedon
- Explains why Chalcedon did not use Cyril's preferred terminology
- Acknowledges real terminological shift

**Weaknesses:**
- May overstate contradiction between Cyril and Chalcedon
- Does not fully account for Chalcedon's explicit approval of Cyril's Second Letter
- May conflate later Nestorianizing interpretations with Chalcedon itself

---

### Assessment

Both positions have merit and reflect genuine tensions in the historical evidence. The relationship between Cyril, Ephesus, and Chalcedon cannot be reduced to simple identity or simple contradiction. The evidence supports a model of **continuity with development** — core theological commitments preserved, but terminology refined and precision added in response to new controversies.

## X. DEVELOPMENT DISTINCTIONS

### Seven Categories of Development

**A. Continuity**
- Same concept, same terminology, same emphasis
- Example: Affirmation of one Christ, one Son across all three stages

**B. Clarification**
- Same concept, more precise expression
- Example: Theotokos affirmed at Ephesus, clarified at Reunion and Chalcedon

**C. Terminological Development**
- Same concept, different terminology
- Example: Hypostatic union (Cyril) → one hypostasis (Chalcedon)

**D. Conceptual Development**
- Expanded concept to address new questions
- Example: Four adverbs at Chalcedon (not present at Ephesus)

**E. Substantive Doctrinal Development**
- New doctrine addressing new controversy
- Example: Rejection of Eutychianism at Chalcedon (not addressed at Ephesus)

**F. Contradiction**
- Later stage explicitly rejects earlier stage
- Example: NONE IDENTIFIED in this bridge (though Oriental Orthodox see Chalcedon as contradicting Cyril)

**G. Unresolved Ambiguity**
- Tension remains without resolution
- Example: Mia physis vs. two natures (tension persists between Chalcedonian and Oriental Orthodox traditions)

## XI. LIMITATIONS

### 11A. Incomplete Surviving Evidence

- Acts of Ephesus 431 exist in multiple versions (Greek, Latin, Coptic) with different details
- Formula of Reunion survives primarily in Cyril's Letter 39
- Chalcedon Acts edited by later tradition
- Many documents lost or fragmentary

### 11B. Mediated Transmission

- All sources transmitted through later copyists
- Editorial shaping of conciliar Acts (Graumann 2020)
- Polemical purposes shape presentation

### 11C. Terminology Translation Problems

- Greek physis, hypostasis, prosopon, ousia difficult to translate precisely
- Latin translations may shift meaning
- Modern English translations impose later conceptual frameworks

### 11D. Polemical Sources

- Cyril's characterization of Nestorius polemical
- Nestorius's self-defense apologetic
- Later sources shaped by Miaphysite vs. Dyophysite controversies

### 11E. Retrospective Interpretations

- Later Chalcedonian tradition interprets earlier stages through Chalcedonian lens
- Later Oriental Orthodox tradition interprets earlier stages through Miaphysite lens
- Modern historians influenced by denominational commitments

### 11F. Regional Reception Differences

- Chalcedon accepted in West and Byzantine East
- Chalcedon rejected in Oriental Orthodox churches (Egypt, Syria, Armenia, Ethiopia)
- Church of the East associated with "Nestorian" label
- Regional differences shape interpretation

### 11G. Theological Presuppositions of Modern Historians

- Historians' denominational commitments shape interpretation
- "Development" vs. "corruption" judgments reflect theological presuppositions
- Objective historical reconstruction difficult when sources themselves theologically motivated

### 12C. Source Independence Rule

Later conciliar reception does NOT count as independent corroboration of original proposition.

**Example:** Chalcedon's approval of Cyril's Second Letter does not independently verify Cyril's theology; it shows Chalcedon's reception of Cyril.

## XIII. CLAIM CLASSIFICATIONS

### A. Ephesus, Reunion, and Chalcedon form a historically connected sequence.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Direct chronological succession; Formula explicitly addresses post-Ephesus schism; Chalcedon explicitly references Ephesus.

### B. They are identical theological formulations.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Significant terminological development; different controversies addressed; different levels of precision.

### C. There is significant continuity across the three.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Core affirmations (one Christ, Theotokos, anti-Nestorian) maintained throughout.

### D. There is terminological development.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Physis, hypostasis, prosopon acquire more technical precision; "two natures" language introduced; four adverbs added.

### E. There is conceptual development.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Chalcedon addresses Eutychianism (not present at Ephesus); Formula introduces distinction principle.

### F. Chalcedon incorporated Cyrilline material.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter; used Cyril's Theotokos language; upheld Nestorius's condemnation.

### G. Chalcedon fully resolved the earlier dispute.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Chalcedon led to Oriental Orthodox schism; Three Chapters controversy followed; debates continued for centuries.

### H. Cyril's theology can simply be equated with Chalcedon.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Cyril used "mia physis" formula Chalcedon did not adopt; Cyril's terminology less precise; Chalcedon addressed controversies Cyril did not face.

### I. Cyril's theology necessarily contradicts Chalcedon.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Chalcedon approved Cyril's Second Letter; Chalcedon can be read as compatible with Cyril under certain terminological frameworks; Constantinople II reaffirmed both.

### J. Mia physis and two natures are automatically contradictory.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Depends on semantic range of physis; under Cyril's framework (physis = concrete reality), not necessarily contradictory to Chalcedon's framework (physis = set of properties).

### K. Mia physis and two natures can be reconciled under different terminological frameworks.
**Classification:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION
**Evidence:** Scholarly consensus acknowledges terminological shift; Constantinople II (553) attempted reconciliation; but Oriental Orthodox and Chalcedonian traditions continue to disagree.

### L. Chalcedon should be interpreted independently as a historical event.
**Classification:** SAFE
**Evidence:** Different context, participants, controversies than Ephesus; treating as identical obscures historical development.

### M. Political influence alone invalidates Chalcedon's theology.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Political influence present but does not automatically invalidate theological content; theological arguments must be evaluated on their merits.

### N. Conciliar success alone proves theological truth.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Conciliar victory reflects institutional and political factors, not necessarily theological correctness; Arian councils succeeded temporarily but were later rejected.

### O. Historical development alone proves doctrinal corruption.
**Classification:** UNSAFE
**Evidence:** Development can be legitimate clarification or corruption; must be evaluated case by case; not all change is corruption.
