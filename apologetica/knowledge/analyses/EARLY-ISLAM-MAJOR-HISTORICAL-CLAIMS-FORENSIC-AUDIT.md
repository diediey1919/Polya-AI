## A. OBJECTIVE & CORE METHOD

Perform a forensic audit of major historical claims concerning the first century of Islam (c. 600–700 CE).

The purpose is to distinguish proposition by proposition:
- WHAT IS HISTORICALLY ESTABLISHED
- WHAT IS STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- WHAT IS PROBABLE
- WHAT IS TRADITIONAL
- WHAT IS DISPUTED
- WHAT IS CURRENTLY UNSUPPORTED.

Method: Every major claim is evaluated across 12 source layers (Qur'an, early MSS, Hadith, early Maghazi, Sirah, early historiography, non-Muslim sources, inscriptions, papyri, coins, archaeology, and later traditions).

## B. AUDIT OF MAJOR HISTORICAL CLAIMS (1–20)

1. **Muhammad's Historical Existence**: Historically Established across Qur'anic text, Thomas the Presbyter (c. 640), Sebeos (c. 660), and early epigraphy.
2. **Muhammad's Early Message**: Monotheism, eschatological judgment, and prophetic/scriptural continuity established via early Qur'anic strata.
3. **Mecca**: Existence and association with Muhammad/Ka'bah established; exact pre-Islamic regional economic trade dominance remains debated.
4. **Medina / Yathrib**: Pre-Islamic existence, migration destination, political community, and Constitution of Medina historically established across multiple layers.
5. **Hijrah**: Migration event and calendar anchor (*AH*) established by 22 AH (PERF 558) and 24 AH (Zuhayr inscription); miraculous cave details belong to traditional/hagiographical layers.
6. **Early Qur'an**: Early 7th-century existence established by carbon-dated MSS (Birmingham, Sana'a); every later transmission detail remains traditional/disputed.
7. **Abu Bakr**: Caliphate and political consolidation after Muhammad's death strongly supported across early chronicles and external references to Arab expansion.
8. **Umar**: Caliphate, expansion, and administrative foundation historically established; confirmed by Zuhayr Inscription (24 AH).
9. **Uthman**: Caliphate, political conflict, and textual rasm standardization historically established across manuscripts and early historiography.
10. **Ali**: Caliphate, First Fitna leadership, and assassination historically established across early historiography, epigraphy, and coinage.
11. **First Fitna**: Civil war between Ali, Aisha/Talha/Zubayr, and Mu'awiya historically established in broad outline; specific speech verbatim dialogues remain traditional.
12. **Early Arab Conquests**: Conquest of Syria, Iraq, Egypt, and Persia established by contemporary non-Muslim witnesses (Syriac, Armenian, Coptic) and papyri.
13. **Early Islamic State Formation**: Administrative transition from Byzantine/Sasanian structures to centralized Arab bureaucracy established by papyrological and numismatic records.
14. **Abd al-Malik**: State consolidation, Dome of the Rock epigraphy (72 AH), administrative Arabicization, and reform coinage (77 AH) historically established.
15. **Early Islamic Identity**: Shift from early *Mu'minun* (Believers) movement to institutionalized *Muslim* identity tracked empirically across 7th-century epigraphy and papyri.
16. **Early Christian-Muslim Relations**: Theological demarcation over Christology (rejection of divine sonship/trinity) established via Q. 4:171 and Dome of the Rock mosaic inscriptions.
17. **Jewish-Muslim Relations**: Early Medinan interaction, political pacts, and subsequent conflict historically established via Constitution of Medina and early Maghazi.
18. **Early Islam and Late Antiquity**: Deep cultural and administrative continuity with Byzantine/Sasanian Late Antiquity historically established.
19. **Traditional Chronology**: Key milestones (622 Hijrah, 632 Death, 644 Umar death, 656 Uthman death, 661 Ali death, 691 Dome of Rock) historically anchored; exact monthly/daily dates remain traditional.
20. **Historical Core of the Sirah**: Recoverable 7th-century historical framework distinct from 2nd/3rd-century hagiographical additions.

   - Muhammad's 7th-century historical existence and monotheistic preaching in Hijaz.
   - The Qur'an as the early textual baseline of the movement.
   - The Hijrah (c. 622 CE) establishing the Islamic calendar.
   - Early territorial expansion and Caliphate succession (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali).
   - Uthmanic standardization of the consonantal rasm.
   - Abd al-Malik's state consolidation, Dome of the Rock epigraphy, and coinage reform.

   - Broad outline of the First Fitna civil war.
   - General framework of early military engagements (Badr, Uhud, Khandaq).
   - Early administrative transition from Byzantine/Sasanian structures to Arab governance.

3. **C. Plausible Reconstruction**:
   - Specific diplomatic pacts (e.g., Constitution of Medina core clauses).
   - Phased evolution of *Mu'minun* (Believers) identity into institutionalized *Islam*.

4. **D. Disputed Material**:
   - Precise economic extent of pre-Islamic Meccan trade.
   - Complete attribution of all 10 canonical *qira'at* reading details to 1st-century Companions.

5. **E. Unresolved Questions**:
   - Micro-chronology of pre-Hijrah Meccan revelatory sequence.
   - Verbatim accuracy of long speeches recorded in 3rd-century historiography.

6. **F. Unsupported Claims**:
   - Revisionist theories claiming Muhammad did not exist or that Islam was invented under Abd al-Malik.
   - Maximalist traditional claims that every late Hadith/Sirah detail is an established historical fact.
