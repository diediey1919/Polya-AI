## SECTION 1: METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK & CORE RELATIONSHIP

A counterargument is treated as a rigorous epistemic object rather than a rhetorical obstacle to be dismissed. Every canonical counterargument in this database preserves the strict chain:
`ARGUMENT (ARG-145-XXX) -> COUNTERARGUMENT -> STEELMAN -> RESPONSE -> REBUTTAL -> RESIDUAL OBJECTION -> CONFIDENCE`

### 1.1 Counterargument Status Definitions
- **DECISIVE:** Objection decisively refutes the target argument by demonstrating fatal logical contradiction or decisive counter-evidence.
- **STRONG:** High deductive force or compelling empirical/textual evidence that severely compromises the target argument's soundness.
- **SUBSTANTIAL:** Serious objection that shifts the burden of proof and exposes significant interpretive vulnerabilities.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** Internally coherent objection grounded in viable historical or philosophical hypotheses.
- **WEAK:** Fails basic logical or historical scrutiny, relying on strawman constructions or unverified assumptions.
- **ANSWERED:** Addressed by robust, independently verified apologetic or historical rebuttals.
- **PARTIALLY ANSWERED:** Addressed in part, but leaving residual interpretive gaps or probabilistic uncertainties.
- **UNDERDETERMINED:** Available evidence permits both the target argument and the counterargument with equal weight.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** Fails basic empirical, textual, or logical verification.
