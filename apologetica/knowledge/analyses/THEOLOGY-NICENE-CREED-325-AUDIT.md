## 2. HISTORICAL CONTEXT & PURPOSE
* **Context:** Convened by Constantine in 325 CE to resolve the Arian controversy regarding the divine status of the Son. Arius argued that the Son was a created being ("there was when he was not"), whereas orthodox bishops (led by Athanasius and Alexander of Alexandria) insisted upon the eternal, uncreated divinity of the Son.
* **Function:** The creed formulated precise ontological boundaries using technical philosophical and biblical language to affirm that the Son is true God from true God.

## 3. KEY TERMINOLOGY & HOMOOUSIOS
* **Homoousios (ὁμοούσιος):** Literally "consubstantial" or "of the same essence/substance" with the Father. Its historical function at Nicaea was to rule out Arianism by asserting that the Son shares the exact divine nature/substance as the Father, rather than being a created intermediary (*ex nihilo*).
* **Semantic Nuance:** While establishing true divinity, it does not instantly encompass every nuance of later 4th-century Cappadocian Trinitarian terminology (*mia ousia, treis hypostaseis*), which developed subsequently.

## 4. SCRIPTURE VS CREED & JOHN 1:1 COMPARISON
* **Chain:** New Testament Text (e.g., John 1:1) → Patristic Interpretation → Theological Controversy → Conciliar Formulation (Nicaea) → Creed.
* **John 1:1 vs. Creed:** John 1:1 ("In the beginning was the Word...") asserts the eternal divine status of the Logos. The Nicene Creed systematizes this into polemical ontological categories (*true God of true God, homoousios with the Father*). Nicaea did *not* invent the divinity of Christ, nor did John 1:1 explicitly contain the later philosophical term *homoousios*; rather, Nicaea provided a conciliar formulation to protect the biblical truth of Christ's divinity against subordinationist redefinitions.

## 5. IRENAEUS vs NICAEA CONTINUITY
* **Continuity & Development:** Irenaeus (~180 CE) already articulated a high Christology and identified Jesus as divine and eternal Creator alongside the Father. Nicaea (325 CE) did not invent this high Christology, but it codified it into precise ontological terminology when challenged by Arianism.
