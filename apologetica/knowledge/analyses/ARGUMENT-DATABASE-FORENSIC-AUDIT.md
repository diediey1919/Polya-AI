## SECTION 1: METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK & TAXONOMY

An argument is rigorously distinguished from a raw fact, a primary source, a theological doctrine, or an apologetic slogan. Every canonical argument in this database preserves the strict chain:
`SOURCE -> EVIDENCE -> CLAIM -> PREMISE -> INFERENCE -> CONCLUSION -> COUNTERARGUMENT -> RESPONSE -> CONFIDENCE`

### 1.1 Argument Status Definitions
- **ESTABLISHED:** Logically valid, sound premises backed by independent primary evidence with zero viable counter-explanations.
- **STRONG:** High deductive validity or inductive force, robust evidence, minor unresolved objections.
- **PROBABLE:** More likely than not, supported by multiple convergent lines of evidence with minor gaps.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** Internally coherent and consistent with available data, though underdetermined.
- **DISPUTED:** Active, unresolved scholarly or theological disagreement regarding premises or inference.
- **WEAK:** Flawed inference, dependent upon weak or circular premises, or failing basic historical/textual checks.
- **UNDERDETERMINED:** Available evidence permits multiple mutually exclusive explanations with equal weight.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** Fails basic empirical, textual, or logical verification.
