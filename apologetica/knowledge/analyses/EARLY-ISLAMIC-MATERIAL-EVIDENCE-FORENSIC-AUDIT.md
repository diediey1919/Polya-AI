## A. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Conduct a forensic audit of material evidence for early Islam (c. 600–750 CE), with special attention to inscriptions, papyri, coins, monumental architecture, graffiti, documentary texts, archaeological remains, seals, and administrative artifacts.

The objective is to determine:
1. What material evidence exists.
2. How securely it is dated.
3. What it actually says.
4. What historical claims it can support.
5. What it cannot establish.
6. Whether it independently corroborates literary traditions.
7. Whether it challenges particular literary claims.

## C. EPIGRAPHY & ZUHAYR INSCRIPTION

- **Zuhayr Inscription (24 AH / 644–645 CE)**: Located near Najran/Tebuk region; records the death of Zuhayr who wrote it in the year Umar ibn al-Khattab died. Provides secure epigraphic attestation for early Islamic politico-religious terminology and calendar dating (*AH*) in the mid-7th century.

---

## D. DOME OF THE ROCK INSCRIPTIONS

- **Construction**: 72 AH / 691–692 CE under Caliph Abd al-Malik in Jerusalem.
- **Inscriptions**: Contains extensive Qur'anic citations (e.g., Q. 4:171, Q. 19:33-35, Q. 112) and polemical/confessional statements concerning Jesus (Isa), rejecting divine sonship while affirming his prophetic and messianic status.
- **Significance**: Serves as monumental epigraphic evidence demonstrating early 7th/8th-century Islamic theological self-definition and state consolidation.

---

## E. PAPYROLOGY & `PERF 558`

- **PERF 558 (Palaeographische Studien / Papyrus Erzherzog Rainer 558)**: Dated to 22 AH / 643 CE (bilingual Greek-Arabic papyrus from Herakleopolis, Egypt).
- **Significance**: Earliest securely dated Arabic documentary papyrus; utilizes early Islamic administrative formulae (`bismillah`, tax terminology).

---

## F. NUMISMATICS & ABD AL-MALIK REFORMS

- **Coinage Evolution**: Transition from Byzantine-Arab and Arab-Sasanian transitional bronze/silver to the comprehensive monetary reform of Abd al-Malik (c. 77 AH / 697 CE), replacing figurative iconography with aniconic epigraphic formulae (Surah 112, shahada).

## H. CASE STUDIES (15 MANDATORY AUDITS)

1. **Zuhayr Inscription**: 24 AH / 644 CE; epigraphic benchmark corroborating early Islamic calendar (*AH*) and Umar's death year.
2. **Dome of the Rock**: 72 AH / 691 CE; monumental mosaic epigraphy demonstrating early Islamic Christological claims and Umayyad state identity.
3. **Early Qur'anic Inscriptions**: Epigraphic fragments quoting Qur'anic phrases on rocks and monuments; confirms early currency of Qur'anic rasm.
4. **PERF 558**: 22 AH / 643 CE; earliest dated Arabic documentary papyrus, establishing early administrative formulae.
5. **Early Arabic Administrative Papyri**: Nessana and Aphrodito papyri; tracking taxation and governance transitions from Byzantine/Coptic norms.
6. **Arab-Byzantine Coinage**: Transitional copper coins retaining modified imperial imagery; reflects early administrative continuity.
7. **Arab-Sasanian Coinage**: Silver dirhams maintaining Sasanian royal bust with added Islamic marginal formulae (`bismillah`).
8. **Abd al-Malik Coin Reform (77 AH)**: Complete removal of figural imagery; establishment of standard epigraphic gold dinars and silver dirhams.
9. **Early Islamic Graffiti**: Desert pilgrim scrawls expressing personal devotion and early monotheistic formulae.
10. **Early Mosque Archaeology**: Excavations of Kufa, Basra, and Fustat; demonstrates hypostyle evolution and qibla shifts.
11. **Qibla Evidence**: Archaeological alignment of early mosques; complex transition from Jerusalem/northern orientations to Mecca.
12. **Mecca Material Evidence**: Epigraphic and limited archaeological traces; challenges of urban archaeology in the Hijaz.
13. **Medina / Yathrib Material Evidence**: Settlement strata and oasis archaeology; corroborates agrarian and political base.
14. **Early Islamic State Administration**: Integration of local bureaucracy with new Arab elite leadership as documented in papyri.
15. **Christian / Byzantine Material Interaction**: Shared architectural and decorative vocabulary in Umayyad desert palaces (e.g., Qusayr Amra).
