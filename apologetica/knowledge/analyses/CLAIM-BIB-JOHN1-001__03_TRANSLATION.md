# SPECIALIST ANALYSIS: CLAIM-BIB-JOHN1-001__03_TRANSLATION.md

* **CLAIM_ID:** CLAIM-BIB-JOHN1-001
* **SOURCE_ID:** SRC-BIB-002
* **SPECIALIST_ID:** 03_TRANSLATION
* **ANALYSIS_TYPE:** COMPARATIVE TRANSLATION ANALYSIS
* **QUESTION:** How do major English translations render John 1:1, and what translational choices are reflected in BibleHub's comparative view?
* **EVIDENCE_USED:** BibleHub parallel translations of John 1:1.
* **ANALYSIS:** 
  1. *Literal/Standard Translations* (ESV, NASB, NIV, BSB): "and the Word was God," preserving the word order and capturing the qualitative/definite force of anarthrous *Theos*.
  2. *Alternative/Non-Trinitarian Renderings* (e.g., NWT): "and the Word was a god," interpreting the anarthrous predicate nominative as indefinite due to the absence of the article.
  3. *Translational Challenge*: Translating an anarthrous pre-verbal predicate nominative into English requires balancing structural literalness against English article rules, where rendering it literally as "God was the word" reverses subject and predicate, while "the Word was a god" introduces lexical indefiniteness foreign to Johannine theology.
* **COUNTERARGUMENT:** Translation choices are inherently interpretive; no single English rendering fully captures all lexical and syntactic nuances of Koine Greek without contextual glossing.
* **LIMITATIONS:** Translation comparison reveals linguistic options but does not settle original intent.
