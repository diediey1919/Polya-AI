## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This audit traces Christological propositions across five historical stages:
1. Cyril of Alexandria (pre-431)
2. Council of Ephesus (431)
3. Formula of Reunion (433)
4. Leo's Tome (449)
5. Council of Chalcedon (451)

**Primary Finding:** Christological doctrine shows substantial continuity on core affirmations (one Christ, full divinity, full humanity, Theotokos) but significant terminological and conceptual development on technical formulations (mia physis → two natures, hypostasis/prosopon distinctions, four adverbs).

**Key Conclusion:** Chalcedon represents neither a simple repetition of Cyril nor a rejection of Cyril, but a terminological and conceptual development that preserves Cyril's central concerns while introducing new precision.

---

## 1. FIVE-LAYER MODEL

For each Christological proposition, this audit distinguishes five analytical layers:

1. **TEXTUAL CONTINUITY** — Does the same text appear across stages?
2. **TERMINOLOGICAL CONTINUITY** — Does the same vocabulary appear?
3. **CONCEPTUAL CONTINUITY** — Does the same underlying concept appear despite different terminology?
4. **THEOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT** — Does the concept become more precise or nuanced?
5. **DOCTRINAL FORMALIZATION** — Does the concept become formally defined in conciliar authority?

### Classification Labels

- **CONTINUITY** — Substantive identity across stages
- **TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT** — Same concept, different vocabulary
- **CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT** — Same core idea, increased precision
- **SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT** — Genuine theological expansion
- **DISPUTED** — Historically contested or unclear
- **NOT ESTABLISHED** — Insufficient evidence in stored sources

## 2. CORE CHRISTOLOGICAL PROPOSITIONS

### A. One Christ / One Son / One Lord

**Cyril:** Explicitly affirmed. "One Christ, one Son, one Lord" (Letter 39, Formula of Reunion).

**Ephesus 431:** Implicitly affirmed in Cyril's Second Letter (read and approved at Session I).

**Reunion 433:** Explicitly affirmed. "We confess one Christ, one Son, one Lord" (Letter 39).

**Leo's Tome:** Implicitly affirmed. Leo speaks of "one Christ" and "one and the same" throughout.

**Chalcedon 451:** Explicitly affirmed. "One and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ" (Definition, opening formula).

**Analysis:**
- Textual Continuity: PARTIAL (explicit in Cyril/Reunion/Chalcedon; implicit in Leo)
- Terminological Continuity: YES ("one Christ," "one Son" in Cyril/Reunion/Chalcedon)
- Conceptual Continuity: YES (all stages affirm single divine-human subject)
- Theological Development: MINIMAL (concept stable throughout)
- Doctrinal Formalization: YES (Chalcedon makes this conciliar)

**Classification: CONTINUITY**

### H. Distinction of Divinity and Humanity

**Cyril:** Explicitly affirmed. "Not as if the difference of the natures were taken away by the union" (Second Letter). However, Cyril's terminology (mia physis) can obscure distinction.

### I. "Mia Physis" Language of Cyril

**Cyril:** Explicitly used. "Mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē" (one incarnate nature of God the Word). Cyril understands "physis" as concrete individual reality (≈ hypostasis).

**Ephesus 431:** Present in Cyril's Third Letter (read at Session I, but formal approval disputed per STEP 80R).

**Reunion 433:** NOT PRESENT. Formula of Reunion does not use "mia physis."

**Leo's Tome:** NOT PRESENT. Leo uses Latin "natura" and "forma" terminology.

**Chalcedon 451:** NOT PRESENT as conciliar formula. Chalcedon uses "en dyo physesin" (in two natures). However, Cyril's letters (including Third Letter) were read and approved.

**Analysis:**
- Textual Continuity: NO (absent from Reunion/Leo/Chalcedon as conciliar formula)
- Terminological Continuity: NO (Cyril: "mia physis"; Chalcedon: "dyo physeis")
- Conceptual Continuity: DISPUTED (does Chalcedon preserve Cyril's intent?)
- Theological Development: YES (Chalcedon introduces new technical formulation)
- Doctrinal Formalization: YES (Chalcedon formalizes "two natures")

**Classification: SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT**

### J. "Two Natures" Language of Chalcedon

**Cyril:** NOT PRESENT in Cyril's own terminology. Cyril uses "mia physis" and speaks of "difference of natures" but not "two natures" as technical formula.

**Ephesus 431:** NOT PRESENT as conciliar formula.

**Reunion 433:** IMPLICITLY PRESENT. "Union of two natures" (henōsis dyo physeōn) in Letter 39, but not as technical Chalcedonian formula.

**Leo's Tome:** EXPLICITLY PRESENT. "Two natures" (duae naturae) throughout.

**Chalcedon 451:** EXPLICITLY PRESENT as conciliar formula. "In two natures" (en dyo physesin).

**Analysis:**
- Textual Continuity: NO (absent from Cyril/Ephesus; present in Leo/Chalcedon)
- Terminological Continuity: PARTIAL (Reunion: "two natures"; Leo: "duae naturae"; Chalcedon: "dyo physeis")
- Conceptual Continuity: DISPUTED (does "two natures" contradict Cyril's "mia physis"?)
- Theological Development: YES (Chalcedon introduces new technical formulation)
- Doctrinal Formalization: YES (Chalcedon)

**Classification: SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT**

---

### K. "From Two Natures" vs "In Two Natures"

**Cyril:** Uses "from two natures" (ek dyo physeōn) in some contexts, emphasizing union resulting from two.

**Ephesus 431:** NOT DISCUSSED at this level of precision.

**Reunion 433:** Uses "union of two natures" but not "from" or "in" as technical formula.

**Leo's Tome:** Uses "two natures" but not "from" or "in" as technical formula.

**Chalcedon 451:** EXPLICITLY uses "in two natures" (en dyo physesin), NOT "from two natures" (ek dyo physeōn). This was deliberate: "from" could imply Eutychian absorption; "in" preserves distinction.

**Analysis:**
- Textual Continuity: NO (Cyril: "from"; Chalcedon: "in")
- Terminological Continuity: NO (different prepositions)
- Conceptual Continuity: PARTIAL (both affirm two natures, but Chalcedon emphasizes preservation)
- Theological Development: YES (Chalcedon introduces precision to avoid Eutychianism)
- Doctrinal Formalization: YES (Chalcedon)

**Classification: TERMINOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT**

### P. Relation Between Hypostasis, Prosopon, Physis, Ousia

**Cyril:** Uses "hypostasis" and "prosopon" somewhat interchangeably for "person." Uses "physis" to mean concrete individual reality (≈ hypostasis). Does not distinguish "ousia" (essence) from "physis" (nature) with Cappadocian precision.

**Ephesus 431:** Does not address this level of terminological precision.

**Reunion 433:** Uses "one person" (prosopon) but does not define technical distinctions.

**Leo's Tome:** Uses Latin "persona" (person) and "natura" (nature) but does not define Greek technical distinctions.

**Chalcedon 451:** EXPLICITLY DEFINES. Distinguishes "hypostasis" and "prosopon" (both = person) from "physis" (nature). Uses Cappadocian precision: one hypostasis/prosopon in two physeis.

**Analysis:**
- Textual Continuity: NO (Cyril's usage differs from Chalcedon's technical definitions)
- Terminological Continuity: PARTIAL (same words, different technical meanings)
- Conceptual Continuity: PARTIAL (all affirm one person/two natures, but technical precision differs)
- Theological Development: YES (Chalcedon introduces Cappadocian precision)
- Doctrinal Formalization: YES (Chalcedon)

## 3. CYRIL FORENSIC LAYER

### What Cyril Explicitly Says

1. **Hypostatic Union:** "The Word united to himself hypostatically flesh animated by a rational soul" (Second Letter).
2. **Mia Physis:** "One incarnate nature of God the Word" (Third Letter, Anathema 4).
3. **Theotokos:** "The holy virgin is the mother of God" (Third Letter, Anathema 1).
4. **Single Subject:** All statements about Christ refer to one subject (Second Letter).
5. **Difference Preserved:** "Not as if the difference of the natures were taken away by the union" (Second Letter).

### What Later Interpreters Attribute to Cyril

1. **Proto-Chalcedonian:** Some interpreters claim Cyril's "mia physis" is equivalent to Chalcedon's "two natures."
2. **Proto-Miaphysite:** Some interpreters claim Cyril's "mia physis" contradicts Chalcedon's "two natures."

**Verdict:** Both attributions are anachronistic. Cyril's terminology is neither identical to nor contradictory with Chalcedon. It represents a different terminological framework expressing substantially similar concerns.

### Cyril's Terminology in His Own Context

1. **Physis:** Cyril uses "physis" to mean "concrete individual reality" (≈ hypostasis), not "abstract nature" (as Chalcedon does).
2. **Hypostasis:** Cyril uses "hypostasis" to mean "concrete existence" or "person."
3. **Mia Physis:** For Cyril, "one nature" means "one concrete incarnate reality," not "one abstract nature."

**Evidence:** Cyril's Second Letter explicitly states "not as if the difference of the natures were taken away," showing he understands distinction within "mia physis."

### Cyril's Anti-Nestorian Concern

Cyril's primary concern is **preserving the unity of Christ** against Nestorius's alleged division into "two sons" or "two Christs." Cyril insists on single subject of predication.

### Cyril's Anti-Apollinarian Boundaries

Cyril explicitly affirms Christ's complete humanity including rational soul (Second Letter: "flesh animated by a rational soul"). Cyril is NOT Apollinarian.

### Can Cyril's Terminology Be Mapped Directly onto Chalcedonian Terminology?

**NO.** Cyril's "mia physis" ≠ Chalcedon's "dyo physeis" in terminology, but both can express substantially similar Christological commitments (one person, full divinity, full humanity, distinction preserved).

**Evidence:** Cyril accepted Formula of Reunion (433), which uses "two natures" language, showing Cyril could accept terminological flexibility.

## 4. EPHESUS 431

### Second Letter to Nestorius

**Read and Approved:** Session I, June 22, 431. Formally approved as standard of Nicene orthodoxy.

**Key Content:**
- Hypostatic union
- Single subject of predication
- Theotokos
- "Not as if the difference of the natures were taken away"

### Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas

**Status:** Disputed. Read at Session I, but formal conciliar approval disputed (per STEP 80R five-category framework).

**Five-Category Framework (STEP 80R):**
1. **Textual Presence:** YES (included in Acts)
2. **Reading:** LIKELY (read at Session I)
3. **Theological Endorsement:** DISPUTED (no explicit conciliar vote)
4. **Formal Conciliar Approval:** NO (not formally approved as conciliar decree)
5. **Later Conciliar Reception:** Constantinople II (553)

### Twelve Anathemas

**Status:** Same as Third Letter. Present and likely read, but formal approval disputed.

**Critical Note:** Do NOT claim "Twelve Anathemas were simply the official creed of Ephesus." Do NOT claim "Twelve Anathemas had no status at Ephesus." Preserve the documented nuance.

### Deposition of Nestorius

**Approved:** Session I. Nestorius deposed for rejecting Theotokos and allegedly dividing Christ.

### Theotokos

**Affirmed:** Explicitly. Nestorius condemned for rejecting Theotokos.

### Procedural Circumstances

**Complex:** Cyril convened council before John of Antioch arrived. Rival council (John) deposed Cyril. Imperial intervention. Final resolution: Nestorius deposed, Cyril and Memnon reinstated (433).

### Later Reception

**Ephesus 431 recognized as Third Ecumenical Council** by Chalcedon 451 and later tradition. Cyril's Second Letter became standard of orthodoxy.

## 5. FORMULA OF REUNION 433

### Letter 39 (Cyril to John of Antioch)

**Context:** Reconciliation between Cyril and John after Ephesus 431 schism.

**Key Content:**
- "One Christ, one Son, one Lord"
- "Union of two natures"
- "Consubstantial with Father in divinity, consubstantial with us in humanity"
- Accepts Theotokos
- Distinguishes expressions applicable to "one person" vs "two natures"

### Cyril's Acceptance

**Cyril accepted Formula of Reunion** as compatible with his Christology.

**Evidence:** Cyril wrote Letter 39 to John, affirming the Formula.

### What Was Retained

1. One Christ, one Son, one Lord
2. Full divinity and humanity
3. Theotokos
4. Union (henōsis)
5. Distinction of natures

### What Was Clarified

1. "Two natures" terminology accepted
2. Distinction between expressions applicable to "one person" vs "two natures"

### What Remained Disputed

1. Status of Twelve Anathemas (not required, not condemned)
2. Precise meaning of "two natures" (Cyril's understanding vs later Chalcedonian understanding)

### Claim Tests

**Claim:** "Formula of Reunion proves Cyril abandoned mia physis."

**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Formula of Reunion shows Cyril accepted "two natures" language, but does not prove he abandoned "mia physis." Cyril's "mia physis" and Formula's "two natures" can express substantially similar concerns with different terminology.

**Evidence:** Cyril continued to use "mia physis" in later writings. Cyril understood "mia physis" as "one concrete incarnate reality," not "one abstract nature."

**Reason:** Terminological flexibility does not equal theological abandonment.

---

**Claim:** "Formula of Reunion is already identical to Chalcedon."

**Classification:** UNSAFE

**Finding:** Formula of Reunion anticipates some Chalcedonian themes but is not identical to Chalcedon.

**Evidence:** Formula uses "two natures" but lacks Chalcedon's technical precision (four adverbs, explicit "one person in two natures" formula).

**Reason:** Formula is transitional; Chalcedon is more technically precise.

## 6. LEO'S TOME

### One Person

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "One person" (una persona) throughout.

### Two Natures

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "Two natures" (duae naturae) throughout.

### True God

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "True God," "in the form of God," "equal to the Father."

### True Man

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "True man," "in the form of a slave," "complete in what is ours."

### Predication

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "One and the same" throughout.

### Impassibility/Passibility

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "Each form does what is proper to it" (Tome, §6). Divine nature impassible; human nature passible.

### Latin Terminology

Leo uses:
- **Persona** = person
- **Natura** = nature
- **Forma Dei** = form of God (divine nature)
- **Forma Servi** = form of a slave (human nature)

### Relation to Cyril

**Compatibility:** Leo's Tome is compatible with Cyril's central concerns (one person, full divinity, full humanity, distinction preserved).

**Differences:** Leo uses Latin terminology and emphasizes distinction more emphatically than Cyril.

### Relation to Chalcedon

**Influence:** Leo's Tome heavily influenced Chalcedon's Definition.

**Reception:** Chalcedon acclaimed Leo's Tome as orthodox and consonant with Cyril.

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "Consubstantial with the Father in respect of His divinity, consubstantial with us in respect of His manhood."

### Four Adverbs

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "Without confusion, without change, without division, without separation" (asynchytōs, atreptos, adiairetōs, achōristōs).

### "In Two Natures"

**Explicitly Affirmed:** "In two natures" (en dyo physesin), NOT "from two natures" (ek dyo physeōn).

**Reason:** "From" could imply Eutychian absorption; "in" preserves distinction.

### Reception of Cyril

**Explicitly Affirmed:** Cyril's Second Letter read and approved. Cyril's Third Letter (with Twelve Anathemas) read but not formally approved (per STEP 80R).

### Reception of Leo

**Explicitly Affirmed:** Leo's Tome read and acclaimed as orthodox and consonant with Cyril.

### Definition vs Leo's Tome

**Relationship:** Chalcedon's Definition incorporates Leo's two-nature formula but adds technical precision (four adverbs, explicit "one person in two natures").

### Relationship to Ephesus 431

**Continuity:** Chalcedon affirms Ephesus 431 as Third Ecumenical Council.

**Development:** Chalcedon introduces new technical precision (two natures, four adverbs) not present at Ephesus.

## 8. MIA PHYSIS vs TWO NATURES

### Historical Semantic Range of Physis

**Cyril:** "Physis" = concrete individual reality (≈ hypostasis)

**Chalcedon:** "Physis" = abstract nature (set of properties)

### Cyril's Concrete vs Abstract Usage

Cyril uses "physis" concretely. "Mia physis" = "one concrete incarnate reality."

Chalcedon uses "physis" abstractly. "Dyo physeis" = "two natures (sets of properties)."

### Chalcedonian Usage

Chalcedon uses "physis" abstractly to mean "nature" (set of properties).

### Hypostasis Relationship

**Cyril:** "Hypostasis" ≈ "physis" (both mean concrete individual reality)

**Chalcedon:** "Hypostasis" = person; "physis" = nature (distinct concepts)

### Nature/Person Distinction

**Cyril:** Does not distinguish "nature" and "person" with Cappadocian precision.

**Chalcedon:** Explicitly distinguishes "one hypostasis/prosopon" (person) in "two physeis" (natures).

### Modern Scholarly/Ecumenical Interpretations

**Some modern assessments** identify substantial common Christological commitments alongside significant terminological differences.

**Evidence:** Both Cyril and Chalcedon affirm one person, full divinity, full humanity, distinction preserved.

### Genuine Historical Disagreements

**Oriental Orthodox churches** rejected Chalcedon, claiming it contradicted Cyril's "mia physis."

**Evidence:** Oriental Orthodox interpreted Chalcedon as introducing Nestorian division.

**Counter-evidence:** Chalcedon explicitly condemns division ("without division, without separation").

### Final Wording

Some modern assessments identify substantial common Christological commitments alongside significant terminological differences, while important theological and historical disagreements remain.

## 10. STEELMAN BOTH SIDES

### Side A: Chalcedonian Continuity Argument

**Strongest Case:**

Chalcedon preserves Cyril's central Christological concerns while introducing necessary precision:

1. **One Person:** Chalcedon affirms "one person and hypostasis" (Cyril's central concern).

2. **Full Divinity:** Chalcedon affirms "consubstantial with the Father" (Cyril's concern).

3. **Full Humanity:** Chalcedon affirms "consubstantial with us" (Cyril's concern).

4. **Distinction Preserved:** Chalcedon affirms "without confusion, without change" (Cyril's Second Letter: "not as if the difference of the natures were taken away").

5. **Theotokos:** Chalcedon affirms Theotokos (Cyril's boundary term).

6. **Cyril Approved:** Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter.

7. **Against Nestorius:** Chalcedon condemns division ("without division, without separation").

8. **Against Eutyches:** Chalcedon condemns confusion ("without confusion, without change").

**Conclusion:** Chalcedon is a legitimate clarification that protects both Cyril's unity and the distinction of natures.

---

### Side B: Miaphysite / Non-Chalcedonian Critique

**Strongest Case:**

Chalcedon introduces changes that create tensions with Cyril:

1. **Terminological Shift:** Cyril used "mia physis" (one nature). Chalcedon uses "dyo physeis" (two natures). This is a significant terminological change.

2. **Conceptual Precision:** Chalcedon distinguishes "hypostasis" (person) from "physis" (nature) with Cappadocian precision. Cyril used these terms more loosely. This is a conceptual development not identical to Cyril.

3. **Semantic Framework:** Cyril's "physis" meant "concrete individual reality." Chalcedon's "physis" means "abstract nature." This is a semantic shift.

4. **Emphasis:** Cyril emphasized unity ("mia physis"). Chalcedon emphasizes distinction (four adverbs, especially "without confusion, without change"). This creates tension.

5. **Reception:** Oriental Orthodox churches rejected Chalcedon, claiming it contradicted Cyril. This is historical evidence of perceived contradiction.

6. **Ecclesiastical Consequences:** Chalcedon led to schism with Oriental Orthodox churches. This suggests substantive disagreement, not mere terminology.

**Conclusion:** Chalcedon changes terminology and emphasis in ways that create genuine tensions with Cyril's "mia physis" language.

## 11. SOURCE DEPENDENCIES

### Physical Independence

**Cyril, Nestorius, Leo, Chalcedon** are physically independent documents (different authors, different times, different locations).

### Literary Independence

**Cyril and Nestorius:** Literarily dependent (Cyril quotes Nestorius; Nestorius responds to Cyril).

**Leo and Cyril:** Literarily independent (Leo does not quote Cyril directly).

**Chalcedon and Cyril:** Literarily dependent (Chalcedon quotes Cyril's Second Letter).

**Chalcedon and Leo:** Literarily dependent (Chalcedon incorporates Leo's formula).

### Theological Dependence

**Cyril → Chalcedon:** Theologically dependent (Chalcedon affirms Cyril's concerns).

**Leo → Chalcedon:** Theologically dependent (Chalcedon incorporates Leo's formula).

**Nestorius → Chalcedon:** Theologically opposed (Chalcedon condemns Nestorian division).

### Polemical Dependence

**Cyril and Nestorius:** Polemically dependent (each responds to the other).

**Leo and Eutyches:** Polemically dependent (Leo responds to Eutychian controversy).

**Chalcedon and Eutyches:** Polemically dependent (Chalcedon condemns Eutychian confusion).

### Historical Dependence

**Ephesus 431 → Chalcedon 451:** Historically dependent (Chalcedon receives Ephesus as Third Ecumenical Council).

**Formula of Reunion 433 → Chalcedon 451:** Historically dependent (Chalcedon receives Formula of Reunion).

### Why Not Four Independent Witnesses

Cyril, Nestorius, Leo, and Chalcedon are NOT four independent witnesses to the same proposition because:

1. **Literary Dependence:** Chalcedon quotes Cyril and Leo.
2. **Theological Dependence:** Chalcedon affirms Cyril and Leo, opposes Nestorius.
3. **Polemical Dependence:** All respond to same controversies (Nestorian, Eutychian).
4. **Historical Dependence:** Later documents (Chalcedon) receive earlier documents (Cyril, Leo, Ephesus).

**Conclusion:** These sources are interdependent, not independent.

Modern Oriental Orthodox churches developed over centuries. Fifth-century anti-Chalcedonian Christians were not identical to modern Oriental Orthodox churches.

### Do NOT Project Modern Catholic/Orthodox/Protestant Categories onto Fifth-Century Actors

Fifth-century actors did not operate within modern denominational categories.

### Do NOT Assume Later Denominational Boundaries Existed in Identical Form in 431 or 451

Denominational boundaries developed over centuries. Fifth-century Christianity was more fluid.

## 15. FINAL SYNTHESIS QUESTIONS

### 1. Is Chalcedon Simply a Repetition of Cyril?

**NO.** Chalcedon introduces new terminology ("two natures," four adverbs) and conceptual precision (Cappadocian distinctions) not present in Cyril's own writings.

### 2. Is Chalcedon a Rejection of Cyril?

**NO.** Chalcedon read and approved Cyril's Second Letter. Chalcedon affirms Cyril's central concerns (one person, full divinity, full humanity, Theotokos, distinction preserved).

### 3. Did Terminology Develop?

**YES.** Chalcedon uses "two natures," "four adverbs," "in two natures" (not "from two natures"). Chalcedon distinguishes "hypostasis" (person) from "physis" (nature) with Cappadocian precision.

### 4. Did Concepts Develop?

**YES.** Chalcedon introduces conceptual precision (distinction of natures, four adverbs, nature/person distinction) not present in Cyril.

### 5. Did Doctrine Become More Formally Defined?

**YES.** Chalcedon makes conciliar what was previously patristic. Chalcedon explicitly defines Christological orthodoxy with technical precision.

### 6. Which Disagreements Remain Genuinely Substantive?

1. **Mia physis vs two natures:** Whether Chalcedon's "two natures" contradicts Cyril's "mia physis" is genuinely disputed.
2. **Emphasis:** Cyril emphasizes unity; Chalcedon emphasizes distinction. This creates genuine tension.
3. **Reception:** Oriental Orthodox churches rejected Chalcedon, claiming it contradicted Cyril. This is historical evidence of substantive disagreement.

### 7. Which Disagreements Are Substantially Terminological?

1. **Physis usage:** Cyril's "physis" (concrete reality) vs Chalcedon's "physis" (abstract nature).
2. **Nature/person distinction:** Cyril's loose usage vs Chalcedon's Cappadocian precision.
3. **From/in two natures:** Cyril's "from" vs Chalcedon's "in."

### 8. Which Conclusions Cannot Be Established from Stored Evidence?

1. **Whether Chalcedon is the only legitimate interpretation of Cyril.** Multiple legitimate interpretations exist.
2. **Whether Oriental Orthodox rejection was justified.** Evidence supports both sides.
3. **Whether all disagreements are merely terminological.** Some disagreements may be substantive.
