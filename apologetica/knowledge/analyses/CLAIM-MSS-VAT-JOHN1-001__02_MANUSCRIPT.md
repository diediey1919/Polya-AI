## 1. Paleographic & Codicological Verification
Codex Vaticanus (Vat. gr. 1209, designated as Gregory-Aland B or 03) is written in a fine, elegant biblical uncial script on high-quality vellum sheets. The layout utilizes three columns per page, with 42 lines per column (except for poetic books written in two columns). The writing is continuous (*scriptio continua*), characteristic of 4th-century uncial manuscripts.

## 2. Examination of John 1:1 Textual Witness
In John 1:1 (Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος), Codex Vaticanus is extant, legible, and unblemished by lacunae or destructive corrections in this specific verse. 

## 3. Physical Source Independence from Codex Sinaiticus
Codex Vaticanus is demonstrably a physically independent manuscript witness from Codex Sinaiticus ($\aleph$ / 01):
- **Codicological Format:** Vaticanus uses 3 columns per page; Sinaiticus uses 4 columns per page.
- **Material & Hand:** Distinct uncial hands and vellum preparation characteristics.
- **Textual Recension:** Both belong to the Alexandrian textual family, displaying exceptionally high textual affinity, but they represent independent codicological lines descending from early archetypes.

## 4. Textual Comparison
