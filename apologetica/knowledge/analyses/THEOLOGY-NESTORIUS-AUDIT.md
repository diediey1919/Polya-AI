## 2. HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### 2A. CHRONOLOGICAL SEQUENCE
* **c. 386 CE:** Nestorius born in Germanicia, Province of Syria
* **Pre-428:** Trained under Theodore of Mopsuestia in Antioch
* **428 CE:** Appointed Archbishop of Constantinople by Theodosius II
* **428–430 CE:** Theotokos controversy erupts
* **430 CE:** Cyril appeals to Pope Celestine; Celestine delegates authority to Cyril
* **430 CE:** Cyril sends Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas to Nestorius
* **June 22, 431 CE:** Council of Ephesus opens (before Antiochene delegation arrives)
* **June 22, 431 CE:** Nestorius condemned and deposed
* **Late June 431 CE:** John of Antioch arrives; counter-council deposes Cyril
* **433 CE:** Formula of Reunion (Cyril and John reconcile; Nestorius abandoned)
* **435 CE:** Imperial edict exiles Nestorius to Great Oasis of Hibis, Egypt
* **450 CE:** Death of Theodosius II (July 28)
* **451 CE:** Council of Chalcedon (Nestorius in exile; dies shortly after)
* **451/452 CE:** Bazaar of Heracleides composed

### 2B. CRITICAL DISTINCTION
**Chronological facts (verifiable):**
* Nestorius appointed 428, deposed 431, exiled 435, died c. 451
* Council of Ephesus condemned Nestorius
* Council of Chalcedon adopted dyophysite language while upholding Nestorius's condemnation

**Theological judgments (not verifiable by historical evidence alone):**
* Whether Nestorius was heretical or orthodox
* Whether Ephesus's condemnation was theologically correct
* Whether Chalcedon "vindicated" Nestorius

## 3. THEOTOKOS ANALYSIS

### 3A. NESTORIUS'S OWN EVIDENCE
**Direct Quotation (Sermon XVIII):**
> "If the babe and the Lord of the babe are one and the same person, the suspected phrase 'inhabitant of the babe' must be simply intended as a safeguard against identification of the Word with the flesh. So Mary is Theotokos – because the Word was united to the temple… which is in nature consubstantial with the holy Virgin… It is in virtue of this union that the holy Virgin is called Theotokos."

**Nestorius's Position:**
1. Did NOT categorically reject Theotokos
2. Accepted Theotokos in qualified sense (by virtue of union)
3. Preferred Christotokos as more precise
4. Also proposed dual title: Theotokos kai Anthropotokos
5. Objected to confusion of natures (God as eternal/impassible cannot be "born" in divine nature)
6. Birth pertains to humanity; Word united to human nature but did not suffer birth in divinity

### 3B. CYRIL'S REPRESENTATION
**Cyril's Accusation:**
* Nestorius denies Theotokos
* Nestorius divides Christ into two sons
* Nestorius undermines reality of Incarnation

**Anathema 1:**
> "If anyone does not confess that Emmanuel is God in truth, and therefore that the holy virgin is the mother of God (Theotokos)... let him be anathema."

### 3C. CRITICAL COMPARISON
**What Nestorius Actually Said:**
* "Mary is Theotokos... in virtue of this union"
* Qualified acceptance, not absolute rejection

**What Cyril Claimed Nestorius Said:**
* Nestorius denies Theotokos
* Nestorius teaches two sons

**Gap Analysis:**
* Cyril's polemic presents Nestorius as absolute rejecter of Theotokos
* Nestorius's own evidence shows qualified acceptance
* **Conclusion:** Cyril's representation is polemical caricature, not accurate summary

### 3D. CLASSIFICATION
**Claim:** "Nestorius denied that Mary was the mother of Jesus."
**Status:** UNSUPPORTED (hostile caricature; no evidence in Nestorius's own writings)

**Claim:** "Nestorius denied that Mary could be called Theotokos."
**Status:** UNSUPPORTED (Nestorius's Sermon XVIII explicitly accepts Theotokos in qualified sense)

**Claim:** "Nestorius objected to unqualified use of Theotokos without distinction of natures."
**Status:** SAFE (supported by Nestorius's own evidence)

**Claim:** "Nestorius preferred Christotokos as more precise title."
**Status:** SAFE (supported by multiple sources)

## 4. CHRISTOLOGICAL UNITY ANALYSIS

### 4A. NESTORIUS'S TERMINOLOGY
**Key Terms:**
* **Prosopon (πρόσωπον):** Observable character, defining properties, manifestation of reality
* **Two prosopa:** Divine and human prosopa subsist in respective natures
* **One prosopon of union:** Two prosopa coalesce in one prosopon (Christ)
* **Synapheia (συνάφεια):** Conjunction (Nestorius's preferred union term)
* **Voluntary union:** Union of godhead and manhood is voluntary, not natural composition

**Direct Quotation (Bazaar):**
> "I predicate two natures... the union of the prosopa took place for the prosopon and not for the ousia and the nature... the natures subsist in their prosopa and in their natures and in the prosopon of the union... thus one prosopon of the two natures."

### 4B. NESTORIUS'S AFFIRMATIONS
1. One Christ (not two Christs)
2. One Son (not two Sons)
3. One prosopon of union
4. Two natures (divine and human)
5. Full reality of both natures
6. Distinction of predicates according to nature
7. Real incarnation (not mere indwelling)

### 4C. NESTORIUS'S DENIALS
1. Denies "Two Sons" teaching
2. Denies Apollinarianism (mixing/confusion of natures)
3. Denies natural composition
4. Denies change in godhead
5. Denies suffering of Divine Logos (as divine, impassible)
6. Denies duality of sonship

### 4D. CLASSIFICATION
**Does Nestorius's evidence support:**
* **One Christ:** YES (explicit affirmation)
* **Two natures:** YES (explicit affirmation)
* **One subject:** AMBIGUOUS (one prosopon of union, but two prosopa subsist)
* **Two subjects:** AMBIGUOUS (two prosopa, but one prosopon of union)
* **Two prosopa:** YES (divine and human prosopa subsist)
* **One prosopon:** YES (prosopon of union)

**Critical:** Nestorius's formulation is internally complex. He affirms both "two prosopa" and "one prosopon of union" — this is not a simple "two persons" or "one person" formulation.

## 5. "TWO CHRISTS" CLAIM TEST

### 5A. THE CLAIM
**"Nestorius taught two Christs."**

### 5B. NESTORIUS'S OWN EVIDENCE
**Explicit Denial:**
* "I predicate two natures... but of one man who is moved in the same manner by the other"
* "with no two prosopa of the sons conceived nor again two prosopa of the men"
* "one prosopon of the two natures"
* Bazaar explicitly denies "Two Sons" teaching

**Affirmation:**
* One Christ
* One Son
* One prosopon of union

### 5C. CYRIL'S ACCUSATION
**Cyril's Claim:**
* Nestorius divides Christ into two sons
* Nestorius teaches mere conjunction of dignity/authority
* Nestorius's Christology is effectively adoptionist

### 5D. EPHESUS CONDEMNATION
**Conciliar Judgment:**
* Nestorius condemned as heretic
* His teaching judged contrary to Nicaea

### 5E. MODERN SCHOLARSHIP
**Bethune-Baker (1908):** Nestorius did not teach two Christs; his formulation was misunderstood
**Anastos (1962):** "Nestorius Was Orthodox" — argues against two-Christ charge
**Grillmeier (1975):** Nestorius's formulations ambiguous but not necessarily teaching two Christs
**McGuckin (1994):** Nestorius's prosopon terminology problematic but not simple "two persons"

### 5F. CLASSIFICATION
**Claim:** "Nestorius taught two completely separate Christs."
**Status:** UNSUPPORTED (Nestorius explicitly denies two sons/Christs; affirms one prosopon of union)

**Claim:** "Nestorius's formulation risked implying two subjects despite his intention."
**Status:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION (his prosopon language is complex; two prosopa subsist even in one prosopon of union — this is the genuine theological problem Cyril identified)

**Claim:** "Cyril's 'two Christs' accusation accurately represents Nestorius's teaching."
**Status:** NOT ESTABLISHED (Cyril's polemic does not match Nestorius's own evidence)

## 6. NESTORIUS VS CYRIL — SYMMETRIC COMPARISON

### 6A. CHRISTOLOGICAL UNITY

| Issue | Nestorius's Evidence | Cyril's Evidence |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Union Mode** | Prosopic union (synapheia) | Hypostatic union (henōsis kat' hypostasin) |
| **Key Term** | Prosopon (one prosopon of union) | Hypostasis (one hypostasis) |
| **Natures** | Two natures (divine/human) | One incarnate nature (mia physis) |
| **Subject** | One Christ, one prosopon of union | One Christ, one hypostasis |
| **Problem** | Two prosopa subsist even in union | Mia physis language risks monophysitism |

### 6B. THEOTOKOS

| Issue | Nestorius's Evidence | Cyril's Evidence |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Position** | Qualified acceptance | Absolute insistence |
| **Preferred** | Christotokos (or dual title) | Theotokos (non-negotiable) |
| **Objection** | Confusion of natures (birth pertains to humanity) | None — Theotokos is boundary of orthodoxy |
| **Rationale** | Word did not suffer birth in divinity | Word incarnate is one subject; Mary bore that one subject |

### 6C. LOGOS / HUMANITY

| Issue | Nestorius's Evidence | Cyril's Evidence |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Logos** | Impassible (as divine) | Impassible in divinity, suffered in flesh |
| **Humanity** | Full humanity with complete characteristics | Full humanity (but mia physis formulation risks absorption) |
| **Predication** | Distinguish predicates according to nature | One subject receives all predicates |
| **Suffering** | Word did not suffer (as divine) | Word suffered in flesh |

### 6D. CRITICAL OBSERVATION
**Both Nestorius and Cyril:**
* Affirm two natures (divine and human)
* Affirm one Christ
* Affirm reality of incarnation
* Reject Apollinarianism
* Reject Arianism

**Key Difference:**
* **Terminology:** Prosopic union vs hypostatic union
* **Predication:** Distinguish predicates by nature vs one subject receives all
* **Theotokos:** Qualified vs absolute

**Conciliar Interpretation:**
* Ephesus (431): Condemned Nestorius, approved Cyril
* Chalcedon (451): Adopted dyophysite language (two natures) while upholding Nestorius's condemnation and approving Cyril's Second Letter

## 7. EPHESUS 431 REASSESSMENT

### 7A. HISTORICAL FACTS
* Cyril opened council before Antiochene delegation arrived
* Nestorius did not attend (requested retirement)
* Cyril presided (Nestorius's primary opponent)
* Nestorius condemned in single session
* John of Antioch arrived later; convened counter-council, deposed Cyril
* Emperor initially ordered both deposed
* Cyril eventually returned after bribing courtiers

### 7B. POLITICAL CONTEXT
* Alexandria vs Constantinople ecclesiastical rivalry
* Imperial politics (Theodosius II initially supported Nestorius)
* Papal involvement (Celestine supported Cyril)
* Popular devotion to Theotokos in Ephesus
* Cyril's political skill vs Nestorius's political ineptitude

### 7C. THEOLOGICAL CONTEXT
* Genuine Christological disagreement (not merely political)
* Cyril's miaphysite formulation vs Nestorius's dyophysite formulation
* Legitimate concern on both sides (Cyril: unity; Nestorius: distinction)
* Theotokos as boundary term (Cyril) vs precision term (Nestorius)

### 7D. CLASSIFICATION
**Claim:** "The condemnation of Nestorius at Ephesus automatically proves the theological truth of every Cyrilline formulation."
**Status:** UNSAFE (historical condemnation ≠ theological correctness; Cyril's miaphysite language later required Chalcedonian clarification)

**Claim:** "Ephesus was procedurally irregular (Cyril opened before Antiochenes arrived; Nestorius did not attend)."
**Status:** SAFE (historical fact)

**Claim:** "Ephesus was politically motivated."
**Status:** SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION (political factors present, but genuine theological disagreement also present)

**Claim:** "Historical condemnation and theological truth must be analytically separated."
**Status:** SAFE (methodological principle, not historical claim)

## 8. CYRIL'S TWELVE ANATHEMAS COMPARISON

### 8A. ANATHEMA 1 (Theotokos)
**Cyril:** "If anyone does not confess that Emmanuel is God in truth, and therefore that the holy virgin is the mother of God (Theotokos)... let him be anathema."

**Nestorius's Evidence:**
* Sermon XVIII: "Mary is Theotokos... in virtue of this union"
* Qualified acceptance, not absolute rejection

**Assessment:** Cyril's anathema assumes Nestorius absolutely rejects Theotokos. Nestorius's own evidence shows qualified acceptance. **Gap between polemic and evidence.**

### 8B. ANATHEMA 2 (Hypostatic Union)
**Cyril:** "If anyone does not confess that the Word from God the Father has been united by hypostasis (kat' hypostasin) with the flesh and is one Christ with his own flesh... let him be anathema."

**Nestorius's Evidence:**
* Affirms one Christ
* Affirms prosopic union (not hypostatic union)
* Uses different terminology (prosopon vs hypostasis)

**Assessment:** Nestorius affirms union but uses different terminology. **Terminological mismatch, not necessarily substantive denial.**

### 8C. ANATHEMA 3 (Rejecting Mere Conjunction)
**Cyril:** "If anyone divides in the one Christ the hypostases after the union, joining them only by a conjunction (synapheia) of dignity or authority or power... let him be anathema."

**Nestorius's Evidence:**
* Uses synapheia (conjunction)
* But also affirms "one prosopon of union"
* Denies mere external conjunction (insists on real incarnation)

**Assessment:** Cyril's anathema targets "mere conjunction of dignity/authority/power." Nestorius's own evidence suggests he meant more than mere external conjunction. **Possible misrepresentation.**

### 8D. ANATHEMA 12 (Impassible Suffered)
**Cyril:** "If anyone does not confess that the Word of God suffered in the flesh and was crucified in the flesh and tasted death in the flesh... let him be anathema."

**Nestorius's Evidence:**
* Affirms Word is impassible (as divine)
* Affirms Christ suffered (in flesh)
* Distinguishes predicates according to nature

**Assessment:** Nestorius would say "Word suffered in flesh" but mean "Christ (one prosopon of union) suffered in human nature." **Terminological difference, not necessarily denial.**

### 8E. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
**Do Cyril's anathemas accurately represent Nestorius's position?**
* **Anathema 1:** PARTIALLY MISREPRESENTS (Nestorius accepts Theotokos in qualified sense)
* **Anathema 2:** TERMINOLOGICAL MISMATCH (Nestorius uses prosopon, not hypostasis)
* **Anathema 3:** POSSIBLY MISREPRESENTS (Nestorius means more than "mere conjunction")
* **Anathema 12:** TERMINOLOGICAL DIFFERENCE (Nestorius distinguishes predicates but does not deny suffering)

**Critical:** Do not assume that every later interpretation of an anathema accurately represents Nestorius's own position.

## 9. NESTORIUS VS CHALCEDON

### 9A. CHALCEDONIAN DEFINITION (451 CE)
**Key Formula:**
* One hypostasis (ὑπόστασις)
* Two natures (φύσεις)
* Without confusion, without change, without division, without separation
* Distinction of natures not abolished by union

### 9B. NESTORIUS'S EVIDENCE
**Key Formula:**
* One prosopon of union
* Two natures
* Two prosopa subsist in respective natures
* Prosopic union (synapheia)

### 9C. COMPARISON

| Element | Nestorius | Chalcedon |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Unity** | One prosopon of union | One hypostasis |
| **Duality** | Two natures | Two natures |
| **Union Mode** | Prosopic (synapheia) | Hypostatic (henōsis) |
| **Terminology** | Prosopon, synapheia | Hypostasis, physis |
| **Distinction** | Distinguish predicates by nature | Without confusion/change/division/separation |

### 9D. CONTINUITY
* Both affirm two natures
* Both affirm one Christ
* Both reject confusion of natures
* Both reject division into two sons

### 9E. DIFFERENCE
* **Terminology:** Prosopon vs hypostasis
* **Subsistence:** Nestorius's "two prosopa subsist" vs Chalcedon's "one hypostasis"
* **Union Mode:** Synapheia vs henōsis

### 9F. HISTORICAL RECEPTION
* Chalcedon upheld Nestorius's condemnation
* Chalcedon adopted dyophysite language
* Nestorius claimed Chalcedon vindicated him (Bazaar)
* Chalcedon approved Cyril's Second Letter and Leo's Tome

### 9G. CLASSIFICATION
**Claim:** "Chalcedon vindicated Nestorius."
**Status:** UNSAFE (Chalcedon upheld his condemnation; Nestorius's claim is self-serving interpretation)

**Claim:** "Chalcedon completely contradicted everything Nestorius taught."
**Status:** UNSAFE (Chalcedon adopted dyophysite language Nestorius championed; terminological differences remain)

**Claim:** "Chalcedon and Nestorius share dyophysite commitment but differ in terminology and union mode."
**Status:** SAFE (supported by evidence comparison)

**Claim:** "Chalcedonian Christology and Nestorian Christology require careful terminology comparison."
**Status:** SAFE (terminological mismatch is demonstrable)

## 10. TERMINOLOGY ANALYSIS

### 10A. PHYSIS (φύσις)
* **Nestorius:** Two physes (divine and human) — concrete realities
* **Cyril:** Mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē — "one incarnate nature of God the Word"
* **Chalcedon:** Two physes without confusion/change/division/separation
* **Semantic Range:** Not fixed across 4th/5th century usage

### 10B. OUSIA (οὐσία)
* **Nestorius:** Divine and human ousiai remain distinct
* **Cyril:** Uses less frequently than physis/hypostasis
* **Chalcedon:** Homoousios with Father (divinity) and homoousios with us (humanity)
* **Semantic Range:** Essence/substance — relatively stable

### 10C. HYPOSTASIS (ὑπόστασις)
* **Nestorius:** Closely aligned with physis (concrete reality)
* **Cyril:** Concrete subject of union (henōsis kat' hypostasin)
* **Chalcedon:** One hypostasis, two natures
* **Semantic Range:** Shifted significantly between Cappadocians (381) and Chalcedon (451)

### 10D. PROSOPON (πρόσωπον)
* **Nestorius:** Observable character, defining properties; key term for union
* **Cyril:** Uses less frequently; suspects Nestorius's usage
* **Chalcedon:** One prosopon (implicitly; explicit in later reception)
* **Semantic Range:** Face/person/character — highly variable

### 10E. UNION TERMS
* **Nestorius:** Synapheia (conjunction), prosopic union
* **Cyril:** Henōsis (union), hypostatic union
* **Chalcedon:** Henōsis (union)

### 10F. CRITICAL OBSERVATION
**Do NOT assume 4th/5th-century terminology has one fixed meaning.**
* Nestorius and Cyril used same Greek terms with different semantic ranges
* Terminological mismatch is real, not merely apparent
* Chalcedon attempted to standardize terminology but ambiguities remained

## 12. STEELMAN ANALYSIS

### 12A. INTERPRETATION A (Legitimate Theological Concern)
**Nestorius's Christology represents a legitimate concern to preserve the full reality of Christ's humanity and the distinction between divine and human predicates.**

**Supporting Evidence:**
* Nestorius explicitly affirms both natures (divine and human)
* Nestorius insists on distinction of predicates (Word is impassible; Christ suffered in flesh)
* Nestorius rejects Apollinarianism (mixing/confusion of natures)
* Nestorius rejects Arianism (denial of divinity)
* Nestorius affirms real incarnation (not mere indwelling)
* Nestorius's concern: preserve divine impassibility while affirming real humanity
* Chalcedon later adopted dyophysite language (two natures without confusion/change/division/separation)
* Modern scholarship (Bethune-Baker, Anastos) argues Nestorius was substantially orthodox

**Strengths:**
* Protects divine impassibility
* Preserves full reality of humanity
* Avoids monophysitism
* Aligns with later Chalcedonian dyophysitism

**Weaknesses:**
* Prosopon terminology is complex (two prosopa subsist even in one prosopon of union)
* Risks implying two subjects despite intention
* Synapheia (conjunction) language weaker than henōsis (union)
* Qualified acceptance of Theotokos insufficient for Cyril and later tradition

### 12B. INTERPRETATION B (Genuine Theological Problem)
**Nestorius's formulations risked dividing the unity of Christ and therefore required correction from Cyril and later conciliar theology.**

**Supporting Evidence:**
* Nestorius's "two prosopa subsist" language is problematic (even in one prosopon of union)
* Nestorius's prosopic union (synapheia) is weaker than hypostatic union (henōsis)
* Nestorius's qualified acceptance of Theotokos undermines Christological unity
* Nestorius's distinction of predicates risks dividing Christ into two subjects
* Cyril identified genuine problem: if Word is only loosely conjoined to humanity, incarnation is undermined
* Ephesus condemned Nestorius (ecclesiastical judgment)
* Chalcedon upheld Nestorius's condemnation (despite dyophysite language)
* Church of the East later rejected "Nestorian" label (Patriarch Dinkha IV, 1976)

**Strengths:**
* Protects Christological unity
* Preserves reality of incarnation
* Aligns with conciliar tradition
* Identifies genuine terminological problem in Nestorius's formulation

**Weaknesses:**
* Cyril's miaphysite language (mia physis) risks monophysitism
* Cyril's polemic misrepresents Nestorius's actual position
* Ephesus was procedurally irregular (Cyril opened before Antiochenes arrived)
* Historical condemnation ≠ theological correctness

### 12C. CRITICAL OBSERVATION
**Both interpretations have merit:**
* Nestorius's concern (preserve distinction) is legitimate
* Cyril's concern (preserve unity) is legitimate
* Both identify real theological problems in the other's formulation
* Terminological mismatch is real, not merely apparent
* Chalcedon attempted to synthesize both concerns (two natures, one hypostasis)

**Do not decide between these solely by historical labels.**

## 13. TRANSMISSION LIMITATIONS

### 13A. GREEK ORIGINALS LOST
* Bazaar of Heracleides: Greek original not extant
* Sermons: Only fragments survive
* Letters: Fragments in conciliar acts
* Tragoedia: Fragments only

### 13B. SYRIAC TRANSMISSION
* Bazaar survives only in Syriac translation
* Translation acknowledged as sometimes inaccurate
* Possible interpolations (Bevan 2013)
* Manuscript damage (133 pages lost)

### 13C. HOSTILE PRESERVATION
* Sermons and letters preserved in Acts of Council of Ephesus (compiled by opponents)
* Cyril's works quote Nestorius selectively
* Conciliar acts serve polemical purpose

### 13D. RECONSTRUCTION DIFFICULTIES
* Cannot verify completeness of Nestorius's responses
* Cannot verify original context of fragments
* Cannot determine whether Nestorius refined his position over time (428 vs 451)

### 13E. MODERN SCHOLARLY DISAGREEMENT
* Bethune-Baker (1908): Nestorius substantially orthodox
* Chapman (1911): Nestorius genuinely heretical
* Grillmeier (1975): Balanced historical analysis
* McGuckin (1994): Contextualizes controversy
* Bevan (2009, 2013): Identifies interpolations

**No scholarly consensus on Nestorius's orthodoxy.**

## 14. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE

### 14A. PHYSICAL INDEPENDENCE
* Cyril, Nestorius, Leo, Chalcedon are distinct documents
* Different authors, different contexts, different dates

### 14B. LITERARY DEPENDENCE
* Cyril quotes Nestorius (polemical context)
* Chalcedon quotes Cyril's Second Letter and Leo's Tome
* Nestorius responds to Cyril (Bazaar)

### 14C. POLEMICAL DEPENDENCE
* Cyril's representation of Nestorius is polemical
* Nestorius's representation of Cyril is polemical
* Conciliar acts compiled by Nestorius's opponents

### 14D. THEOLOGICAL INFLUENCE
* Cyril influences Chalcedon
* Leo influences Chalcedon
* Nestorius influences Church of the East

### 14E. CONCILIAR RECEPTION
