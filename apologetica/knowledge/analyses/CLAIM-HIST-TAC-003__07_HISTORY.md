# Analysis 07_HISTORY: Nero and the Persecution of Christians
Tacitus documents the use of Christians as scapegoats following the Great Fire of Rome (64 CE), providing external Roman evidence of imperial persecution of the early Christian movement. This complements Josephus's accounts of first-century Jewish-Roman conflict and Irenaeus's later patristic testimony about Christian reception.

## Relationship to Josephus
* Josephus's *Testimonium Flavianum* (Antiquities XVIII.3.3) — DISPUTED authenticity.
* Josephus's James Passage (Antiquities XX.9.1) — QUALIFIED authenticity.
* Tacitus's *Annals* 15.44 — STRONG EVIDENCE; provides independent Roman historical perspective.

## Counterargument / Limitations
* Tacitus is not contemporary with Jesus.
* Annals composed c. 115–120 CE; event 64 CE.
* Surviving manuscripts are medieval (Second Medicean, 11th century).
* Tacitus may have relied on Roman administrative records, public knowledge, or prior historians.
* Tacitus does not provide eyewitness testimony.
* Passage does not establish resurrection, divinity, or revelation.
