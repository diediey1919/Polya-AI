## 4. THE THREE CHAPTERS

### 4.1 Chapter 1: Theodore of Mopsuestia

**Identity:**
- Theodore of Mopsuestia (c. 350–428)
- Bishop of Mopsuestia (Cilicia)
- Teacher of Nestorius
- Prominent Antiochene exegete

**Writings Condemned:**
- Selected exegetical works (commentaries on Pauline epistles, Psalms, Minor Prophets)
- Christological treatises (On the Incarnation, Against the Eunomians)
- Specific passages deemed Nestorian

**Theological Issues:**
- Emphasis on distinction of natures (Antiochene tradition)
- Perceived adoptionism (Word "indwelt" the man Jesus)
- Reluctance to apply divine predicates to the human Christ
- Criticism of Cyril's Twelve Anathemas

**Relationship to Nestorius:**
- Theodore was Nestorius's teacher
- Nestorius drew on Theodore's exegetical methods
- But Theodore ≠ Nestorius (Theodore died 20 years before Nestorian controversy)

**Relationship to Cyril:**
- Theodore criticized Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Cyril never directly attacked Theodore (Theodore died before controversy)

**Relationship to Ephesus:**
- Theodore not discussed at Ephesus (431)
- Theodore's student Nestorius was condemned

**Relationship to Chalcedon:**
- Theodore not mentioned at Chalcedon (451)
- Chalcedon did not condemn Theodore
- Theodore was dead 23 years before Chalcedon

**What Was Condemned:**
- Specific writings (not the person, who was dead)
- Passages deemed Nestorian
- Anti-Cyrillian polemics

**What Was NOT Condemned:**
- Theodore's exegetical method (still influential)
- Theodore's non-controversial writings
- Theodore's personal orthodoxy (deceased, cannot defend himself)

### 4.2 Chapter 2: Theodoret of Cyrus

**Identity:**
- Theodoret of Cyrus (c. 393–457)
- Bishop of Cyrus (Syria)
- Prominent Antiochene theologian
- Defended Nestorius at Ephesus (431)

**Writings Condemned:**
- Against Cyril's Twelve Anathemas (Eranistes, five dialogues)
- Selected letters defending Nestorius
- Specific passages criticizing Cyril

**Theological Issues:**
- Defense of Nestorius against Cyril's anathemas
- Emphasis on distinction of natures
- Criticism of Theotokos terminology (preferred Christotokos)
- Opposition to Cyril's "one nature" formula

**Relationship to Nestorius:**
- Theodoret defended Nestorius at Ephesus
- Theodoret was friend and ally of Nestorius
- But Theodoret ≠ Nestorius (Theodoret had his own theological agenda)

**Relationship to Cyril:**
- Theodoret wrote extensively against Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Cyril viewed Theodoret as Nestorian
- Theodoret viewed Cyril as Apollinarian

**Relationship to Ephesus:**
- Theodoret attended Ephesus (431) with John of Antioch's delegation
- Theodoret was condemned by Cyril's council
- Theodoret participated in John's counter-council

**Relationship to Chalcedon:**
- Theodoret attended Chalcedon (451)
- Theodoret was restored to his see (after condemning Nestorius)
- Chalcedon did not condemn Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings
- Chalcedon read Theodoret's Second Letter to Cyril (pro-Chalcedonian)

**Critical Distinction:**
- **Person:** Theodoret (restored at Chalcedon, died in communion)
- **Writings:** Anti-Cyrillian works (condemned at 553)
- **Specific Passages:** Selected texts (not all writings)
- **Later Reception:** Person honored, writings condemned

**What Was Condemned:**
- Anti-Cyrillian writings (Eranistes, Against the Twelve Anathemas)
- Letters defending Nestorius
- Specific passages criticizing Cyril

**What Was NOT Condemned:**
- Theodoret's person (died in communion with Chalcedon)
- Theodoret's pro-Chalcedonian writings
- Theodoret's exegetical works (still influential)

### 4.3 Chapter 3: Letter of Ibas to Mari the Persian

**Identity:**
- Ibas of Edessa (d. 457)
- Bishop of Edessa
- Associated with Antiochene school
- Translated Theodore's works into Syriac

**Letter Condemned:**
- Letter to Mari the Persian (c. 433)
- Criticized Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Defended Theodore
- Reported on Nestorian controversy from Antiochene perspective

**Theological Issues:**
- Criticism of Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Defense of Theodore
- Report that Cyril had "repented" of his anathemas (disputed)
- Antiochene perspective on Nestorian controversy

**Relationship to Nestorius:**
- Ibas was not a direct associate of Nestorius
- Ibas defended Theodore (Nestorius's teacher)
- Ibas's letter reported on Nestorian controversy

**Relationship to Cyril:**
- Ibas criticized Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- Ibas claimed Cyril had "repented" (disputed)
- Cyril never directly attacked Ibas

**Relationship to Ephesus:**
- Ibas not present at Ephesus (431)
- Ibas's letter written after Ephesus (c. 433)

**Relationship to Chalcedon:**
- Ibas attended Chalcedon (451)
- Ibas was restored to his see (after condemning Nestorius)
- Chalcedon did not condemn Ibas's letter
- Chalcedon read the letter (but did not anathematize it)

**Critical Question:**
How can the Letter of Ibas be condemned in 553 while Ibas himself had been restored at Chalcedon?

**Competing Interpretations:**

**Interpretation A (Chalcedonian Defense):**
- Chalcedon restored Ibas as a person (after he condemned Nestorius)
- Chalcedon did not endorse Ibas's letter
- 553 condemned the letter, not the person
- Distinction between person and writings is legitimate

**Interpretation B (Non-Chalcedonian Critique):**
- Chalcedon read the letter and did not condemn it
- 553 contradicts Chalcedon by condemning what Chalcedon accepted
- This proves Chalcedon was Nestorian
- 553 corrected Chalcedon's error

**Interpretation C (Historical Reconstruction):**
- Chalcedon read the letter but did not formally endorse it
- Chalcedon's silence ≠ endorsement
- 553's condemnation reflects later theological development
- Both councils operated in different historical contexts

**What Was Condemned:**
- The Letter to Mari the Persian (specific document)
- Anti-Cyrillian content
- Defense of Theodore

**What Was NOT Condemned:**
- Ibas's person (died in communion with Chalcedon)
- Ibas's other writings
- Ibas's episcopal ministry

**Attribution Dispute:**
- Some scholars question whether the letter is authentic
- Others accept authenticity but question transmission
- If disputed, label: **DISPUTED**

## 5. THEODORE OF MOPSUESTIA (DETAILED)

### 5.1 Theodore's Christology

**Antiochene Tradition:**
- Emphasis on distinction of natures
- Historical-grammatical exegesis
- Reluctance to apply divine predicates to human Christ
- Concern for full humanity

**Key Themes:**
- Word "indwelt" the man Jesus (inhabitation model)
- Two natures, one person (but person defined differently than Cyril)
- Prosopic union (union in one prosopon)
- Distinction of operations (divine and human)

**Relationship to Nestorius:**
- Theodore was Nestorius's teacher
- Nestorius drew on Theodore's methods
- But Theodore's Christology was more nuanced than Nestorius's popular preaching

### 5.2 Condemnation After Death

**Historical Precedent:**
- Condemnation of deceased persons was controversial
- Theodore could not defend himself
- But precedent existed (Origen condemned posthumously)

**Justinian's Rationale:**
- Theodore was source of Nestorian error
- Condemning Theodore would undercut Nestorianism
- Theological genealogy: Theodore → Nestorius

### 5.3 Relationship to Chalcedon

**Chalcedon's Silence:**
- Chalcedon did not mention Theodore
- Chalcedon did not condemn Theodore
- Chalcedon focused on Nestorius and Eutyches

**553's Condemnation:**
- 553 explicitly condemned Theodore's writings
- 553 argued Theodore was source of Nestorian error
- 553 extended Chalcedon's anti-Nestorian logic

## 6. THEODORET OF CYRUS (DETAILED)

### 6.1 Theodoret's Anti-Cyrillian Writings

**Eranistes (447):**
- Three dialogues attacking Cyril's Christology
- Argued Cyril was Apollinarian
- Defended Antiochene distinction of natures

**Against Cyril's Twelve Anathemas:**
- Systematic refutation of each anathema
- Argued Cyril's formulas were heretical
- Defended Nestorius against Cyril

### 6.2 Reception at Chalcedon

**Chalcedon's Actions:**
- Theodoret attended Chalcedon (451)
- Theodoret condemned Nestorius (verbally)
- Theodoret was restored to his see
- Chalcedon read Theodoret's Second Letter to Cyril (pro-Chalcedonian)

**Chalcedon's Silence on Anti-Cyrillian Writings:**
- Chalcedon did not condemn Eranistes
- Chalcedon did not condemn Against the Twelve Anathemas
- Chalcedon focused on current controversy, not past polemics

### 6.3 Later Condemnation (553)

**553's Actions:**
- 553 condemned Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings
- 553 did not condemn Theodoret's person
- 553 argued these writings were Nestorian

**Critical Distinction:**
- Person: restored at Chalcedon, died in communion
- Writings: condemned at 553
- This is not a contradiction if person ≠ writings

## 8. JUSTINIAN

### 8.1 Theological Policy

**Justinian's Goal:**
- Ecclesiastical unity to strengthen imperial cohesion
- Reconciliation with non-Chalcedonians
- Defense of Chalcedon (with Cyrillian interpretation)

**Justinian's Strategy:**
- Condemn Nestorian-associated writings (Three Chapters)
- Reaffirm Chalcedon (with Cyrillian emphasis)
- Demonstrate that Chalcedon was not Nestorian

### 8.2 Three Chapters Edict (543-544)

**Content:**
- Condemned Theodore's writings
- Condemned Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings
- Condemned Ibas's letter to Mari

**Rationale:**
- These writings were source of Nestorian error
- Condemning them would undercut Nestorianism
- This would reconcile non-Chalcedonians

### 8.3 Imperial Pressure

**Pressure on Pope Vigilius:**
- Summoned Vigilius to Constantinople (547)
- Pressured Vigilius to condemn Three Chapters
- Threatened Vigilius with deposition
- Isolated Vigilius from Western support

**Pressure on Western Bishops:**
- Imperial coercion of African bishops
- Exile of resistant bishops
- Political pressure on Italian bishops

**Important Distinction:**
- Imperial initiative ≠ theological invalidity
- Imperial pressure ≠ automatic proof of fabrication
- Justinian's motives may have been mixed, but theological issues were real

## 10. CHALCEDON 451 AND CONSTANTINOPLE II 553

### 10.1 Critical Question

**Did Constantinople II reject Chalcedon?**

**Test Separately:**

**A. Rejection of Chalcedon's Definition:**
- **UNSAFE:** 553 explicitly reaffirmed Chalcedon
- 553's fourteen anathemas presuppose Chalcedon
- 553 argued it was clarifying Chalcedon, not rejecting it

**B. Reinterpretation of Chalcedon:**
- **SAFE:** 553 reinterpreted Chalcedon in Cyrillian terms
- 553 emphasized "one person" over "two natures"
- 553 condemned writings Chalcedon had not condemned

**C. Condemnation of Persons/Writings:**
- **SAFE:** 553 condemned Theodore, Theodoret's writings, Ibas's letter
- Chalcedon had not condemned these
- 553 extended Chalcedon's anti-Nestorian logic

**D. Clarification of Chalcedonian Christology:**
- **SAFE:** 553 clarified Chalcedon's Christology
- 553 emphasized Cyrillian themes (one person, Theotokos, suffering of the Word)
- 553 developed Chalcedon's implications

**E. Reception of Cyril:**
- **SAFE:** 553 strengthened Cyril's authority within Chalcedonian framework
- 553 cited Cyril extensively
- 553 argued Cyril and Chalcedon were compatible

### 10.2 Council's Own Claim

**553's Self-Understanding:**
- 553 claimed to receive previous councils (Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Chalcedon)
- 553 claimed to clarify Chalcedon, not reject it
- 553 claimed to defend orthodoxy against Nestorian error

**Modern Interpretations:**

**Interpretation A (Chalcedonian):**
- 553 clarified Chalcedon
- 553 did not reject Chalcedon
- 553 strengthened Cyrillian interpretation

**Interpretation B (Non-Chalcedonian):**

- 553 proves Chalcedon was Nestorian

## 14. FOURTEEN ANATHEMAS

### 14.1 Overview

**553's Fourteen Anathemas:**
- Theological propositions condemned as heretical
- Targeted Nestorian and anti-Cyrillian positions
- Presupposed Chalcedon's framework

### 14.2 Key Anathemas

**Anathema 1:**
- Condemns denial of Theotokos
- Reaffirms Ephesus (431)

**Anathema 2:**
- Condemns "two sons" or "two Christs"
- Reaffirms Ephesus and Chalcedon

**Anathema 3:**
- Condemns "conjunction" (συνάφεια) instead of "union" (ἕνωσις)
- Targets Nestorian prosopic union

**Anathema 4:**
- Condemns denial of hypostatic union
- Reaffirms Cyril and Chalcedon

**Anathema 5:**
- Condemns denial that the Word suffered in the flesh
- Theopaschite emphasis (Cyrillian)

**Anathema 8:**
- Condemns Theodore of Mopsuestia
- Condemns Theodore's writings

**Anathema 9:**
- Condemns Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings
- Condemns defense of Nestorius

**Anathema 10:**
- Condemns Ibas's letter to Mari
- Condemns anti-Cyrillian content

### 14.3 Relationship to Cyril's Twelve Anathemas

**Similarities:**
- Both target Nestorian positions
- Both emphasize hypostatic union
- Both affirm Theotokos

**Differences:**
- Cyril's Twelve: directed at Nestorius personally
- 553's Fourteen: directed at Theodore, Theodoret, Ibas
- 553's Fourteen: more extensive, more detailed

**Classification:**
- Not identical to Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
- But conceptually related
- Developed in 6th-century context

## 17. "DID 553 CANCEL CHALCEDON?"

### 17.1 Claim

**"Constantinople II cancelled/reversed Chalcedon."**

### 17.2 Classification

**UNSAFE**

### 17.3 Evidence

**553 Explicitly Received Previous Councils:**
- 553's acts explicitly reaffirmed Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Chalcedon
- 553 claimed to clarify Chalcedon, not reject it

**Condemnation Targeted Three Chapters:**
- 553 condemned Theodore, Theodoret's writings, Ibas's letter
- 553 did not condemn Chalcedon's Definition

**Historical Relationship Was Contested:**
- Some contemporaries viewed 553 as contradicting Chalcedon
- Others viewed 553 as clarifying Chalcedon
- Reception varied by region

**Reception Was Complex:**
- Eastern bishops: predominantly accepted 553
- Western bishops: initially resisted, eventually accepted
- Non-Chalcedonians: rejected as insufficient

**Later Theological Interpretation:**
- Eastern Orthodox: 553 clarified Chalcedon
- Roman Catholic: 553 clarified Chalcedon

### 17.4 Conclusion

553 did not cancel Chalcedon. It reinterpreted Chalcedon in Cyrillian terms and condemned writings Chalcedon had not condemned. This is a development, not a cancellation.

## 18. "DID 553 PROVE CHALCEDON WAS WRONG?"

### 18.1 Claim

### 18.2 Classification

### 18.3 Evidence

**553's Condemnation ≠ Proof of Chalcedon's Error:**
- 553 argued these writings were Nestorian
- But 553's condemnation does not prove Chalcedon was Nestorian
- Chalcedon's Definition is compatible with 553's condemnation

### 18.4 Conclusion

Condemnation of Three Chapters does not logically establish that Chalcedon's Definition was false. It establishes that 553 viewed these writings as Nestorian, but Chalcedon's Definition itself was not condemned.

## 23. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### 23.1 Side A: Chalcedonian Interpretation

**Strongest Case:**

553 clarified Chalcedon by:
1. **Condemning Nestorian-associated writings:** Theodore, Theodoret's anti-Cyrillian writings, Ibas's letter were sources of Nestorian error. Condemning them undercut Nestorianism.

2. **Reinforcing Cyril:** 553 cited Cyril extensively, demonstrating that Chalcedon was compatible with Cyril. This strengthened Cyrillian interpretation of Chalcedon.

3. **Not rejecting Chalcedon's Definition:** 553 explicitly reaffirmed Chalcedon. 553's fourteen anathemas presuppose Chalcedon's framework. 553 claimed to clarify Chalcedon, not reject it.

4. **Developing Chalcedonian Christology:** 553 emphasized "one person" over "two natures," used theopaschite language ("One of the Trinity suffered"), and strengthened Theotokos. This developed Chalcedon's implications without contradicting its Definition.

5. **Addressing legitimate concerns:** Non-Chalcedonians had legitimate concerns about Nestorian tendencies in some Antiochene writings. 553 addressed these concerns by condemning problematic writings.

6. **Imperial initiative ≠ theological invalidity:** Justinian's involvement does not automatically invalidate 553's theological decisions. Theological issues were real, regardless of imperial motives.

**Conclusion:** 553 is a legitimate development of Chalcedonian Christology, clarifying Chalcedon in Cyrillian terms and condemning Nestorian-associated writings.

---

### 23.2 Side B: Critical / Non-Chalcedonian Interpretation

**Strongest Case:**

553 demonstrates that:
1. **Chalcedon remained contested:** 553's need to condemn Three Chapters shows Chalcedon's reception was incomplete. Non-Chalcedonians had legitimate concerns about Chalcedon's Nestorian tendencies.

2. **Later councils modified interpretive framework:** 553 reinterpreted Chalcedon in Cyrillian terms, emphasizing "one person" over "two natures." This shows Chalcedon's framework was insufficient and required modification.

3. **Cyrillian language gained stronger conciliar prominence:** 553 cited Cyril extensively, used theopaschite language, and strengthened Theotokos. This shows Cyril's theology was more orthodox than Chalcedon's formulation.

4. **Imperial intervention materially shaped settlement:** Justinian convoked 553, pressured Vigilius, and coerced Western bishops. This shows 553 was driven by imperial policy, not purely theological concerns.

5. **Chalcedon's silence on Three Chapters was problematic:** Chalcedon did not condemn Theodore, Theodoret's writings, or Ibas's letter. This shows Chalcedon tolerated Nestorian-associated writings, which 553 had to correct.

6. **Non-Chalcedonian rejection shows insufficiency:** Non-Chalcedonians rejected 553 as insufficient. They argued Chalcedon was still Nestorian. This shows 553 did not adequately address their concerns.

**Conclusion:** 553 demonstrates that Chalcedon remained contested, required modification, and was insufficient for reconciliation. 553 vindicated Cyril but did not go far enough.
