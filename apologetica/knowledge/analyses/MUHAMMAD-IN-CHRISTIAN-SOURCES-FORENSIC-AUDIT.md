## 1. Executive Summary

Forensic audit of **Muhammad in Christian sources**: early Christian witnesses (Doctrina Jacobi c.634, Thomas the Presbyter c.640, Sebeos c.660s, John bar Penkaye c.687, John of Damascus c.730–750) independently attest the 7th-century emergence of an Arab prophetic leader/movement associated with Muhammad, while uniformly framing him polemically, apocalyptically, or heresiologically — never as a true prophet. Biblical prophecy claims (Deut 18, Deut 33, Isaiah, Song 5:16 mahamadim, John 14–16 Paraclete/Periklutos) rest on contested confessional exegesis; lexical claims (mahamadim as proper name, Periklutos textual corruption) lack philological/manuscript support. Mention never equals endorsement; every escalation (mention → explicit identification → prophetic recognition → biblical prediction) is proven separately.

## 6. Doctrina Jacobi

Doctrina Jacobi nuper baptizati — Greek anti-Jewish dialogue, composed c.634 (Palestine/North Africa context). Manuscript tradition: Greek; Christian polemical setting. Content: report of a "false/deceptive prophet" appearing among the Saracens, armed movement context matching 630s conquests. Muhammad NOT explicitly named in primary witness — Arab prophet report, not explicit Muhammad identification. Theological attitude: hostile/apocalyptic. Independence: near-contemporary, high historical value for Arab prophetic movement; alternative identification (tribal leader/prophet figure) noted. Verdict: attests Arab prophet phenomenon c.634; does NOT prove Muhammad by name without qualification.

## 7. Thomas the Presbyter

Syriac chronicle entry covering c.640 material. Syriac text; manuscript transmission via later chronicle compilations. Records battle (634, near Palestine/Gaza region) of Romans against "the Arabs of Muhammad" (Tayyaye d-Mhmd). Chronological proximity: within ~6 years of event — highest-value external witness. Historical value: establishes "Muhammad" as leader-designation of Arab forces by c.634–640. Independence: eastern Syriac observation, corroborated by STEP 114/118 triangulation. No prophetic endorsement; neutral-military notice.

## 8. Sebeos

Armenian historical tradition attributed to Bishop Sebeos, compilation c.660s. Armenian text; manuscript transmission through Armenian chronicle tradition. Explicitly names Mahmet: merchant, preacher of Abrahamic monotheism/way of truth to Ishmaelites, unifier of Arab movement; Palestine/Syria context; monotheism and Abrahamic lineage emphasized. Historical value: first explicit narrative identification with religious description. Description ≠ theological endorsement — providential/anti-imperial polemical lens, not prophetic recognition.

## 9. John bar Penkaye

East-Syriac monk, Ktaba d-resh melle (Book of Main Points), composed c.687. Syriac; manuscript transmission via later copies. Treats Arab conquest and dominion; references Muhammad (Mhmd) as leader/guide of Hagarenes; religious description framed as divine chastisement/permission motif. Historical distance: ~50 years post-Muhammad — early but non-eyewitness. Independence: Mesopotamian monastic perspective distinct from Greek/Armenian lines. Manuscript caution observed.

## 15. John of Damascus

John of Damascus (d.c.749), De Haeresibus ch.100/101 (De haeresibus compendium), Greek, Umayyad court context (Mar Saba/Palestine). Heresiological framework: Islam as "heresy of the Ishmaelites," 100th heresy. Muhammad (Mamed): self-styled prophet, Arian-monk influence (Bahira/Sergius motif), Zayd/Zaynab and Aqaba narratives. Source dependence: draws on circulating Syrian polemic, not eyewitness. Value: theological/polemical (first systematic Greek heresiology), NOT historical biography. Never cited as eyewitness.

## 22. Song of Songs

Song 5:16 מחמדים (mahamadim): morphology — masculine plural noun/adjective from root h-m-d ("desire, delight"), suffix -im plural; semantic range "lovely/desirable things"; context: erotic encomium of lover's mouth/speech ("his mouth is sweetness, and all of him is delights"). Not a personal name (no proper-name syntax, plural form incompatible with singular Muhammad). Translation traditions (LXX, Vulgate, Targum) render common noun. Jewish/Christian: beloved's praise. Muslim apologetic: consonantal similarity claim. Rule: root similarity ≠ personal-name reference. Status: UNSUPPORTED as Muhammad prediction.

## 23. John 14–16

Farewell Discourse Paraclete sayings (14:16–17, 14:26; 15:26; 16:7–14). Discourse context: Jesus' departure, disciples' present distress, Spirit's imminent sending to same community. 14:26 explicitly identifies Paraclete as Holy Spirit. Function: teaching/reminding, testifying, convicting world, guiding into truth. Dating: Johannine corpus c.90–100. Muslim reading: future human prophet (6 centuries later). Problem: discourse immediacy ("you know him," "with you") resists distant-future human referent without relecture.

## 24. Paraclete

παράκλητος (parakletos): lexical meaning advocate/intercessor/comforter/counselor (legal-paracletic Greek). Johannine usage: 4 Gospel sayings + 1 John 2:1 (Jesus as parakletos). Grammar: masculine ekeinos, personal agency verbs. Manuscript evidence: unanimous parakletos in papyri/uncials. Christian interpretation: Holy Spirit (14:26 anchor). Muslim interpretation: Ahmad/Muhammad. Linguistic verdict: parakletos→Muhammad requires breaking 14:26 identification and discourse proximity.
