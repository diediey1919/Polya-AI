## 3. CRITICAL TEXT FUNCTION
A critical Greek New Testament is an academic reconstruction of the Greek text based on manuscript and patristic evidence. It:
- Reconstructs an edited Greek text from manuscript and other textual evidence.
- Is NOT itself a manuscript.
- Is NOT an autograph.
- Represents editorial decisions by textual scholars.
- May differ from individual manuscript readings.
- Requires an apparatus or supporting documentation to evaluate individual textual decisions.
- Does NOT equal "the original New Testament" in an unmediated physical sense.

## 4. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE ANALYSIS
- **Physical Source Independence:** Primary manuscripts (P66, P75, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus) are physically independent ancient artifacts.
- **Editorial Independence:** NA28 and SBLGNT share significant reliance on the Alexandrian textual tradition and overlapping manuscript citation bases, but editorial committees (INTF vs. Michael W. Holmes) operate independently with distinct methodological criteria (e.g., SBLGNT's independent evaluation of text-critical decisions and regularization). NA28 and SBLGNT are **NOT** automatically independent textual witnesses simply because they are different editions.
- **Genealogical & Textual Agreement:** High overlap in early witnesses (particularly Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus), resulting in close textual agreement in most passages while retaining distinct localized variant choices.

## 5. TEXTUAL EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
MANUSCRIPT → TRANSCRIPTION → CRITICAL EDITION → TRANSLATION → COMMENTARY → THEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
- *Rule:* Movement downward in this chain does not automatically preserve every property of the previous layer (e.g., a translation or commentary does not inherit the complete variant apparatus of a critical edition, nor does a critical edition inherit the physical manuscript materiality).

## 7. CANON CONTROL
- **Rule:** Do NOT infer the New Testament canon from the existence of a Greek critical edition. Textual transmission, canon formation, authorship, apostolic attribution, and doctrine remain strictly separate analytical categories.

## 8. AUTHORS AND TEXTS
- **Rule:** Traditional attribution is not automatically equated with historical authorship. Separate fields maintained:
  - TRADITIONAL ATTRIBUTION: e.g., John the Apostle (Gospel of John).
  - EARLY ATTRIBUTION: e.g., 2nd-century patristic testimony (Irenaeus, Muratorian Canon).
  - MODERN SCHOLARLY POSITION: DEBATED / MULTI-VALUED (ranging from apostolic eyewitness to Johannine community redaction).
  - EVIDENCE STATUS: DISPUTED / UNKNOWN in raw textual critical layers.

## 9. APOLOGETIC CLAIM TEST
A. "The critical Greek text is the original autograph." — **UNSAFE** (Critical texts are editorial reconstructions, not surviving autographs).
B. "The critical Greek text is an editorial reconstruction." — **SAFE** (Accurately describes the nature of critical editions).
C. "The critical text contains no variants." — **UNSAFE** (Critical editions explicitly record variants in their apparatus).
D. "Every manuscript agrees with the critical text." — **UNSAFE** (Manuscripts diverge frequently; critical texts choose among them).
E. "A critical edition proves the New Testament canon." — **UNSUPPORTED** (Textual recovery does not establish canonical boundary decisions).
F. "A critical edition proves apostolic authorship." — **UNSUPPORTED** (Textual reconstruction establishes wording, not historical authorship).
G. "A critical edition provides a scholarly reconstruction based on textual evidence." — **SAFE** (Accurately reflects academic methodology).
