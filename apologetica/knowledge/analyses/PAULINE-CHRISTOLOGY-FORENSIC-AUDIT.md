3. Disputed epistles are never used as sole evidence to prove Paul's personal first-century historical theology without qualification.

## 3. PAUL'S JEWISH CONTEXT & MONOTHEISM

### 3A. Second Temple Jewish Monotheism
- Paul was a Pharisaic Jew (Phil 3:5, Gal 1:13–14) whose worldview was grounded in Israel's covenantal monotheism (*Shema*, Deut 6:4: "Hear, O Israel: YHWH our God, YHWH is one").
- Second Temple Jewish monotheism was not a modern abstract philosophical unitarianism; it was a *covenantal and creator/creature monotheism*.
- God was uniquely distinguished from all created reality as Creator of all things and sole rightful recipient of divine worship (*latreia*).

### 3B. The Critical Historical Question
*How did Paul include Jesus of Nazareth in his theological framework without abandoning Jewish monotheism?*
- Paul did NOT abandon monotheism to adopt Pagan polytheism or hero-apotheosis.
- Paul included Jesus *within the unique identity of the one God* (Christological inclusion / binitarian devotional pattern).
- Jesus is portrayed not as a second separate god, nor as a mere exalted human creature, but as the divine Agent through whom the one God created all things and executes eschatological salvation.

## 4. FORENSIC AUDIT OF CORE PAULINE TEXTS

### 4A. 1 Corinthians 8:6 — The Shema Reframed
- **Greek Text:**
  `ἀλλ᾽ ἡμῖν εἷς θεὸς ὁ πατήρ, ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἡμεῖς εἰς αὐτόν, καὶ εἷς κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστός, δι᾽ οὗ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἡμεῖς δι᾽ αὐτοῦ.`
- **Syntactical Analysis:**
  - Paul takes the Greek text of the *Shema* (LXX Deut 6:4: `κύριος ὁ θεὸς ἡμῶν κύριος εἷς ἐστιν` — "Lord our God, Lord is one") and splits the divine title `θεός` (God) and `κύριος` (Lord):
    - `εἷς θεὸς ὁ πατήρ` ("one God, the Father") — source (`ἐξ οὗ`) and goal (`εἰς αὐτόν`) of creation.
    - `εἷς κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστός` ("one Lord, Jesus Christ") — divine instrument (`δι᾽ οὗ`) of creation and redemption.
- **Forensic Findings:**
  - *Reframing Monotheism:* Paul places Jesus inside the *Shema* itself, assigning him the cosmic role of creation agent (`δι᾽ οὗ τὰ πάντα`).
  - *Testing "1 Cor 8:6 teaches Nicene Trinity":* **UNSAFE.** Paul does not state technical 4th-century formulations (*homoousios*, *treis hypostaseis*).
  - *Testing "1 Cor 8:6 proves Jesus is merely human":* **UNSAFE.** Assigning pre-existent cosmic creation agency to Jesus excludes mere creaturely status.

### 4B. Philippians 2:6–11 — The Christ Poem / Hymn
- **Key Terms & Syntax:**
  - `ἐν μορφῇ θεοῦ ὑπάρχων` ("subsisting in the form of God"): Indicates divine status/pre-existence.
  - `οὐχ ἁρπαγμὸν ἡγήσατο τὸ εἶναι ἴσα θεῷ` ("did not consider equality with God something to be grasped/exploited").
  - `ἑαυτὸν ἐκένωσεν` ("emptied himself"): Taking the `μορφὴν δούλου` ("form of a servant").
  - `ἐταπείνωσεν ἑαυτὸν γενόμενος ὑπήκοος μέχρι θανάτου, θανάτου δὲ σταυροῦ` ("humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross").
  - `διὸ καὶ ὁ θεὸς αὐτὸν ὑπερύψωσεν... ἵνα ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ πᾶν γόνυ κάμψῃ... καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσηται ὅτι ΚΥΡΙΟΣ ΙΗΣΟΥΣ ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ` ("therefore God highly exalted him... that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow... and every tongue confess that JESUS CHRIST IS LORD").
- **Isaiah 45:23 Parallel:**
  - Isaiah 45:23 (LXX): YHWH swears, "To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall confess."
  - Paul applies YHWH's exclusive eschatological homage directly to the exalted Jesus.
- **Precision Terminology Guardrails:**
  - *Status of Tradition:* Philippians 2:6–11 is analyzed by critical scholars as poetic or hymnic material, and many argue for a pre-Pauline origin; however, its precise pre-Pauline compositional history remains a matter of scholarly analysis rather than an absolute demonstrable fact.
  - *Title Kyrios:* `κύριος` here represents the divine name above every name granted upon exaltation, bringing glory to God the Father.

### 4C. Romans 9:5 — Textual & Punctuation Audit
- **Greek Text:** `ὧν οἱ πατέρες, καὶ ἐξ ὧν ὁ Χριστὸς τὸ κατὰ σάρκα, ὁ ὢν ἐπὶ πάντων θεὸς εὐλογητὸς εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας, ἀμήν.`
- **Punctuation Options in Critical Scholarship:**
  1. *Option A (Christological Apposition):* "...from whom is the Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed forever, Amen." (Applies `θεός` directly to Christ).
  2. *Option B (Doxology to Father):* "...from whom is the Christ according to the flesh. May God who is over all be blessed forever, Amen." (Independent doxology to God the Father).
- **Classification:** **DISPUTED / SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION.**
  - Syntax strongly favors Option A in Greek grammar, but punctuation was absent in earliest uncial manuscripts. Therefore, claiming Romans 9:5 *unquestionably* calls Jesus God is **SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION** (noting syntax preference and punctuation ambiguity).

### 4D. Titus 2:13 — Grammatical Audit & Corpus Control
- **Greek Text:** `τοῦ μεγάλου θεοῦ καὶ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ` ("of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ").
- **Granville Sharp Rule:** Single article (`τοῦ`) governing two nouns (`θεοῦ` and `σωτῆρος`) connected by `καί` indicates both titles refer to the same person (Jesus Christ).
- **Corpus Limitation:** Pastoral Epistles (Titus) are disputed/deutero-Pauline and absent in P46 (`SRC-MS-005`).
- **Classification:** **NOT ESTABLISHED / LIMITED BY STORED EVIDENCE** as an undisputed 1st-century letter of Paul, though **SAFE** as evidence for late 1st/early 2nd-century Pauline tradition.

### 4E. Colossians 1:15–20 — Cosmic Christology
- **Key Terms:** `εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ἀοράτου` ("image of the invisible God"), `πρωτότοκος πάσης κτίσεως` ("firstborn of all creation"), "in him all things were created in heaven and on earth... all things were created through him (`δι᾽ αὐτοῦ`) and for him (`εἰς αὐτόν`)."
- **Corpus & Manuscript Control:** Present in P46 (`SRC-MS-005`). Authorship debated (disputed/deutero-Pauline).
- **Classification:** Provides high cosmic Wisdom Christology within the Pauline school; distinguishes creation agency from creaturely status (`prototokos` indicates priority and supremacy, not creation as first temporal creature).

## 6. EARLY DEVOTION TO JESUS (DEVOTIONAL BINITARIANISM)

### 6A. Devotional Practices Attested in Undisputed Paul
1. **Prayer & Invocation:** Calling upon the name of the Lord Jesus Christ (`ἐπικαλουμένοις τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ`, 1 Cor 1:2).
2. **Aramaic Liturgical Formula:** *Maranatha* (`Μαράνα θά` — "Our Lord, come!", 1 Cor 16:22), demonstrating that calling Jesus *Lord* (*Mara*) in prayer originated in the earliest Aramaic-speaking Judean movement.
3. **Baptism in the Name of Jesus:** Initiation into the community performed in Jesus's name (1 Cor 6:11, Gal 3:27).
4. **The Lord's Supper (Eucharist):** Sacred meal centered on the remembered and present Lord (1 Cor 11:23–26).
5. **Doxologies & Benedictions:** Grace requested jointly from "God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ" (e.g., 2 Cor 13:14).

### 6B. Devotion vs. Metaphysical Ontology
- **Historical Finding:** First-century Jewish Christians offered cultic devotion to Jesus that Second Temple Jews previously reserved for YHWH alone.
- **Methodological Rule:** Cultic worship and binitarian devotion are Layer B historical facts. They do NOT require or prove 4th-century Nicene Greek metaphysical vocabulary (*homoousios*), but they provide the devotional practice that later Nicene theology sought to defend.

## 8. PAUL VS. JERUSALEM LEADERSHIP (PETER, JAMES, JOHN)

### 8A. Historical Audit of Relationship
- **Testing "Paul Invented Christianity Independently":** **UNSAFE.**
  - In Galatians 1:18–19, Paul records visiting Jerusalem three years after his call to confer with Cephas (Peter) and staying 15 days, seeing also James the Lord's brother.
  - In Galatians 2:1–10, Paul attends the Jerusalem consultation, presenting his gospel to the "pillars" (James, Cephas, John), who extend the "right hand of fellowship" (`δεξιὰς κοινωνίας`).
  - Paul explicitly notes that he transmitted received tradition (`parelabon`) shared with the Jerusalem movement (1 Cor 15:3–11: "Whether then it was I or they, so we preach and so you believed").
- **Testing "Complete Harmony Without Conflict":** **UNSAFE.**
  - Galatians 2:11–14 records the sharp Incident at Antioch, where Paul publicly confronted Peter/Cephas over food fellowship withdrawal with Gentiles under pressure from "certain men from James."

## 9. EVALUATION OF CORE HISTORICAL/THEOLOGICAL CLAIMS

### 9A. "Did Paul Invent Christianity?" → **UNSAFE**
- **Finding:** Paul did not invent Christianity ex nihilo. He inherited received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5), recognized the authority of Jerusalem pillars (James, Peter, John), and built upon the pre-existing Jesus movement.
- **Qualification:** Paul was the primary theological articulators and missionary catalysts for Gentile Christianity, developing distinctive soteriological and ecclesiological frameworks.

### 9B. "Did Paul Change Jesus's Religion?" → **DISPUTED / UNSAFE**
- **Finding:** Jesus operated within 1st-century Jewish restorationism; Paul proclaimed the crucified and risen Jesus to the Greco-Roman world.
- **Qualification:** While the social boundary markers changed (Torah observance for Gentiles rejected), Paul understood his mission as the fulfillment of Israel's prophetic destiny to bring light to the Gentiles.

### 9C. "Did Paul Teach the Trinity?" → **UNSAFE**
- **Finding:** Paul contains triadic patterns (e.g., 2 Cor 13:14: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit"), but does NOT explicitly teach 4th-century Nicene/Constantinopolitan Trinitarian metaphysics (*one ousia, three hypostaseis*).

### 9D. "Did Paul Believe Jesus Was Divine?" → **SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION**
- **Finding:** Paul included Jesus in the unique divine identity (1 Cor 8:6), assigned him cosmic creation agency, applied YHWH Old Testament texts to him (Rom 10:13 / Joel 2:32), and directed prayer and worship to him.
- **Qualification:** Paul's Christology is expressed in functional, relational, and devotional categories within Jewish monotheism, not in 4th-century Greek ontological terms.

## 13. STEELMAN ARGUMENTS

### SIDE A — PAULINE CONTINUITY MODEL
**Strongest Historical & Theological Case:**
- Paul was a deeply committed Jewish monotheist who inherited early traditions from the Judean Jesus movement (1 Cor 15:3–5).
- Rather than abandoning monotheism, Paul included Jesus inside the unique identity of the one God of Israel (1 Cor 8:6 reframing the *Shema*).
- Early Christian communities offered cultic devotion, prayer, and baptism to Jesus as Lord (*Kyrios*), practicing binitarian devotion long before Nicaea.
- Fourth-century ecumenical councils did not invent Christ's divine status, but supplied technical Greek terms (*homoousios*) to protect the early devotional reality against Arian reductionism.

### SIDE B — PAULINE DEVELOPMENTAL / CRITICAL MODEL
**Strongest Historical & Critical Case:**
- Paul significantly transformed the Jesus movement from an apocalyptic Judean Kingdom movement into a Greco-Roman Gentile religion.
- Paul shifted the focus from Jesus's message (the *Kingdom of God*) to the person and cosmic death/resurrection of Christ.
- Paul's Christological language was functional, relational, and apocalyptic, utilizing Jewish Wisdom and Lordship categories rather than 4th-century Greek metaphysics.
- Projecting 4th-century Nicene dogma (*homoousios*, *three persons in one substance*) backward into Paul's 1st-century letters commits anachronism and obscures the genuine historical development of Christian doctrine.

## 16. FINAL SYNTHESIS & CORE QUESTIONS ANSWERED

1. **Did Paul invent Christianity?**
   - **No.** Paul inherited received traditions (1 Cor 15:3–5), recognized Jerusalem leadership (James, Peter, John), and operated within the pre-existing Jesus movement.

2. **Did Paul invent high Christology?**
   - **No.** High devotional claims (invoking Jesus in prayer, *Maranatha*, 1 Cor 8:6 *Shema* reframing, Phil 2 hymn) were part of early traditions shared with or preceding Paul.

3. **Did Paul teach the Nicene Trinity?**
   - **No.** Paul contains triadic patterns (Father, Son, Spirit), but does not use 4th-century technical metaphysical terms (*homoousios*, *three hypostaseis in one ousia*).

4. **Did Paul believe Jesus was divine?**
   - **Yes.** Paul included Jesus inside the unique divine identity of the one God of Israel, attributing to him cosmic creation agency, divine Lordship, and cultic worship.

5. **Did Paul remain within Jewish monotheism?**
   - **Yes.** Paul practiced a reframed *binitarian devotional monotheism*, maintaining that there is only one God (the Father) and one Lord (Jesus Christ).

6. **Did Paul develop Jesus traditions?**
   - **Yes.** Paul developed the theological interpretation of Jesus's death and resurrection, articulated justification by faith apart from Torah works for Gentiles, and expanded the cosmic scope of Christ's Lordship.

7. **How much continuity exists with Jerusalem Christianity?**
   - **Substantial.** Shared core proclamation of Christ's messiahship, death for sins, resurrection, received traditions, and mutual recognition, despite operational tension over Gentile Torah practice.

8. **What is genuinely developed in Paul's theology?**
   - The explicit soteriological formulation of justification apart from Torah works, the ecclesiological unity of Jew and Gentile in one body, and the full cosmic scope of Christ's death and resurrection.

9. **What remains uncertain?**
   - Exact pre-Pauline composition dates of specific liturgical fragments, exact original punctuation of uncial manuscripts in passages like Romans 9:5, and granular details of disputed/deutero-Pauline epistolary context.

10. **What cannot be projected backward from Nicaea?**
    - Fourth-century Greek ontological vocabulary (*homoousios*, *hypostasis*, *physis*), imperial conciliar enforcement, and fully articulated post-Arian Trinitarian definitions cannot be retrojected into 1st-century Pauline epistles.
