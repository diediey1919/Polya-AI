## 0. AUDIT SCOPE AND METHOD

This audit reconstructs the First Council of Ephesus (431 CE) as a historical event with verifiable participants, procedures, documents, decisions, disputes, irregularities, and reception history. It does NOT reduce the council to "Cyril defeated Nestorius" or "Nestorius was proven correct." It does NOT determine theological truth from conciliar outcome.

**Method:** Every claim is traced to a verifiable source (ACO, McGuckin 1994, Wessel 2004, Graumann 2020/2021, Price & Graumann 2020, Catholic Encyclopedia, Nestorius's Bazaar). Where sources conflict, the conflict is recorded. Where scholarly consensus exists, it is noted. Where evidence is insufficient, the gap is stated.

### 1.2 Distinct Source Required?

**Verdict: YES.** The Acts of Ephesus 431 constitute a conciliar document set — proceedings, decrees, canons, imperial correspondence, rival assembly records — that is genuinely distinct from any single participant's theological corpus. Created as **SRC-THEO-009**.

Neither contains the conciliar procedural record: the session-by-session acts, the summonses, the readings, the votes, the depositions, the counter-council records, the imperial rescripts, or the canons. The council as an institutional event requires its own source record.

### 2.1 Document Classification

| Document Type | Specific Documents | Status at Council |
|---------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **Conciliar Acts** | Sessions I–VII (Greek, Latin, Coptic versions) | Primary record — multiple versions with propagandistic intent (Graumann 2020) |
| **Imperial Sacra** | Theodosius II convocation letter (19 Nov 430); rescripts during council | Read aloud at Session I; used as legal authorization |
| **Letters** | Cyril's Second Letter (*Intelligo quosdam*); Cyril's Third Letter (*Cum Salvator noster*) with 12 Anathemas; Celestine's letters; Capreolus of Carthage's letter | Read aloud and voted on at Session I–II |
| **Patristic Florilegium** | Citations from Athanasius, Basil, Gregory Nazianzen, Gregory of Nyssa, Ambrose, Cyprian, Atticus, Amphilochius, Julius of Rome, Felix of Rome, Peter of Alexandria, Theophilus of Alexandria | Read at Session I as standard of orthodoxy |
| **Nestorius's Writings** | Second letter to Cyril; passages from sermons and writings | Read aloud and condemned at Session I |
| **Canons** | Six canons (Session VII) | Promulgated as conciliar legislation |
| **Decrees** | Deposition of Nestorius (Session I); Nicene exclusivity (Session VI) | Formal conciliar acts |
| **Rival Assembly Records** | John of Antioch's counter-council decrees (June 26 – late July 431) | NOT part of Cyril's Acts; preserved in Theodoret's Letter CLVII and ACO Latin collection |

### 2.2 Critical Distinction: Acts vs. Minutes

The surviving Acts are NOT neutral stenographic minutes. Graumann (2020, 2021) demonstrates they are *publizistische Sammlungen* — editorial collections designed to present each side's case to the emperor. Both Cyril and John produced documentation. The Acts were "exercises in textual practices" from commencement — works-in-progress shaped by political necessity.

**Implication:** The Acts preserve real events and real documents, but the SELECTIVITY, ARRANGEMENT, and FRAMING serve rhetorical purposes. This does not invalidate the record but requires source-critical reading.

## 3. HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### 3.1 Chronological Trajectory

```
325    Nicaea — homoousios; Nicene Creed established as standard
        ↓
381    Constantinople — Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed; pneumatological expansion
        ↓
428    Nestorius consecrated Bishop of Constantinople by Theodosius II
        ↓
429    Cyril's 17th Paschal Letter addresses Nestorian teaching
       Cyril writes Letter to the Monks condemning Nestorius's sermons
        ↓
430    Aug 11: Roman synod under Pope Celestine I condemns Nestorius
       Celestine issues ten-day ultimatum for Nestorius to recant
       Nov 19: Theodosius II issues imperial convocation for council at Ephesus
       Nov/Dec: Four bishops serve Cyril's ultimatum on Nestorius
        ↓
431    April: Celestine commissions papal legates
       June 7: Scheduled opening date (Pentecost) — key parties absent
       June 22: Cyril opens council without John of Antioch
       June 26–27: John of Antioch arrives; counter-council convened
       July 10–11: Papal legates arrive; confirm Nestorius's deposition
       July 16–31: Sessions IV–VII; John + 34 bishops excommunicated
       August: St. Dalmatius leads monastic procession in Constantinople
       September: Theodosius dissolves council; Nestorius retires to monastery
       Oct 25: Maximianus ordained patriarch of Constantinople
       Oct 30: Cyril returns to Alexandria
        ↓
432    July 31: Pope Celestine dies; succeeded by Sixtus III
       Intensive shuttle diplomacy between Cyril and John
        ↓
433    April 12 (or 23): Formula of Reunion — Cyril and John reconciled
       Twelve Anathemas not required as condition of reconciliation
        ↓
435    August 3: Imperial edict exiles Nestorius to Great Oasis, Egypt
        ↓
449    Robber Council of Ephesus — Eutyches controversy; Leo's Tome rejected
        ↓
451    Chalcedon — reads Cyril's Second Letter; omits Third Letter/12 Anathemas
       Extends "from two natures" (ἐκ) to "in two natures" (ἐν)
       Upholds Nestorius's condemnation; reads Leo's Tome
        ↓
553    Constantinople II — restores Third Letter and 12 Anathemas
       Anathematizes anti-Cyrilline writings of Theodoret and Ibas
```

### 3.2 Non-Linear Caveat

This trajectory is NOT a smooth doctrinal evolution. Key ruptures:

* The Twelve Anathemas (430–431) were present at Ephesus 431 and likely read, but whether they received formal conciliar approval as an independent decree is historically disputed. The Formula of Reunion (433) did not require John of Antioch to accept them as a condition of reconciliation. Chalcedon (451) read Cyril's Second Letter but did not read the Third Letter with the Twelve Anathemas as an independent document. Constantinople II (553) provided explicit conciliar reaffirmation of the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas in the context of the Three Chapters controversy. The reception history shows a spectrum from textual presence to explicit conciliar endorsement, not a simple binary of absence/presence.
* Chalcedon adopted "two natures" language that Cyril had previously resisted and that Nestorius had championed.
* Theodoret of Cyrus and Ibas of Edessa, who had written against Cyril's Twelve Anathemas, were readmitted at Chalcedon without explicit retraction of their anti-Anathema writings.
* The "Miaphysite" controversy (one nature vs. two natures) persisted and produced the Oriental Orthodox schism that continues today.
* Ephesus 431 was not universally accepted as ecumenical until papal ratification (Sixtus III, 432) and the Formula of Reunion (433).

### 5.3 Session I — 22 June 431 (Monday)

**Venue:** Cathedral of the Theotokos (Church of Mary), Ephesus
**President:** Cyril of Alexandria ("filling the place of Celestine, Archbishop of the Roman Church")
**Attendance:** ~160 (morning) → ~198 (evening, after signatures)
**Notary:** Peter of Alexandria

**Proceedings in order:**

1. **Justification of Opening:** Cyril justifies decision to delay no longer, citing John of Antioch's prolonged absence.

2. **Imperial Sacra Read:** Juvenal of Jerusalem requests reading of the imperial convocation letter. Theodosius's words: "no excuse will be accepted for those who fail to present themselves at the appointed time and place — the time being June 7." (McGuckin; ACO)

3. **Three Summons to Nestorius:**
   * **First Summons** (sent 21 June): Nestorius replied "he would come if he chose." (Catholic Encyclopedia)
   * **Second Summons** (dispatched morning of 22 June): Nestorius sent message from his house (surrounded by Candidianus's troops) that "he would appear when all the bishops had come together." (McGuckin; Catholic Encyclopedia)
   * **Third Summons** (final canonical summons): Nestorius gave no answer. Bishops sent to his lodgings were left waiting outside in scorching midday heat and eventually departed. (McGuckin; Catholic Encyclopedia)
   * **Legal Effect:** By canon law, three refusals permitted proceedings in absentia. (McGuckin)

4. **Nicene Creed Read Aloud** (standard of orthodoxy established)

5. **Cyril's Second Letter to Nestorius (*Intelligo quosdam*) Read:**
   * Cyril asks council to judge whether his letter conforms to Nicene faith
   * **124–126 bishops** give individual oral attestations of conformity (speaking in turn); Peter the notary records that remaining delegates also assented
   * Verdict: **APPROVED** as in accordance with Nicene faith

6. **Nestorius's Second Letter to Cyril Read:**
   * Cyril asks whether this letter conforms to Nicaea
   * **34 direct interventions** speak against Nestorius's reply; general acclamation cements unanimous verdict
   * Verdict: **CONDEMNED** as contrary to Nicene faith; bishops cry "Anathema to Nestorius"

7. **Pope Celestine's Letter to Cyril Read** (the letter commissioning Cyril to execute the Roman sentence)

8. **Cyril's Third Letter to Nestorius (*Cum Salvator noster*) Read:**
   * Contains the **Twelve Anathemas**
   * Bishops who served the ultimatum testify they delivered the letter; Nestorius promised answer "on the morrow" but never responded
   * **CRITICAL DISTINCTION (Graumann 2020):** The Third Letter appears in the Acts as having been presented/read, but unlike the Second Letter (which received 124–126 individual oral attestations of conformity to the Nicene faith), the Third Letter with the Twelve Anathemas received NO comparable formal conciliar vote of approval. Five categories must be distinguished:
     - **(A) Textual presence:** The Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas appears in the Acts — PRESENT.
     - **(B) Reading of the document:** Likely read or presented during Session I proceedings — LIKELY (based on its placement in the Acts and testimony of bishops who delivered it).
     - **(C) Theological endorsement:** The council's theological orientation was clearly Cyrilline; bishops who supported Cyril's Second Letter would naturally endorse the theological substance of the Third — IMPLICIT but not formally recorded.
     - **(D) Formal conciliar approval:** No formal conciliar pronouncement of orthodoxy comparable to the Second Letter's individual attestations — NOT RECORDED (Graumann 2020; Price & Graumann 2020).
     - **(E) Later explicit conciliar reception:** Constantinople II (553) explicitly reaffirmed the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas — YES, but in the specific context of the Three Chapters controversy.
   * This distinction became critical at Chalcedon 451, where the Third Letter was not read as an independent document.

9. **Testimony of Conversations with Nestorius:**
   * **Theodotus of Ancyra** and **Acacius of Melitene** (former associates) give testimony
   * Acacius reports Nestorius declared: "We must not say that God is two or three months old" (*dimeniaion e trimeniaion me dein legesthai Theon*)
   * Nestorius's own account in his "Apology" (Bethune-Baker, p. 71) confirms the phrase but with different theological intent
   * Acacius further accuses Nestorius of teaching that "the Son who died is to be distinguished from the Word of God"

10. **Patristic Florilegium Read:**
    * Citations from: Peter I of Alexandria, Athanasius, Julius of Rome, Felix of Rome (later shown to be Apollinarian forgeries — Cyril unaware), Theophilus of Alexandria, Cyprian of Carthage, Ambrose of Milan, Gregory Nazianzen, Basil the Great, Gregory of Nyssa, Atticus of Constantinople, Amphilochius of Iconium

11. **Passages from Nestorius's Writings and Sermons Read** (contrasted with patristic texts)

12. **Letter from Capreolus, Bishop of Carthage Read:**
    * Written amid Vandal devastation of Africa; could not hold synod or send bishops
    * Bishops acclaim his words against novelty and in praise of ancient faith

13. **DECREE OF DEPOSITION Pronounced** (text in Section 7)

14. **Signatures:** ~197–198 bishops sign immediately

15. **Notification to Nestorius:** Brief notification sent to "the new Judas"; Coptic Acts report it was affixed to his door

16. **Conclusion:** Evening. People of Ephesus escort bishops with torches and incense. Candidianus has deposition notices torn down.

17. **Immediate Letters Dispatched:** To Theodosius II; to clergy and people of Constantinople; to Egyptian bishops in Constantinople; to Abbot Dalmatius

### 5.4 John of Antioch's Arrival — 26–27 June 431

| Detail | Data |
|--------|------|
| Arrival date | 26 or 27 June 431 (five days after Session I) |
| Party size | ~68 bishops of the Antiochene delegation |
| Cyril's deputation | Sent to meet John honorably; John was surrounded by soldiers and complained bishops were "creating a disturbance" |

**John's Counter-Council ("Conciliabulum"):**
* John holds assembly he designates "the holy synod"
* **Count Candidianus's Deposition:** Claims he disapproved of assembly before John's arrival; claims he attended Session I and read emperor's letter — but "of this not a word in the Acts, so Candidianus was apparently lying" (Catholic Encyclopedia)
* **Charges Against Cyril and Memnon:** Apollinarianism, Eunomianism, Arianism (based on Twelve Anathemas); violence; shutting churches; mobilizing mob
* **Deposition:** Cyril and Memnon deposed by **43 bishops** present (Catholic Encyclopedia)
* **Conditional Forgiveness:** Members of Cyril's council to be forgiven *provided* they condemn Cyril's Twelve Anathemas
* **Notable:** Not a word said in favor of Nestorius at this assembly (Catholic Encyclopedia)

### 5.5 Session II — 10 July 431

**Venue:** Episcopal residence
**President:** Cyril of Alexandria
**Key Event:** Arrival of Papal Legates

1. **Papal Legates Arrive:** Bishops Arcadius and Projectus, priest Philip (delayed by storms)
2. **Legate Philip Opens:** Celestine's former letter (already read in Session I) had decided the question; Pope has sent another letter
3. **Celestine's Second Letter Read:** General exhortation; legates have instructions to carry out what Pope formerly decided
4. **Acclamations:** "This is a just judgment. To Celestine the new Paul! To the new Paul Cyril! To Celestine, the guardian of the Faith! To Celestine agreeing to the Synod! The Synod gives thanks to Cyril. One Celestine, one Cyril!"
5. **Firmus, Exarch of Caesarea:** Pope had long since given his sentence; synod had executed the papal sentence
6. **Legate Arcadius:** Expresses regret for late arrival; asks to see decrees

### 5.6 Session III — 11 July 431

**Venue:** Episcopal residence
**President:** Cyril of Alexandria

1. **Legates Review Acts:** Read Acts of Session I overnight; demand condemnation of Nestorius formally read in their presence
2. **Condemnation Read Aloud** in presence of legates
3. **Three Legates Severally Pronounce Confirmation** in Pope's name
4. **Priest Philip's Celebrated Exordium:**
   > "It is doubtful to none, nay it has been known to all ages, that holy and blessed Peter, the prince and head of the Apostles, the column of the Faith, the foundation of the Catholic Church, received from our Lord Jesus Christ, the Saviour and Redeemer of the human race, the keys of the Kingdom, and that to him was given the power of binding and loosing sins, who until this day and for ever lives and judges in his successors. His successor in order and his representative, our holy and most blessed Pope Celestine..."
5. **Subscription:** Legates sign Acts of all three sessions
6. **Letter to Theodosius Dispatched:** "The whole world has therefore agreed"; requests bishops be allowed to go home; new bishop must be appointed for Constantinople

### 5.7 Session IV — 16 July 431

**Venue:** Cathedral of the Theotokos
**President:** Cyril of Alexandria

1. **Written Protest Presented:** Cyril and Memnon present formal written protest against John of Antioch's counter-council
2. **Summons to John of Antioch:** John cited to appear; he "would not even admit the envoys"

### 5.8 Session V — 17 July 431

**Venue:** Cathedral of the Theotokos
**President:** Cyril of Alexandria

1. **John's Placard:** John had set up placard in city accusing Cyril's synod of Apollinarian heresy
2. **Third Canonical Summons to John:** Final summons; John pays no attention
3. **Sentence Against John of Antioch:**
   * Council **suspends and excommunicates** John together with **34 bishops** of his party
   * **Refrains from deposing them** (suspension/excommunication only, not full deposition)
   * Some of John's party had already deserted him
4. **Letters Dispatched:** To Theodosius II; to Pope Celestine (full account); synod describes itself as consisting of **210 bishops**

### 5.9 Session VI — 22 July 431

**President:** Cyril of Alexandria

1. **Case of Two Nestorianizing Priests** addressed
2. **DECREE ON THE FAITH:**
   > No one must produce or compose any other creed than the Nicene Creed; anyone who should propose any such to pagans, Jews, or heretics who wished to be converted should be **deposed** if a bishop or cleric, or **anathematized** if a layman.
   * **Later Significance:** This decree became source of objections to later synodal definitions and to the *filioque* addition to the Constantinopolitan Creed.
3. **Definition Against the Messalians** (heretical sect condemned)

### 5.10 Session VII — 31 July 431

**President:** Cyril of Alexandria

1. **Autonomy of Church of Cyprus:** Bishops of Cyprus persuade council to approve their claim of ancient exemption from jurisdiction of Antioch
2. **Six Canons Promulgated:**
   * Canon I: Against the counter-council of John of Antioch
   * Canon IV: On Pelagianism
   * Canon VII: On the words *πίστιν ἑτέραν* ("another faith") — reinforcing Session VI's Nicene exclusivity
   * Additional canons against adherents and supporters of Nestorius

### 6.1 Two Competing Assemblies

| Dimension | Cyril's Council | John of Antioch's Council |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Designation** | "The holy and ecumenical council" | "The holy synod" |

| **Imperial Response** | Initially annulled by Theodosius; eventually accepted | Initially annulled by Theodosius; eventually abandoned |
| **Papal Response** | Confirmed by legates (July 10–11); ratified by Sixtus III (432) | Rejected by Rome |

### 6.2 Mutual Depositions

| Action | By Whom | Against Whom | Date |
|--------|---------|-------------|------|
| Nestorius deposed | Cyril's council | Nestorius | 22 June 431 |
| Cyril and Memnon deposed | John's counter-council | Cyril and Memnon | ~26 June 431 |
| John + 34 bishops excommunicated | Cyril's council | John of Antioch and party | 17 July 431 |

### 6.3 Imperial Response to Rival Councils

Theodosius II's initial response was to treat BOTH assemblies as problematic:
* **Rescript of ~29 June:** Annulled Cyril's decisions of 22 June
* **August 431:** Deposed ALL THREE — Nestorius, Cyril, and Memnon
* Ordered all three held under arrest
* Dissolved the council and ordered bishops to return home
* **Did not formally approve either assembly**

**Eventual resolution:**
* Nestorius accepted resignation; retired to monastery at Antioch
* Maximianus installed as successor (25 October 431)
* Cyril and Memnon eventually restored to their sees (under pressure from Pulcheria, monastic supporters, public opinion, and Cyril's Letter 96 payments)
* Sixtus III ratified Cyril's council as the legitimate ecumenical council (432)

## 7. NESTORIUS'S DEPOSITION

### 7.1 Formal Accusation

The council formally accused Nestorius of:
1. **Impious doctrines** — blasphemy against Christ
2. **Teaching contrary to the Nicene faith** — specifically, Nestorius's Second Letter to Cyril was judged as contradicting the Nicene Creed
3. **Refusal to obey canonical summons** (contumacy) — three summonses refused
4. **Contempt for papal authority** — Celestine's letter had already condemned him

### 7.2 Documents Used Against Nestorius

| Document | How Used |
|----------|----------|
| Nestorius's Second Letter to Cyril | Read aloud; 34 bishops speak against it; condemned as contrary to Nicaea |
| Passages from Nestorius's sermons and writings | Read aloud and contrasted with patristic florilegium |
| Acacius of Melitene's testimony | "God is not two or three months old" — used to demonstrate Nestorius denied Theotokos |
| Acacius's further accusation | Nestorius taught "the Son who died is to be distinguished from the Word of God" |
| Celestine's letter to Cyril | Read as evidence that Rome had already condemned Nestorius |

### 7.3 Physical Presence

**Nestorius was NOT physically present at any session.** He remained in his residence throughout, surrounded by Candidianus's troops. He refused all three canonical summonses.

### 7.4 Decree of Deposition — Full Text

> "Since in addition to the rest the most impious Nestorius has neither been willing to obey our citation, nor to receive the most holy and god-fearing bishops whom we sent to him, we have necessarily betaken ourselves to the examination of his impieties; and, having apprehended from his letters and from his writings, and from his recent sayings in this metropolis which have been reported, that his opinions and teachings are impious, we being necessarily impelled thereto both by the canons [for his contumacy] and by the letter [to Cyril] of our most holy father and colleague Celestine, Bishop of the Roman Church, with many tears have arrived at the following grievous sentence against him: Our Lord, Jesus Christ, Who has been blasphemed by him, has defined by this holy synod that the same Nestorius is excluded from all episcopal dignity and from every assembly of bishops."

**Key Greek terms:** ἀναγκαίως (*anankaiōs*, "necessarily") and κατεπειχθέντες (*katepeichthentes*, "hard pressed/urged") — the council understood itself as *compelled* by both canonical procedure (contumacy) and papal authority.

**Dual grounds:**
1. **Canonical:** Contumacy (three summonses refused — standard procedure for judgment in absentia)
2. **Papal:** Celestine's letter commissioning Cyril to execute the Roman sentence

### 7.5 Nestorius's Response

Nestorius's own account (Bazaar of Heracleides, Book 2):
> "Who was judge? Cyril. And who was the accuser? Cyril. Who was bishop of Rome? Cyril. Cyril was everything."

Nestorius claimed:
* Cyril was both accuser and judge — a conflict of interest
* The council opened before the Antiochene delegation arrived — procedural violation
* Ephesus was dominated by Cyril's partisans and the pro-Theotokos populace — hostile environment
* He feared violence if he appeared

**Assessment:** Nestorius's procedural objections have historical merit — the opening WAS premature, the imperial commissioner DID protest, and 68 bishops DID formally object. However, his theological defense (that Cyril's interpretation was a misreading) is a separate question from the procedural record.

## 8. PROCEDURAL IRREGULARITIES

### 8.1 Premature Opening

**Issue:** Cyril opened the council on June 22, sixteen days after the imperial opening date of June 7, but BEFORE the Antiochene delegation (the second-largest patriarchate) had arrived.

**Evidence for irregularity:**
* Theodosius's imperial sacra called for "open-ended discussion" and "amicable consensus" — not predetermined verdict (Schwartz ACO I.1.1)
* Count Candidianus, the imperial commissioner, explicitly protested the opening as illegal
* 68 bishops signed a formal protest on June 21 against opening before John's arrival
* John of Antioch's metropolitans had stated John said "do not defer on my account" — but Cyril's interpretation of this as authorization to proceed is DISPUTED
* The imperial sacra's deadline ("no excuse will be accepted") was a deadline for ARRIVAL, not authorization to proceed without key parties

**Evidence against irregularity (Cyril's defense):**
* The opening was already 15 days past the imperial date — heat, illness, and deaths among bishops were mounting
* John's metropolitans had conveyed that John did not wish further delay
* Celestine's papal mandate (11 Aug 430) gave Cyril authority to execute the Roman sentence
* Nestorius himself had requested a council, expecting vindication — he could not claim surprise

**Scholarly assessment:** McGuckin (1994) acknowledges Cyril acted with "imprudence" but argues he had papal mandate. Wessel (2004) emphasizes Cyril's strategic documentation. Graumann (2020) notes both sides needed to present their case to the emperor and the Acts were shaped by this political necessity.

### 8.2 Accuser-as-Judge Problem

**Issue:** Cyril was both Nestorius's chief theological opponent AND the president of the council that judged Nestorius.

**Evidence:**
* Nestorius explicitly raised this: "Who was judge? Cyril. And who was the accuser? Cyril." (Bazaar, Book 2)
* Cyril's Second and Third Letters were read as the STANDARD of orthodoxy against which Nestorius's writings were measured — Cyril judged by his own criteria
* The council voted on Cyril's own theological output as conforming to Nicaea

**Counter-evidence:**
* The council included 197+ bishops — not merely Cyril's personal decision
* Papal legates confirmed the judgment retroactively
* Celestine had already condemned Nestorius at the Roman synod (Aug 430) — Cyril was executing a papal sentence, not imposing his own

### 8.3 Cyril's Bribery of the Imperial Court

**Issue:** Cyril distributed substantial wealth to imperial courtiers to influence the emperor's response.

**Primary evidence:** Cyril's Letter 96 (preserved in Collectio Atheniensis, ACO) — NOT from Nestorius's Bazaar.

**Content of Letter 96:**
* Written by Epiphanius (Cyril's archdeacon) to Maximianus (Cyril's agent in Constantinople)
* Rebukes Maximianus for failing to contribute to Cyril's efforts
* **Attached list:** 17 recipients by name/title, including:
  * **1,080 pounds of gold** total
  * Valuable textiles, furniture, ivory chairs, ostriches
  * Recipients: chamberlains (Chryseros, Scholasticius, Romanus), prefects (Paul, Heleniana), quaestor, domestics
  * Explicit instructions: "so that he may help us," "to persuade him," "that he would cease to oppose us"

**Source assessment:**
* Letter 96 is AUTHENTIC Cyril correspondence — not a Nestorian fabrication
* Confirmed by Beers (peer-reviewed), CEU research project "Blessings, Bribes, and Bishops" (2019)
* McGuckin and Wessel acknowledge the payments but contextualize them as customary "eulogiae" (blessings) — standard late Roman patronage
* Critics call it the "largest bribe in antiquity" — direct evidence of corruption
* The Bazaar of Heracleides corroborates Cyril's corruption but is a hostile source; the PRIMARY evidence is Cyril's own correspondence

**Verdict:** The payments are VERIFIED from Cyril's own correspondence. Whether they constitute "bribery" or "customary patronage" is interpretive, but the strategic intent to influence the imperial court is explicit in the letter's own language ("to persuade him," "that he would cease to oppose us").

### 8.4 Candidianus's Role and Replacement

**Issue:** The imperial commissioner Candidianus was appointed to maintain order and remain "absolutely impartial" — but he abandoned neutrality.

**Evidence:**
* Candidianus initially protested the premature opening
* He declared the assembly illegal when Cyril's faction proceeded
* He tore down the deposition notice and sent it to the emperor
* He forbade city criers from announcing Nestorius's deposition
* He collected the anti-Cyrillian bishops to await John of Antioch
* He sided with John's counter-council, abandoning neutrality
* He falsely claimed in his deposition that he had attended Session I and read the emperor's letter — "of this not a word in the Acts" (Catholic Encyclopedia)

**Resolution:** Theodosius II judged Candidianus "hopelessly compromised" and replaced him with John, the Imperial High Treasurer.

### 8.5 Third Letter / Twelve Anathemas Status

**Issue:** The status of Cyril's Third Letter (with the Twelve Anathemas) at the council is historically disputed and requires careful distinction between multiple categories of reception.

**Evidence (Graumann 2020; Price & Graumann 2020):**
* The Third Letter APPEARS in the Acts as having been presented/read at Session I
* However, no formal conciliar pronouncement of its orthodoxy exists in Session I proceedings
* Unlike the Second Letter (which received 124–126 individual oral attestations of conformity), the Third Letter received NO comparable formal vote
* The Formula of Reunion (433) did not require John of Antioch to accept the Twelve Anathemas as a condition of reconciliation
* Chalcedon (451) read Cyril's Second Letter but did NOT read the Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas as an independent document
* Constantinople II (553) explicitly reaffirmed the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas in the context of the Three Chapters controversy

**Five-Category Distinction:**

(A) **Textual presence:** The Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas appears in the Acts — PRESENT.

(B) **Reading of the document:** Likely read or presented during Session I — LIKELY (based on placement in Acts and testimony of bishops who delivered it).

(C) **Theological endorsement:** The council's theological orientation was clearly Cyrilline; bishops who approved Cyril's Second Letter would naturally endorse the theological substance of the Third — IMPLICIT but not formally recorded.

(D) **Formal conciliar approval:** No formal conciliar pronouncement of orthodoxy comparable to the Second Letter's individual attestations — NOT RECORDED. Whether the Twelve Anathemas were formally approved as an independent conciliar decree at Ephesus 431 is historically disputed.

(E) **Later explicit conciliar reception:** Constantinople II (553) explicitly reaffirmed the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas — YES, but this must not be projected retroactively onto 431.

**Verdict:** The Third Letter's formal conciliar status at Ephesus 431 is WEAKER than traditionally claimed in some accounts, but STRONGER than a simple "not approved" reading suggests. The Twelve Anathemas were present, likely read, theologically aligned with the council's Cyrilline orientation, but lacked the explicit formal approval that the Second Letter received. The reception history shows a spectrum from textual presence to explicit conciliar endorsement, not a simple binary of approved/not approved.

## 9. POST-COUNCIL NEGOTIATIONS AND FORMULA OF REUNION (431–433)

### 9.1 Events 431–433

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Sept 431 | Nestorius requests permission to retire to monastery at Antioch; granted |
| Sept 431 | Council dissolved by Theodosius; bishops sent home |
| 25 Oct 431 | Maximianus ordained patriarch at Constantinople |
| 30 Oct 431 | Cyril arrives in Alexandria |
| 31 July 432 | Pope Celestine dies; succeeded by Sixtus III |
| 432–433 | Intensive shuttle diplomacy between Cyril and John |
| April 12 (or 23) 433 | **Formula of Reunion** signed |

### 9.2 Imperial Mediation

The key imperial mediator was Tribune **Aristolaus** (backed by magistrianos Maximus), who shuttled between Antioch and Alexandria with a blunt ultimatum: agree or face invalidation of Ephesus and Cyril's own banishment. **Acacius of Beroea** served as trusted ecclesiastical intermediary. Cyril's payments (Letter 96) also shifted Theodosius's position.

### 9.3 Theological Content of the Formula of Reunion

**Authorship:** The Formula was an **ANTIOCHENE DRAFT** (likely by Theodoret of Cyrrhus) that Cyril accepted with interpretive glosses.

**Key theological affirmations:**
* Christ is "perfect God and perfect man"
* "Union of two natures" — language Cyril had previously resisted
* Dual consubstantiality (consubstantial with Father in divinity; consubstantial with us in humanity)
* Distinction of Gospel sayings — some refer to divinity, some to humanity
* Theotokos accepted by John

**Cyril's additions/interpretive glosses:**
* No confusion of natures
* No change in divine nature
* Following Athanasius's interpretive tradition

### 9.4 Concessions

| Party | What They Gave | What They Received |
|-------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Antiochenes (John)** | Accepted Nestorius's deposition; accepted Theotokos; accepted "two natures united" | Cyril accepted "two natures" language; distinction of sayings; Cyril dropped "one incarnate nature"; Twelve Anathemas not required |
| **Cyril** | Accepted "two natures" language; accepted distinction of Gospel sayings (contradicting his 4th Anathema); dropped "one incarnate nature" (*mia physis*); accepted Antiochene draft | Nestorius's deposition confirmed; Theotokos affirmed; restored to his see; recognized as orthodox |

### 9.5 Status of the Twelve Anathemas

**Not required as a condition of reconciliation.** The Formula's distinction-of-sayings clause stands in tension with Anathema 4 (which forbids distributing Christ's sayings between two subjects). John of Antioch was never asked to accept the Twelve Anathemas as a condition of reconciliation. This does not necessarily mean the Twelve Anathemas were "rejected" or "condemned" — rather, they were not made the formula of reconciliation. Several interpretations are possible:

* **Interpretation A:** The Twelve Anathemas were not formally approved at Ephesus 431 (Graumann's argument), so there was nothing to "set aside."
* **Interpretation B:** The Twelve Anathemas had implicit theological endorsement at Ephesus but were diplomatically not required for reconciliation with the Antiochene party.
* **Interpretation C:** The Twelve Anathemas were considered too controversial for ecumenical reconciliation and were therefore not used as the primary formula, though their substance remained influential in Cyrillian circles.

The Formula of Reunion should be analyzed as a development in reconciliation, not as simple evidence that the Twelve Anathemas had no prior reception.

### 9.6 Nestorius's Fate

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Sept 431 | Retired to monastery of Euprepius near Antioch |
| Aug 3, 435 | Imperial edict: formally exiled to Great Oasis of Hibis, Egypt |
| ~435–450 | Transferred to Panopolis; injured by Nubian bandits (broken hand and rib) |
| ~450–451 | Died in exile |
| During exile | Wrote the *Liber Heraclidis* (Bazaar of Heracleides) — his self-defense |

## 10. LATER RECEPTION: CHALCEDON 451

### 10.1 What Chalcedon Read from Ephesus

| Document from Ephesus 431 | Read at Chalcedon 451? |
|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Cyril's **Second Letter** (*Intelligo quosdam*) | **YES** — solemnly approved as conforming to Nicene faith |
| Cyril's **Third Letter** with Twelve Anathemas | **NOT READ as an independent document** — not presented alongside the Second Letter; Cyrilline theological substance remained influential in conciliar tradition |
| Formula of Reunion (Cyril's Letter 39) | **YES** — read and affirmed |
| Nicene exclusivity decree (Session VI) | Referenced but superseded by Chalcedon's own Definition |

### 10.2 Chalcedon's Modifications of Ephesus

| Ephesus 431 | Chalcedon 451 |
|-------------|---------------|
| "From two natures" (*ek dyo physeōn*) | "In two natures" (*en dyo physesin*) — stronger formulation |
| Nestorius condemned | Nestorius's condemnation UPHELD |
| Cyril's Second Letter approved | Cyril's Second Letter REAFFIRMED |
| Twelve Anathemas: present at council; formal approval disputed | Twelve Anathemas: NOT READ as an independent document; Cyrilline theology received through Second Letter and Formula of Reunion |
| Formula of Reunion as reconciliation | Formula of Reunion READ but extended into dyophysite formulation |
| — | Leo's Tome READ and APPROVED (new element not present at Ephesus) |

**Chalcedon 451 and the Twelve Anathemas — Precision Statement:**

The question is not simply whether the Twelve Anathemas were "present" or "absent" at Chalcedon. The evidence shows:
- The Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas was NOT read as an independent document at Chalcedon's formal sessions.
- Cyrilline theological substance — Theotokos, hypostatic union, rejection of Nestorian division — WAS received and affirmed at Chalcedon through Cyril's Second Letter and the Formula of Reunion.
- Whether the non-reading of the Third Letter constituted deliberate exclusion, diplomatic silence, or simply procedural selectivity is historically debated.
- Theodoret's and Ibas's anti-Anathema writings were not retracted, but neither were the Twelve Anathemas explicitly condemned at Chalcedon.

### 10.3 Readmission of Anti-Cyrillian Bishops

Chalcedon readmitted **Theodoret of Cyrus** and **Ibas of Edessa** — both had condemned Cyril's Twelve Anathemas — **without requiring any retraction** of their anti-Cyrilline writings. This is significant because it means the council that supposedly vindicated Cyril's theology actually restored his most vocal theological opponents.

### 10.4 Nestorius's Claim of Vindication

Nestorius claimed (Bazaar of Heracleides) that Chalcedon vindicated him because it adopted "two natures" language he had championed.

**Assessment (from SRC-THEO-008 audit, STEP 79):**
* **Claim: "Chalcedon vindicated Nestorius"** — UNSAFE (Chalcedon upheld his condemnation; Nestorius's claim is self-serving interpretation)
* **Claim: "Chalcedon completely contradicted everything Nestorius taught"** — UNSAFE (Chalcedon adopted dyophysite language Nestorius championed; terminological differences remain)
* **Claim: "Chalcedon and Nestorius share dyophysite commitment but differ in terminology and union mode"** — SAFE (supported by evidence comparison)

### 10.5 Constantinople II (553) — Explicit Conciliar Reaffirmation of the Twelve Anathemas

The Fifth Ecumenical Council (553) explicitly reaffirmed Cyril's Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas:
* Reaffirmed Cyril's Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas as orthodox
* Anathematized Theodoret's and Ibas's anti-Cyrilline writings (the "Three Chapters")
* Stated that both expressions — "in two natures" (Antiochene/Chalcedonian) and "one incarnate nature" (Alexandrian) — are orthodox if rightly understood

**Historical Context:** This explicit reaffirmation occurred in the specific context of the Three Chapters controversy, where Emperor Justinian sought to reconcile the Oriental Orthodox by explicitly endorsing Cyrilline theology and condemning the anti-Cyrilline writings that Chalcedon had tolerated.

**Precision Statement:** Constantinople II (553) represents the most explicit conciliar reception of the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas in the conciliar record. However, this does not mean the Twelve Anathemas had no reception between 431 and 553. The evidence shows:
- The Twelve Anathemas were present at Ephesus 431 and likely read.
- Their formal approval at 431 is disputed (no comparable vote to the Second Letter).
- The Formula of Reunion (433) did not require their acceptance.
- Chalcedon (451) did not read them as an independent document.
- Cyrilline theology remained influential throughout the period 431–553.
- Constantinople II (553) provided explicit conciliar endorsement in the context of the Three Chapters controversy.

This reception history should not be described as a "122-year gap" or as if the Twelve Anathemas "did not appear at all" between 431 and 553. The reception shows a spectrum from textual presence to explicit conciliar endorsement, with varying degrees of theological influence and diplomatic handling throughout the period.
