## 2. FOUR ENTITIES DISTINGUISHED

### 2A. Nestorius the Historical Person

**Identity:** Archbishop of Constantinople (428–431); died in exile c. 451.
**Evidence Base:** Bazaar of Heracleides (Syriac translation), ~30 sermon fragments, several letters, conciliar acts (SRC-THEO-008).
**Key Findings (from STEP 79):**
- One Christ, one prosopon of union
- Two natures (divine and human)
- Two prosopa subsist in respective natures
- Prosopic union (synapheia)
- Qualified acceptance of Theotokos
- Explicit denial of "Two Sons"
- Condemned at Ephesus 431

### 2B. "Nestorianism" as Later Doctrinal Category

**Definition:** A polemical label applied by opponents (principally Cyril and his successors) to designate teachings perceived as dividing Christ into two subjects.
**Evidence (from STEP 79, Claim G):** "Nestorianism" is a polemical label applied by opponents; Church of the East's Christology differs from Nestorius's own formulations. **UNSAFE to equate automatically with Nestorius's own theology.**
**Layers:**
- Cyril's polemic (430s): targeting Nestorius's prosopic union as inadequate
- Conciliar usage (431 onward): designation of condemned teaching
- Medieval usage: broad label for East Syriac Christianity
- Modern academic usage: increasingly contested (Brock 1996: "lamentable misnomer")

### 2C. Church of the East (Historical)

**Identity:** Ecclesial body centered in the Sasanian Persian Empire, with catholicate at Seleucia-Ctesiphon; using East Syriac liturgical tradition.
**Evidence (from SRC-THEO-008 §5A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §5A):**
- Never accepted Nestorius's condemnation
- Venerated Nestorius as one of three "Greek Teachers" (with Diodore of Tarsus and Theodore of Mopsuestia)
- East Syriac Eucharistic Service incorporates prayers attributed to Nestorius
- Preserved Bazaar of Heracleides in monastery libraries
- 14th-century Ebed Jesu lists Bazaar among Nestorius's works

### 2D. Modern Denominational Bodies

**Assyrian Church of the East (from SRC-THEO-008 §5B):**
- Descendant of historical Church of the East
- Reveres Nestorius as saint
- Does NOT subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" as traditionally understood in West
- Rejects that Nestorius taught any heresy
- Patriarch Dinkha IV repudiated exonym "Nestorian" in 1976

**Ancient Church of the East:**
- Distinct modern body, separated from Assyrian Church of the East in 20th century
- NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources (beyond recognition as distinct entity)

**Chaldean Catholic Church (from SRC-THEO-008 §5C):**
- In communion with Rome
- Distinct from Assyrian Church of the East
- Historical connection to East Syriac tradition but different ecclesial status

**Governance:** These four entities MUST NOT be collapsed. Nestorius ≠ Nestorianism ≠ Church of the East ≠ modern Assyrian Church of the East.

## 3. NESTORIUS HIMSELF

### 3A. Episcopate (428–431)

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §4A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §7A):**
- 428: Consecrated Archbishop of Constantinople
- 428–430: Theotokos controversy erupts
- 430: Pope Celestine delegates authority to Cyril to excommunicate Nestorius
- 431: Ephesus condemns Nestorius
- 433: Formula of Reunion; Nestorius abandoned by Antiochene supporters
- 435: Imperial edict exiles Nestorius to Great Oasis of Hibis, Egypt
- c. 451: Dies in exile; Bazaar of Heracleides composed before death

### 3B. Christological Terminology (from STEP 79 §4A, §10)

| Term | Nestorius's Usage |
|------|-------------------|
| Prosopon | Observable character, defining properties, manifestation of reality |
| Two prosopa | Divine and human prosopa subsist in respective natures |
| One prosopon of union | Two prosopa coalesce in one prosopon (Christ) |
| Synapheia | Conjunction — preferred union term |
| Physis | Nature — affirms two (divine and human) |
| Hypostasis | Closely aligned with physis (not identical to Cyril's usage) |
| Theotokos | Accepted in qualified sense |
| Christotokos | Preferred title |

### 3C. Prosopic Union (from STEP 79 §4A)

**Direct Quotation (Bazaar):**
> "I predicate two natures... the union of the prosopa took place for the prosopon and not for the ousia and the nature... the natures subsist in their prosopa and in their natures and in the prosopon of the union... thus one prosopon of the two natures."

### 3D. Book of Heraclides (Bazaar)

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §2, §13):**
- Survives only in Syriac translation; Greek original lost
- Translation acknowledged as sometimes inaccurate
- Possible interpolations (Bevan 2013)
- Manuscript damage (133 pages lost)
- Discovery in 19th century renewed scholarly interest
- Nestorius's own account: claims he never had fair hearing; accepts personal anathema for sake of preserving dyophysite theology

### 3E. Hostile Reports vs. Own Evidence

**From STEP 79 §3, §8:**
- Cyril's Twelve Anathemas partially misrepresent Nestorius's position
- Terminological mismatch is real (prosopon vs hypostasis; synapheia vs henōsis)
- Gap between polemic and evidence is demonstrable
- Cyril's "two Christs" accusation is polemical caricature

### 3F. Retention from STEP 79

**MAINTAINED:** "Nestorius himself" ≠ "Nestorianism". The historical person's theology is more complex and nuanced than the polemical category created by his opponents.

## 4. DID NESTORIUS FOUND THE CHURCH OF THE EAST?

### 4A. The Claim

**Test:** "Nestorius founded the Church of the East."

### 4B. Chronological Relationship

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §5A):**
- Christianity existed in Mesopotamia/Persia well before Nestorius (428)
- The Church of the East had established structures (Seleucia-Ctesiphon as catholicate) before Nestorius's episcopate
- Nestorius never traveled to Persia as a missionary or founder
- Nestorius was exiled to Egypt (435), not to Persia

**Classification:** The Church of the East existed within Mesopotamian/Persian Christianity before Nestorius's exile.

### 4C. Geographical Relationship

- Nestorius's ministry: Constantinople (428–431), then exile in Egypt (435–c. 451)
- Church of the East: centered in Seleucia-Ctesiphon (Sasanian Empire), with roots in Edessa and Nisibis
- Nestorius was geographically distant from the Church of the East's institutional center

### 4D. Institutional Relationship

- No evidence that Nestorius ordained bishops for the Church of the East
- No evidence that Nestorius established dioceses in Persia
- No evidence that the Church of the East derived its apostolic succession from Nestorius
- Church of the East traced its origins to the Apostle Thomas and the evangelization of Edessa

### 4E. Personal Membership

- Nestorius was never a member of the Church of the East
- Nestorius was Archbishop of Constantinople (Roman imperial church)
- Nestorius died in exile in Egypt

### 4F. Episcopal Succession

- Church of the East had its own catholical succession at Seleucia-Ctesiphon independent of Nestorius
- Nestorius had no role in the appointment of Church of the East catholicoi

### 4G. Political Relationship

- Nestorius operated within the Roman imperial system (Constantinople, then Egyptian exile)
- Church of the East operated within the Sasanian Persian political system
- These were rival empires; the Roman-Persian political divide was a structural factor

### 4H. Classification

**Claim: "Nestorius founded the Church of the East."**
**Classification: UNSAFE**

**Evidence:** The Church of the East existed as an institutional body within Persian Christianity before Nestorius's exile. Nestorius had no institutional, geographical, or personal founding relationship with it. He was never a member, never traveled to Persia, and never established its structures.

**Qualification:** This does NOT preclude later influence. Nestorius's theological legacy may have been received by the Church of the East after his death. The non-founding relationship and later influence are separate propositions.

## 5. PERSIAN CHRISTIANITY

### 5A. Mesopotamian Christianity

**Evidence from existing sources:** SRC-THEO-008 §5 mentions the Church of the East as centered in the Persian/Sasanian Empire. The Constantinople II audit (§4.3) mentions Ibas's "Letter to Mari the Persian," indicating Persian Christian contacts in the 5th century.

**What can be established from existing sources:**
- Christianity was present in Mesopotamia and Persia in the early centuries
- Edessa was a significant center of Syriac Christianity
- The Church of the East had its catholicate at Seleucia-Ctesiphon

**What is NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources:**
- Detailed chronology of early Mesopotamian Christianization
- Specific early synods of the Church of the East (e.g., Seleucia 410, Dadisho 424)
- Full institutional structure before the 5th century
- Specific details of the Roman-Persian political boundary's effect on ecclesial development

### 5B. Sasanian Context

**From existing sources:** The Church of the East operated within the Sasanian Persian political system (SRC-THEO-008 §5). The Roman-Persian political divide is referenced implicitly throughout the Nestorian controversy materials.

**NOT ESTABLISHED in detail:** Specific Sasanian imperial policies toward Christianity, Zoroastrian religious dynamics, or the specific political pressures on Persian Christians.

### 5C. Edessa

**Evidence (from CONSTANTINOPLE-II-553-THREE-CHAPTERS-FORENSIC-AUDIT §4.3, §7):**
- Ibas of Edessa was Bishop of Edessa (d. 457)
- Ibas translated Theodore's works into Syriac
- Ibas wrote to Mari the Persian (c. 433) about the Nestorian controversy
- Edessa was a center of Antiochene theological transmission

**NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources:**
- The School of the Persians at Edessa in detail
- The 489 closure of the Edessa school
- Specific details of Ibas's role in theological transmission to Persia

### 5D. Nisibis

**NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources.** Nisibis is not mentioned in any existing source record or analysis. Any claims about the School of Nisibis, its relationship to Edessa, or its role in transmitting Antiochene theology to the Church of the East cannot be established from the knowledge store.

### 5E. Theological Schools

**What can be established:**
- Antiochene theological tradition existed (Theodore, Diodore, Theodoret, Ibas)
- Edessa was a transmission point (Ibas translated Theodore into Syriac)
- The Church of the East venerated the "three Greek Teachers" (Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius)

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** Detailed institutional history of the schools of Edessa and Nisibis, their curricula, their chronology, or the specific mechanisms of theological transmission to the Church of the East.

### 5F. Relationship with Antiochene Tradition

**Evidence (from CONSTANTINOPLE-II-553, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT):**
- Theodore of Mopsuestia was Nestorius's teacher (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.1, §5.1)
- Nestorius drew on Theodore's exegetical methods
- Theodoret of Cyrus defended Nestorius at Ephesus
- Ibas of Edessa translated Theodore's works and defended him
- The Church of the East venerated Diodore, Theodore, and Nestorius

**Assessment:** The Church of the East's connection to Antiochene theology is INDIRECT — mediated through Edessa, Syriac translation, and the movement of scholars after Ephesus 431. It is NOT a direct institutional relationship.

## 6. THEODORE OF MOPSUESTIA

### 6A. Theodore's Identity

**Evidence (CONSTANTINOPLE-II-553 §4.1, §5):**
- Theodore of Mopsuestia (c. 350–428)
- Bishop of Mopsuestia (Cilicia)
- Teacher of Nestorius
- Prominent Antiochene exegete
- Died 20 years before Nestorian controversy

### 6B. Theodore's Christology

**Evidence (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §5.1):**
- Emphasis on distinction of natures (Antiochene tradition)
- Historical-grammatical exegesis
- Reluctance to apply divine predicates to human Christ
- Concern for full humanity
- Word "indwelt" the man Jesus (inhabitation model)
- Two natures, one person (but person defined differently than Cyril)
- Prosopic union (union in one prosopon)
- Distinction of operations (divine and human)

### 6C. Theodore ≠ Nestorius

**Evidence (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.1):**
- Theodore was Nestorius's teacher
- Nestorius drew on Theodore's methods
- But Theodore's Christology was more nuanced than Nestorius's popular preaching
- Theodore died 20 years before the Nestorian controversy
- Theodore was not discussed at Ephesus (431)

**MAINTAINED:** Theodore ≠ Nestorius.

### 6D. Theodore's Influence on Edessa/Nisibis

**Evidence from existing sources:**
- Ibas of Edessa translated Theodore's works into Syriac (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.3)
- Ibas defended Theodore in his Letter to Mari the Persian
- The Church of the East venerated Theodore as one of three "Greek Teachers" (SRC-THEO-008 §5A)

**Assessment:** Theodore's influence reached Edessa through Ibas's translations and was received by the Church of the East. The mechanism of transmission to Nisibis is NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources.

### 6E. Test: Theodore's Influence on Church of the East

**Claim:** "The Church of the East's Christology was more directly influenced by Theodore than by Nestorius."

**Evidence:**
1. Church of the East venerated three "Greek Teachers": Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius (SRC-THEO-008 §5A)
2. Theodore is listed first among the three in some traditions, suggesting primary theological authority
3. Ibas translated Theodore into Syriac (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.3)
4. Theodore's Christological vocabulary (prosopic union, distinction of natures) parallels Church of the East terminology
5. Nestorius himself drew on Theodore (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §5.1)
6. Theodore died 20 years before the controversy, so his theology was already established
7. The Church of the East's Christological terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa) reflects Syriac theological development, not direct Nestorian translation

**Classification: SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION**

The evidence supports that Theodore was a primary theological source for the Church of the East's Antiochene Christology. However:
- "More directly influenced" cannot be proven with precision from existing sources
- Both Theodore and Nestorius contributed to the Church of the East's theological heritage
- The Church of the East developed its own Syriac Christological synthesis that cannot be reduced to either figure
- Scholarly consensus on this point is not uniform

### 6F. Theodore's Condemnation at Constantinople II (553)

**Evidence (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.1, §5):**
- Justinian's edict (543–544) condemned Theodore's writings
- Constantinople II (553) explicitly condemned Theodore's writings
- Rationale: Theodore was source of Nestorian error
- Theological genealogy: Theodore → Nestorius
- Chalcedon had NOT condemned Theodore
- Theodore could not defend himself (died 428)

## 7. EPHESUS 431 — CONSEQUENCES FOR EASTERN CHRISTIANITY

### 7A. Nestorius's Deposition

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-009, THEOLOGY-EPHESUS-431-FORENSIC-AUDIT):**
- June 22, 431: Nestorius condemned and deposed in absentia
- 433: Formula of Reunion; Nestorius abandoned by Antiochene supporters
- 435: Imperial exile to Egypt
- Nestorius never returned to any position of authority

### 7B. Theological Controversy

**Evidence (THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §7, THEOLOGY-EPHESUS-431):**
- Genuine Christological disagreement (not merely political)
- Cyril's miaphysite formulation vs Nestorius's dyophysite formulation
- Theotokos as boundary term
- The controversy continued after Ephesus (Formula of Reunion 433, Chalcedon 451, Constantinople II 553)

### 7C. Episcopal Networks

**Evidence (THEOLOGY-EPHESUS-431 §4, §5):**
- John of Antioch arrived late; convened counter-council
- Theodoret of Cyrus among 68 bishops who protested opening
- John's counter-council deposed Cyril and Memnon
- Formula of Reunion (433) reconciled Cyril and John; Nestorius abandoned
- Antiochene bishops who supported Nestorius eventually reconciled with Cyril (without accepting Twelve Anathemas)

### 7D. Persian Political Environment

**NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources.** The specific reaction of the Sasanian court to the Nestorian controversy, or the effect of Roman imperial persecution of Nestorius's supporters on Persian Christianity, cannot be established from the knowledge store.

### 7E. Movement of Scholars

**Evidence (from CONSTANTINOPLE-II, SRC-THEO-008):**
- Ibas of Edessa: translated Theodore's works into Syriac; wrote Letter to Mari the Persian
- Theodoret of Cyrus: restored at Chalcedon; anti-Cyrillian writings condemned at 553
- After Ephesus, Antiochene scholars and their students were displaced

**NOT ESTABLISHED in detail:** The specific movement of scholars from Edessa to Nisibis to Persia after 431 or after the 489 Edessa closure.

### 7F. Classification

**Claim: "Ephesus created the Church of the East."**
**Classification: UNSAFE** — The Church of the East existed before Ephesus as an institutional body in Persia.

**Claim: "The Church of the East was founded as a reaction to Ephesus."**
**Classification: UNSAFE** — The Church of the East's institutional origins predate Ephesus. Ephesus may have affected theological reception and scholarly migration, but it did not create the Church of the East.

**Claim: "Ephesus affected the theological trajectory of the Church of the East."**
**Classification: SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION** — The condemnation of Nestorius, the displacement of Antiochene scholars, and the reception of Theodore's works through Syriac translation are plausible mechanisms. However, the exact causal chain is not fully established in existing sources.

## 9. "NESTORIAN" LABEL — TERMINOLOGY AUDIT

### 9A. External Polemical Usage

**Evidence (THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT, SRC-THEO-008 §5):**
- Cyril and his successors used "Nestorian" as a polemical designation for teachings perceived as dividing Christ
- Byzantine/Roman historiography used "Nestorian" for the Church of the East
- Medieval Western sources used "Nestorian" broadly for East Syriac Christians
- Oriental Orthodox historiography used "Nestorian" for Chalcedonians and East Syriac Christians alike

**Character:** Pejorative; implies theological error associated with the condemned Nestorius.

### 9B. Internal Historical Usage

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A):**
- Church of the East venerated Nestorius as one of three "Greek Teachers"
- East Syriac Eucharistic Service incorporates prayers attributed to Nestorius
- 14th-century Ebed Jesu lists Bazaar among Nestorius's works
- Church of the East preserved Nestorius's writings

**Assessment:** The Church of the East honored Nestorius and preserved his works. Whether the Church of the East historically used "Nestorian" as a self-designation is NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources. What is established is positive reception of Nestorius as a theological authority.

### 9C. Medieval Usage

**NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources.** The specific medieval usage patterns of "Nestorian" by external and internal authors are not documented in the knowledge store.

### 9D. Modern Academic Usage

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §6B, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §6):**
- Sebastian P. Brock (1996): "'Nestorian' Church — A Lamentable Misnomer"
- Modern Syriac scholarship increasingly rejects the label
- Traditional Western scholarship used the label uncritically
- Modern ecumenical scholarship recognizes the complexity

**Assessment:** Modern academic usage is contested. A significant scholarly trend (led by Brock) argues the label is misleading. Not all scholars agree.

### 9E. Modern Denominational Usage

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5B):**
- Patriarch Dinkha IV repudiated the exonym "Nestorian" in 1976
- Assyrian Church of the East officially rejects the label
- The label persists in popular usage and some academic contexts

### 9F. Classification

**"Nestorian" is a historically layered label whose meaning changes by period, author, and context.**

It is NOT simply "always a slur" nor "always accurate." Instead:
- In Cyril's usage: polemical designation for perceived theological error
- In Byzantine usage: broad label for East Syriac Christians (external, often pejorative)
- In medieval Western usage: designation for Church of the East (often without knowledge of its actual theology)
- In Church of the East's positive reception of Nestorius: honoring a theological authority
- In modern academic usage: increasingly contested (Brock 1996)
- In modern denominational usage: officially repudiated (Dinkha IV, 1976)

## 10. CHURCH OF THE EAST — INSTITUTIONAL PROFILE

### 10A. Origins

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §5A):**
- Institutional body centered in the Sasanian Persian Empire
- Catholicate at Seleucia-Ctesiphon
- East Syriac liturgical tradition
- Existed before Nestorius's exile (435)

**NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources:**
- Specific founding date or apostolic origins
- Early synods (Seleucia 410, Dadisho 424)
- Detailed institutional structure before 5th century
- Specific relationship to Edessa before the Nestorian controversy

### 10B. Sasanian Environment

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A):** Church of the East operated within the Persian/Sasanian political context.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** Specific Sasanian policies, Zoroastrian dynamics, or detailed political pressures.

### 10C. Relationship to Roman Imperial Christianity

**Evidence (implicit from all Nestorian controversy sources):**
- Church of the East was geographically and politically separated from Roman imperial Christianity
- The Roman-Persian divide created institutional independence
- Church of the East did not participate in Roman imperial councils (Ephesus 431, Chalcedon 451)
- Church of the East never accepted Nestorius's condemnation (which was a Roman conciliar decision)

### 10D. Councils Recognized/Rejected

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §5A):**
- Church of the East never accepted Nestorius's condemnation (Ephesus 431)
- Venerated Nestorius as theological authority
- Relationship to Chalcedon: NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources (see §12)
- Relationship to Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381: NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources

### 10E. Theological Development

**Evidence (SRC-THEO-008 §5A, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §5A-B):**
- Venerated three "Greek Teachers" (Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius)
- Developed East Syriac Christological terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa)
- Modern Assyrian Church of the East does NOT subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" as traditionally understood in West
- Patriarch Dinkha IV repudiated "Nestorian" exonym (1976)

### 10F. Anti-Anachronism

**Do NOT retroject modern denominational identity into the 5th century.**
The "Assyrian Church of the East" as a modern denominational body is not identical to the 5th-century Church of the East. The institutional, theological, and cultural development spans over 1500 years.

## 12. CHURCH OF THE EAST AND CHALCEDON

### 12A. Evidence Assessment

**What can be established:**
- Church of the East did not participate in Chalcedon (451) — implied by geographical/political separation
- Church of the East never accepted Nestorius's condemnation (SRC-THEO-008 §5A)
- Chalcedon upheld Nestorius's condemnation (THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT §9F)

**What is NOT ESTABLISHED:**
- Whether the Church of the East explicitly accepted or rejected the Chalcedonian Definition
- Whether the Church of the East was aware of Chalcedon's proceedings
- Whether the Church of the East's Christology aligns with or diverges from Chalcedon's formula
- Whether the Church of the East viewed Chalcedon as a legitimate council

### 12B. Analysis

**Do NOT assume:** Church of the East = Chalcedonian.
**Do NOT assume:** Church of the East = anti-Chalcedonian.

**What can be established:** The Church of the East existed in a separate political and institutional context (Sasanian Empire). Its relationship to Chalcedon is not documented in existing sources. The Church of the East's dyophysite commitments (veneration of Theodore, Nestorius) partially overlap with Chalcedon's dyophysite language, but the institutional and terminological relationship is NOT ESTABLISHED.

### 12C. Classification

**Claim: "Church of the East was Chalcedonian."**
**Classification: NOT ESTABLISHED**

**Claim: "Church of the East was anti-Chalcedonian."**
**Classification: NOT ESTABLISHED**

**Claim: "Church of the East's Christology partially overlaps with Chalcedonian dyophysitism but differs in terminology and institutional allegiance."**

## 15. NESTORIANISM VS DYOPHYSITISM — TERMINOLOGY AUDIT

### 15A. Definitions from Existing Sources

**Nestorianism (from THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT, SRC-THEO-008 §5):**
- Polemical label for teachings perceived as dividing Christ into two subjects
- Applied to Nestorius's followers and later to the Church of the East
- Modern scholarship contests the label (Brock 1996)

**Dyophysitism (from THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT, CHALCEDON-451-RECEPTION, MIAPHYSITE-MONOPHYSITE-EUTYCHIAN):**
- Christological position affirming two natures (divine and human) in Christ
- Shared by Nestorius, Chalcedon, and the Church of the East
- Does NOT imply Nestorianism (two subjects)

**Miaphysitism (from MIAPHYSITE-MONOPHYSITE-EUTYCHIAN-FORENSIC-AUDIT):**
- Christological position: "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (mia physis sesarkōmenē)
- Associated with Cyril of Alexandria and the Oriental Orthodox tradition
- NOT equivalent to Monophysitism (Eutychian confusion of natures)

**Chalcedonian Christology (from SRC-THEO-005, CHALCEDON-451-RECEPTION):**
- One hypostasis, two natures
- Without confusion, without change, without division, without separation
- Upholds Nestorius's condemnation while adopting dyophysite language

**Antiochene Christology (from CONSTANTINOPLE-II §5.1, THEOLOGY-NESTORIUS-AUDIT):**
- Emphasis on distinction of natures
- Historical-grammatical exegesis
- Prosopic union
- Associated with Theodore, Theodoret, Ibas, Nestorius, Diodore

**Church of the East Christology (from SRC-THEO-008 §5):**
- Venerated Theodore, Nestorius, Diodore
- East Syriac terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa)
- Dyophysite orientation
- NOT simply identical to Nestorius's own formulations

### 15B. These Are NOT Synonyms

| Term | Scope |
|------|-------|
| Nestorianism | Polemical label; dividing Christ into two subjects |
| Dyophysitism | Affirming two natures (broad category) |
| Miaphysitism | One incarnate nature (Cyrillian) |
| Chalcedonian | One hypostasis, two natures (conciliar definition) |
| Antiochene | Distinction of natures, prosopic union (school tradition) |
| Church of the East | Syriac dyophysite synthesis (distinct tradition) |

### 15C. Test: "All dyophysites are Nestorians."

**Classification: UNSAFE**

**Evidence:**
- Chalcedon affirms two natures (dyophysite) but upholds Nestorius's condemnation
- Leo's Tome affirms two natures but is not "Nestorian"
- The Church of the East is dyophysite but its Christology differs from Nestorius's own formulations
- "Dyophysitism" is a broad category; "Nestorianism" is a specific polemical designation
- Equating all dyophysites with Nestorians is a polemical reduction, not a historical finding

### 15D. Test: "Church of the East is historically associated with a dyophysite Christology."

**Classification: SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION**

**Evidence:**
- Church of the East venerated Theodore and Nestorius (both dyophysite)
- Church of the East developed East Syriac Christological terminology affirming two natures
- Modern Assyrian Church of the East does not subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" as traditionally understood
- The specific formulation (qnoma, kyana, parsopa) differs from Greek dyophysite terminology

## 17. HISTORIOGRAPHICAL DEBATE — THREE MODELS

### MODEL A: Church of the East as Substantially Shaped by Nestorian/Antiochene Theology

**Claim:** The Church of the East's Christology was substantially formed by reception of Nestorian and Antiochene theology (Nestorius, Theodore, Diodore).

**Evidence:**
- Church of the East venerated three "Greek Teachers" (Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius)
- East Syriac liturgy incorporates prayers attributed to Nestorius
- Church of the East preserved Nestorius's Bazaar
- Theodore's works translated into Syriac by Ibas
- Antiochene theological vocabulary parallels Church of the East terminology

**Strength:** Explains the positive reception of Nestorius and Theodore; accounts for dyophysite orientation.

**Limitations:**
- Reduces Church of the East to passive reception of Greek theology
- Does not account for independent Syriac theological development
- Does not explain the Church of the East's distinct terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa)
- Overstates Nestorius's role relative to Theodore's

**Confidence:** MODERATE

### MODEL B: Church of the East Primarily Shaped by Theodore and Syriac Development; Nestorius Important Later

**Claim:** Theodore was the primary theological influence; Nestorius became important as a symbol of resistance to Ephesus and as a preserved theological authority.

**Evidence:**
- Theodore was Nestorius's teacher (more foundational)
- Theodore's Christology was more nuanced than Nestorius's popular preaching
- Ibas translated Theodore's works into Syriac (direct transmission)
- Church of the East listed Theodore among "Greek Teachers"
- Nestorius was never a member of the Church of the East; his influence was indirect

**Strength:** Accounts for Theodore's foundational role; explains the distinction between Nestorius's personal theology and Church of the East's developed Christology.

**Limitations:**
- "Primarily shaped" is difficult to prove from existing sources
- Does not fully account for Nestorius's positive veneration
- The relative weight of Theodore vs. Nestorius is not precisely determinable

**Confidence:** MODERATE

### MODEL C: Church of the East Developed Its Own Christological Synthesis

**Claim:** The Church of the East developed a distinct Syriac Christological synthesis (qnoma, kyana, parsopa) that cannot be reduced to either Nestorius or Theodore.

**Evidence:**
- Modern Assyrian Church of the East does NOT subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" (SRC-THEO-008 §5B)
- Patriarch Dinkha IV repudiated "Nestorian" exonym (1976)
- East Syriac terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa) differs from Greek terminology
- Church of the East existed institutionally before receiving Nestorius's legacy
- Brock (1996): "'Nestorian' Church — A Lamentable Misnomer"

**Strength:** Accounts for the Church of the East's distinct identity; avoids reducing Syriac theology to Greek categories; aligns with modern scholarly critique.

**Limitations:**
- Details of the Church of the East's distinct synthesis are NOT ESTABLISHED in existing sources (Babai, qnoma terminology, etc.)
- Does not deny Antiochene influence — only claims the synthesis is independent
- The degree of independence from Theodore/Nestorius cannot be precisely determined

**Confidence:** MODERATE

### 17A. Assessment

No single model can be established as correct from existing sources. All three have evidence and limitations. The most defensible position is a synthesis:

**The Church of the East received Antiochene theology (principally Theodore, secondarily Nestorius) through Syriac translation and developed its own Christological synthesis that cannot be simply equated with either Nestorius's or Theodore's personal theology.**

## 18. MODERN SCHOLARSHIP — NO FALSE CONSENSUS

### 18A. Distinguished Perspectives

**Traditional Church Historiography:**
- Church of the East = Nestorian (uncritical usage)
- Nestorius = founder of the heresy
- "Nestorian" label used without qualification

**Roman/Byzantine Polemical Historiography:**
- Church of the East as heretical body outside orthodoxy
- "Nestorian" as theological error designation

**Oriental Orthodox Historiography:**
- Chalcedonian and East Syriac traditions both as "Nestorian" (broad usage)
- Church of the East as extreme dyophysite

**Church of the East Self-Understanding:**
- Venerated Nestorius as theological authority
- Rejected the "Nestorian" label as misrepresentation
- Emphasized continuity with apostolic tradition

**Modern Syriac Scholarship:**
- Increasingly rejects "Nestorian" label (Brock 1996)
- Emphasizes independent Syriac theological development
- Distinguishes Nestorius from Church of the East

**Modern Ecumenical Scholarship:**
- Recognizes complexity of the label
- Notes Nestorius's own theology differs from polemical caricature
- Acknowledges Church of the East's distinct identity

### 18B. No Unanimous Position

**Modern scholarship is NOT unanimous on:**
- Whether "Nestorian" is an appropriate label for the Church of the East
- The relative weight of Theodore vs. Nestorius in Church of the East theology
- Whether the Church of the East's Christology is substantially Nestorian
- The historical relationship between Nestorius and the Church of the East

**Avoid false consensus.**

**Evidence:** Church of the East existed within Persian Christianity before Nestorius's exile (435). Nestorius never traveled to Persia, never established dioceses there, and had no institutional founding role. He was Archbishop of Constantinople and died in exile in Egypt. ## 20. STEELMAN ANALYSIS

### SIDE A: Strongest Argument FOR Calling the Tradition "Nestorian"

1. **Association with Nestorius:** The Church of the East explicitly venerated Nestorius as one of three "Greek Teachers" and preserved his writings (SRC-THEO-008 §5A). This is not merely passive reception; it is active theological endorsement.

2. **Refusal to Accept Ephesus:** The Church of the East never accepted Nestorius's condemnation at Ephesus 431. By maintaining communion with Nestorius's theological legacy while the rest of Christendom condemned him, the Church of the East aligned itself with the "Nestorian" position in the eyes of external observers.

3. **Antiochene Christology:** The Church of the East's dyophysite Christology, its emphasis on distinction of natures, and its veneration of Antiochene theologians (Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius) place it within the Antiochene tradition that Nestorius represented.

4. **Later Positive Use of Nestorius:** The Church of the East incorporated Nestorius's prayers into its Eucharistic liturgy and preserved his Bazaar — actions that go beyond mere historical interest and indicate theological identification.

5. **Theological Continuity Claims:** The Church of the East's Christology (prosopic union language, distinction of natures, two-nature affirmation) parallels Nestorius's own formulations, suggesting theological continuity even if not direct institutional descent.

**Strength of Side A:** The positive reception, liturgical incorporation, and theological parallels give the "Nestorian" label a genuine historical basis. External observers were not simply inventing a connection — the Church of the East actively honored Nestorius.

### SIDE B: Strongest Argument AGAINST the Label

1. **Church of the East Predates Nestorius:** The Church of the East existed as an institutional body in Persia before Nestorius's exile (435). It was not founded by Nestorius and did not derive its institutional identity from him. (SRC-THEO-008 §5A)

2. **Theodore's Influence:** Theodore of Mopsuestia was Nestorius's teacher and a more foundational theological influence. Ibas translated Theodore's works into Syriac. If the Church of the East should be named after any single figure, Theodore has a stronger claim than Nestorius. (CONSTANTINOPLE-II §4.1, §5.1)

3. **Nestorius Was Never Founder:** Nestorius was Archbishop of Constantinople, exiled to Egypt. He never traveled to Persia, never established dioceses, and was never a member of the Church of the East. Calling it "Nestorian" implies a founding relationship that never existed.

4. **Independent Development:** The Church of the East developed its own Syriac Christological terminology (qnoma, kyana, parsopa) that cannot be reduced to Nestorius's Greek terminology. Modern Assyrian Church of the East does NOT subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" as traditionally understood. (SRC-THEO-008 §5B)

5. **"Nestorian" Has Multiple Meanings:** In Cyril's usage: polemical slur. In Byzantine usage: broad external label. In modern scholarship: increasingly contested (Brock 1996). In modern denominational usage: officially repudiated (Dinkha IV, 1976). The label's meaning is unstable and context-dependent.

**Strength of Side B:** The Church of the East's pre-existence, Theodore's greater influence, Nestorius's non-membership, independent Syriac development, and the label's polemical origins all undermine "Nestorian" as a historically accurate primary designation.

### 20A. Assessment

**Neither side is correct merely because it is historically simpler.**

Side A correctly identifies that the Church of the East actively honored Nestorius and maintained theological parallels. Side B correctly identifies that the label is misleading in scope, origin, and implication. The truth lies in recognizing that:

- The label has a historical basis (positive reception of Nestorius)
- The label is misleading (implies founding, reduces to one figure, ignores Theodore, ignores independent development)
- The label's meaning changes by period and context
- Modern scholarship and the Church itself increasingly reject the label

## 21. FINAL ANSWER TO THE LABEL QUESTION

### "Is it historically accurate to call the Church of the East 'Nestorian'?"

### Classification: SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION / DISPUTED

The answer depends on which dimension of "Nestorian" is being invoked:

| Dimension | Classification | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Historical Association** | SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION | Church of the East positively received Nestorius as theological authority. But association ≠ identity. |
| **Theological Influence** | SAFE WITH QUALIFICATION | Nestorius's theology influenced the Church of the East, but Theodore's influence was at least equally important, and the Church developed its own synthesis. |
| **Institutional Origin** | UNSAFE | The Church of the East existed before Nestorius and was not founded by him. |
| **Self-Identification** | DISPUTED | Church of the East honored Nestorius but modern Church repudiated "Nestorian" label (1976). Historical self-designation as "Nestorian" is NOT ESTABLISHED. |
| **Modern Scholarly Terminology** | DISPUTED | Increasingly contested (Brock 1996); not all scholars agree. |

### Summary Statement

**The Church of the East can be described as historically associated with Nestorius's theological legacy, but calling it "the Nestorian Church" is misleading because:**
1. It existed before Nestorius and was not founded by him
2. Theodore's influence was at least equally important
3. The Church developed its own Syriac Christological synthesis
4. The label was primarily an external polemical designation
5. The modern Church officially rejects the label

**The most historically defensible formulation:** "The Church of the East, historically called 'Nestorian' by external opponents, was an East Syriac Christian tradition that received the theological legacy of Theodore of Mopsuestia and Nestorius while developing its own distinct Christological synthesis."

## 22. SOURCE DEPENDENCE

### 22A. Physical Independence

| Source | Independence |
|--------|-------------|
| SRC-THEO-005 (Chalcedon) | Independent document |

| SRC-THEO-007 (Cyril) | Independent document |

All five sources are physically independent documents from different authors/contexts.

### 22B. Literary Dependence

- Cyril quotes Nestorius (polemical context)
- Chalcedon quotes Cyril's Second Letter and Leo's Tome
- Nestorius responds to Cyril (Bazaar)
- Ephesus 431 acts incorporate both Cyril and Nestorius materials

### 22C. Theological Dependence

- Cyril influences Chalcedon (terminology, condemnation of Nestorius)
- Leo influences Chalcedon (two-natures language)
- Theodore influences Nestorius (teacher-student)
- Theodore influences Church of the East (via Ibas's Syriac translations)

### 22D. Institutional Continuity

- Church of the East → Assyrian Church of the East (institutional descent)
- Church of the East → Chaldean Catholic Church (partial institutional continuity, with Roman communion)

### 22E. Polemical Dependence

- Cyril's representation of Nestorius: polemical
- Nestorius's representation of Cyril: polemical
- Byzantine "Nestorian" label: polemical
- Modern scholarly rejection of label: corrective polemic

### 22F. Critical Observation

**Do NOT count multiple modern descriptions as independent historical witnesses.** The five primary sources used here (SRC-THEO-005 through SRC-THEO-009) are physically independent but literarily and polemically interdependent. They do not constitute five independent confirmations of any single proposition.
