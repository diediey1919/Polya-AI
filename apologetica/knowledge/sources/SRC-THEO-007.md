## Included Works & Transmission
This single source record groups three primary Christological documents of Cyril of Alexandria composed between 430 and 433 CE. They are grouped together because they form an integrated manuscript and conciliar transmission unit—all three were preserved and read in the official acts of Ephesus (431) and Chalcedon (451), and together they define Cyril's Christological trajectory from anti-Nestorian polemic to conciliar agreement.

### 1. Second Letter to Nestorius (*Epistola 4 ad Nestorium*)
* **Date:** February 430 CE
* **Recipient:** Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople
* **Language / Text:** Greek (Migne *PG* 77, Cols. 44–50; ACO I.1.1, pp. 25–28).
* **Transmission / Reception:** Formally read and approved at the Council of Ephesus (Session 1, June 22, 431) as the standard of Nicene orthodoxy; subsequently read and acclaimed at Chalcedon (Session 2, 451 CE).
* **Key Passages (Verified Greek / English):**
  * **Hypostatic Union:** σάρκα ἐμψυχωμένην ψυχῇ λογικῇ ἑνώσας ὁ Λόγος ἑαυτῷ καθ' ὑπόστασιν, ἀφράστως τε καὶ ἀπερινοήτως γέγονεν ἄνθρωπος ("the Word, having united to himself hypostatically flesh animated by a rational soul, in an ineffable and incomprehensible manner became man").
  * **No Removal of Difference:** οὐχ ὡς τῆς τῶν φύσεων διαφορᾶς ἀνῃρημένης διὰ τὴν ἕνωσιν ("not as if the difference of the natures were taken away by the union").
  * **Theotokos:** οὕτω τεθαρσήκασι Θεοτόκον εἰπεῖν τὴν ἁγίαν Παρθένον ("thus they [the holy Fathers] have ventured to call the holy Virgin *Theotokos*").

### 2. Third Letter to Nestorius with Twelve Anathemas (*Epistola 17 ad Nestorium*)
* **Date:** November 430 CE (synodal letter from the Synod of Alexandria)
* **Recipient:** Nestorius of Constantinople
* **Language / Text:** Greek (Migne *PG* 77, Cols. 105–121; ACO I.1.1, pp. 33–42).
* **Transmission / Reception:** Appended twelve explicit anathemas (*Twelve Chapters*) demanding Nestorius's signature; read at Ephesus (431) and included in conciliar acts, though not formally incorporated into the short Ephesine decree; intensely debated by Antiochene theologians (Theodoret of Cyrrhus, Andrew of Samosata); later cited at Constantinople II (553 CE).
* **Key Passages (Verified English):**
  * **Anathema 1 (Theotokos):** "If anyone does not confess that Emmanuel is God in truth, and therefore that the holy virgin is the mother of God (*Theotokos*)... let him be anathema."
  * **Anathema 2 (Hypostatic Union):** "If anyone does not confess that the Word from God the Father has been united by hypostasis (*kat' hypostasin*) with the flesh and is one Christ with his own flesh... let him be anathema."
  * **Anathema 3 (Rejecting Mere Conjunction):** "If anyone divides in the one Christ the hypostases after the union, joining them only by a conjunction (*synapheia*) of dignity or authority or power... let him be anathema."
  * **Anathema 12 (Impassible Suffered):** "If anyone does not confess that the Word of God suffered in the flesh and was crucified in the flesh and tasted death in the flesh... let him be anathema."

### 3. Letter of Reunion / Formula of Reunion 433 (*Epistola 39 ad Joannem Antiochenum* / *Laetentur caeli*)
* **Date:** April 433 CE
* **Recipient:** John, Patriarch of Antioch
* **Language / Text:** Greek (Migne *PG* 77, Cols. 173–182; ACO I.1.4, pp. 15–20).
* **Transmission / Reception:** Reconciled Alexandria and Antioch after the 431 Ephesine schism; incorporates the Antiochene draft text (*Formula of Reunion*) as Cyril's accepted confession; read and approved at Chalcedon (451 CE) alongside Cyril's Second Letter.
* **Key Passages (Verified English):**
  * **Two Natures Union:** "For the union (*henosis*) of two natures has been accomplished. Hence we confess one Christ, one Son, one Lord."
  * **Double Homoousios:** "consubstantial (*homoousios*) with the Father in respect of His divinity and at the same time consubstantial with us in respect of His manhood."
  * **Distinguishing Expressions:** "...recognize that theologians employ some [statements] indifferently in view of the unity of person (*hos eph' henos prosōpou*) but distinguish others in view of the duality of natures (*hos epi dyo physeōn*)..."

## Evidence Boundary & Epistemic Scope
* **Can Establish:** The primary fifth-century Alexandrian Christological formulation defining the hypostatic union (*henōsis kat' hypostasin*), defending *Theotokos* as a Christological boundary term, insisting on a single divine-human subject of predication, utilizing *mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē* in an Alexandrian framework, and accepting two-nature terminology in the 433 Formula of Reunion.
* **Cannot Establish:** That Cyril's 5th-century Greek vocabulary is automatically identical to Chalcedonian terminology in every respect, that *Theotokos* proves post-patristic Marian dogmas, or that Cyril's patristic exegesis dictates the 1st-century original grammar of John 1:1.
