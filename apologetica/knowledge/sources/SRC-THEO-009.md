The Acts of Ephesus 431 are a conciliar document set — proceedings, decrees, canons, imperial correspondence, rival assembly records — that cannot be reduced to any single participant's theological corpus. They constitute a procedural and institutional record layer absent from SRC-THEO-007 and SRC-THEO-008.

## 3. DOCUMENT COMPONENTS

### 3.1 Conciliar Sessions (Cyril's Assembly)

| Session | Date | Key Content |
|---------|------|-------------|
| I | 22 June 431 | Opening; three summonses to Nestorius; reading of Cyril's Second Letter; reading/condemnation of Nestorius's letter; Cyril's Third Letter read; patristic florilegium; decree of deposition; 198 bishops sign |
| II | 10 July 431 | Arrival of papal legates (Arcadius, Projectus, Philip); Celestine's second letter read; retroactive confirmation of Session I |
| III | 11 July 431 | Legates review Acts; formally confirm Nestorius's condemnation; priest Philip's Petrine primacy speech |
| IV | 16 July 431 | Cyril and Memnon present formal protest against John of Antioch's counter-council; summons to John refused |
| V | 17 July 431 | Third canonical summons to John; John + 34 bishops excommunicated (suspended, not fully deposed); council claims 210 bishops |
| VI | 22 July 431 | Nicene Creed exclusivity decree (no other creed permitted); case of Nestorianizing priests; Messalian condemnation |
| VII | 31 July 431 | Cyprus autonomy approved; six canons promulgated |

### 3.2 Rival Assembly Records (John of Antioch's Council)

* Arrival: 26–27 June 431
* Composition: ~43–53 bishops
* Decrees: Deposition of Cyril and Memnon; conditional excommunication of all Cyrillian participants until they reject Twelve Anathemas
* Duration: June 26 – late July 431

### 3.3 Imperial Correspondence

* Theodosius II's convocation letter (19 November 430)
* Imperial instructions to Count Candidianus
* Theodosius's rescripts during the council (annulment, counter-orders)
* Final dissolution order and deposition of Nestorius, Cyril, and Memnon

### 3.4 Canons

* Six canons from Session VII
* Canon IV: Pelagian condemnation
* Canon VII: Nicene exclusivity reinforcement
* Canons against Nestorian adherents

### 3.5 Post-Council Documents

* Letters to Emperor Theodosius II
* Letters to clergy and people of Constantinople
* Cyril's Letter 96 (bribery documentation)
* Formula of Reunion (April 433)

## 4. SOURCE CRITICAL NOTES

### 4.1 Acts as "Publizistische Sammlungen"
Graumann (2020, 2021) demonstrates that the Acts are NOT neutral minutes. They are *publizistische Sammlungen* — collections with propagandistic purpose, designed to present each side's case to the emperor. Both Cyril and John's parties produced documentation. The Acts were "exercises in textual practices" from commencement — works-in-progress, not finalized minutes.

### 4.2 Multiple Versions
Greek, Latin, and Coptic versions preserve different details. The Coptic Acts uniquely report:
* Abbot Victor as imperial agent (vs. Candidianus in Greek acts)
* Nestorius and Cyril arriving on the same day
* Vesper service incident
* Deposition notice affixed to Nestorius's door

### 4.3 Third Letter Question
Graumann (2020) argues Cyril's Third Letter (with Twelve Anathemas) is unlikely to have been OFFICIALLY read or approved at Session I, despite appearing in the Acts. No evidence of formal conciliar pronouncement of its orthodoxy exists in Session I proceedings. This became critical at Chalcedon 451, where the Third Letter was deliberately OMITTED.

### 4.4 Schwartz ACO Citation Format
* ACO I.1.5:45 = Tome I, Volume 1 (Greek), Part 5, page 45
* Schwartz presented each version separately rather than attempting one definitive text
