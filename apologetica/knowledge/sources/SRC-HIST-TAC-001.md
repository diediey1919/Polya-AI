# SRC-HIST-TAC-001: Tacitus Annals 15.44

## Metadata
* **SOURCE_ID:** `SRC-HIST-TAC-001`
* **SOURCE_CATALOG_ID:** `HIST-TAC-001`
* **Author:** Publius Cornelius Tacitus (c. 56–120 CE).
* **Source Type:** ANCIENT HISTORICAL SOURCE (Non-Christian Roman Historiography).
* **Work:** *Annals* (15.44), composed c. 115–120 CE.
* **Event Referenced:** Great Fire of Rome (64 CE) and Nero's response.

## Evidence Nodes

### Evidence Node A: TACITUS-CHRISTUS-001
* **Passage Reference:** Annals 15.44.
* **Claim Boundary:** Tacitus references "Christus" (Christ) as the historical figure from whom "Christians" derive their name.
* **Confidence:** STRONG EVIDENCE.
* **Limitations:** Tacitus is not contemporary with Christ; transmission is medieval; "Christus" is reported via Roman administrative/popular understanding.

### Evidence Node B: TACITUS-ROME-001
* **Passage Reference:** Annals 15.44.
* **Claim Boundary:** Christians were present in Rome by the time of Nero's reign.
* **Confidence:** STRONG EVIDENCE.
* **Limitations:** Tacitus is a 2nd-century Roman historian writing about 1st-century events.

### Evidence Node C: TACITUS-NERO-001
* **Passage Reference:** Annals 15.44.
* **Claim Boundary:** Tacitus reports Nero's response to the Great Fire and the use of Christians as scapegoats, including extreme punishment.
* **Confidence:** STRONG EVIDENCE.
* **Limitations:** Roman rhetorical/polemical framing; details depend on later manuscript tradition.

### Evidence Node D: TACITUS-PILATE-001
* **Passage Reference:** Annals 15.44.
* **Claim Boundary:** Tacitus connects Christus with execution under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius.
* **Confidence:** STRONG EVIDENCE.
* **Limitations:** Tacitus relies on information source (administrative, popular, or prior Roman historian) that is not specified in the text.

## Methodological Limitations
* Tacitus is not contemporary with Jesus; Annals 15.44 was written c. 50+ years after the events.
* Surviving manuscripts are medieval (Second Medicean, 11th century), not Tacitus' autograph.
* Tacitus is not an eyewitness to the events he describes.
* The exact information source (Roman administrative records, public knowledge, prior historians) is uncertain.
* The passage does not establish resurrection, divinity, or revelation.
