# SRC-THEO-006: Leo's Tome (Epistle 28 to Flavian)

## Metadata
* **SOURCE_ID:** `SRC-THEO-006`
* **SOURCE_CATALOG_ID:** `THEO-LEO-TOME-449`
* **Title:** Leo's Tome / Epistle 28 to Flavian of Constantinople
* **Author / Attribution:** Pope Leo I (Leo the Great), Bishop of Rome
* **Date:** 13 June 449 CE
* **Recipient:** Flavian, Archbishop of Constantinople
* **Source Type:** Primary Patristic Theological Source / Dogmatic Letter (NOT a Biblical Manuscript, Autograph, Canon List, or Conciliar Definition).

## Evidence Boundary & Epistemic Scope
* **Can Establish:** Fifth-century Western Christological formulation articulating one Christ as true God and true man, with two natures (*natura divina*, *natura humana*) united in one person (*persona*) without confusion, change, division, or separation; employing Latin philosophical vocabulary (*forma Dei*, *forma servi*, *natura*, *persona*) to address the Eutychian controversy and prepare the ground for Chalcedonian orthodoxy.
* **Cannot Establish:** That Leo's Latin terminology is semantically identical in every respect to Greek Cappadocian or Cyrilline terminology; that Leo's formulation is verbally identical to New Testament Christology; that Leo single-handedly authored the Definition of Chalcedon.

## Historical Context
* **Occasion:** Response to the Eutychian controversy (448-449 CE). Eutyches, an archimandrite in Constantinople, taught that Christ had "two natures before the union but after the union one nature," effectively absorbing Christ's humanity into His divinity (a position later condemned as Monophysitism). Flavian, Archbishop of Constantinople, convened a local synod (November 448) that condemned Eutyches. Eutyches appealed to Pope Leo, who composed this letter to articulate orthodox Christology.
* **Historical Chain:**
  - Ephesus 431: Cyril vs. Nestorius; Theotokos affirmed; Nestorius condemned for dividing Christ.
  - Eutyches controversy 448-449: Eutyches denied Christ's full humanity post-incarnation.
  - Second Council of Ephesus (449): Dioscorus presided; Eutyches reinstated; Flavian deposed and died; later called "Latrocinium" (Robber Council).
  - **Leo's Tome (13 June 449):** Composed before Ephesus II but arrived too late to prevent its proceedings.
  - Chalcedon 451: Leo's Tome read and acclaimed as consonant with Cyril and orthodox Christology; Definition of Chalcedon drafted incorporating Leonine two-nature formula.

## Core Christological Formulation (Key Passages)
* **Two Natures in One Person:** "Without detriment therefore to the properties of either nature and substance which then came together in one person (*Salva igitur proprietate utriusque naturae et substantiae, et in unam coeunte personam*), majesty took on humility, strength weakness, eternity mortality."
* **True God and True Man:** "Thus in the whole and perfect nature of true man was true God born, complete in what was His own, complete in what was ours (*In integra ergo veri hominis perfectaque natura verus natus est Deus, totus in suis, totus in nostris*)."
* **Forma Dei / Forma Servi:** "He who while remaining in the form of God (*in forma Dei*) made man, was also made man in the form of a slave (*in forma servi*). For both natures retain their own proper character without loss (*Tenet enim sine defectu proprietatem suam utraque natura*)."
* **Communication of Idioms:** "For each form does what is proper to it with the co-operation of the other; that is the Word performing what appertains to the Word, and the flesh carrying out what appertains to the flesh (*Agit enim utraque forma cum alterius communione quod proprium est, Verbo scilicet operante quod Verbi est, et carne exsequente quod carnis est*)."
* **Against Eutyches:** "The impiety of saying that the Son of God was of two natures before His incarnation is only equalled by the iniquity of asserting that there was but one nature in Him after the Word became flesh (*cum tam impie duarum naturarum ante incarnationem unigenitus Dei Filius fuisse dicatur, quam nefarie postquam Verbum caro factum est, natura in eo singularis asseritur*)."

## Transmission & Authenticity
* **AUTHENTICITY:** ESTABLISHED. Leo's Tome is multiply attested in conciliar acts (ACO), Latin patristic collections, and cited at Chalcedon 451 where it was read, translated into Greek, and acclaimed by the bishops as orthodox and consonant with Cyril's letters.
* **Transmission:** Complete Latin text survives; Greek translations circulated at Chalcedon; standard critical editions available (Silva-Tarouca 1932; NPNF Series II Vol. 12).
* **Reception at Chalcedon:** The bishops acclaimed: "This is the faith of the fathers! This is the faith of the Apostles! Peter has spoken through Leo!" (ACO II.1.2, sess. II).

## Source Classification
* **Primary Patristic Theological Source:** Direct witness to 5th-century Western Christological formulation.
* **NOT a Biblical Manuscript:** Leo's Tome is a dogmatic letter interpreting Scripture; it is not itself a New Testament manuscript, autograph, or canon list.
* **NOT a Conciliar Definition:** Leo's Tome is a papal letter; the Definition of Chalcedon (SRC-THEO-005) is a separate conciliar document that received Leo's formulation as orthodox but drafted its own technical Greek formula.
