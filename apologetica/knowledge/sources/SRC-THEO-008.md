## 1. SOURCE IDENTITY & TRANSMISSION

### 1A. BAZAAR OF HERACLEIDES (Liber Heraclidis / Tegurta d'Heraqleidus)
* **Work:** *The Book Which Is Called the Bazaar of Heracleides of Damascus* (Greek original title likely *Pragmateia* / "Treatise")
* **Date:** Composed 451 or 452 CE (post-Chalcedon; after death of Theodosius II, 28 July 450)
* **Place:** Great Oasis of Hibis (al-Khargah), Egypt — written in exile
* **Original Language:** Greek (LOST)
* **Transmission Language:** Syriac (sole surviving translation)
* **Genre:** Theological autobiography and apologetic treatise
* **Authenticity:** ESTABLISHED (with scholarly caveats on interpolations; George A. Bevan 2013 identifies possible second-half 5th-century interpolations in Syriac translation)
* **Transmission Status:** 
  * Greek original: DESTROYED / NOT EXTANT
  * Syriac manuscript: One primary copy preserved in Church of the East monastery at Qudshanis, Hakkari
  * Additional copies surfaced in Urmia (19th century)
  * 1843 massacre damage: 133 pages lost (~1/6 of work)
  * Original 16th-century manuscript destroyed 1915 (Assyrian genocide)
* **Scholarly Editions:**
  * Bedjan, P., Nau, F., & Brière, M. (eds.), *Nestorius - Le livre d'Héraclide de Damas* (Paris: Letouzey et Ané, 1910) — Syriac text
  * Driver, G. R. & Hodgson, L. (eds.), *Nestorius: The Bazaar of Heracleides* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1925) — English translation from Syriac (criticized as somewhat inaccurate; generally aimed at conveying sense rather than word-for-word)
* **Pseudonym:** "Heracleides of Damascus" — translator's preface states this was done "lest since his own name was a bugbear to many, they should be unwilling to read it and be converted to the truth"
* **Limitations:** 
  * Survives only in Syriac translation, not original Greek
  * Translation acknowledged as sometimes inaccurate
  * Manuscript damage (missing pages, reordered sections)
  * Possible later interpolations
  * Composed in exile under house arrest — apologetic context shapes presentation
  * Nestorius may have refined or rehabilitated his earlier positions in retrospect
* **Reliable Source:** YES, with qualifications above. This is Nestorius's own mature self-presentation, but mediated through Syriac translation and composed decades after the 428–431 controversy.

## 2. NESTORIUS'S OWN CHRISTOLOGICAL EVIDENCE

### 2A. PROSOPON AND UNION
**Direct Quotation (Bazaar of Heracleides, Syriac translation):**
> "I predicate two natures, that he indeed who is clothed is one and he wherewith he is clothed another, and these two prosopa of him who is clothed and of him wherewith he is clothed; but thou also confessest 'of two natures', neither of them known without prosopon and without hypostasis in the diversities of the natures, with no two prosopa of the sons conceived nor again two prosopa of the men, but of one man who is moved in the same manner by the other, for the union of the prosopa took place for the prosopon and not for the ousia and the nature, not that one ousia without hypostasis should be conceived, as if by union into one ousia with no prosopon of one ousia, but the natures subsist in their prosopa and in their natures and in the prosopon of the union, for in respect to the natural prosopon of the one the other also makes use of the same on account of the union; and thus one prosopon of the two natures, the prosopon of the one ousia makes use of the prosopon of the other ousia in the same way."

**Key Terminology:**
* **Prosopon (πρόσωπον):** For Nestorius, the "observable character, defining properties, manifestation of a reality" (McGuckin's definition)
* **Two prosopa:** Divine and human prosopa each subsist in their respective natures
* **One prosopon of union:** The two prosopa coalesce in one prosopon (Christ)
* **Not for ousia/nature:** Union occurs at the level of prosopon, not essence/nature
* **Synapheia (συνάφεια):** "Connection" or "conjunction" — Nestorius's preferred term for union mode
* **Voluntary union:** Union of godhead and manhood is voluntary, not natural composition

### 2B. EIGHT POINTS FROM BAZAAR (Hodgson's Summary)
1. Denies that unity of Christ is a "natural composition" combining two elements by external creator's will
2. Denies that Incarnation was effected by changing godhead into manhood or vice versa, or by forming a *tertium quid* from two ousiai
3. Denies that God was in Christ in same way as in saints
4. Denies that either godhead or manhood of Christ are "fictitious" or "phantasmal"
5. Denies that Incarnation involved any change in godhead, or any suffering on part of Divine Logos (who, as divine, is by nature impassible)
6. Denies that union of two natures in one Christ involves any duality of sonship
7. Asserts that union is voluntary union of godhead and manhood
8. Asserts that principle of union is found in prosopa of godhead and manhood — these two prosopa coalesced in one prosopon of Christ incarnate

### 2C. THEOTOKOS POSITION
**Nestorius's Own Statement (Sermon XVIII, fragment):**
> "If the babe and the Lord of the babe are one and the same person, the suspected phrase 'inhabitant of the babe' must be simply intended as a safeguard against identification of the Word with the flesh. So Mary is Theotokos – because the Word was united to the temple… which is in nature consubstantial with the holy Virgin… It is in virtue of this union that the holy Virgin is called Theotokos."

**Position Summary:**
* **Did NOT categorically reject Theotokos** — accepted it in qualified sense
* **Preferred Christotokos** (Χριστοτόκος, "Christ-bearer") as more precise
* **Also proposed dual title:** *Theotokos kai Anthropotokos* ("God-bearer and Man-bearer")
* **Objection:** Not to title itself, but to confusion of natures — God (as eternal, impassible) cannot be "born" in divine nature; birth pertains to humanity
* **Key Distinction:** Mary bore Christ according to humanity; Word was united to human nature but did not suffer birth in divinity
* **NOT:** "Mary denied that Mary was mother of Jesus" — this is a hostile caricature
* **NOT:** Absolute denial that Mary could be called Theotokos — Nestorius's own evidence shows qualified acceptance

### 2D. TWO NATURES AFFIRMATION
**Direct Evidence:**
* Explicitly affirms two natures (divine and human) in Christ
* Explicitly denies "Two Sons" teaching
* Explicitly denies Apollinarianism (mixing/confusion of natures)
* Explicitly affirms full reality of both natures (neither fictitious nor phantasmal)
* Maintains distinction between divine and human predicates
* Asserts that each nature retains its complete characteristics

### 2E. CHRISTOLOGICAL UNITY
**Nestorius's Evidence:**
* One Christ (not two Christs)
* One Son (not two Sons)
* One prosopon of union
* Two natures subsisting in their respective prosopa
* Voluntary union, not natural composition
* Real incarnation (not mere indwelling as in saints)
* Distinction of predicates according to nature

**Key Distinction from Cyril:**
* Nestorius: Union at level of prosopon (prosopic union)
* Cyril: Union at level of hypostasis (hypostatic union: *henōsis kat' hypostasin*)
* Nestorius: *synapheia* (conjunction)
* Cyril: *henōsis* (union)

## 3. CYRIL'S REPRESENTATION OF NESTORIUS

### 3A. CYRIL'S ACCUSATIONS (Second Letter to Nestorius)
* Nestorius divides Christ into two sons
* Nestorius teaches mere conjunction (*synapheia*) of dignity/authority, not hypostatic union
* Nestorius's formulation risks adoptionism
* Nestorius's Christology undermines reality of Incarnation
* Theotokos must be defended as boundary of orthodoxy

### 3B. TWELVE ANATHEMAS (430 CE)
* Anathema 1: Targets refusal to confess Mary as Theotokos
* Anathema 2: Targets refusal to confess hypostatic union of Word with flesh
* Anathema 3: Targets division of hypostases after union, joining only by conjunction of dignity/authority/power
* Anathema 12: Targets refusal to confess Word of God suffered in flesh

**Critical Question:** Do Cyril's anathemas accurately represent Nestorius's own position, or do they present a polemical caricature?

## 4. CONCILIAR CONDEMNATION (EPHESUS 431)

### 4A. HISTORICAL SEQUENCE
* **428 CE:** Nestorius becomes Archbishop of Constantinople
* **428–430 CE:** Theotokos controversy erupts
* **430 CE:** Cyril writes to Pope Celestine; Nestorius writes to Celestine
* **430 CE:** Celestine delegates to Cyril authority to excommunicate Nestorius if he does not recant within 10 days
* **430 CE:** Cyril sends Third Letter with Twelve Anathemas
* **Summer 431 CE:** Emperor Theodosius II convokes Council at Ephesus
* **June 22, 431 CE:** Cyril opens council before Antiochene delegation arrives
* **June 22, 431 CE:** Nestorius condemned and deposed (did not attend)
* **Late June 431 CE:** John of Antioch and Eastern bishops arrive; convene counter-council, depose Cyril
* **Imperial Response:** Initially ordered both Nestorius and Cyril deposed
* **433 CE:** Formula of Reunion reconciles Cyril and John of Antioch; Nestorius abandoned by Antiochene supporters
* **435 CE:** Imperial edict exiles Nestorius to Great Oasis of Hibis, Egypt
* **451 CE:** Council of Chalcedon (Nestorius already in exile; dies shortly after)

### 4B. CONCILIAR JUDGMENT
* **Cyril's Second Letter:** Declared in agreement with Nicaea
* **Nestorius's Second Letter to Cyril:** Condemned as contrary to Nicaea
* **Outcome:** Nestorius deposed and declared heretic
* **Imperial Enforcement:** Theodosius II enforces deposition; later exiles Nestorius

### 4C. NESTORIUS'S OWN ACCOUNT (Bazaar)
* Claims he never had fair hearing
* Claims condemnation was political, not theological
* Summarizes: "Ephesus is Cyril" (reduces council to Cyril's dominance)
* Records betrayal by Antiochene supporters (including mentors, former students, friends)
* Expresses satisfaction at Chalcedon's vindication of Flavian and Leo's Tome
* Accepts personal anathema for sake of preserving dyophysite theology: "But as for Nestorius, let him be anathema"

## 5. LATER NESTORIAN / EAST SYRIAC RECEPTION

### 5A. CHURCH OF THE EAST (Historical)
* Never accepted Nestorius's condemnation
* Venerated Nestorius as one of three "Greek Teachers" (with Diodore of Tarsus and Theodore of Mopsuestia)
* East Syriac Eucharistic Service incorporates prayers attributed to Nestorius
* Preserved Bazaar of Heracleides in monastery libraries
* 14th-century Ebed Jesu lists Bazaar among Nestorius's works

### 5B. ASSYRIAN CHURCH OF THE EAST (Modern)
* Descendant of historical Church of the East
* Reveres Nestorius as saint
* Modern church does NOT subscribe to entirety of "Nestorian doctrine" as traditionally understood in West
* Rejects that Nestorius taught any heresy
* Patriarch Dinkha IV repudiated exonym "Nestorian" in 1976

### 5C. CHALDEAN CATHOLIC TRADITION
* In communion with Rome
* Distinct from Assyrian Church of the East
* Historical connection to East Syriac tradition but different ecclesial status

### 5D. CRITICAL DISTINCTION
**These are historically distinct categories:**
1. Nestorius himself (historical person, 428–451 CE)
2. "Nestorianism" as polemical label (applied by opponents)
3. Church of the East (historical ecclesial body, Persian Empire)
4. Assyrian Church of the East (modern denomination)
5. Chaldean Catholic tradition (modern, in communion with Rome)

**Governance:** Do NOT assume Nestorius = Nestorianism = Church of the East = all East Syriac Christology.

## 6. MODERN SCHOLARLY REASSESSMENT

### 6A. KEY SCHOLARS
* **James Franklin Bethune-Baker (1908):** *Nestorius and His Teaching: A Fresh Examination of the Evidence* — argued Nestorius was substantially orthodox
* **Friedrich Loofs (1914):** *Nestorius and his Place in the History of Christian Doctrine* — critical edition and analysis
* **Milton V. Anastos (1962):** "Nestorius Was Orthodox" (*Dumbarton Oaks Papers* 16: 117–140)
* **Aloys Grillmeier (1975):** *Christ in Christian Tradition* — balanced historical analysis
* **John A. McGuckin (1994):** *St. Cyril of Alexandria* — contextualizes controversy
* **Sebastian P. Brock (1996):** "The 'Nestorian' Church - A Lamentable Misnomer" — distinguishes Nestorius from Church of the East
* **Susan Wessel (2004):** *Cyril of Alexandria and the Nestorian Controversy* — political dimensions
* **George A. Bevan (2009, 2013):** Identifies interpolations in Syriac Bazaar

### 6B. SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS / DISAGREEMENT
* **Agreement:** Bazaar discovery (19th century) renewed scholarly interest; Nestorius's own evidence must be distinguished from Cyril's polemic
* **Disagreement:** Whether Nestorius was substantially orthodox (Bethune-Baker, Anastos) or genuinely problematic (Chapman, traditional view)
* **Middle Position:** Nestorius's formulations were ambiguous/imprecise, not necessarily heretical in intent, but genuinely problematic in effect (Grillmeier, McGuckin)
* **Critical:** Do not assume modern scholarly consensus exists on Nestorius's orthodoxy — scholarship remains divided

## 7. TERMINOLOGY AUDIT

### 7A. NESTORIUS'S VOCABULARY
* **Physis (φύσις):** Nature — Nestorius affirms two physes (divine and human)
* **Ousia (οὐσία):** Essence — Nestorius uses to distinguish divine and human essences
* **Hypostasis (ὑπόστασις):** For Nestorius, closely aligned with physis (not identical to Cyril's usage)
* **Prosopon (πρόσωπον):** For Nestorius, the "observable character, defining properties, manifestation of a reality" — key term for union
* **Synapheia (συνάφεια):** Conjunction — Nestorius's preferred term for union mode
* **Theotokos (Θεοτόκος):** God-bearer — Nestorius accepts in qualified sense
* **Christotokos (Χριστοτόκος):** Christ-bearer — Nestorius's preferred title
* **Anthropotokos (Ἀνθρωποτόκος):** Man-bearer — Nestorius proposes as complementary to Theotokos

### 7B. CYRIL'S VOCABULARY
* **Physis (φύσις):** Nature — Cyril uses in *mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē* ("one incarnate nature of God the Word")
* **Hypostasis (ὑπόστασις):** For Cyril, the concrete subject of union (*henōsis kat' hypostasin*)
* **Henōsis (ἕνωσις):** Union — Cyril's preferred term (not synapheia)
* **Theotokos (Θεοτόκος):** God-bearer — Cyril insists as non-negotiable boundary

### 7C. TERMINOLOGICAL MISMATCH
* Nestorius and Cyril used same Greek terms with different semantic ranges
* Nestorius's *hypostasis* ≈ Cyril's *physis* (both pointing to concrete reality)
* Nestorius's *prosopon* ≠ Cyril's *hypostasis* (different levels of union)
* **Critical:** Do not impose later Chalcedonian terminology onto Nestorius's 5th-century Antiochene vocabulary

## 8. EVIDENCE BOUNDARY & EPISTEMIC SCOPE

### 8A. CAN ESTABLISH
* Nestorius's own surviving Christological formulations (from Bazaar and fragments)
* Distinction between Nestorius's self-presentation and Cyril's polemic
* Nestorius's qualified acceptance of Theotokos
* Nestorius's explicit denial of "Two Sons" teaching
* Nestorius's two-nature Christology (prosopic union)
* Historical sequence of controversy and condemnation
* Transmission status of sources (Syriac mediation, hostile preservation)

### 8B. CANNOT ESTABLISH
* Whether Nestorius's earlier (428–431) formulations were more problematic than his later (451) Bazaar presentation
* Whether Nestorius's Christology is "orthodox" or "heretical" (this is theological judgment, not historical evidence)
* Whether Chalcedon "vindicated" Nestorius (Chalcedon upheld his condemnation while adopting dyophysite language)
* Whether Church of the East's Christology is identical to Nestorius's own
* Whether Nestorius's prosopic union is metaphysically adequate (this requires philosophical/theological analysis, not historical evidence alone)
* That Nestorius's patristic exegesis dictates 1st-century biblical grammar (Nestorius's interpretation is reception history)
