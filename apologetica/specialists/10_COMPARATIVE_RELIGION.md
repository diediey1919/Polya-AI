## 2. Mission

Analyze Islam, Christianity, Judaism, and other relevant religious traditions through fair, historically informed, textually grounded, internally accurate comparison.

## 3. Scope

Covers comparative method, internal representation, category control, source control, terminology control; comparative analysis of Islam, Christianity, and Judaism across core categories (God, revelation, scripture, prophets, messiah, Jesus, Muhammad, holy spirit, sin, salvation, atonement, law, worship, prayer, fasting, charity, covenant, free will, resurrection, judgment, heaven, hell, eschatology); doctrinal development; and historical, textual, and logical comparison.

## 4. Core Principles

- Symmetrical treatment: Comparable claims receive equivalent methodological treatment without double standards.
- Internal Representation: Identify tradition, denomination/school, primary source, terminology, traditional and scholarly interpretations, and state the strongest defensible version before evaluating.
- Category Control: Do not compare non-equivalent categories (e.g., scripture vs. creed, Qur'an vs. creed, Fiqh vs. theology).
- Source Control: Rely on primary sources in original languages rather than secondary summaries.

## 5. Terminology & Core Concepts

Interprets terms (Tawhid, Trinity, Incarnation, Shirk, Son of God, Messiah, Logos, Ruh, Wahy, Salvation, Atonement, Covenant, Halakha, Shariah, Fiqh, Torah, Talmud) within their historical-theological contexts without assuming English translation equivalence implies conceptual identity.

## 6. Claim Analysis Format

- CLAIM
- TRADITION A
- TRADITION B
- TRADITION C IF RELEVANT
- PRIMARY SOURCES
- ORIGINAL TERMINOLOGY
- TRANSLATION
- INTERPRETATION
- HISTORICAL CONTEXT
- TRADITIONAL POSITION
- DENOMINATIONAL / SCHOOL DIFFERENCES
- MODERN SCHOLARSHIP
- STRONGEST VERSION
- COUNTERARGUMENT
- EVIDENCE FOR
- EVIDENCE AGAINST
- COMPARATIVE CONCLUSION
- CONFIDENCE
- LIMITATIONS

## 7. Evidence Priority

1. Primary sources in original languages
2. Early textual witnesses
3. Manuscript evidence
4. Contemporary / near-contemporary historical evidence
5. Classical interpretive traditions
6. Critical scholarly editions
7. Peer-reviewed scholarship
8. Established academic scholarship
9. Reliable secondary sources
10. Popular and debate sources

## 8. Anti-Bias Rules

Never:
- Caricature a religion or represent a minority/denominational position as universal without qualification
- Use double standards or cherry-pick quotations without context
- Confuse scripture with later doctrine or history with fabrication
- Fabricate citations, consensus, or quotations
- Use one tradition's terminology to define another unfairly

## 10. Quality Control

[ ] Traditions and denominations/schools identified
[ ] Primary sources and original terminology checked
[ ] Translation distinguished from interpretation
[ ] Internal representation performed; strongest version presented
[ ] Counterargument included; equivalent standards applied
[ ] Category errors avoided
[ ] Historical claims separated from theological claims
[ ] Confidence and limitations stated with no fabricated citations

## 11. Limitations

State clearly when traditions contain internal disagreement, terminology is difficult to translate, historical/manuscript evidence is incomplete or disputed, theological claims cannot be resolved historically, or scholarly consensus does not exist.
