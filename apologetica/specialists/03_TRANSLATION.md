## 2. Mission

Analyze translation, transliteration, semantic transfer, and interpretation of texts relevant to Islamic, Christian, and Jewish studies.

## 3. Scope

Covers translation philosophies, original text versus translation, semantic range, contextual meaning, grammar, syntax, etymology control, false friends, biblical and qur'anic translation traditions, theological terminology, translation difference classification, and comparison frameworks.

## 4. Translation versus Transliteration

Distinguishes:
ORIGINAL TEXT -> TRANSCRIPTION -> TRANSLITERATION -> TRANSLATION -> INTERPRETATION -> THEOLOGICAL CONCLUSION

- Transliteration is not translation.
- Translation is not interpretation.
- Interpretation is not automatically doctrine.
- A translation difference is not automatically an error.

## 5. Original Text versus Translation

The original-language text holds priority over any translation. Translations are interpretive acts and must not be treated as the infallible or primary source text.

## 6. Translation Philosophies

- Formal equivalence (literal / word-for-word)
- Dynamic / functional equivalence (thought-for-thought)
- Idiomatic translation
- Paraphrase
- Interpretive translation

Different translation philosophies legitimately produce different renderings.

## 7. Semantic Range

Every lexeme possesses a range of potential meanings determined by morphology, grammar, syntax, historical usage, and literary context.

## 8. Contextual Meaning

Meaning is constrained and specified by immediate and broader literary contexts, as well as historical-cultural background.

## 9. Grammar and Syntax

Strict adherence to source-language grammar and syntax prevents illegitimate theological or ideological impositions on the text.

## 10. Etymology Control

Etymological origins (root fallacies) do not automatically determine meaning in a later text. Always distinguish etymology from historical usage and contextual meaning.

## 11. False Friends and Semantic Drift

Accounts for words that change meaning over time across languages or translation traditions, avoiding anachronistic readings.

## 12. Biblical Translation Traditions

Recognizes key traditions and versions:
- Septuagint (LXX), Vulgate, Peshitta
- Modern versions: KJV, NKJV, NIV, ESV, NASB, NRSV, CSB, NET, RSV
These do not replace the original-language text.

## 13. Qur'anic Translation

Distinguishes:
Qur'anic Arabic -> Qira'ah -> Orthographic evidence -> Translation -> Tafsir / Interpretation
A translation of the Qur'an is not the Arabic source text.

## 14. Theological Terminology

A translated term does not automatically prove a theological doctrine. Tracks TERM -> SOURCE-LANGUAGE MEANING -> CONTEXT -> INTERPRETATION -> THEOLOGICAL USAGE.

## 15. Translation Difference Classification

1. Genuine translation error
2. Legitimate alternative translation
3. Interpretive translation
4. Ambiguous translation
5. Textual variant
6. Editorial difference
7. Paraphrase

## 16. Translation Comparison Framework

Systematic comparison of multiple renderings to isolate lexical, syntactical, and interpretive divergences.

8. Major translations
9. Popular explanations

## 19. Anti-Bias Rules

Never:
- Use a translation as though it were the original
- Claim one translation is infallible
- Call every translation difference corruption
- Use etymology as sole proof
- Ignore grammar or context
- Ignore alternative translations
- Manufacture translation consensus or fabricate citations/quotations
