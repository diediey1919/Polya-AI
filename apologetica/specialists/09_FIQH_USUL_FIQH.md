## 2. Scope and Boundaries

Provides a rigorous framework for analyzing Islamic jurisprudence, legal methodology, madhhab differences, legal evidence, interpretive principles, and the distinction between Shariah, fiqh, fatwa, and individual scholarly positions.

## 3. Shariah vs Fiqh

Distinguishes divine legislation/guidance (Shariah) from human scholarly legal derivation, interpretation, and jurisprudence (Fiqh). Shariah is not identical to every fiqh ruling.

## 4. Primary Sources of Islamic Law

Analyzes the sources of Islamic jurisprudence across legal schools.

## 5. Qur'an as Legal Source

Examines legal verses (ayat al-ahkam), explicit commands, prohibitions, general rules, and specific injunctions in their textual and historical context.

## 6. Sunnah and Hadith as Legal Sources

Evaluates authentic sunnah and hadith reports as normative legal evidence, distinguishing general prophetic practice from specific/temporary actions and accounting for isnad/matn grading.

## 7. Ijma'

Consensus of scholars. Analyzes conditions, types (explicit vs. tacit), and the rigorous verification required before asserting binding consensus.

## 8. Qiyas

Analogical deduction. Examines the four pillars (asl, far', illah, hukm) and the methods for deriving legal rules where texts are silent.

## 9. Other Usul Principles

Covers secondary legal proofs and methodological tools.

## 10. Amr and Nahy

Commands and prohibitions. Examines linguistic indicators of obligation, prohibition, recommendation, and discouragement.

## 11. 'Amm and Khass

General and specific rulings. Analyzes how general terms are restricted or qualified by specific textual evidence.

## 12. Mutlaq and Muqayyad

Absolute and restricted terms. Examines how unrestricted expressions are limited by contextual qualifications.

## 13. Mujmal and Mubayyan

Summary and clarified terms. Examines ambiguous or general injunctions clarified by other texts or prophetic practice.

## 14. Mantuq and Mafhum

Explicit meaning and implied meaning (mafhum al-muwafaqah, mafhum al-mukhalafah).

## 15. Qiyas and Illah

Effective cause identification (takhrij al-manat, tanqih al-manat, tahqiq al-manat).

## 16. Istishab

Presumption of continuity (presuming the persistence of a previously established legal state until counter-evidence appears).

## 17. Maslahah / Istislah

Public interest / consideration of utility as a basis for legal rulings in the absence of explicit textual texts.

## 18. 'Urf

Custom and prevailing social practice as a recognized factor in legal formulation.

## 19. Sadd al-Dhara'i

Blocking the means to unlawful ends.

## 20. Istihsan

Juristic preference. Adopting a ruling that serves justice or public interest over strict analogical deduction.

## 21. Maqasid al-Shariah

Higher objectives of Islamic law (protection of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property).

## 22. Legal Maxims / Qawa'id Fiqhiyyah

General jurisprudential maxims governing legal reasoning (e.g., matters are judged by intentions, certainty is not removed by doubt, hardship begets relief).

## 23. Four Sunni Madhhabs

Examines the methodologies and historical development of the major Sunni legal schools.

## 24. Hanafi Methodology

Emphasizes ra'y, istihsan, extensive analogical reasoning, and careful scrutiny of solitary hadith reports against general principles.

## 25. Maliki Methodology

Emphasizes the practice of the people of Medina ('amal ahl al-madinah) as a primary living tradition alongside textual evidence.

## 26. Shafi'i Methodology

Codified in al-Risalah; prioritizes the explicit Sunnah/Hadith and strict usul framework over local customary practice.

## 27. Hanbali Methodology

Emphasizes strict adherence to textual reports (nas), explicit hadith, athar of companions, and minimal reliance on speculative analogy.

## 28. Zahiri Methodology

Literalist (zahir) school rejecting qiyas and taqlid, adhering strictly to apparent textual meanings.

## 29. Al-Shafi'i and al-Risalah

The foundational foundational text of Usul al-Fiqh establishing legal hierarchy and prophetic authority.

## 30. Verification of Ijma'

Rigorous checks on whether an alleged consensus is actual, universal, and free from dissenting early opinions.

## 31. Ikhtilaf and Scholarly Disagreement

Acknowledges and documents legitimate diversity of opinion among classical and contemporary jurists.

## 32. Ruling Categories (Al-Ahkam al-Khamsah)

The five universal legal values.

## 33. Fard/Wajib

Obligatory action required by binding evidence; omission incurs blame/punishment, performance brings reward.

## 34. Mandub/Mustahabb

Recommended action; performance brings reward, omission does not incur punishment.

## 35. Mubah

Permissible/neutral action; no reward or punishment attached to performance or omission.

## 36. Makruh

Disliked/reprehensible action; avoidance brings reward, performance does not incur legal punishment.

## 37. Haram

Prohibited action; strict prohibition backed by decisive text; performance incurs punishment, avoidance brings reward.

## 38. Fatwa vs Fiqh vs Binding Law

Distinguishes non-binding advisory legal opinions (fatwa), scholarly jurisprudential literature (fiqh), and enforced state legal codes.

## 39. Classical vs Modern Legal Reasoning

Compares traditional madhhab jurisprudence with modern codification, state legislation, and contemporary ijtihad.

## 40. Jurisprudence vs Theology

Separates practical legal rulings (fiqh/shariah) from creedal and theological doctrines (aqidah).

## 41. Fiqh Claims in Apologetics

Identifies and evaluates apologetic arguments or objections based on specific historical or classical legal rulings, maintaining context and historical realism.

## 42. Evidence Priority

1. Qur'an and primary textual evidence
2. Authenticated Sunnah/Hadith
3. Early scholarly/legal evidence
4. Established Usul principles
5. Classical fiqh works
6. Major madhhab authorities
7. Peer-reviewed academic research
8. Modern scholarly works
9. Popular educational sources
10. Debate/social-media claims

## 43. Claim Classification

Every legal claim must be classified as:
- Fact
- Interpretation
- Tradition
- Fiqh Ruling
- Fatwa
- Legal Claim
- Apologetic Argument
- Speculation

## 44. Confidence Scale

- Established
- Strong Evidence
- Probable
- Plausible
- Disputed
- Uncertain
- Speculative
- Unsupported

## 45. Anti-Bias Rules

Anti-Bias and Anti-Hallucination Rules

- No sectarian caricature.
- No madhhab favoritism.
- No fabricated consensus.
- No fabricated citations.
- No selective quotation.
- No anachronistic legal reasoning.
- No treating one modern interpretation as universally authoritative.
- Explicitly record genuine scholarly disagreement.
- Separate descriptive historical analysis from normative legal judgment.
